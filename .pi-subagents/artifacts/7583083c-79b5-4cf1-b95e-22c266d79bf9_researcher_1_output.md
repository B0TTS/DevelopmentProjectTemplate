# Research: Drake (Aubrey Drake Graham) — The "Drake Melody" Hit Formula

**Lane:** Drake (OVO Sound/Republic) — most Billboard Hot 100 entries in history; the genre-hopping melodic-rap-singing hit machine.

## Summary

Drake's repeatable method, documented across 15 years of interviews, is: **melody first, words second, recorded the same night the beat arrives, tested in the room, and shipped fast.** He works with a rotating army of producers (Boi-1da, Noah "40" Shebib, Gordo, Southside, PartyNextDoor, Bnyx, Tay Keith, Vinylz, OZ, Elkan…), who send him beats; he mostly ignores submissions until one hits, then he locks himself in and lays a hook + first verse in one sitting ("five songs in one night" — Gordo). Hooks are built on short, repeated, singable phrases over **minor-key** production ("40 produces in minor and I'll find the melodies to slip around it" — Drake), with emotion as the quality gate ("that it penetrates"). His words serve the melody — conversational, direct-address, low literary density — and his biggest 2020-2025 records ("Slime You Out", "First Person Shooter", "Rich Baby Daddy", "Nokia") all follow the same skeleton: a proven or sampled hook melody, a sparse beat that clears space for the vocal, and a fast release cadence that converts cultural moment into chart moment (7 No. 1s in 2020-2025; 13 career No. 1s through 2025).

## 2) The core repeatable workflow/formula (idea → finished master)

Synthesized from producer/engineer interviews and Drake's own accounts. This is the pattern that recurs across *every* era, including 2020-2025:

**Step 1 — Beats come to him, batch-style.** Drake doesn't sit with producers making beats; producers send packs. Gordo: Drake's request is literally "send me some stuff"; Gordo + collaborators (Richard Zestenker, Johannes Klahr) made **10-18 beats** for one song ("Rich Baby Daddy") and shipped them. Boi-1da: "I may pull up stuff and Drake will pick something or he'll have something he wants me to work on." [Complex/Gordo](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/gordo-drake-for-all-the-dogs-interview); [REVOLT/Boi-1da](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2021-10-28/126267/studio-sessions-boi-1da-talks-making-hits-with-drake-nfts-and-his-upcoming-album)

**Step 2 — The silent audition.** "The process is sending him stuff and him not saying anything and then sending him stuff and him being like, 'oh my God, this is it.'" (Gordo). Boi-1da: he never suggests anything to Drake — "you just give him what he needs and it's over from there." High rejection, instant acceptance. [Complex/Gordo](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/gordo-drake-for-all-the-dogs-interview); [REVOLT/Boi-1da](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2021-10-28/126267/studio-sessions-boi-1da-talks-making-hits-with-drake-nfts-and-his-upcoming-album)

**Step 3 — Melody + hook + first verse in one sitting, same night.** The beat arrives → Drake records immediately. Gordo made "Sticky" on a plane, texted Drake, and "by the time he replied at 4pm I was already in the kitchen at his house. I played him the beat and he was like, 'Woah'... **They recorded that night.**" [GQ/Gordo](https://www.gq-magazine.co.uk/culture/article/drake-honestly-nevermind-gordo-club-music-influences). Same pattern in 2012: "The Motto" — T-Minus voice-clipped him a beat, Drake "did the verse and I did the hook" on the spot. [Rap Radar/RESPECT 2012](https://rapradarpodcast.substack.com/p/lost-words-2012-drake-talks-take). God's Plan: "a song that I recorded on a whim in my condo... I only had the first verse and the hook." [HowToRap transcription](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/drake-teaches-how-to-write-a-melody-in-3-steps-7fb12d2f427c)

**Step 4 — Solo writing, machine pace.** Gordo: "He usually just locks himself up and does everything by himself. And in the studio he just does everything and then **you just wake up and he does like five songs in one night**... He's a mad man. He doesn't sleep... He'll call me at 9 a.m., 10 a.m. in the morning." [Complex/Gordo](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/gordo-drake-for-all-the-dogs-interview)

**Step 5 — Low discard rate; the demo unit is hook + verse.** "If I'm gonna go as far as to track over a beat I usually have the utmost faith in it that it'll end up somewhere. I don't really dispose of too many songs" (Drake, 2013). God's Plan leaked with only a hook + verse and Drake "had to rush and turn it around" — the song was finished because the room demanded it. [HowToRap](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/drake-teaches-how-to-write-a-melody-in-3-steps-7fb12d2f427c)

**Step 6 — The room test.** After laying a record, Drake plays it loud and watches: "the girls that just walked in the room. Are they going to start dancing to it right when they walk in? Do my friends already know the lyrics if I played it like six, seven times? Just little things, so I know this might be one of those ones." [Rap Radar/RESPECT 2012](https://rapradarpodcast.substack.com/p/lost-words-2012-drake-talks-take)

**Step 7 — Features and finishes are last-minute.** "First Person Shooter" was **recorded October 5, 2023 — the day before *For All the Dogs* dropped**; Drake confirmed J. Cole's verse hours before release ("fourth quarter magic", "we just got out from the studio"). [Wikipedia/FPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Person_Shooter_(song)). Album structure is decided late: he moved "Needle" (Nicki Minaj) off FATD because it didn't fit sonically. [Wikipedia/FATD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_All_the_Dogs)

**Step 8 — Engineering chain (fixed team):** Noel Cadastre records; **40 mixes** (Her Loss credits: 40 mixed every track; the album was delayed when 40 got COVID mid-mix); Chris Athens masters. SSS4U is the first Drake album **without 40 at all** — recorded mostly at Sanctuary Studios (Nassau) and S.O.T.A. (Florida/Toronto). [Wikipedia/Her Loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Loss); [Wikipedia/SSS4U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Some_Sexy_Songs_4_U)

**Step 9 — Ship fast, on cultural timing.** Surprise drops (HN, DLDT), holiday-aligned drops (Valentine's Day SSS4U), and tour-adjacent albums (FATD "developed mostly during the It's All a Blur Tour"). [Wikipedia/FATD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_All_the_Dogs)

**The rotating team (per-album evidence):** CLB: 40, Nineteen85, PartyNextDoor, OZ, Vinylz, Metro Boomin, TM88, WondaGurl, Travis Scott. HN: Gordo (6 tracks), 40, Black Coffee (exec), &Me, Rampa, Klahr. Her Loss: Boi-1da, Metro Boomin, OZ, Tay Keith, Vinylz, Wheezy, F1lthy, Lil Yachty. FATD: 40, Boi-1da, Sango, Southside, Bnyx, Gordo, Lil Yachty, OZ, The Alchemist, Conductor Williams, DJ Screw (posthumous). SSS4U: PartyNextDoor, Noel Cadastre, Gordo, Jordan Ullman (Majid Jordan), Elkan, Niketaz. [Wikipedia album pages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certified_Lover_Boy)

## 3) Lyric method

- **Top-down, phrase-first.** Every 2020-2025 hit's hook is a repeated catchphrase that functions as the song's title: "Slime You Out", "First Person Shooter", "Rich Baby Daddy", "Nokia", "Gimme a Hug", "Toosie Slide", "Way 2 Sexy", "Rich Flex", "Jimmy Cooks". The hook line is the seed; verses fill around it.
- **Conversational direct address, not literature.** Drake writes to a "you" (usually a woman, sometimes the industry): low metaphor density, real-time thought ("stream of consciousness"), brand names and memes as texture. Critics have flagged the shallowness openly — Rolling Stone on FPS called his bars "desultory lyrics where he discusses women like he's stocking a meat freezer." [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-album-reviews/drake-for-all-the-dogs-review-1234848477/). The words are optimized to ride the melody, not to read well on Genius.
- **Writes on his phone, in the booth, not on paper.** 2010: "It shouldn't be your tagline, I don't even write... It's an individual creative process, there's no standard... **I'll pull out my phone and do that in front of everybody.** It's just about quality man and taking pride in yourself." [MTV 2010 via HowToRap](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/drake-teaches-how-to-write-a-melody-in-3-steps-7fb12d2f427c). Corroborated by the "whim" origin of God's Plan and "The Motto" (verse + hook written in the room in minutes).
- **Never repeats himself sonically.** "I don't wanna sound like No New Friends, I don't wanna sound like I'm On One. I did that already." (CRWN, 2015). Each project requires a new lane — which explains the genre-hopping: R&B-trap (CLB), house (HN), Atlanta drill (Her Loss), Miami bass (FATD), ringtone-pop (SSS4U). [CRWN via HowToRap](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/drake-teaches-how-to-write-a-melody-in-3-steps-7fb12d2f427c)
- **Emotion is the quality gate, not craft.** "There's points where you feel something and that's all that matters to me, that it penetrates." [Q TV 2013 via HowToRap](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/drake-teaches-how-to-write-a-melody-in-3-steps-7fb12d2f427c)
- **Inner-circle input.** Rich Paul pushed "Best I Ever Had" as the first single; manager Oliver El-Khatib feeds him playlists ("He gives me music that no-one else listens to, so that music combined with the extreme hood shit that I listen to like Lil Herb... I balance it all out"). [CRWN via HowToRap](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/drake-teaches-how-to-write-a-melody-in-3-steps-7fb12d2f427c)
- **Revision discipline: low.** Documented approach is fast completion, not iteration: "A lot of my hit songs I've written in a very short period of time. 'Hold On, We're Going Home,' I felt like we finished that in like two hours." When something is wrong he *knows* and scraps it rather than fixing it: "A lot of artists don't even know when they sound wrong, or off... if you don't know when something's wrong, you're not gonna be one of the greats." [MTV 2010 via HowToRap](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/drake-teaches-how-to-write-a-melody-in-3-steps-7fb12d2f427c)
- **Collab/credit reality (documented facts, no taboos):**

## 4) Topline / singing method (how the hook is built and sung)

- **Melody over minor-key beds.** "I do shy away from major, like major chords, major chord progressions. Those are the classic pop, those aren't the melodies that hit you in your heart. For me, it's minor. 40 produces in minor and I'll find the melodies to slip around it." [Q TV 2013 via HowToRap](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/drake-teaches-how-to-write-a-melody-in-3-steps-7fb12d2f427c)
- **The "Drake melody" device:** a short phrase repeated 2-4x with a tiny melodic arc (usually 2-3 notes), delivered in a sing-speak hybrid — rhythm like speech, pitch like a nursery rhyme, so the listener can reproduce it after one play. Examples: "Slime You Out" (title phrase as descending refrain), "Rich Baby Daddy" (chant-title + Sexyy Red's "Looking for the Hoes" interpolation), "Nokia" (the ringtone's 3-note motif sung/rapped over an Atlanta bass beat), "Toosie Slide" ("Slide, slide, slide" as dance instruction), "Way 2 Sexy" (Right Said Fred's chorus sung straight). Hooks are built to be bellowed back at stadiums. [HowToRap analysis](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/drake-teaches-how-to-write-a-melody-in-3-steps-7fb12d2f427c)
- **Lazy, conversational delivery as the default.** Chest-voice half-singing with murmured, off-the-beat phrasing; he snaps into full singing for emotional peaks (the SZA duet sections, HN's dance tracks, "Nokia"'s second half where "the beat stutters and slows into a crunchy groove and Drake raps in a Whodini-type flow"). [Pitchfork on Nokia](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/tracks/drake-partynextdoor-nokia/)
- **Pre-proven melodies as hooks.** A core 2020-2025 trick: anchor the hook on a melody the public already knows — Right Said Fred ("Way 2 Sexy"), Florence + the Machine's "Dog Days Are Over" ("Rich Baby Daddy"), the Nokia ringtone ("Nokia"), Daft Punk's "One More Time" ("Circo Loco"), K.P. & Envyi's "Swing My Way" as the bounce blueprint for "Rich Baby Daddy" (Gordo: "I was like, no one's done that in a long time"). [Complex/Gordo](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/gordo-drake-for-all-the-dogs-interview); [Wikipedia/Rich Baby Daddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rich_Baby_Daddy)
- **Vocal texture:** doubled/layered choruses and ad-libbed asides are audible on the records (observable, not producer-documented in this run); heavy autotune as an *instrument* on HN/SSS4U (observable). Producer Elkan contributes "androgynous vocals" and background vocals on "Nokia" to thicken the top line. [Wikipedia/Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia_(song))
- **The writer-count tells the story:** "Nokia" has exactly **2 writers** (Drake + Elkan) — one singer, one beatmaker, no co-writer room; "First Person Shooter" has 9 writers (Drake, J. Cole + 7 producers/sample writers); "Rich Baby Daddy" has 13 (including Florence Welch and Isabella Summers for the interpolation). Toplines are Drake's; credits balloon with sample/interpolation rights and producer contributions. [Wikipedia/Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia_(song)); [Wikipedia/FPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Person_Shooter_(song)); [Wikipedia/Rich Baby Daddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rich_Baby_Daddy)

## 5) Vocal-anchoring production (what the track does around the vocal)

Brief — production only as scaffolding for the hook:

- **Sparse, minor, sample-based beds that leave air for the voice.** Drake's description of 40's method: "He wouldn't just let a beat sit. I would leave the studio and by the time I came back the next day, he'd be like 'I added some shit'... He's just been so amazing at making a piece grow or shrink... He was the mastermind behind the music." [VIBE 2013](https://www.vibe.com/gallery/outtakes-drake-talks-beyonce-trophies-and-next-album/)
- **One famous melodic element + one groove, then get out of the way.** "Slime You Out": a looped "Just Ask Me" (The Soul Superbs) vocal sample with soft drums so the Drake/SZA duet sits on top. "First Person Shooter": warped Joe Washington vocal sample + a down-pitched orchestral loop ("Redemption"), with a beat switch to host two rappers. "Nokia": flashy synth (the ringtone) + "sweaty Atlanta bass beat" (Pitchfork). "Rich Baby Daddy": Miami-bass bounce interpolating "Dog Days Are Over". [Wikipedia/Slime You Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slime_You_Out); [Wikipedia/FPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Person_Shooter_(song)); [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/tracks/drake-partynextdoor-nokia/)
- **The mix is the finish line, and it's 40's.** Her Loss's official delay reason was 40 contracting COVID during mixing/mastering; Chris Athens masters. [Wikipedia/Her Loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Loss); [Wikipedia/Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia_(song))

## 8) "The trick" in their words

**Drake's own statement of the trick** (closest thing he's said to a formula, Q TV 2013): **make the melody slip around a minor-key bed and make it penetrate emotionally** — "Is anything too sugary? It just has to have emotion behind it... that it penetrates." Combined with his other recurring principle: **know instantly when it's right, don't overthink, record fast** — "A lot of this is organic, it's not that calculated" (God's Plan).

**Is it repeatable?** Yes — and the evidence is the record run itself: the same skeleton (minor beat → short repeated hook phrase → sung/rapped same night → room test → fast release) produced 7 No. 1s and ~40 top-10s in 2020-2025. What is *not* replicable is the layer underneath: a decade-deep producer bench, instant cultural-timing release machinery, and a catalogue that makes every drop an event. Gordo's formulation of the actual mechanic: "send him stuff and him not saying anything and then... 'oh my God, this is it'" — i.e., the bar is brutal, and once it clears, the song is finished in hours, not weeks.
