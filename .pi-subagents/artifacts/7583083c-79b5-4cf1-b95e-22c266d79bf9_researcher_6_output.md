# Research: Jack Antonoff — "In the Room" Producer-Writer (Bleachers → Taylor Swift / Lana Del Rey / Sabrina Carpenter)

## Summary
Jack Antonoff's repeatable method is **co-writing in the room, not track-and-hook**: he sits on the same side of the glass as the artist, starts sessions with emotional excavation ("What's the worst thing that's ever happened to you?"), builds loops live on vintage synths while the artist sings over them, records first takes and punch-in vocals with engineer Laura Sisk, and wraps the vocal in 80s-indebted drums/strings/synth pads that frame the singer rather than stamp a brand ("Production is the *idea*"). The transferable Bleachers→pop-star seam is that Bleachers is his sonic laboratory — the 80s reverb, Juno/LinnDrum palette, sax/strings, and "unearned hope" choruses are rehearsed there and then re-deployed *behind* another artist's voice, with lyric control staying with the artist. Verified 2020–2025 receipts: three consecutive Producer of the Year Grammys (2022–2024), 13 career Grammys, co-producer/co-writer on "Anti-Hero" (8 weeks at #1 Hot 100), the first-ever Hot 100 top-10 sweep (Midnights), and Sabrina Carpenter's first #1 ("Please Please Please").

## 1) Verified credentials & 2020–2025 relevance (Billboard Hot 100 facts)

1. **Three straight Producer of the Year (Non-Classical) Grammys: 2022, 2023, 2024** — first producer to win three consecutive since Babyface's 1995–97 run; total of 13 Grammys including Album of the Year for *1989*, *folklore*, and *Midnights*. [Pitchfork: "Jack Antonoff Wins Producer of the Year... for Third Straight Year" (Feb 2024)](https://pitchfork.com/news/2024-grammys-jack-antonoff-wins-producer-of-the-year-non-classical-for-third-straight-year/) · [Wikipedia: Jack Antonoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Antonoff)
2. ***Midnights* (2022)**: co-wrote 11 of 13 and co-produced all 13 standard tracks with Swift; recorded at Electric Lady and Antonoff's Rough Customer studio (Brooklyn); best-selling US album of 2022; **the album's songs made Swift the first artist to occupy all top-10 slots of the Hot 100 simultaneously** (chart dated Nov 5, 2022); won Album of the Year at the 2024 Grammys. [Wikipedia: Midnights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnights) · [Wikipedia: Jack Antonoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Antonoff)
3. **"Anti-Hero" (2022)**: written/produced by Swift + Antonoff; **8 weeks at #1 Hot 100** (6 consecutive + 2 in Jan 2023); biggest opening day in Spotify history at the time (17.4M plays); best-selling song of 2022 in the US; Antonoff built the track on an OB-8 and a **tremolo-processed LinnDrum loop**. [Wikipedia: Anti-Hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Hero_(song))
4. **Other Antonoff-affiliated Hot 100 #1s in the 2020s**: "All Too Well (10 Minute Version)" (2021), "Is It Over Now?" (2023), "Fortnight" (2024), "Cruel Summer" (2019 cut that hit #1 in 2023 via the Eras Tour), plus Kendrick Lamar's "Squabble Up" and "Luther" (2024–25) — he co-wrote/produced 11 of 12 *GNX* tracks. [Wikipedia: Jack Antonoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Antonoff)
5. **Sabrina Carpenter, "Please Please Please" (2024)**: produced by Antonoff (written with Carpenter + Amy Allen, recorded at Electric Lady); became **Carpenter's first Hot 100 #1** (June 29, 2024 chart), a week after "Espresso" peaked at #3; Grammy Song of the Year nominee. **Correction for the LANE brief: "Espresso" is NOT an Antonoff production** — it was written by Carpenter, Amy Allen, Steph Jones and Julian Bunetta, and produced by Bunetta. [Wikipedia: Please Please Please](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Please_Please_Please_(Sabrina_Carpenter_song)) · [Wikipedia: Espresso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espresso_(Sabrina_Carpenter_song)) · [Variety: "How Sabrina Carpenter's 'Please Please Please' Was Written in One Day"](https://variety.com/2024/music/news/how-sabrina-carpenter-please-please-please-written-in-one-day-1236235829/)
6. **Lana Del Rey, *Did You Know That There's a Tunnel Under Ocean Blvd* (2023)**: Antonoff co-produced (brought in near the end of production); album hit #3 Billboard 200 and earned an Album of the Year nomination; "A&W" never entered the Hot 100 proper (peaked at #5 Bubbling Under, #10 Hot Rock & Alternative Songs) — the song's status is critical, not chart, which itself is an Antonoff-era hallmark. [Wikipedia: Ocean Blvd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Did_You_Know_That_There%27s_a_Tunnel_Under_Ocean_Blvd)
7. **Lorde, *Solar Power* (2021)**: co-written/produced by Lorde + Antonoff (with Malay); #1 in Australia/NZ, top-10 in the UK/US (peaked #5 Billboard 200); polarizing reviews — the key documented case of the method's *limits* when the artist's material doesn't land. [Wikipedia: Solar Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Power_(album))

## 2) Core repeatable workflow — the in-room method

1. **Sessions are conversational and on the same side of the glass.** "An Antonoff session is a looser, more intimate thing. You walk into a studio and sit and schmooze for a while, on the same side of the glass... At some point, without drawing too much attention to it, he might drift toward a Wurlitzer, or start messing with a drum machine. Maybe you lean back on the couch and share a feeling you've been having, or a throwaway lyric, or a general vibe, and he builds on that." [The New Yorker: "Jack Antonoff's Gift for Pop-Music Collaboration" (May 2022)](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift)
2. **He is an anti-track-and-hook producer.** He doesn't send beats to stars or produce pre-written songs; he writes *with* the artist. The Ringer contrasts him with the Max Martin/Dr. Luke factory model: "Antonoff, on the other hand, describes his production style as less of a factory shift than a therapy session. He begins many writing sessions by asking the artist, 'What's the worst thing that's ever happened to you?' and then working from there... he prefers to be a part of the writing process alongside the artist, drawing deeper emotions out of them." [The Ringer: "The Cure for Dr. Luke?" (Nov 2017)](https://www.theringer.com/music/2017/11/30/16720386/jack-antonoff-dr-luke-taylor-swift-kesha-pink-pop-music)
3. **Emotional status-check before music.** "With someone like Claire or Ella [Lorde], the process usually starts with a lot of talking, seeing where they're at emotionally, before you start writing or recording anything." [The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift)
4. **Build in the room, let the song change shape.** The New Yorker documents a full Electric Lady session with Sam Dew: Antonoff set a simple keyboard voicing + drum-machine loop, the "magic moment" arrived ~15 minutes in when Dew sang a stepwise melody, and over the next hour Antonoff "darted from instrument to instrument — Mellotron, twelve-string guitar, live drums... tweaking some tiny elements, removing others... By the end of the session, all the instruments he'd started with were gone; the tempo and the chords remained the same, but the feel of the song had transformed almost entirely." Open-ended sessions: "We just see what we come up with, then later we decide where it wants to live." [The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift)
5. **Multiple songs per session.** "It's an iconic day in my life because we sat there and made 'Please Please Please,' 'Lie to Girls' and 'Slim Pickins' all in that one day — not finished, but the general feeling and writing and production." (Antonoff, on one day at Electric Lady with Sabrina Carpenter and Amy Allen) [Variety, Dec 2024](https://variety.com/2024/music/news/how-sabrina-carpenter-please-please-please-written-in-one-day-1236235829/)
6. **Phone voice-memos are a core artifact.** With Lana Del Rey: "Much of the record was written with Antonoff and Del Rey sitting alone at the piano for hours recording onto their phones... 'I would play stuff, and she would sing, and we'd talk about words... Lana would cut up the notes — she's got this weird editing system — and she'd grab the pieces she liked.'" [LA Times: "For today's confessional pop queens, Jack Antonoff is more than a producer: He's a confidant" (Dec 2019)](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2019-12-04/jack-antonoff-grammys-lana-del-rey-taylor-swift)
7. **First-take instrumental aesthetic is a trademark.** "His first-take instrumental style has become something of a trademark. 'Solar Power,' the Lorde record, is full of Antonoff's fingers squeaking across frets. On 'Sling,' the Clairo album, he is credited with playing eighteen instruments, some of which he'd never picked up before." [The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift) — and for the *Minions* soundtrack he recorded "like 80% in person," e.g., H.E.R. at Electric Lady: "I started playing drums, she started playing drums. That drum take is both of us playing drums on different parts." [Billboard, June 2022](https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/minions-rise-of-gru-soundtrack-jack-antonoff-interview-1235109164/)
8. **Bleachers is the laboratory; the pop sessions are the factory outlet.** "Bleachers' two albums don't feel like stand-alone experiences so much as sonic laboratories for the songs he'll later write with other people." [The Ringer, 2017](https://www.theringer.com/music/2017/11/30/16720386/jack-antonoff-dr-luke-taylor-swift-kesha-pink-pop-music) — the John Hughes/80s Springsteen palette is rehearsed in his own band, then applied to others.
9. **Fixed small crew**: engineer Laura Sisk works with him on nearly everything; they "communicate with a near-telepathic concision." [NYT Magazine, 2020](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2020/03/11/magazine/jack-antonoff-profile.html) · [The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift)
10. **Studios**: Electric Lady (basement room, NYC), his home studio in his Brooklyn Heights apartment, Rough Customer (Brooklyn, used for *Midnights*), Allaire (Catskills retreat for Clairo's *Sling*), Henson (LA, Lana sessions). Note: the "2088" home-studio name from the LANE brief could not be verified in indexed print sources (see Gaps). [LA Times 2019](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2019-12-04/jack-antonoff-grammys-lana-del-rey-taylor-swift) · [Wikipedia: Midnights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnights)

## 3) Lyric method — how he writes/co-writes with stars

1. **The method is excavation, not imposition.** "He describes his songwriting method as rooting out 'the saddest, most upsetting, most real things someone might go through, and then finding a way to sew those into pop songs.'" [The Guardian (Nov 2017)](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2017/nov/09/taylor-swift-producer-jack-antonoff-im-drawn-to-female-artists-who-are-brutally-honest) — In sessions: "I'll be, like, 'What about that thing you just told me about? What about that whole psychotic experience that so-and-so put you through?'" [LA Times, 2019](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2019-12-04/jack-antonoff-grammys-lana-del-rey-taylor-swift)
2. **Push the second verse deeper.** "When I work with other people, I'm always trying to find out: Where can we go even further? What can we do here that is for the people putting a microscope on it? In the second verse, can you fire out a few lines about something that happened to you when you were 9? ... 'All right, well let's write something,' and I'm like, 'Well let's write *that*!'" [Pitchfork interview (March 2017)](https://pitchfork.com/features/interview/10050-bleachers-jack-antonoff-is-your-favorite-pop-stars-secret-weapon/)
3. **Plain-spokenness is the house style.** "I personally gravitate towards some level of cutting the s— and saying it like it is... I just love when I'm being spoken to in a very unafraid way. I push myself to do that, so it's easy to push others to do that." [LA Times, 2019](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2019-12-04/jack-antonoff-grammys-lana-del-rey-taylor-swift) — and the universality rule: "That's what pop writing is — find something that no one can relate to and make it something that everyone can." [The Guardian, 2017](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2017/nov/09/taylor-swift-producer-jack-antonoff-im-drawn-to-female-artists-who-are-brutally-honest)
4. **Contribution rules: the artist's voice and story dominate.** "The women I work with are powerhouses. I'm not in there telling them what to do. I'm on a ride with them." [The Guardian, 2017](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2017/nov/09/taylor-swift-producer-jack-antonoff-im-drawn-to-female-artists-who-are-brutally-honest) · Lana: "Jack very much takes my lead... he liked for me to know where I was going." [NME Big Read, via BBC (Aug 2021)](https://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-58085468) · Lorde: "I haven't made a Jack Antonoff record... I've made a Lorde record and he's helped me make it and very much deferred to me." [Lorde to The Times (UK), quoted in The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift)
5. **He's a lyric *editor/tormentor*, not the lyricist.** The Miss Americana "Getaway Car" scene is the canonical example: Swift and Antonoff are stuck on a bridge-to-chorus quatrain; he sings a fragment ("I'm in a getaway car / And I'm losing my... something"), she counters ("I'm in a getaway car / You're in the motel bar"), he pushes ("Left you in the motel bar / Took the money..."), she completes it with the payoff: "Put the money in the bag and I stole the keys / That was the last time you ever saw me!" — the whole breakthrough takes ~25 seconds, with Antonoff pausing the track so ideas don't slip away. [The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift)
6. **With Bleachers he gets outside lyric help himself** — Zadie Smith fixed struggling lyrics on "91" and sang background vocals. [The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift) — proof the "who owns the words" rule inverts when he's the artist.
7. **Therapy-style word association as a writing tool**: in a 2023 Guardian interview the session devolves into a word-association game ("Mother." → "Want!") — "which is also how Antonoff approaches his songwriting." [The Guardian (Nov 2023)](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2023/nov/24/criticism-doesnt-keep-me-up-at-night-superproducer-jack-antonoff-on-his-haters-and-taylor-swifts-greatness)
8. **Lyric motifs that repeat across artists**: the back seat of a car ("I am very focused on the back seat as a metaphorical place" — songs about crying in taxis recur across his catalog). [LA Times, 2019](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2019-12-04/jack-antonoff-grammys-lana-del-rey-taylor-swift)

## 4) Topline/singing method — melodies made live in the room

1. **He writes toplines an octave above his own voice.** "I write a full octave above where I sing. I think about Kate Bush and those registers when I'm writing, because I always imagine that vocals should be dancing on top of the track. There's just a lot of melodic DNA that works better for women than men." [Pitchfork, 2017](https://pitchfork.com/features/interview/10050-bleachers-jack-antonoff-is-your-favorite-pop-stars-secret-weapon/)
2. **Guide vocals sung by Antonoff in falsetto during the session.** In the Dew session: Antonoff "threw out some revenge-themed lines in a pinched falsetto: 'You're not safe! In your home!' That's the cheesy version, but something in that zone." The topline then emerges by trial: "The two didn't need sentences anymore — they just sang little overlapping phrases, editing themselves as they went ('No, but...'; 'Closer'; '*That's* the one')." [The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift)
3. **Punch-in vocals are the documented recording technique.** Antonoff to Sisk mid-session: "Kill that wonky one, then punch me in on that high thing?" — punch-ins are how he comps guide vocals and stacks; on Dew's harmonies he asked "Are you tuning that?" and Sisk answered "Nope, that's just his voice." [The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift)
4. **Live, imperfect takes are prized over comped perfection.** Lana at Henson: "we had this one weird live take where she was singing along to a voice memo on her phone, with her headphones on, and I was playing piano latent to what she was singing, and we just both went, 'Yep, there it is — our one magic moment.'" [The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift) · Bleachers' "How Dare You Want More" kept the exact live guitar-sax take filmed at Electric Lady. [BBC, Aug 2021](https://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-58085468)
5. **Melody can hang on a single instrument discovery.** The *Solar Power* title track: Lorde brought a voice-memo sketch with the directive "It has to sound like skateboarding" — "it wasn't until Antonoff played it on a 1965 Fender Jaguar, which she said 'sounded like sunshine,' that the song came together." [The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift)
6. **Rhyme/payoff discipline — kept, not forced.** Sabrina/Amy Allen on "Please Please Please": "There's a key change, and it's, like, grandiose — it doesn't necessarily sound like a pop hit, you know?" and "We weren't particularly thinking about writing a hit." Antonoff on the session's reference frame: "We talked about references like ABBA, ELO, the Beatles and Dolly Parton, and how songs can go from, like, roller skating with a disco ball overhead and then five seconds later you're weeping in bed. That's my favorite kind of music — songs that really embody what the human mind is feeling." [Variety, Dec 2024](https://variety.com/2024/music/news/how-sabrina-carpenter-please-please-please-written-in-one-day-1236235829/)
7. **Vocal character beats vocal polish** — on "Anti-Hero," critics noted Swift's "out of breath" final choruses and "flustered, stammering" delivery were kept in the final production. [Variety / The Ringer, via Wikipedia: Anti-Hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Hero_(song))

## 5) Vocal-anchoring production — the Antonoff signature

1. **The philosophy**: "Production isn't someone with the coolest snare sound. Production is the *idea*... You think of a song as the most special thing in the world, and then the production holds it or it doesn't. And if it doesn't, it's horrible... If it does, it's beautiful, and it's protected." [LA Times, 2019](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2019-12-04/jack-antonoff-grammys-lana-del-rey-taylor-swift)
2. **The documented 80s palette (his own credits)**: "Please Please Please" — LinnDrum programming, Roland Juno-60, Prophet-5, Korg M1, Moog, bass, guitars, all played by Antonoff; "Anti-Hero" — LinnDrum with tremolo + OB-8 ("a sound that he compared to 'an old organ in a baseball stadium'"); *Midnights* demos built on Yamaha DX7 and Oberheim OB-8, final sounds from Moog/Mellotron/Juno-6 analog synths. [Wikipedia: Please Please Please](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Please_Please_Please_(Sabrina_Carpenter_song)) · [Wikipedia: Anti-Hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Hero_(song)) · [TIME, 2023 Person of the Year profile (as cited in Wikipedia)](https://time.com/6342806/person-of-the-year-2023-taylor-swift/)
3. **The signature described by critics**: "arpeggiated made-in-the-'80s synths, cavernous choruses, and routine moments of pin-drop intimacy" — and the unifying trait: "his ability to foreground the distinct personality of whoever he's working with." [The Ringer, 2017](https://www.theringer.com/music/2017/11/30/16720386/jack-antonoff-dr-luke-taylor-swift-kesha-pink-pop-music) · NME's summary: "'80s synths and megawatt choruses while pushing the focus onto intimate songwriting and honest lyrics — all while supporting them with effervescent instrumentals." [NME Big Read, 2021](https://www.nme.com/big-reads/bleachers-jack-antonoff-cover-interview-2021-take-the-sadness-out-of-saturday-night-3006942)
4. **Strings/brass as vocal halo**: Bleachers members (saxophonist Evan Smith, violinist Bobby Hawk) play on his productions — *Midnights* features subdued sax, clarinet, flute; "Please Please Please" credits violin and flute. Strings and horns swell *behind* the vocal rather than competing. [Wikipedia: Midnights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnights) · [Wikipedia: Please Please Please](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Please_Please_Please_(Sabrina_Carpenter_song))
5. **The "half modern, half super analog" rule**: for the *Minions* soundtrack he recorded "modern artists... with this half modern technique, half super analog technique." [Billboard, 2022](https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/minions-rise-of-gru-soundtrack-jack-antonoff-interview-1235109164/) — and the *Norman Fucking Rockwell!* pivot that defined the 2020s sound: "There were just sets of instruments around and we were like, 'Screw it, let's make some sounds... Let's unplug the drum machine.'" [BBC, 2021](https://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-58085468)
6. **Reverb/spatial choices frame the voice**: the LA Times describes the "space-echo snare hits and reverby vocals" of "Lover"; the Drift's "Antonoffication" essay (critical view) catalogues "dry double-tracked vocals that sound like they are whispering directly into your ear" on *Sling* — intimacy as production. [LA Times, 2019](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2019-12-04/jack-antonoff-grammys-lana-del-rey-taylor-swift) · [The Drift: "Dream of Antonoffication" (July 2023)](https://www.thedriftmag.com/dream-of-antonoffication/)
7. **His self-description of the role**: "I like to be looking at the North Star with someone, and we're both trying to get there... It's a glimmer at first... Every day we inch forward." [LA Times, 2019](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2019-12-04/jack-antonoff-grammys-lana-del-rey-taylor-swift)

## 8) "The trick" in their words — repeatable vs. one-off

- **The repeatable part is the room, the craft, and the palette.** "There's craft and there's magic. I've got craft out the ass, but all that really matters is finding those rare magic moments." [The New Yorker, 2022](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2022/05/23/jack-antonoff-pop-music-collaboration-lorde-taylor-swift) — the craft (conversation → excavation → loop → topline in the room → punch-in takes → 80s frame) is the transferable system; the magic (a Dew melody at minute 15, a Lana voice-memo take) is explicitly *not* manufacturable. His own framing of the split: "We just see what we come up with, then later we decide where it wants to live" (open-endedness is the method, not indecision).
- **The one-off is the artist.** "I like to be looking at the North Star with someone, and we're both trying to get there." [LA Times, 2019](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/music/story/2019-12-04/jack-antonoff-grammys-lana-del-rey-taylor-swift) — the North Star changes per artist; the production is the container ("holds the song or it doesn't"). The *Solar Power* backlash and the "Antonoffication" essay (["hollow, cinematic bigness"](https://www.thedriftmag.com/dream-of-antonoffication/)) are the evidence for what happens when the container over-determines the song — critics accuse him of exactly the homogeneity he denies, and he counters: "Does *anyone* think that's true?" [The Guardian, 2023](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2023/nov/24/criticism-doesnt-keep-me-up-at-night-superproducer-jack-antonoff-on-his-haters-and-taylor-swifts-greatness)
- **Practical rule he states for choosing collaborators (repeatable filter):** "If I ever work with someone else, all that I think about is: *Do you want to make the best album you've ever made in your life, or not?*" [Pitchfork, 2017](https://pitchfork.com/features/interview/10050-bleachers-jack-antonoff-is-your-favorite-pop-stars-secret-weapon/)
