# Research: Tyler, The Creator — Songwriting/Production Formula (LANE 14)

## Summary
Tyler Okonma is the rare pop-adjacent auteur who writes, produces, and arranges entire albums alone (IGOR, CMIYGL, CHROMAKOPIA are all solely self-produced), with a stated, repeatable priority order: **beat first, melody second, lyrics last**. His topline method is a decade-long arc from voice-hating rapper ("I hate rapping" — Larry King, 2015) to the sung-hook artist who now claims "I genuinely think I'm better at my R&B singing s–t as a whole than my rap s–t" (Billboard, Dec 2024) — while using featured vocalists (Kali Uchis, Daniel Caesar, Teezo Touchdown) precisely as tonal contrast against his own low, worn-in voice. The 2024–25 proof: "St. Chroma" (No. 7) and "Noid" (No. 10) were his **first-ever Hot 100 top 10s**, and CHROMAKOPIA earned an Album of the Year nomination plus a Best Album Cover win at the 68th Grammys.

## Findings

### 1) Verified credentials & 2020–2025 relevance (Billboard + Grammys, verified)
1. **Grammy record (per Grammy.com artist page): 3 wins / 11 nominations.** Wins: Best Rap Album — *IGOR* (62nd, 2020); Best Rap Album — *CALL ME IF YOU GET LOST* (64th, 2022); Best Album Cover — *CHROMAKOPIA* (68th, Feb 2025). 2025-era nominations: Album of the Year (CHROMAKOPIA), Best Rap Album (CHROMAKOPIA), Best Rap Performance ("Darling, I" w/ Teezo Touchdown), Best Rap Song ("Sticky"), plus Best Alternative Music Album for *DON'T TAP THE GLASS*. [Grammy.com artist page](https://www.grammy.com/artists/tyler-creator/6629/)
2. **First-ever Hot 100 top 10s, Nov 2024:** "St. Chroma" (feat. Daniel Caesar) **debuted at No. 7** — the week's most-streamed song in the U.S. (24.3M streams in just 4 tracking days) and Tyler's first No. 1 on the Streaming Songs chart — while "Noid" surged 43→10. Prior career high was "EARFQUAKE" at No. 13 (2019). [Billboard Hot 100 column, Nov 4, 2024](https://www.billboard.com/lists/shaboozey-a-bar-song-tipsy-ties-decade-hot-100-number-one-16-weeks/)
3. **CHROMAKOPIA commercial peak:** debuted No. 1 on Billboard 200 with 299,500 units in a *four-day* partial week (released Monday Oct 28, 2024, vs. industry-standard Friday), his best week ever and the year's biggest rap debut; held No. 1 three straight weeks. Third consecutive No. 1 (after IGOR and CMIYGL). [Billboard chart-beat](https://www.billboard.com/music/chart-beat/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-debuts-no-1-billboard-200-1235818700/) / [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-cover-story-1235850306/)
4. **"See You Again" TikTok-era rechart (verified):** originally peaked at No. 13 *Bubbling Under* (2017) and missed the Hot 100; after going viral on TikTok (the "OK OK OK / la la la" overlap trend) plus a Coachella duet with Kali Uchis, it debuted at **No. 65 (Apr 29, 2023)** and peaked at **No. 44 (May 13, 2023)** — belatedly the biggest Hot 100 hit from *Flower Boy*. [Billboard Pro: Trending Up](https://www.billboard.com/pro/tyler-the-creator-see-you-again-trending-up/) / [Wikipedia song page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/See_You_Again_(Tyler,_the_Creator_song))

### 2) Core repeatable workflow/formula — chord-loop-first, lyrics last, album-as-movie, self-production
1. **Priority order is explicit and repeated across interviews (Broken Record, 2019):** "I like beat first, then melody, and then lyrics." On IGOR's opening track: "I'm working on this and I couldn't come up with words, but I had this melody…" (Lil Uzi Vert then freestyled the hook as a reference vocal: "Just lay that reference vocal down for me because your voice sounds good doing it"). [Genius article quoting Broken Record](https://genius.com/a/tyler-the-creator-explains-why-lyrics-are-less-important-than-beats-melody)
2. **Chord loops as the seed:** writing "Boredom" over a looped Stevie Wonder chord progression — "I figured out how the chords looped and just had it playing for hours in my room… I literally wrote that verse in like 10 seconds because of these questions and these legit feelings of boredom." [Billboard: Jerrod Carmichael Flower Boy conversation](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-jerrod-carmichael-video-justin-bieber-glitter-8095406/)
3. **He keeps songs he knows are "bad" if the sound is right** — "There are songs that I have that are horrible lyrically. Like really bad, but I just like the notes in the background… I love the beat, love the chords, and I just love the melody… Then I care about what the lyrics are. It's all based on sound." "I care about lyrics and wordplay. It's just number three." [Genius article](https://genius.com/a/tyler-the-creator-explains-why-lyrics-are-less-important-than-beats-melody)
4. **Full self-production as a brand:** IGOR was "fully self-produced, fully self-arranged" — his first No. 1 as a *solo* rap artist, a fact he insisted be printed on the cover: "me being adamant about having that on the cover… lets people know I'm not just doing voiceovers or a verse." [Apple Music/Zane Lowe IGOR interview transcript (Pickscribe)](https://pickscribe.com/v/5pIpVK3Gecg). CHROMAKOPIA likewise: "written, produced, and arranged by Tyler" on every track (all 14 tracks credited to Okonma). [Complex credits](https://www.complex.com/music/a/tracewilliamcowen/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-credits)
5. **Albums as sequenced worlds/characters ("movies"):** every era gets a persona (Wolf Haley, Tyler Baudelaire, St. Chroma) and a narrative spine — CHROMAKOPIA is a diary narrated by his mother, with tracks designed to segue ("Rah Tah Tah" ends on "I'm paranoid now…," flowing straight into "Noid"). Sequencing craft is explicit: "I think the way you end an album is so important!… I knew that's how I wanted to end it, with the synth, just letting n—as sit there and think about whatever the f–k just happened." [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-cover-story-1235850306/) / [Lampoon review of sequencing](https://lampoonmagazine.com/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-album-noir-music-rapper-horror-new-phase-turning-thirty/)
6. **Process evidence on tape:** the *Mask Is Off: Chromakopia* mini-doc shows him laying down "unflattering" scratch vocals, mouth-percussion/snap production, and live musicians tracking parts — "The album utilizes live instrumentation and the clip shows multiple musicians laying down their parts." [XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-behind-the-scenes/)
7. **Workfast-and-tight rollouts (repeatable):** IGOR announced ~10 days before release, CMIYGL ~11, CHROMAKOPIA ~12, all dropping on his own schedule (Monday morning for CHROMAKOPIA). [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/tyler-the-creator-noid-celebrity-culture-1235140842/)

### 3) Lyric method — emotional specificity, sound-driven, slow revision
1. **Songs are autobiographical time capsules, not audience-engineering:** "Everything is self-indulgent to me" (on making songs); "It's people saying that they can't relate to the song [Noid]… Of course you can't. That's why I made the song, because you don't know what it's like…" [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-cover-story-1235850306/)
2. **Honesty requires a character/mask:** "It's so honest that I think I had to wear a mask on my own face to get that s–t out" (CHROMAKOPIA, after-release show in Atlanta, quoted in Billboard). CHROMAKOPIA = "the 30s album" — "This album is probably s–t that folks go through at 24, but I've lived a different life." [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-cover-story-1235850306/)
3. **The famous bittersweet mood line:** "I'm lonely, but I'm having the most fun of my goddamn life" (Flower Boy conversation) — the tonal template of his love songs (longing + self-aware humor + specific detail). [Billboard: Jerrod Carmichael conversation](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-jerrod-carmichael-video-justin-bieber-glitter-8095406/)
4. **Chord-loop-driven vowels — partially attested:** the *attested* version is sound-over-words: he listens "to the flow or the melody and the runs second. That's what really makes me know. Then I care about what the lyrics are." He frames the voice as an instrument that must *fit the beat*: Jay-Z "knows how to hear a beat and say, 'I shouldn't yell on this… this is how my vocal instrument can mesh on this perfectly.'" [Genius article](https://genius.com/a/tyler-the-creator-explains-why-lyrics-are-less-important-than-beats-melody). His own practice: pitch-shifting his voice up (2008) and down (2009–10) to fit sounds, then a "fake accent" on "I Ain't Got Time" — i.e., he shapes vocal delivery to the chordal mood, not the other way around. [Billboard: Jerrod Carmichael conversation](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-jerrod-carmichael-video-justin-bieber-glitter-8095406/)
5. **Revision over years (documented):** "See You Again" — instrumental from 2013 (Hodgy posted a snippet on Vine), written for Zayn Malik ("flaked on studio time twice," NME), recorded with Kali Uchis 2014, finished/released 2017, became a hit 2023. [Wikipedia song page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/See_You_Again_(Tyler,_the_Creator_song)) / [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/tyler-the-creator-zayn-malik-see-you-again-2141603). Same pattern: "Smuckers" built 2011 and offered to Jay-Z/Kanye, released 2015 ("They were going to use it, but I kind of took it back"); "Glitter" written for Justin Bieber, kept when he "never returned the call — F— it, I'll write it and just make it cool." [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/tyler-the-creator/smuckers) / [Billboard: Jerrod Carmichael conversation](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-jerrod-carmichael-video-justin-bieber-glitter-8095406/)

### 4) Topline/singing method — own sung hooks, melodic-rap topline, feature contrast
1. **The documented singing arc (verified across 5 primary interviews):**
   - 2015 (Larry King): "I hate rapping" — wants to sing; voice too deep, so studying Barry White and Isaac Hayes. [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/news/55447-tyler-the-creator-tells-larry-king-that-he-hates-rapping-says-he-wants-to-sing-more/)
   - 2018 (Jerrod Carmichael): "I've always hated my voice… Let me give myself a fake accent and let me start pronouncing shit." On Flower Boy: "My goal on this album was to shut the f— up and let the features be the leaders." [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-jerrod-carmichael-video-justin-bieber-glitter-8095406/)
   - 2019 (Zane Lowe, IGOR): "Honestly, I think I ruin songs. I hate my voice… when you want to write singing songs like Stevie Wonder, and you can't because your voice is holding you back…" — so on IGOR he sang almost everything himself. [Highsnobiety quoting the interview](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/tyler-the-creator-voice-ruins-songs-produce-other-artists/)
   - 2019 (Q&A, per HotNewHipHop): Kendrick heard IGOR demos and said the vocals were "filling… you weren't worried about the technical — being perfect with your vocals, it was actual emotion… That's why I don't get people to sing my [parts] — sometimes I'm just like f–k it, I'll sing it because someone else can't really sing my truth and what I'm trying to say." [HotNewHipHop](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/238967-tyler-the-creator-credits-kendrick-lamar-for-why-he-sang-more-on-igor-news)
   - 2024 (Billboard cover): "I genuinely think I'm better at my R&B singing s–t as a whole than my rap s–t. And those are usually my biggest records." [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-cover-story-1235850306/)
2. **Melodic-rap topline on CHROMAKOPIA:** "St. Chroma" — whispered/near-spoken topline over militaristic stomps with a sung Daniel Caesar hook; "Noid" — voice "shifts from a smooth, honeyed baritone to something like caffeinated panic" over a 1977 Zamrock sample (Ngozi Family's "Nizakupanga Ngozi," Chewa-language vocal retained, Willow Smith on backing vocals). [Rolling Stone on "Noid"](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/tyler-the-creator-noid-celebrity-culture-1235140842/) / [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/tyler-the-creator/noid) / [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noid_(Tyler,_the_Creator_song))
3. **Featured vocalists as topline contrast (his stated design):** "let the features be the leaders" (Flower Boy); Kali Uchis sang the actual chorus of "See You Again" (Tyler's own sung verse + her floaty hook = the contrast that later blew up on TikTok); Daniel Caesar supplies the sung hook on "St. Chroma" and co-wrote "Tomorrow" and "St. Chroma" with him; Teezo Touchdown handles the chorus of "Darling, I" with "responses, a second voice" behind Tyler's hushed rap-sung lead. [Complex credits](https://www.complex.com/music/a/tracewilliamcowen/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-credits) / [Neon Music](https://neonmusic.co.uk/tyler-the-creator-darling-i-lyrics-meaning-love-vs-commitment-video-credits) / [Billboard: Jerrod Carmichael conversation](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-jerrod-carmichael-video-justin-bieber-glitter-8095406/)
4. **He uses singers as production advisors too:** Daniel Caesar, who features on two CHROMAKOPIA tracks, says Tyler "entrusted Caesar with giving notes on production" and calls him "a genius." [Billboard: Daniel Caesar on Tyler](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/daniel-caesar-praises-tyler-the-creator-justin-bieber-1236029129/)

### 5) Vocal-anchoring production — brass, soul, live instruments
1. **Brass as vocal support/energy:** his stated long-term goal — "Since day one, I've always wanted to make the prettiest shit that's borderline boring, or the hardest fucking shit. And I've been trying to mix those together since my first album." [The FADER quoting Zane Lowe interview](https://www.thefader.com/2019/07/18/tyler-the-creator-igor-odd-future-interview-apple-music). Horns recur as the "pretty/hard" hybrid: "Smuckers" was "me trying to make horn sections and drum kicks that sound epic" (built 2011, released 2015); "Sticky" — "I wanted something for the drill team at the f–king pep rallies, something for the band to play at halftime." [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/tyler-the-creator/smuckers) / [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-cover-story-1235850306/)
2. **Soul/neo-soul harmonic base:** "Boredom" over Stevie Wonder chords; IGOR built on "pretty chords" + "progressions… and these melodies" (his own words on why IGOR's first-week No. 1 shocked him); "Darling, I" is built on Q-Tip's "Vivrant Thing" (hence Barry White and Kamaal Ibn John Fareed credits) with a "Drop It Like It's Hot"-style Neptunes snap. [Billboard: Jerrod Carmichael conversation](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-jerrod-carmichael-video-justin-bieber-glitter-8095406/) / [Complex credits](https://www.complex.com/music/a/tracewilliamcowen/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-credits) / [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darling,_I)
3. **Live-instrument framing around the voice:** CHROMAKOPIA sessions were tracked with live players (drums, keys, bass, strings) visible in *Mask Is Off*; the album closes with Kevin Kendricks' synthesizer + Tyler's own background vocals alongside Daniel Caesar and Solange — "I knew that's how I wanted to end it, with the synth, just letting n—as sit there and think." [XXL](https://www.xxlmag.com/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-behind-the-scenes/) / [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/tyler-the-creator-chromakopia-cover-story-1235850306/)

### 8) "The trick" in his words — repeatable vs. one-off
- **The repeatable engine (his own hierarchy):** beat → melody → lyrics; chords looped for hours as the song seed; sing it yourself when the emotion demands it ("someone else can't really sing my truth"); features placed at the topline for contrast ("let the features be the leaders"); albums as sequenced, character-driven worlds with intentional endings ("the way you end an album is so important"); total self-production ("I'm ridiculously talented [as a] producer" + insisting "produced and arranged" credit is visible).
- **Deliberately one-off (and he says so):** the *depth* is the variable. CHROMAKOPIA was "the 30s album" and "so… for me" that he pivoted hard to "silly," upbeat music on DON'T TAP THE GLASS ("I just wanted to make super urgent, upbeat shit" — Rolling Stone quoting the same Zane Lowe interview). His anti-formula statement is itself the formula: "Any version y'all see me in is the most honest version at that time." The recurring *method* (sound-first, world-building, self-production) is repeatable; the *mood and persona* are deliberately reset every cycle. He also explicitly rejects genre-boxing: "I hate rapping" (2015) → "better at my R&B singing s–t" (2024) is one continuous, stated trajectory, not an accident.

### 9) Gaps
1. **"Chord-loop-driven vowels" is not directly attested.** No primary quote says he shapes vowel choice to chord tones. The attested proxy: melody-then-lyrics ordering, "the runs" as a listening priority, and voice-pitching to fit tracks. Any formula brief should mark vowel-shaping as inference, not Tyler doctrine.
2. **The "album as movie" quote.** Widely reported framing (IGOR as a three-act love-triangle narrative; CHROMAKOPIA as diary/world), and Tyler describes sequencing craft, but I could not verify a clean verbatim "it's like a movie" quote in a primary transcript (Apple Music interview transcripts are paywalled/partial). Best verified stand-ins: quotes #3, #13, and the Complex "shrewd sequencing decisions" description.
