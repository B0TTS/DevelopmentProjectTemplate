# Research: Kendrick Lamar — Songwriting / Topline Formula (LANE brief)

**Artist:** Kendrick Lamar (Kendrick Lamar Duckworth, b. 1987, Compton, CA) — TDE → pgLang (co-founded with Dave Free, 2020), released via Interscope/ICLG. Pulitzer winner, 2024–25 Drake-diss run, Super Bowl LIX headliner.
**Scope:** lyric writing + sung/rapped topline. Production covered only as scaffolding for the vocal.
**Method:** primary-source interviews only (Kendrick, Sounwave, DJ Dahi, Mustard, Mike WiLL Made-It, Dave Free, Terrace Martin, Jack Antonoff, Pluss, Kid Capri, 9th Wonder). Claims marked [LORE] are internet legend, not verified in primary sources.

## 2) The core repeatable workflow — idea → finished master (as actually documented)

Synthesized from Sounwave, DJ Dahi, Mustard, Mike WiLL, Dave Free, and Kendrick's own interviews. This is the repeatable pipeline across *DAMN.*, *Mr. Morale*, and *GNX*:

1. **Life-first intake, no deadlines.** Kendrick and DJ Dahi "pretty much talk every day, just about life in general" — the conversations ARE the song ideas ("what is actually moving you, what is triggering you to want to make something that day"). [Rolling Stone / DJ Dahi](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/dj-dahi-breaks-down-the-five-year-process-behind-kendrick-lamars-new-album-1357269/)
2. **Concept/architecture decided top-down BEFORE beats.** Sounwave: "He goes into these phases where basically his mind is this big storyboard and he's picking ideas: 'What if we did this? What if we did that?'" [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/feature-kendrick-lamar-pluss-terrace-martin-more-making-damn-album-year/) — *DAMN.*: one-word title = one emotion per song, "hybrid of my first two commercial albums," designed to work front-to-back AND in reverse. [Kendrick via Grammy.com / Zane Lowe](https://www.grammy.com/news/feature-kendrick-lamar-pluss-terrace-martin-more-making-damn-album-year/) — *Mr. Morale*: "a person stripped down of everything, locked into a white room with just their thoughts and a piano" (Sounwave's assigned world). [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/sounwave-kendrick-lamar-mr-morale-big-steppers-interview) — *GNX*: "this massive funky West groove" that "met the moment head-on." [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/music/news/jack-antonoff-mustard-sounwave-production-kendrick-gnx-1236598023/)
3. **Write the words BEFORE the music.** DJ Dahi: "I remember he was just writing. Writing songs with no beat, no instrumentals... hearing him write 'Auntie Diaries,' and it was literally skeletons of ideas." "With Kendrick it's kind of like an old way of songwriting, where either you write something on piano or you write something on just a basic kick-snare rhythm." [Rolling Stone / DJ Dahi](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/dj-dahi-breaks-down-the-five-year-process-behind-kendrick-lamars-new-album-1357269/) — *Contrast:* on *DAMN.*'s hits the beat came first ("HUMBLE." — Mike WiLL beat, then "The first thing that came to my head was, 'Be humble'"). Both directions are in the formula; the constant is that the **vocal is the anchor**, not the beat.
4. **Band-style jam camps.** Sounwave + DJ Dahi + Baby Keem + Bekon locked in a London studio for a week (early 2019): "In those sessions we create like a band. Literally all together working and jamming." Only "Father Time" survived from that camp — but it set the album's core. For *DAMN.*, "everybody literally had sleeping bags in the studio… 'If you leave, you're not serious.'" [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/sounwave-kendrick-lamar-mr-morale-big-steppers-interview) / [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2017/04/24/sounwave-interview-kendrick-lamar-damn-track-explainer)
5. **High-volume idea generation + ruthless culling.** GNX: "somewhere between 80 and 100 songs, later pared down to the dozen." "The beginning of it was throwing paint on the wall." [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/music/news/jack-antonoff-mustard-sounwave-production-kendrick-gnx-1236598023/) Kendrick reportedly has "thousands" of unreleased songs (he told Big Boy), and lost 2005–06 hard drives. [Medium transcript of Big Boy interview](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/kendrick-lamar-teaches-how-to-write-rap-songs-faster-in-3-steps-1664c20b6524)
6. **The vocal is cut first, production builds around it.** "DNA.": Kendrick "went the whole way [through] and then he just started rapping a cappella... 'I just want to see if you can put some drums around this.'" Mike WiLL built the beat to match the rap: "I wanted it to sound like he's battling the beat." [NPR](https://www-s1.npr.org/sections/therecord/2017/04/25/525450544/how-mike-will-made-it-and-kendrick-lamar-created-the-years-most-urgent-music-yet) "Luther": "There were no drums. It was literally me chopping the sample and Dot just humming melodies… once he hit this one specific melody, I knew that this had to be bigger." [Rolling Stone / Sounwave on GNX](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-gnx-collaborators-1235480949/)
7. **Relentless revision until the emotional test passes.** "Count Me Out" went through "10, no, actually 20, 30 versions… A record is a demo until it actually comes out" (DJ Dahi). [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/dj-dahi-breaks-down-the-five-year-process-behind-kendrick-lamars-new-album-1357269/) Sounwave inserts beat-switches wherever his attention drifts: "If I'm listening to a song and I find myself drifting… that's the part where I have to do something completely different." [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/sounwave-kendrick-lamar-mr-morale-big-steppers-interview)
8. **The car-ride test, then the tear test.** Every album: "You know it's not going to come out unless we take this car ride… I have to feel this overwhelming feeling of 'Wow, this is it. It's completed.'" And: "If I don't shed a tear by the last second of the album, it is not ready… Once that last second of the last song stopped, 6 a.m. in the morning, I shed a tear." [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/sounwave-kendrick-lamar-mr-morale-big-steppers-interview) / [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-gnx-collaborators-1235480949/)
9. **pgLang release gate ("break and repeat").** Dave Free: "Kendrick or I will dream something up… we'll figure out how to create a system around the idea… When it's developed, we ask, 'Does it move you?'… Once there are no more holes to poke, I feel comfortable putting it into the universe. That's what we call 'break and repeat.'" [Elephant interview via Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/dave-free-no-yapping-rule-kendrick-lamar-pglang-success-1235731044/)

## 3) Lyric method — theme, story, rhyme, hook

- **Writes in his head, on paper, and on a device — "all three."** Big Boy: "When you write Kendrick, do you write in the head or on a device?" Kendrick: "All three. However I can get to it, might just jump on the mic, might get a pen and just a tablet right here." [Big Boy interview transcript](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/kendrick-lamar-teaches-how-to-write-rap-songs-faster-in-3-steps-1664c20b6524) (Voice Memos specifically: no primary evidence — see Gaps.)
- **He used to write nothing — pure head-memory; now he writes for precision.** Sounwave: "He used to never write — it was a lyrical exercise thing in his head. Now, I'll catch him writing because he wants everything to be pronounced and felt that much more." And: "He's all in his head and you won't know what he's thinking until he gets in the booth and actually says it. He has everything planned out perfectly in his head." [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2017/04/24/sounwave-interview-kendrick-lamar-damn-track-explainer)
- **Concept-first, theme-carrying hooks.** *DAMN.*: "Kendrick definitely wanted to do one word titles… He just wanted to make a song that made you feel like that one word." [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2017/04/24/sounwave-interview-kendrick-lamar-damn-track-explainer) The hook is the thesis: "The first thing that came to my head was, 'Be humble.'" — and the song is written "ultimately… looking in a mirror" (the command is self-directed). [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-the-rolling-stone-interview-199817/)
- **Density inside simplicity — the double/triple entendre method.** Mustard on the *Not Like Us* aftermath: "It's opened the door for… the real rappers that love rap music and lyrics and the double, triple, quadruple entendres and all that shit cool again." [Billboard cover](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/mustard-not-like-us-producer-cover-story-1235793061/) Example: "My left stroke just went VI-RAL" — deliberately ambiguous (pen strokes / fight strokes / golf), inviting dissection (triple j's analysis: "we want to dissect each phrase… 'there's levels to it, you and I know'"). [triple j](https://www.abc.net.au/triplej/news/hottest-100-kendrick-lamar-humble-feature/9366884)
- **Wordplay as craft-gamesmanship.** "I just love words; love how to bend 'em, love how to break 'em, twist 'em, turn 'em, make 'em in couplets… it's all acrobatics, it excites me." [Zane Lowe via triple j](https://www.abc.net.au/triplej/news/hottest-100-kendrick-lamar-humble-feature/9366884)
- **Honesty as the engine — and the gate.** "It's more like my views rather than throw it on the listener… it's not necessarily preachy, it's my own ups and downs… putting them all into one." [Big Boy via Medium](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/kendrick-lamar-teaches-how-to-write-rap-songs-faster-in-3-steps-1664c20b6524) / "I always said to myself, if I said it on a record, I never retract my statements." [GQ/Rick Rubin](https://www.gq.com/story/kendrick-lamar-rick-rubin-gq-style-cover-interview)
- **Studied craft, not just talent.** Eminem for clarity/time ("His time is impeccable. When he wants to fall off the beat, it's impeccable"), Jay-Z for conversational verse carry, André 3000 for singing, and — per Terrace Martin — "a lot of the chords that you pick are jazz-influenced… you a jazz musician by default" (this reframe shaped *To Pimp a Butterfly*). [GQ/Rick Rubin](https://www.gq.com/story/kendrick-lamar-rick-rubin-gq-style-cover-interview)
- **Room vs. top-down:** the *concept* is top-down (Kendrick's storyboard; "You can't make them type of albums just by producers sending you beats. You have to be in the grit with 'em… every snare, every 808, every transition, every arrangement"); the *execution* is room-based — jams, sleeping-bag lock-ins, collaborators who are allowed to kill songs. Sounwave: "There's no yes-men in our circle." [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/sounwave-kendrick-lamar-mr-morale-big-steppers-interview) / [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-the-rolling-stone-interview-199817/)
- **Co-writers are real and credited** — but they mostly supply hooks, melodies, and production, not his raps: Baby Keem co-wrote/co-produced "N95"; Ink wrote SZA's melodies on "Luther"; Sam Dew co-wrote on *Mr. Morale*; Pharrell supplied the "Alright" hook phrase ("P had the hook… That's him on the hook"). [NME credits](https://www.nme.com/news/music/kendrick-lamar-mr-morale-and-the-big-steppers-full-credits-3224800) / [Variety Luther credits](https://variety.com/2025/music/news/jack-antonoff-mustard-sounwave-production-kendrick-gnx-1236598023/) / [GQ/Rick Rubin](https://www.gq.com/story/kendrick-lamar-rick-rubin-gq-style-cover-interview)
- **Revision discipline:** "I want to go deeper. I shoulda went deeper" is his standard regret; he sits on beats for months until the words come ("Alright" — beat held ~6 months, lyrics texted to Pharrell when they finally arrived). [GQ/Rick Rubin](https://www.gq.com/story/kendrick-lamar-rick-rubin-gq-style-cover-interview)

## 4) Topline / singing method — how the hook is built and SUNG

- **He writes ~95% of his own melodies.** "I write a lot of the melodies. Shit, usually 95 percent. May jump in and jump out. Might give you a hook like 'ELEMENT.' Might give you a verse on a Travis Scott record with the 'ghetto falsetto.' That's what I call it." [Rolling Stone 2017](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-the-rolling-stone-interview-199817/)
- **Melody-first demos: hum/scat over a loop, words later.** "Luther": "Dot just humming melodies" over a chopped Vandross sample until "he hit this one specific melody." [Rolling Stone / Sounwave](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-gnx-collaborators-1235480949/) "Alright": "I had a scat… Different cadences" before words existed. [GQ/Rick Rubin](https://www.gq.com/story/kendrick-lamar-rick-rubin-gq-style-cover-interview)
- **The hook is a chantable, repeatable phrase — usually RAPPED-SUNG, pentatonic-ish and narrow in range** so it works shouted in a stadium: "Sit down, be humble"; "They not like us"; "I'm finna squabble up"; N95's "take off your…" (sung-rapped over a beat switch with Baby Keem's "let's go" ad-libs as melodic punctuation). [Songfacts HUMBLE](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/kendrick-lamar/humble) / [Songfacts N95](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/kendrick-lamar/n95)
- **Hooks can also be delegated to a co-writer/singer for contrast** — "LOVE." (Zacari's sung hook; Sounwave: "his vocals… everything was perfect"), "Luther" (SZA's part with Ink's melodies). The formula: **one simple repeated phrase from Kendrick + one sung foil.** [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2017/04/24/sounwave-interview-kendrick-lamar-damn-track-explainer) / [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/music/news/jack-antonoff-mustard-sounwave-production-kendrick-gnx-1236598023/)
- **Delivery craft — rhythm and pocket over range.** DJ Dahi on "Rich Spirit": "He naturally did that flow… the pocket he was spitting in was hard." [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/dj-dahi-breaks-down-the-five-year-process-behind-kendrick-lamars-new-album-1357269/) Kendrick on his own vocal attack: "You go somewhere emotionally and then you damn near become a robot to the emotion. You want to keep on doing takes over and over. That's when you really zone out and when you really connect with the audience." [Rolling Stone 2017](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-the-rolling-stone-interview-199817/)
- **Voices/characters are part of the topline.** "We Cry Together": Kendrick demoed the woman's part himself — "he had pitched his voice up to sound like a girl and he was literally arguing with himself" (Sounwave). [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/sounwave-kendrick-lamar-mr-morale-big-steppers-interview) His range is a tool ("ghetto falsetto," Curtis Mayfield influence), but the default hit-register is a mid-tenor bark-sing with heavy rhythmic syncopation. [Rolling Stone 2017](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-the-rolling-stone-interview-199817/)
- **"Being present" in the vocal moment** is his stated method: "As long as I was present in the studio or present in whatever the line was, or whatever the mistake was, rapping or doing the hooks or choruses or whatnot, I was present in that moment, I felt that passion." [Super Bowl LIX press conference, Apple Music](https://ameyawdebrah.com/the-official-kendrick-lamar-interview-at-the-apple-music-super-bowl-lix-halftime-show-press-conference/)

## 5) Vocal-anchoring production — what the track does so the hook lands

- **The beat is stripped to let the voice be the melody.** Mustard's formula: "a bassline, clap and it's over… maybe an 808" and "Simplicity is key for me… it still needs to be ratchet enough to be fun, too." [Billboard cover](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/mustard-not-like-us-producer-cover-story-1235793061/) HUMBLE.'s "complex simplicity": piano + 808 + siren — the hook lands because nothing competes with the phrase. [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/feature-kendrick-lamar-pluss-terrace-martin-more-making-damn-album-year/)
- **Beat switches are arranged around the vocal's dramatic beats.** Sounwave engineers "something completely different" at drift-points; Kendrick calls the geometry himself: "Right here, I want the beat to switch up on that 15th bar and then come back in. And it always works." [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/sounwave-kendrick-lamar-mr-morale-big-steppers-interview) / [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2017/04/24/sounwave-interview-kendrick-lamar-damn-track-explainer) — "TV Off": two Mustard beats stitched (Kendrick's idea, per Sounwave) with the "MUSTARD!" shout as the hook's punctuation. [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/music/news/jack-antonoff-mustard-sounwave-production-kendrick-gnx-1236598023/)
- **When the vocal is sung, production creates space (not density).** "Luther" started drum-less: "me chopping the sample and Dot just humming melodies"; Antonoff then "carv[ed] out all these little spaces" around the two voices. [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-gnx-collaborators-1235480949/) / [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/music/news/jack-antonoff-mustard-sounwave-production-kendrick-gnx-1236598023/)
- **World-building as vocal backdrop.** *Mr. Morale*: "98 percent of the song is going to have a piano in there" — a white-room piano world so the rapped confessions read as theatre. [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/sounwave-kendrick-lamar-mr-morale-big-steppers-interview)
- **Speed/feel adjustments are made for the hook's bounce.** "Squabble Up" "was way too fast. Once we slowed it down, we knew we had something special." [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-gnx-collaborators-1235480949/)

## 8) "The trick" in their words — the ONE thing that makes it land

- **Kendrick's version:** *Feel first, craft second* — "if I'm not inspired, I can't really do the music… it's gonna feel *me*" [GQ/Rubin](https://www.gq.com/story/kendrick-lamar-rick-rubin-gq-style-cover-interview), with melody as the carrier ("I write a lot of the melodies, usually 95 percent" [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-the-rolling-stone-interview-199817/)).
- **Sounwave's version (the quality gate):** the album isn't done until the last-second **tear test** passes in a **car ride** — an emotional-completion check that is *repeatable, not mystical*: it's the same ritual every project. [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/kendrick-lamar-gnx-collaborators-1235480949/) / [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/sounwave-kendrick-lamar-mr-morale-big-steppers-interview)
- **Dave Free's version (the release gate):** "**Does it move you?**" — poke holes until none remain ("break and repeat"). [Elephant via Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/dave-free-no-yapping-rule-kendrick-lamar-pglang-success-1235731044/)
- **Mustard's version (the hit gate):** "**Simplicity is key… but it still needs to be ratchet enough to be fun**" — one bassline, clap, 808, and a chant the whole room can shout. [Billboard cover](https://www.billboard.com/music/rb-hip-hop/mustard-not-like-us-producer-cover-story-1235793061/)

**Verdict on repeatability:** The formula is NOT "write a genius verse." It is: (1) concept-first album architecture; (2) write the words and the melody before/against a deliberately simple beat; (3) one repeated 3–6 word hook phrase that states the thesis; (4) let a sung or chanted foil (SZA/Zacari/Pharrell hook/ad-libs) carry the ear candy; (5) gate everything through "does it move you" + the tear test; (6) kill without mercy (30+ versions, 80–100 songs → 12). Every step is documented by at least one primary source; none of it depends on "genius inspiration" — Kendrick says the opposite ("I put in enough hours to be able to pen a hundred-bar verse on the spot"). The one-off variable is *when the concept meets the cultural moment* (2024 beef timing); the repeatable variable is the pipeline.
