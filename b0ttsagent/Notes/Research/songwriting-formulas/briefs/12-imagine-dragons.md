# Research: Imagine Dragons — the "rock songs that chart every time" lane (Dan Reynolds + Alex da Kid + Mattman & Robin)

## Summary
Imagine Dragons are the last rock act with mainstream Hot 100 muscle: five top-10s ("Radioactive" #2, "Demons" #6, "Believer" #4, "Thunder" #4, "Enemy" #5), the only group in RIAA history with four Diamond-plus singles, and "Radioactive"'s record 87-week Hot 100 run. The repeatable machine is documented in their own words: Dan Reynolds writes alone from a melodic germ and a "false empowerment" lyric — single-word refrains over universal pain/struggle vocabulary — and producer teams (Alex da Kid's beat-first sessions, then the Swedish minimalists Mattman & Robin) build the trademark verse→pre-chorus-lift→explosive-drop hook, engineered for radio and sync. The press critique is equally documented: Pitchfork ("machine learning output"), Spin ("algorithmic playlists"), and Classic Rock ("as if selected by algorithms") all call the formula obvious — yet Billboard staff concede "they've mastered the formula for a hit."

## 2) The core repeatable workflow — "build → explosive hook," step by step

This is the documented, repeatable assembly line (confirmed by Reynolds, Alex da Kid, Billboard analysts, and the Hit Songs Deconstructed teardown of "Believer"):

1. **Melodic germ first, theme never.** "I always write the same way: I sit down at a piano, with a guitar or at a computer with no theme in mind and create a soundscape that is an honest output of whatever I'm feeling. I then write lyrics and melody to that feeling. It's been that same way since I was 12." — Reynolds. [GRAMMY.com (Loom)](https://grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-new-album-loom-inspirations-dan-reynolds-writing) His training: "I was a classical pianist for 10 years from six to 16 — playing Chopin, Beethoven and Bach… My brain was formed in that classical piano training and that's still where I write melodies from." [GRAMMY.com (Loom)](https://grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-new-album-loom-inspirations-dan-reynolds-writing)
2. **Stream-of-consciousness hook hunt.** He sings nonsense/stream-of-consciousness over the germ and chases whatever line lands: "A lot of times that's how I'll write lyrics: stream-of-consciousness that comes out and I almost don't even know what I'm saying… If it has meaning, then we'll chase it." (Example: "Gods Don't Pray" began with Reynolds riffing "Gods don't…" — Mattman & Robin flagged it.) [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/imagine-dragons-dan-reynolds-tabloids-loom-album-1235050767/)
3. **Volume as filter.** With Alex da Kid: "for about eight months we got into a rhythm of writing 2-3 songs a day." [SongwriterUniverse](https://www.songwriteruniverse.com/alex-da-kid-2017/) He claims "I have recorded a song almost every day since then" since age 12. [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-songs-stories-believer-radioactive-thunder-enemy-mercury-act-1-2-new-album-2022-dan-reynolds-interview/) For every album he arrives with 150–200 demos; for Loom, Mattman & Robin ordered a "wipe the slate clean," and he wrote ~30 songs in 4–6 months in-room with them — the first time he wrote in a room with anyone. [GRAMMY.com (Loom)](https://grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-new-album-loom-inspirations-dan-reynolds-writing); [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/imagine-dragons-dan-reynolds-tabloids-loom-album-1235050767/)
4. **Beat-first production (Alex da Kid era).** "Radioactive" was "just a beat I made"; "Demons" was "me putting down a track, and the band just writing over it" while "Dan Reynolds was just writing hooks." [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/songwriter-u-qa-alex-da-kid-discovering-imagine-dragons-crafting-tracks-rihanna-eminem-u2/) The band then layered live drums/guitar/bass over the EDM skeleton — "adding real instrumentation… brought more live emotion to it." [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-songs-stories-believer-radioactive-thunder-enemy-mercury-act-1-2-new-album-2022-dan-reynolds-interview/)
5. **The verse → pre-chorus lift → explosive hook architecture.** Billboard's description is the best structural summary: "'Believer' frantically overstuffs its verses before exploding into possibly the year's most cathartic chorus," and "Thunder" builds "a triumphant, brain-burrowing singalong refrain mostly out of a single word." [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/rock/imagine-dragons-biggest-band-2017-interview-8046150/) The Hit Songs Deconstructed analysis adds the mechanics: sequential first-line hooks ("First things first / Second things second…"), and a "C.I.A." (chorus impact accentuator) — a cinematic woosh/lull on the last two beats of the pre-chorus that suspends the lyric ("seein' the beauty through the…") before slamming the chorus ("PAIN!"); plus a mix of sung/shouted/rap-hybrid deliveries per section. [Hit Songs Deconstructed](https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/hsd_wire/believer-blending-in-while-standing-out/)
6. **Minimalism imposed from outside.** The band self-admits overproduction on Smoke + Mirrors ("way over-produced… a huge wall of sound on every song") and hires outside restraint: "Mattman and Robin, that's what they do: very few sounds, but really intelligent and well thought out." [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-songs-stories-believer-radioactive-thunder-enemy-mercury-act-1-2-new-album-2022-dan-reynolds-interview/) On Loom: "They'd say, 'We want it to remain simple and have no fat.'" [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/imagine-dragons-dan-reynolds-tabloids-loom-album-1235050767/)
7. **Sync-ready dynamics by design.** Trailer/stadium dynamics plus a standing pipeline of placements: "Believer" in a Super Bowl Nintendo Switch ad, "Thunder" in a Microsoft Surface ad (both hit #1 on Billboard & Clio's Top TV Commercials chart), "Whatever It Takes" (WWE/Madden), "Natural" (ESPN college football), "Enemy" (Arcane), "Eyes Closed" (NHL 25, WrestleMania XL). The Guardian's read: "Evolve sounds, almost impressively so, as though it's been created with telly music coordinators in mind." [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/rock/imagine-dragons-biggest-band-2017-interview-8046150/); [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2017/jun/22/imagine-dragons-evolve-review-lifeless-electronic-tinged-arena-rock)

## 3) Lyric method — single-word refrains; universal emotional vocabulary; sync-friendly abstraction; occasional biography

- **Single-word / one-phrase refrains.** "Thunder" (Spin counted the word "thunder" ~75 times in the song [Spin](https://www.spin.com/2019/01/imagine-dragons-worst-band-halftime-show/)); "Believer" (the word sung as "PAIN!"); "Natural," "Bones," "Enemy," "Demons," "It's OK." One word = the whole hook; repetition is the hook.
- **Universal pain→empowerment vocabulary.** Reynolds' stated method is writing anthems from bad days: "I write a lot of songs because I'm in a bad place and I'm trying to bring about a false sense of confidence, security and empowerment." [GRAMMY.com (Loom)](https://grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-new-album-loom-inspirations-dan-reynolds-writing) On "Eyes Closed": "That's a song with a lot of false bravado… I'll write a song that is a cup of coffee for me… I love that people hear it and they're like, 'Oh, it's this really confident song.' Because it wasn't really written from a place of that." [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/imagine-dragons-dan-reynolds-tabloids-loom-album-1235050767/)
- **Metaphor as universalizer.** "When I listen to Night Visions, it's very metaphorical. 'Radioactive' is a song about depression; yet, most people hear it and think it's a song about the apocalypse!" The abstraction is deliberate (grown from writing "mom-proof" lyrics about his Mormon doubts); Rick Rubin pushed him to "peel back a little bit, become more vulnerable." [GRAMMY.com (Loom)](https://grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-new-album-loom-inspirations-dan-reynolds-writing) The trade-off critics name: "Instagram-quote culture" and "tired aphorisms." [Pitchfork (Origins)](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/imagine-dragons-origins/)
- **Occasional biography, when it charts.** "Wrecked" (sister-in-law's cancer — Pitchfork's rare praise: "touching in its plainspokenness" [Pitchfork (Mercury)](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/imagine-dragons-mercury-act-1/)); "Bones" — "I was really hyper focused on death… My goal was to write a song about death that was not sad. It's the first time we've ever made a dance song… 'Bones' is supposed to be self-aware and a little bit ironic." [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-songs-stories-believer-radioactive-thunder-enemy-mercury-act-1-2-new-album-2022-dan-reynolds-interview/) "Nice to Meet You" is explicitly autobiographical (dating Minka Kelly — see §6).
- **Self-description of the through-line:** "I write from a very narrative perspective with lyrics and melodies. So it's a very through-line, almost predictable thing to our fans. They know what they're getting. It's not lightning in a bottle. It's just a Dan journal entry with a melody." [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/imagine-dragons-dan-reynolds-tabloids-loom-album-1235050767/) Plus the ownership claim: "I've written every single lyric that's ever appeared on any record, and [about] 95 percent of the melodies… It's not a pop machine." [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/rock/imagine-dragons-biggest-band-2017-interview-8046150/)

## 4) Topline/singing method — determined-plain belt, pseudo-chants, rhythmic hook

- **The delivery is the hook's engine.** Billboard's Joe Lynch (on why they chart when other rock doesn't): "Their sound deemphasizes guitars. This is not a riff-based rock act, but a beat-based one; couple that with Dan Reynolds' rhythmic, rap-influenced delivery and you have a band uniquely primed for chart/radio success." [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/chart-beat/imagine-dragons-jid-enemy-five-burning-questions-1235050926/) He also lists the "fist-pumping chorus" and "the falsetto" as signature components. [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/chart-beat/imagine-dragons-jid-enemy-five-burning-questions-1235050926/)
- **Registers in opposition.** The vocal scheme is a narrow, exaggerated set: verse = clipped, syllable-dense rhythmic talk-sing; chorus = strained, plain, square-toned belt on repeated title words; plus chant-filler ("woah-ohs" — The Guardian flagged "chart pop's current obligatory chorus of 'woah-ohs'" [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2017/jun/22/imagine-dragons-evolve-review-lifeless-electronic-tinged-arena-rock)). Critics name the same two registers: "knockoff Chris Martin" and "screaming" [Spin](https://www.spin.com/2019/01/imagine-dragons-worst-band-halftime-show/); "Reynolds' snarl-yell, his signature since 'Radioactive,' is one of the most grating sounds in popular music." [Pitchfork (Mercury)](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/imagine-dragons-mercury-act-1/)
- **The title word is SUNG as a proclamation.** The hook word lands on the highest, longest note of the section, full chest voice, often with a shouted tag ("You made me a, you made me a believer, believer!"; "Thunder, feel the thunder!"). Hit Songs Deconstructed's chart of "Believer" shows the sung→shouted→sung/shouted hybrid mapping section by section — the chorus itself is a shout delivered with melodic pitch, which reads as "anthem" rather than "screamo." [Hit Songs Deconstructed](https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/hsd_wire/believer-blending-in-while-standing-out/)
- **Self-aware plainness.** Reynolds is openly not a "cool" singer — he frames the sound as deliberately unsubtle: "Imagine Dragons is not a subtle band. It's love or hate. It's just Vegas. It's right in your face." [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/imagine-dragons-dan-reynolds-tabloids-loom-album-1235050767/)

## 5) Vocal-anchoring production — the lift, the stomp-clap, the drop

- **Pre-chorus lift is the signature engineering.** The "woosh and lull" spanning the last two beats of the pre-chorus into the first two of the chorus — the C.I.A. — is the single most identifiable device in "Believer," and the same tension-then-catharsis frame repeats across "Natural," "Enemy," "Bones," "Eyes Closed." [Hit Songs Deconstructed](https://www.hitsongsdeconstructed.com/hsd_wire/believer-blending-in-while-standing-out/)
- **Stomp-clap percussion is in the DNA.** The very first hit's beat was a desk: "I wrote 'It's Time' in my dorm room… The stomp clap is me hitting my desk… We added the group's stomps and claps, but we kept the slaps and claps I had done in the dorm room." [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-songs-stories-believer-radioactive-thunder-enemy-mercury-act-1-2-new-album-2022-dan-reynolds-interview/) It remains the live-signature beat of "Radioactive" and the drop sections generally.
- **Music-box / treble piano hooks.** The melodic core is often a thin, high-register piano/synth motif (Thunder's nursery-rhyme riff; the toy-piano hook of "Demons") sitting above a huge low end — "turning down the guitars and turning up every expensive synth pad known to man" per Pitchfork. [Pitchfork (Origins)](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/imagine-dragons-origins/)
- **Beat-first, drop-based, minimal-instrument chorus.** Joe Lynch: not riff-based but beat-based. [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/chart-beat/imagine-dragons-jid-enemy-five-burning-questions-1235050926/) Mattman & Robin's mandate ("simple and no fat" [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/imagine-dragons-dan-reynolds-tabloids-loom-album-1235050767/)) plus Serban Ghenea's mix (Loom and Mercury-era) give the radio sheen; Daniel Platzman described the division of labor: "Songwriting is songwriting… But then we enter the world of production — how are you gonna make that song come to life?" [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/rock/imagine-dragons-biggest-band-2017-interview-8046150/)
- **Home-demo authenticity kept on purpose.** "Thunder" was "recorded at my computer in my front entry room… you can hear a couple of my kids screaming in the background. There was nothing professional about it." [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-songs-stories-believer-radioactive-thunder-enemy-mercury-act-1-2-new-album-2022-dan-reynolds-interview/)

## 6) Proof songs (2020–2025)

- **"Enemy" (w/ JID, 2021)** — #5 Hot 100, #1 Radio Songs, 54 weeks Alternative Airplay (9 at #1), ranked 14th-greatest Alternative Airplay song ever. Craft: the whole song existed first ("We had the whole song complete. But we thought it'd be cool to have a guest appearance… I really love JID and suggested him. What he added to it really brought it up a level") — the JID verse itself was years old (started with Mac Miller). Universal-vocabulary mission statement: "'Enemy' is about reconciling internal conflict in a world where it feels impossible to trust even yourself… meant to be both personal and a critique of a society that seems intent on creating division." [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-songs-stories-believer-radioactive-thunder-enemy-mercury-act-1-2-new-album-2022-dan-reynolds-interview/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enemy_(Imagine_Dragons_and_JID_song)); [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/chart-beat/harry-styles-as-it-was-tops-hot-100-imagine-dragons-jid-enemy-1235065560/)
- **"Bones" (2022)** — #47 Hot 100; the formula inverted into a four-on-the-floor "dance song about death": "I was really hyper focused on death… My goal was to write a song about death that was not sad… When we play 'Bones' live, everybody's dancing. It's not typical for Imagine Dragons… It's usually more angsty." [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-songs-stories-believer-radioactive-thunder-enemy-mercury-act-1-2-new-album-2022-dan-reynolds-interview/)
- **"Eyes Closed" (2024)** — the "false bravado" single: "I could do this with my eyes closed, it's so easy, but the reality is I was not really loving myself or feeling any of those things I was writing about." Did not make the Hot 100 (peaked #2 Bubbling Under) — proof the formula alone no longer guarantees the chart, though it stayed #13 Hot Rock & Alt and picked up NHL 25 + WrestleMania XL syncs. [GRAMMY.com (Loom)](https://grammy.com/news/imagine-dragons-new-album-loom-inspirations-dan-reynolds-writing); [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/imagine-dragons-new-song-eyes-closed-1234998617/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyes_Closed_(Imagine_Dragons_song))
- **"Nice to Meet You" (2024)** — the biographical variant: written about dating Minka Kelly, whose handclaps are on the record; the "room mic" cheer kept from when they finished the album ("we wanted to add more liveliness… when the second chorus starts, you can hear everybody cheering"). Explicitly anecdotal lyric: "I was buying you those flowers / I was listening for hours / I was shutting up those doubters." Chart reality: no Hot 100 entry (#33 Hot Rock & Alt, #98 UK). [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/imagine-dragons-dan-reynolds-tabloids-loom-album-1235050767/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice_to_Meet_You_(Imagine_Dragons_song))

## 8) "The trick" in their words — and the press critique

**The trick (their words):** write every day from feeling, not concept; hunt a one-word chantable hook from stream-of-consciousness; keep the lyric universal ("depression" dressed as apocalypse); let a restrained outside producer strip the track to "no fat"; build verse→pre-chorus-lift→explosive drop; keep dynamics trailer-huge. Reynolds' own summary: "It's not lightning in a bottle. It's just a Dan journal entry with a melody." [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/imagine-dragons-dan-reynolds-tabloids-loom-album-1235050767/) Alex da Kid's: write 2–3 songs a day until the hooks accumulate. [SongwriterUniverse](https://www.songwriteruniverse.com/alex-da-kid-2017/) Mattman & Robin's: minimal sounds, maximum polish — the duo described by The Ringer as "pop music as particle physics." [The Ringer](https://theringer.com/2016/3/18/16044232/dark-knights-meet-mattman-robin-pop-music-s-new-hidden-overlords-2524a8727ff0)

**The critique:** the same repeatability is the indictment. Spin: algorithm-bait "motivational platitudes." Pitchfork: "machine learning output" and "low-hanging-fruit music." Guardian: written for TV music coordinators. Classic Rock 2024: "as if selected by algorithms for mass consumption." The band's rebuttal is explicit and consistent: the process predates the algorithm (journal-entry writing since age 12; "I've written every single lyric"), and they absorb the criticism — "very valid. I've got a lot of work to do" — while noting it has real-world cost ("It has added to the depression I've dealt with since youth"). [Stereogum](https://www.stereogum.com/2034084/imagine-dragons-slipknot-nickelback/news/) The honest bottom line: the formula is real, it is documented by both sides, and its effectiveness decayed measurably in 2024 (no Loom single reached the Hot 100), which is itself evidence that the "charts every time" lane depends on radio/sync ecosystem conditions as much as on craft.
