# Research: Billie Eilish + Finneas O'Connell — the bedroom anti-formula that became the formula for intimate pop

**Lane:** Billie Eilish & FINNEAS (sister–brother writer-producer duo). Focus: lyric writing + singing/topline. Era: 2020–2025 (with 2015–2019 origin facts where needed).

## Summary

Billie Eilish and Finneas O'Connell are the highest-profile example of a **self-contained, two-person writing unit**: no co-writer committees, one room, one producer. Their repeatable method inverts the standard pop pipeline — Finneas builds a small, sonically distinctive production sketch (often from field recordings, mouth-sounds, and bass-first ideas) and Billie writes the topline fast from real feeling, sung close-mic at near-whisper, so the track sounds like a secret told in the same room. The "formula" is deliberately anti-formula: unconventional structures (fake-out two-part songs like "Happier Than Ever"), no reverb on the lead vocal, and hooks that arrive as rhythm/bass/timbre rather than big belted melodies. On streaming, that quiet intimacy is the loudest thing in the feed: "BIRDS OF A FEATHER" was not even the label's chosen single, yet fans streamed it to a Hot 100 No. 2 peak, 52+ weeks on the chart, the most-streamed song of 2024 on Spotify globally, and the 2025 Grammy for Song of the Year. The duo themselves say the lesson is repeatable and emotional, not structural: write the song before the production, keep pace with the singer, carve out space so the voice can live, and don't chase what a "hit" is supposed to sound like.

## 2) The core repeatable workflow/formula — as they actually do it

**Setup: one room, two people, no committee.** Finneas: "probably 75-80% of the songs are written with us sitting next to each other at a piano or with a guitar, singing a melody together… It's like a relay race — we really feel like we both have to kill our portion of it to get to the finish line." (Variety, 2019). [https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/](https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/) The trust is sibling-trust: they grew up together, Finneas knows "the tapestry of each other's lives," so he can ask the right question even when she won't say much (TapeOp, 2024). [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell) They tried outside co-writers as teenagers and rejected it: "all the songs we wrote with [others] were not as good as the songs me and Finneas wrote alone" (Billie, Variety). [https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/](https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/)

**Step-by-step (as documented across TapeOp, Billboard, Mix, Apple Music, Rolling Stone):**

1. **Song first, production second (in the room, in real time).** Finneas: "the song comes first… if I can write a bad song *before* devoting hours of production time into my bad idea, that is smarter." Songwriting happens live at piano/guitar — he reacts in real time ("If Billie starts to slow down, I slow down right with her. If she wants to change keys, I modulate with her") (TapeOp, 2024). [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell)
2. **Vamp and improvise nonsense until a topline appears.** The process looks like "playing random sh–, singing in tandem, making up words and singing words that aren't even real words" (Billie, Variety 2019, describing their Stern interview demo and their normal practice). [https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/](https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/)
3. **Capture everything.** Original voice memos of Billie and Finneas writing "Everything I Wanted" were aired on Song Exploder (Episode 197) — the duo's voice-memo habit is part of the workflow. [https://songexploder.net/billie-eilish](https://songexploder.net/billie-eilish)
4. **Finneas scaffolds the track with distinctive sound sources first** (field recordings, everyday foley, bass/rhythm ideas), then **Billie writes the topline to the production** in many cases. Documented examples: "bad guy" began when *Billie* composed the distorted outro bassline, and the song was built around bass + kick with Billie writing lyrics over it (Rolling Stone breakdown video; FADER). [https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/billie-eilish-bad-guy-finneas-song-breakdown-video-882603/](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/billie-eilish-bad-guy-finneas-song-breakdown-video-882603/), [https://www.thefader.com/2019/12/16/watch-billie-eilish-deconstruct-the-production-of-bad-guy](https://www.thefader.com/2019/12/16/watch-billie-eilish-deconstruct-the-production-of-bad-guy) For "Therefore I Am," Finneas wrote and recorded the chorus hook in his own lower voice as a placeholder and they kept the concept: "I wrote and recorded the chorus in my own voice… and just had it as this hook. I had no idea what to do with it" (Finneas, walkthrough video, via Mixing A Band). [https://mixingaband.com/vocal-production-for-billie-eilish-by-finneas/](https://mixingaband.com/vocal-production-for-billie-eilish-by-finneas/)
5. **No reverb on the lead vocal; carve out space.** "I wanted Billie's voice to have this three-dimensionality in a song – something you could see if you closed your eyes. The only way I could do that was to carve all of this space out" (TapeOp). [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell)
6. **Record many takes; comp manually; keep imperfection.** Billie records dozens of takes and comps them herself since 2021 ("she would make the choices, like 'This is the take, I like this take the best'" — Finneas). No Auto-Tune for pitch correction on Billie ("If there are moments where it's not exactly on the cent, that's probably great" — TapeOp). [https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/](https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/), [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell)
7. **Pre-mix and hand off.** Finneas delivers pre-compressed, pre-panned stems with reverbs/delays printed; Rob Kinelski (through 2023) and Jon Castelli (HMHAS) balance it. [https://www.mixonline.com/recording/finneas-on-producing-billie-eilishs-number-one-album-in-his-bedroom](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/finneas-on-producing-billie-eilishs-number-one-album-in-his-bedroom), [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell)

**Sibling-trust factor:** For *HIT ME HARD AND SOFT* the workflow decentralized — Finneas taught Billie to record and comp herself so she could work alone at her own home studio, with him "just a puppet when it comes to comping her vocals." Billie: "This is the most I've ever written and contributed. I did some production stuff, even. And I did all my own vocals." She recorded a vulnerable near-whispered moment in "BIRDS OF A FEATHER" that she "could never have done" with him in the room (Apple Music / Zane Lowe, via MusicTech). [https://musictech.com/news/music/finneas-billie-eilish-comping-vocals/](https://musictech.com/news/music/finneas-billie-eilish-comping-vocals/) Yet the *writing* stays two-in-a-room: the pair wrote "Happier Than Ever" sitting together on an $80 kids' toy guitar while jet-lagged in Denmark, then finished it in lockdown months later (Finneas, Billboard 2021). [https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/](https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/)

## 3) Lyric method — fast from feeling, diary-confession

- **Write from the real feeling of that exact moment.** "Happier Than Ever" was "correlated to what she was going through in her life at that exact moment" (Finneas). [https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/](https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/) The HMHAS session that produced the album's most honest writing started from a fight in which Billie told Finneas: "Just say how you feel and let's write a song about whatever you're scared about" — she called it "the first day really that I was able to be honest" (Apple Music/Zane Lowe interview). [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R-ZIRAmO60c](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R-ZIRAmO60c)
- **Songs "write themselves" when feelings surface.** "Male Fantasy" (HTE): "Afterwards, I'd listen and I'd be like, 'Oh my God, wow… Things, feelings, emotions. You don't even realize they're there until you do. It's a crazy process, this album." (Billie, Zane Lowe, 2021). [https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/billie-eilish-talks-recording-happier-than-ever-album-9598782/](https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/billie-eilish-talks-recording-happier-than-ever-album-9598782/)
- **Diary-confession as identity.** Billie: "I have overshared my whole life. It's compulsive, like I need to tell you every single detail about something." (Rolling Stone cover, 2024). [https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/billie-eilish-hit-me-hard-and-soft-mental-health-fame-1235003585/](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/billie-eilish-hit-me-hard-and-soft-mental-health-fame-1235003585/) Finneas keeps songs for himself only when "this is like a diary entry… it's the story of my life" (Tonight Show, via E!). [https://www.eonline.com/news/1119212/billie-eilish-s-brother-finneas-o-connell-reveals-the-secret-sounds-mixed-into-her-songs](https://www.eonline.com/news/1119212/billie-eilish-s-brother-finneas-o-connell-reveals-the-secret-sounds-mixed-into-her-songs)
- **Speed is real, but revision is ruthless.** Billie wrote the entire Jessie Reyez session song "in a flash" — "I just came up with this melody and all these lyrics, and then I wrote this whole song and we recorded it — it had never happened that fast for me" (Variety). [https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/](https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/) And yet "BIRDS OF A FEATHER" was labored over for ~7 months: "I don't think we've ever overthought a song more" (Billie); they kept a journal of rejected rhymes ("dismay," "convey," "bouquet") and rewrote the *entire chorus* at the end, landing on "And I don't know what I'm crying for / I don't think I could love you more." Finneas: "we overthought this sort of simple song so hard… we kept getting lost in the maze." (Apple Music *Story of My Song* breakdown, via Audacy). [https://www.audacy.com/new965philly/news/billie-eilish-and-finneas-share-story-of-birds-of-a-feather](https://www.audacy.com/new965philly/news/billie-eilish-and-finneas-share-story-of-birds-of-a-feather)
- **Learned structure, then deliberately broke it.** Their mother's songwriting class taught the Beatles-simple section names; "the main idea was that once you know the basics, then you can change them" (Billie). Billie can't even outline "Bury a Friend"'s structure: "Chorus, verse, prechorus, hook verse, bridge, verse, chorus — no, other bridge, then hook… I don't know… It took us months to figure out what was going on there, but it works — like, it doesn't, but it does." [https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/](https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/)
- **Real-life trigger → immediate song.** "Everything I Wanted" was written after Billie dreamed she killed herself: "I was in a really bad place mentally" (NYT Magazine). Finneas initially refused to write it: "I felt like an enabler in helping her write a song as bleak as that song was… the musical equivalent of giving an alcoholic another beer: 'I'm not going to support this.'" (NYT Magazine, via Business Insider). [https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2020/03/11/magazine/billie-eilish-profile.html](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2020/03/11/magazine/billie-eilish-profile.html), [https://www.businessinsider.com/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-everything-i-wanted-suicidal-thoughts-2020-3](https://www.businessinsider.com/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-everything-i-wanted-suicidal-thoughts-2020-3)
- **"TV" (2022):** first verse and chorus written months earlier on tour; the Roe v. Wade line was written weeks before the ruling as "a placeholder of doom" that became reality — a literal example of writing fast from current feeling (Zane Lowe, via NME). [https://www.nme.com/news/music/billie-eilish-drops-guitar-songs-with-two-surprise-tracks-tv-and-the-30th-3274202](https://www.nme.com/news/music/billie-eilish-drops-guitar-songs-with-two-surprise-tracks-tv-and-the-30th-3274202)
- **Co-writing with each other = one writes the scaffold, the other finishes it.** For "Everything I Wanted," Billie brought the dream; Finneas the chord world; both sing melodies back and forth ("singing a melody together"). For HMHAS, Billie "wrote and contributed" more than ever, including production, while Finneas stayed the engineer of her voice. [https://musictech.com/news/music/finneas-billie-eilish-comping-vocals/](https://musictech.com/news/music/finneas-billie-eilish-comping-vocals/)
- **Meaning belongs to the listener.** "With music, my whole thing is that it's for the listener to decide what it means. It doesn't matter what I wrote it about… as long as you interpret it the way you need to." (Billie, Apple Music breakdown). [https://www.audacy.com/new965philly/news/billie-eilish-and-finneas-share-story-of-birds-of-a-feather](https://www.audacy.com/new965philly/news/billie-eilish-and-finneas-share-story-of-birds-of-a-feather)

## 4) Topline/singing method — whisper-to-belt, close-mic, the "quiet hook"

- **The whisper is the instrument.** Her voice was "very soft" at the start; the AT2020 was too hissy for it, so Finneas upgraded to a TLM 103 and later a Telefunken ELA M 251 (chosen by Billie in a blind mic test). The soft voice dictated the gear. [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell)
- **Close-mic, dry, no reverb = "sitting right next to Billie."** Finneas, on "bad guy": "A lot of the time, in pop music especially, there's reverb… But one of our purposes was to make it feel like you were sitting right next to Billie. You know, we made this whole album in a bedroom… my little bedroom at my parents' house had no reverb. So, we liked how the vocals felt, and we just started to embrace it." (Billboard Chart Beat Podcast). [https://www.billboard.com/pro/finneas-interview-billie-eilish-bad-guy-hit-songs-deconstructed-chart-beat-podcast/](https://www.billboard.com/pro/finneas-interview-billie-eilish-bad-guy-hit-songs-deconstructed-chart-beat-podcast/)
- **The room is a vocal effect.** "The bedroom has a very specific sound, very tight and intimate and closed and quiet. I love the way it makes vocals sound." (Finneas, Mix, 2019). [https://www.mixonline.com/recording/finneas-on-producing-billie-eilishs-number-one-album-in-his-bedroom](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/finneas-on-producing-billie-eilishs-number-one-album-in-his-bedroom)
- **Whisper-to-belt dynamics as arrangement.** "Happier Than Ever" is the canonical example: verse/chorus in a near-whisper over fingerpicked guitar, then a full-band rock second half where she screams ("the screaming she does in the ending of the song where she's literally yelling and screaming curse words" — Finneas's favorite moment of the recording). [https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/](https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/)
- **"BIRDS OF A FEATHER" contains the highest belt of her career** (Billie, Apple Music interview, per Capital). The first verse was "the most challenging… to sing" on the whole album — she auditioned multiple note choices per lyric (Apple Music breakdown via Audacy). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birds_of_a_Feather_(Billie_Eilish_song)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birds_of_a_Feather_(Billie_Eilish_song)), [https://www.audacy.com/new965philly/news/billie-eilish-and-finneas-share-story-of-birds-of-a-feather](https://www.audacy.com/new965philly/news/billie-eilish-and-finneas-share-story-of-birds-of-a-feather)
- **The "quiet hook": hook = rhythm + timbre + spoken-word, not belt.** "bad guy" is built on a bassline the crowd sings like "a soccer chant"; the chorus deliberately "has no hook" in the traditional sense — "The thing we were most worried about was the chorus, and having it have no hook" (Billie). The "duh" ad-lib took an "incredibly high number of takes" (34, per Rolling Stone's breakdown coverage) and was left slightly imperfect for character. [https://www.billboard.com/pro/finneas-interview-billie-eilish-bad-guy-hit-songs-deconstructed-chart-beat-podcast/](https://www.billboard.com/pro/finneas-interview-billie-eilish-bad-guy-hit-songs-deconstructed-chart-beat-podcast/), [https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/billie-eilish-bad-guy-finneas-song-breakdown-video-882603/](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/billie-eilish-bad-guy-finneas-song-breakdown-video-882603/)
- **Melody as speech rhythm.** Finneas on "bad guy" verses: "the thing that's interesting isn't necessarily the vocal melody on the verses; it's that there's three-part harmony and it's the rhythm." [https://www.mixonline.com/recording/finneas-on-producing-billie-eilishs-number-one-album-in-his-bedroom](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/finneas-on-producing-billie-eilishs-number-one-album-in-his-bedroom)
- **Comping as a vocal-writing tool.** Billie comps takes line-by-line from memory of which take had the best word: "I like this take and I like this word from this, taking like this word" (Finneas describing her process). [https://musictech.com/news/music/finneas-billie-eilish-comping-vocals/](https://musictech.com/news/music/finneas-billie-eilish-comping-vocals/)
- **Trained dynamics (2024+).** Eilish believed she'd "always have a soft voice" until singing lessons expanded her range — "the truth is that literally the love of my life is singing. And I didn't realize that you can train that instrument" (Vogue cover, Nov 2024; NPR Fresh Air, Dec 2024). ## 5) Vocal-anchoring production — the scaffolding under the whisper

- **Field recordings as percussion/timbre (the "mouth-sounds" signature).** Finneas records sounds constantly on his iPhone: "I'll walk around with — just an iPhone will work… if I hear an interesting sound, I'll just record it. And then later, I'll listen through them and I'll go like, 'I wonder how can I use that?'" — because "a lot of music producers have… the same toolbox… I want something to set my stuff apart." (Tonight Show, via E!). Examples: the Australian crosswalk-button beep became the rhythmic pulse of "bad guy"'s chorus; the dentist drill (recorded by Billie at her own appointment) became the grating texture of "bury a friend." [https://www.eonline.com/news/1119212/billie-eilish-s-brother-finneas-o-connell-reveals-the-secret-sounds-mixed-into-her-songs](https://www.eonline.com/news/1119212/billie-eilish-s-brother-finneas-o-connell-reveals-the-secret-sounds-mixed-into-her-songs)
- **Foley discipline:** iPhone stereo hack (record video, strip audio) for location sounds; "oftentimes, I'm just looking for something textural – something that is interplaying with five other elements" (TapeOp). [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell)
- **"Less but chosen" production:** he now uses fewer, more intentional elements ("I know what thing is going to go the extra mile"), blending soft layers that leave "oxygen" around the voice. [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell)
- **Signature plug-ins:** Logic's Vocal Transformer for the "rattlesnake" vocal on "bad guy"; harsh zero-percent-smooth tremolo chopped to 8th/16th notes; Valhalla Room/VintageVerb and EchoBoy for the rare reverb/delay. [https://www.mixonline.com/recording/finneas-on-producing-billie-eilishs-number-one-album-in-his-bedroom](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/finneas-on-producing-billie-eilishs-number-one-album-in-his-bedroom), [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell)
- **Keep the mistakes that give life:** the humming at the top of "bad guy" was kept against Finneas's instinct because it makes the bass seem in a different key — "No, no, no" (Billie); breaths had to be "exactly the same." [https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/billie-eilish-bad-guy-finneas-song-breakdown-video-882603/](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/billie-eilish-bad-guy-finneas-song-breakdown-video-882603/)
- **Easter-egg sourcing:** on "BIRDS OF A FEATHER," a heavily edited snippet of Billie's own "Getting Older" vocal was chopped into the production — the duo discovered it mid-breakdown (Apple Music *Story of My Song*). [https://www.audacy.com/new965philly/news/billie-eilish-and-finneas-share-story-of-birds-of-a-feather](https://www.audacy.com/new965philly/news/billie-eilish-and-finneas-share-story-of-birds-of-a-feather)
- **Live quietness reinforces the studio sound:** no monitor wedges on stage because Billie sings quietly; amps backstage, drummer behind plexiglass — the live show preserves the whisper aesthetic (TapeOp). [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell)

## 6) Proof songs (2020–2025 focus)

**"Happier Than Ever" (2021)** — the fake-out structure, documented. Started in 2019 on an $80 toy guitar in Middelfart, Denmark ("I had this little toy guitar… It just sounded really cute"); the "When I'm away from you, I'm happier than ever" chorus melody came first, tied to what Billie was going through "at that exact moment"; finished in lockdown 2020 (8 months later they "wrote what became part two" — acoustically); recording completed summer 2020. The song is a whisper-to-scream two-part structure: fingerpicked guitar + intimate verse, then a full-band rock outro with screamed curse words — Finneas: "We found it cathartic. We were like, 'Oh, this is definitely the title track.'" Chart: Hot 100 debut/peak #11 (Aug 2021), 26 weeks. (Billboard Finneas interview; elpee.jp chart archive). [https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/](https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/), [https://billboard.elpee.jp/single/Happier%20Than%20Ever/Billie%20Eilish](https://billboard.elpee.jp/single/Happier%20Than%20Ever/Billie%20Eilish)

**"BIRDS OF A FEATHER" (2024)** — the over-thought "simple" song; proof that a soft mid-tempo love song can be the biggest song of the year. D major, 105 BPM; title reverses the cliché (everyone sings about dying for love; "I want you to stay / Till I'm in the grave / Till I rot away, dead and buried" flips it to *wanting to die after* the loved one — Finneas: "There's a lot of songs about dying for somebody and loving them till they die, and I thought it was really fun to reverse it"). Written Feb 2023–Jan 2024; ~7 months of deliberation; chorus rewritten at the end; contains her highest belt to date; features a Finneas-sung or heavily-produced vocal stack and a chopped "Getting Older" sample. Result: fan-chosen hit — peaked #2 Hot 100, #1 Global 200 (2 wks), 52+ weeks Hot 100, Spotify's most-streamed song of 2024, 2025 Grammy Song of the Year. [https://www.audacy.com/new965philly/news/billie-eilish-and-finneas-share-story-of-birds-of-a-feather](https://www.audacy.com/new965philly/news/billie-eilish-and-finneas-share-story-of-birds-of-a-feather), [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birds_of_a_Feather_(Billie_Eilish_song)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birds_of_a_Feather_(Billie_Eilish_song)), [https://www.vulture.com/article/billie-eilish-birds-of-a-feather-hot-100.html](https://www.vulture.com/article/billie-eilish-birds-of-a-feather-hot-100.html)

**"LUNCH" (2024)** — the "bad guy"-descendant bass banger; proof the duo can still do thumping hooks. Lo-fi bass-heavy beat; written across her real-life experience — "I wrote some of it before even doing anything with a girl, and then wrote the rest after" (Rolling Stone). Released as the post-album single 12 hours after the album dropped; debuted #5 (highest debut of her career). [https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/billie-eilish-hit-me-hard-and-soft-mental-health-fame-1235003585/](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/billie-eilish-hit-me-hard-and-soft-mental-health-fame-1235003585/), [https://www.vulture.com/article/billie-eilish-birds-of-a-feather-hot-100.html](https://www.vulture.com/article/billie-eilish-birds-of-a-feather-hot-100.html)

**Supporting proof:** "bad guy" (2019; bass-first construction, 34-take "duh," no-hook chorus, #1 Hot 100); "Everything I Wanted" (2019/20; dream-to-song in one day; Song Exploder Ep. 197; Record of the Year Grammy 2021); "TV"/"The 30th" (2022; Guitar Songs era, lyrics from current feeling, placeholders becoming real); "Therefore I Am" (2020; Finneas wrote the chorus hook in his own voice first, #2 Hot 100). ## 8) "The trick" in their words — repeatable vs. one-off

**What they say the trick is (repeatable, transferable):**
- **Intimacy as a production decision:** "make it feel like you were sitting right next to Billie" — no reverb, close-mic, quiet voice (Finneas, Chart Beat Podcast). [https://www.billboard.com/pro/finneas-interview-billie-eilish-bad-guy-hit-songs-deconstructed-chart-beat-podcast/](https://www.billboard.com/pro/finneas-interview-billie-eilish-bad-guy-hit-songs-deconstructed-chart-beat-podcast/)
- **Song before production; the singer leads, the producer keeps pace:** "watch the other person start to run, and then I try to keep pace" (TapeOp). [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell)
- **Uniqueness through self-made sound libraries:** "I want something to set my stuff apart" — field recordings and foley instead of the shared sample toolbox (Tonight Show). [https://www.eonline.com/news/1119212/billie-eilish-s-brother-finneas-o-connell-reveals-the-secret-sounds-mixed-into-her-songs](https://www.eonline.com/news/1119212/billie-eilish-s-brother-finneas-o-connell-reveals-the-secret-sounds-mixed-into-her-songs)
- **Listeners don't need convention:** "I don't think listeners care about what is or is not conventional. Listeners just respond emotionally" (Finneas). [https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/](https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/)
- **Emotional honesty is the engine:** "Just say how you feel and let's write a song about whatever you're scared about" (Billie). [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R-ZIRAmO60c](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R-ZIRAmO60c)

**What they call one-off / not reproducible:**
- **The duo itself.** Finneas refuses to make other artists "sound like Billie": "As soon as you make anything that people like, you get all these new artists hitting you up like 'I want to sound just like Billie Eilish.' And I'm always like, 'Absolutely not.'" (Variety, 2019). [https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/](https://variety.com/2019/music/news/billie-eilish-finneas-oconnell-songwriting-1203421768/)
- **The shared biography.** Sibling trust means he can ask the right question and know the backstory on a day she won't talk (TapeOp). [https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell](https://tapeop.com/interviews/165/finneas-oconnell)
- **Hits are not predictable:** "I don't think we pretend to know when something is or isn't going to be successful, because the few times we've tried to guess, we've been wrong" (Finneas). [https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/](https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/finneas-happier-than-ever-grammys-2022-interview-1235012092/)
- **The bedroom acoustics were a found accident, not a plan:** "we liked how the vocals felt, and we just started to embrace it" — the signature emerged from constraints, not design (Chart Beat Podcast). [https://www.billboard.com/pro/finneas-interview-billie-eilish-bad-guy-hit-songs-deconstructed-chart-beat-podcast/](https://www.billboard.com/pro/finneas-interview-billie-eilish-bad-guy-hit-songs-deconstructed-chart-beat-podcast/)

**Verdict for the formula project:** the *transferable* formula is: (1) two-in-a-room writing, singer co-composing the topline over a sketch; (2) dry, close-mic, quiet lead vocal with space carved around it; (3) self-made sound sources as percussion/timbre; (4) fast first drafts from real feeling + ruthless revision; (5) unconventional structure used deliberately (fake-out dynamics, "no hook" hooks, whisper-to-belt arcs). The *non-transferable* parts are the sibling bond and the specific low-register whisper timbre.
