# Research: Twenty One Pilots (Tyler Joseph + Josh Dun) — Lane 13

## Summary
Twenty One Pilots' repeatable engine is **solo authorship + worldbuilding**: Tyler Joseph writes, produces, programs, and sings nearly everything alone in his Columbus, Ohio basement studio ("You just can't co-write a song with a story behind it that is as deep as some of the stories I've fallen in love with" — AltPress #400), then wraps each album in a lore "world" (Dema, Trench, Banditos, Clancy) that fans decode online — which converts repeat listening into detective work. His hook method is **music-first, lyrics-later**: he builds chord structures and programmed beats he loves, drops topline rap-sung verses over them, then revises endlessly ("It is endless... you really have to force yourself to stop" — The Music, 2015), protecting unusual structures against label pressure by trusting that his fanbase's loyalty buys structural risk (Kerrang!, 2025). The 2020–2025 proof set shows the same engine at every scale: quarantine one-day write ("Level of Concern," #23 Hot 100), remote one-man-band album ("Shy Away" — first time Josh Dun recorded drums alone), and the lore-capping "Overcompensate" (#64 Hot 100), the first song finished for *Clancy* and the "tone setter" everything else was filtered through.

## Findings

### 1) Verified credentials & 2020–2025 relevance (Billboard Hot 100 facts)

1. **Hot 100 peaks (verified via chart archives + Wikipedia discography anchored to Billboard's artist chart history):** "Stressed Out" **#2** (Feb 2016; won Best Pop Duo/Group Performance, 59th Grammys), "Heathens" **#2** (Sep 2016), "Ride" **#5** (2016), "Level of Concern" **#23** (2020), "Shy Away" **#87** (2021), "Overcompensate" **#64** (March 2024), "The Craving" **#83** (2024). "Next Semester" did **not** enter the Hot 100 (peak #22 Hot Rock & Alternative Songs; #23 NZ Hot Singles) yet became a live-centerpiece song — useful proof that their model doesn't require radio singles. [Chart archive](http://musicchartsarchive.com/artists/twenty-one-pilots) | [Wikipedia discography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty_One_Pilots_discography)
2. **Record-level facts:** first alternative artist ever with two concurrent Hot 100 top-10s ("Heathens" + "Ride"); first band since The Beatles with two simultaneous top-five Hot 100 hits; ruled Alternative Songs for 30 combined weeks Nov 2015–Nov 2016; first act atop both Alternative and Pop Songs airplay since Lorde's "Royals" (all per Billboard). [Billboard — Songs That Defined the Decade](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/twenty-one-pilots-stressed-out-songs-that-defined-the-decade-8544249/)
3. **Albums 2020–2025:** *Scaled and Icy* debuted **#3** Billboard 200 (2021); *Clancy* **#3** (2024); *Breach* **#1** with the biggest opening week for a rock album in six years (Sept 2025). The only artist in history with every track from **two** albums (Vessel, Blurryface) RIAA gold-or-better. [Wikipedia discography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty_One_Pilots_discography)
4. "Stressed Out" had 2.2 billion YouTube plays as of May 2021, with "Ride" and "Heathens" past a billion — the back catalog still streams 3–4M/week (Nielsen, 2019), i.e., the lore + specificity gives songs decade-long tails. [NME](https://www.nme.com/big-reads/twenty-one-pilots-cover-interview-2021-scaled-and-icy-2944053) | [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/twenty-one-pilots-stressed-out-songs-that-defined-the-decade-8544249/)

### 2) Core repeatable workflow/formula: home-studio one-man-band + concept sequencing

5. **The one-man-band rule.** Tyler writes, produces, programs, and performs nearly everything himself; Josh Dun's lane is drums + live show. Verified across eras: HuffPost 2013 ("Pretty much all the programming on our CDs is done by me personally... I had complete control from the very beginning of where those songs were going. And I didn't know that there were many rules in music when I first started writing"), Trench (2018, "It was written down in my studio, in my basement, so it was all at home... keeping it very intimate and sticking to the way we've always done it which is asking ourselves 'do we like this?' and that's it" — Music Feeds), and *Scaled and Icy* (2021, first entirely self-produced record; Josh Dun engineered his own drums from LA). [HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/emvesselem-chatting-with_b_2469647) | [Music Feeds](https://musicfeeds.com.au/features/twenty-one-pilots-meaning-new-album-trench-blurryface-real-name/) | [Rolling Stone AU](https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/twenty-one-pilots-scaled-and-icy-26215/)
6. **Why he refuses co-writers** (AltPress #400, Dec 2021): "You just can't co-write a song with a story behind it that is as deep as some of the stories I've fallen in love with. There's something also very powerful about being able to say, 'Hey, this is me.' If this fails, it's me. If it does well, it's me." Josh Dun on the same system: "When Tyler and I first met... we both realized that we were not musicians that ever really enjoy jamming. He has a studio in his basement. He likes to just go down there and create." [iHeart quoting AltPress](https://www.iheart.com/content/2021-12-15-twenty-one-pilots-tyler-joseph-explains-why-he-writes-music-in-isolation/) | [AltPress #400](https://www.altpress.com/twenty-one-pilots-scaled-and-icy-interview-issue-400/)
7. **Music first, lyrics later, endlessly revised.** HuffPost 2013: "I think throughout the day, there are always lines or certain words, and I'll just keep notes in my phone. It might just be one or two words, and then, that could inspire a whole song, lyrically. But really what I do musically is sit down and say, 'I want to write this song'... I use a lot of replacement and MIDI... I come up with the music, and my favorite thing about music is just the chord structure. I love chords and how they kind of rub up against each other. Then from there, a song just kind of grows." [HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/emvesselem-chatting-with_b_2469647)
8. **The "pore over it endlessly" overhaul (verified versions of the lane's claim):** The Music (AU, July 2015): "**It is *endless***. I mean, Josh and I listen to demos and we could go this way with it, we could go that way with it, we could change this sound, and you really have to force yourself to stop. And that's one of the ways I feel like that we've matured as artists — we had to learn to have self-control." Plus Zane Lowe (2018): "**Every moment away from a song is very valuable because the fresh ears aspect of it**." And "Next Semester" went "through multiple variations during the writing process, but he stuck with his original version" (Clancy track-by-track). The exact phrase "pore over" appears in no verifiable primary quote — treat the task's phrasing as a gloss on the "endless revision + fresh ears" method above. [The Music](https://themusic.com.au/features/twenty-one-pilots-tyler-joseph-joshua-dun-mitch-knox/z2DAw8LFxMc/02-07-15) | [Zane Lowe Trench transcript](https://twentyonepilots.fandom.com/wiki/Transcripts/2018/Zane_Lowe_(Trench)) | [Social Casualty track-by-track](https://social-casualty.com/articles-interviews/clancy-track-by-track)
9. **Concept-album sequencing = repeat-play engine.** The lore arc (Blurryface 2015 → Trench 2018 → Scaled and Icy 2021 → Clancy 2024) is run like a serialized puzzle: hidden website (dmaorg.info), letters from "Clancy," anagram titles ("Scaled and Icy" = "Clancy is dead"), video trilogies, and a one-week album delay to finish a music video for **every track**. Tyler (2018): "I wanted to create a world that I could go into and write from. In that world, I could control the exterior and what was pushing down on me." And: "It started to consume me... I know every aspect of this world. I know what the weather's like in this world. I wanted to go there and write from that place." [Grammy.com lore explainer](https://www.grammy.com/news/twenty-one-pilots-clancy-new-album-narrative-videos/) | [Zane Lowe Trench transcript](https://twentyonepilots.fandom.com/wiki/Transcripts/2018/Zane_Lowe_(Trench)) | [Kerrang! on Clancy pushback](https://www.kerrang.com/twenty-one-pilots-push-back-release-of-new-album-clancy-by-one-week)
10. **Fans write the world with him.** "This record is... They really did help write it. This thing wouldn't be breathing without them" (Zane Lowe, 2018). NME 2021: "It's our story. In a sense, we're actually the most free band when it comes to what we want to do next, musically and even lyrically, because it's our story. We've created that world so whatever direction we go inside of it is purely justified. I feel like we're closer to limitless than most bands." [NME](https://www.nme.com/big-reads/twenty-one-pilots-cover-interview-2021-scaled-and-icy-2944053)

### 3) Lyric method — hyper-specific imagery, biography, journal → revision

11. **Journal-first, unfiltered, then edited — not transcribed.** FaceCulture (Aug 2013): "From the beginning, my songwriting was from writing in a journal; it was completely unfiltered... I'm not just gonna straight up write a song word-by-word from what I journaled because, one, it probably wouldn't make much sense, and two, it would be a little, uh... more crude. But when you force yourself to write, you start to realize how creative you are." Also: "I mostly throw away ideas now more than ever because when I try to write I get a lot out of it, like it helps me work through things" (the catharsis = the point, not the artifact). [FaceCulture transcript](https://twentyonepilots.fandom.com/wiki/Transcripts/2013/Face_Culture)
12. **Specificity beats universality — a verified industry-observed principle.** Producer Mike Elizondo tried to get Tyler to change the "My name's Blurryface, and I care what you think" hook ("I was thinking big picture... maybe you should change that; it's a great melody, it's a big hook of the song, but I just don't know what it means"), and later concluded: "though the lyrical content is very specific to him, the listener is able to impose their own story onto it." Named-detail examples: "Out of student loans and treehouse homes, we all would take the latter"; "Panic on the brain, Michael's gone insane" (Tyler's parents, Michael and Julie Joseph, in "Level of Concern" — AltPress confirmed the names are his parents); the real "Clancy" reveal "If you can't see, I am Clancy / Prodigal son, done running, come up with Josh Dun." [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/twenty-one-pilots-stressed-out-songs-that-defined-the-decade-8544249/) | [AltPress Michael & Julie](https://www.altpress.com/twenty-one-pilots-level-of-concern-tyler-joseph-michael-and-julie/) | [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/twenty-one-pilots-clancy-new-album-narrative-videos/)
13. **Biography as material — death, faith, family.** Kerrang! K!1593: "I would rather just die than fake a song. The morning I wake up and I'm done talking about these things, I want to be done with music." and "One of our motivations is to get people to start thinking. When I first started writing songs, I had a lot of questions. A lot of big questions." *Trench*'s "Legend" was written live as his grandfather died: "I was writing it while he was sick... by the time I got to the last verse he'd already passed. So you can kind of feel that chronologically happen in the writing of that song." "Good Day" is denial-of-grief disguised as joy: "If I were to lose my wife and my kid... I'd be saying to my other friends and family: 'I'm fine. Don't worry about me.'... I love that songs can be that." [Kerrang!](https://www.kerrang.com/i-would-rather-die-than-fake-a-song-inside-the-success-of-twenty-one-pilots-blurryface) | [Music Feeds](https://musicfeeds.com.au/features/twenty-one-pilots-meaning-new-album-trench-blurryface-real-name/) | [NME](https://www.nme.com/big-reads/twenty-one-pilots-cover-interview-2021-scaled-and-icy-2944053)
14. **Lyric themes deliberately reused instead of reinvented.** On *Clancy* (Kerrang! 2025): "in the past, I would always try to make my lyrical content different from the last thing that I said... Clancy was the first album where I felt like I was making a circle back to things that I've said before, and being okay with that... It's kind of full-circle, lyrically." Lore payoff examples: *Clancy* closer "Paladin Strait" ends on a callback to a Blurryface lyric ("So few, so proud, so emotional / Hello, Clancy"). [Kerrang!](https://www.kerrang.com/twenty-one-pilots-tyler-joseph-josh-dun-clancy-era-world-tour-interview-cover-story) | [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/twenty-one-pilots-clancy-new-album-narrative-videos/)

### 4) Topline / singing method — rap-singer, nasal falsetto, instrument-first hooks

15. **Delivery = conversational rap-singing over pop chorus.** Billboard's decade essay describes "Stressed Out"'s verses as "almost sounded conversational rather than rapped... like a confessional, an airing of one's anxieties to a close friend" — that's the core topline signature: talk-rapped verses, melody-heavy sung/falsetto choruses. The chorus melody of "Heathens" was originally the verse melody until a crew member whistled it back and Tyler realized it was the hook ("That's the chorus."). [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/twenty-one-pilots-stressed-out-songs-that-defined-the-decade-8544249/) | [Zane Lowe 2018 transcript](https://twentyonepilots.fandom.com/wiki/Transcripts/2018/Zane_Lowe_(Trench))
16. **Falsetto / head-voice crossover is the vocal fingerprint** (secondary vocal-analysis sources, not Tyler quotes): Singing Carrots and The Vocal Files both identify his style as chest-voice rap attack in verses breaking into breathy falsetto in choruses, with a soft F3–Bb2 low register; the "Stressed Out" chorus is commonly taught as a mouth-to-nose (nasal placement) falsetto blend. Note: no primary interview quote from Tyler describing his own technique was found — the "I wish I had a better voice" line in "Stressed Out" is the closest self-documentation. [Singing Carrots](https://singingcarrots.com/learn-to-sing-like/Twenty%20One%20Pilots) | [The Vocal Files](https://thevocalfiles.wordpress.com/)
17. **New instruments generate hooks.** "Shy Away" started when Tyler taught himself electric guitar ("I think that nothing is more inspiring than learning a new instrument that I've never played before, specifically, the electric guitar"); "Level of Concern" was "the first one I ever wrote on an electric guitar... it's simple but hopeful"; *Trench* was bass-guitar-led ("I was very inclined to reach for the bass guitar while writing each of these songs"); early-career hits were piano/ukulele-driven ("House of Gold," "The Judge"). Piano-hook detail from Billboard: "I love the idea of holding that one chord on my left hand on the piano and just letting it ride." [BBC Radio 1 transcript](https://twentyonepilots.fandom.com/wiki/Transcripts/2021/BBCR1_Shy_Away) | [Music Feeds](https://musicfeeds.com.au/features/twenty-one-pilots-meaning-new-album-trench-blurryface-real-name/) | [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/rock/twenty-one-pilots-tyler-joseph-interview-scaled-and-icy-9581253/)
18. **Working within pop-song parameters on purpose.** "You kind of have to celebrate the moment that you get to create something that you love that falls into the parameters of a 3-minute-and-20-second song, to try to be creative inside of those parameters" — Tyler Joseph (widely quoted; **original interview source unverified**, so treat as secondary). The verified structural quote: "Writing songs is kind of like a wrestling match to me. You have to pin it down and make it do what you want it to do" (also unverified-origin, aggregated by BrainyQuote). [BrainyQuote](https://www.brainyquote.com/authors/tyler-joseph-quotes)

### 5) Vocal-anchoring production — self-produced beats framing the rap-singing topline

19. **Tyler builds the beat around his own vocal delivery in one room.** HuffPost 2013: "I find it inside of my programming. I use a lot of replacement and MIDI in a lot of these programs, which help me find the sound I'm looking for. I put together a song, and then I lay down vocals" — i.e., demo vocals are cut against programmed MIDI, and the album versions kept basement vocals (the "Holding On to You" vocal on *Vessel* is literally the one Tyler recorded pre-signing in his basement). [HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/emvesselem-chatting-with_b_2469647)
20. **The "two hands" proof:** *Scaled and Icy* was "the first time he's recorded drums all on his own, no engineer, no one helping him at all" (Tyler on Dun, BBC Radio 1); Tyler wrote/produced in Columbus while Dun self-engineered drums in LA with shared-screen file exchange: "literally my mouse was moving on his computer while he was recording, and vice versa" (Billboard). *Clancy* credits: Tyler — vocals, bass, piano, synths, sampling, programming, production; Dun — drums, drum engineering; Paul Meany — additional production (the only recurring co-producer, working remotely). [BBC Radio 1](https://twentyonepilots.fandom.com/wiki/Transcripts/2021/BBCR1_Shy_Away) | [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/rock/twenty-one-pilots-tyler-joseph-interview-scaled-and-icy-9581253/) | [Wikipedia Overcompensate credits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overcompensate_(song))
21. **Live-first production thinking.** The beat/backing tracks are the same files he controls live: "I can look at Josh and go, 'What do you think about doing this?' Then I can go to the computer, structure it out, and we can just play through it... Pretty much all the programming on our CDs is done by me personally" (HuffPost). Album openers double as concert openers — "Good Day" was designed around the live-show "turning on" feeling. [HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/emvesselem-chatting-with_b_2469647) | [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/rock/twenty-one-pilots-tyler-joseph-interview-scaled-and-icy-9581253/)

### 6) Proof songs (2020–2025)

22. **"Level of Concern" (Apr 2020; Hot 100 #23)** — quarantine speed-write: inspired by his mom Kelly to write for fans, turned into a love song to wife Jenna, first-ever electric-guitar song, written/recorded in isolation during lockdown; #23 Hot 100 debut and a Guinness-record "never-ending" fan video (177 days) — the model scaling down to a single song in a week. [AltPress](https://www.altpress.com/twenty-one-pilots-level-of-concern-tyler-joseph-michael-and-julie/) | [Twenty One Pilots Wiki](https://twentyonepilots.fandom.com/wiki/Level_of_Concern)
23. **"Shy Away" (Apr 2021; Hot 100 #87)** — the process as product: written "almost as a tutorial" for his brother Jay ("How do you start a song? How do you record the song?"); keeps the accidental baby-coo voice memo in the intro; demonstrates the two-hands remote production method. [BBC Radio 1](https://twentyonepilots.fandom.com/wiki/Transcripts/2021/BBCR1_Shy_Away) | [BBC News](http://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-56665750)
24. **"Overcompensate" (Feb 2024; Hot 100 #64)** — the lore payoff + the album's yardstick: first song finished for *Clancy*, "a bit of a tone setter for us, and definitely the song that we filtered all the rest of the songs through" (Audacy On The Record); Tyler on BBC Radio 1: "There's a bit of a confidence and swagger in it that the character needed to embody in order to take on the new role in the story we've been telling, and Clancy is gonna rise up as that person." German/French/Spanish spoken-word intro (a "Voldsoy" lore device), psych-funk groove, polyrhythmic rap flow in the verse. [Audacy](https://www.audacy.com/national/music/twenty-one-pilots-on-the-record-clancy-takeover) | [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/twenty-one-pilots-clancy-new-album-narrative-videos/) | [Kerrang!](https://www.kerrang.com/twenty-one-pilots-tyler-joseph-josh-dun-clancy-era-world-tour-interview-cover-story)
25. **"Next Semester" (Mar 2024; no Hot 100, #22 Hot Rock & Alt)** — structural-risk case study: Tyler kept the "twists and turns" despite advisors pushing for "more simple and easier to digest," and the fan reaction vindicated it; his favorite to play live "because of that backstory." Written about the panic-attack-in-the-road student image ("Stand up straight now, can't break down, graduate now"; yellow street lines) — hyper-specific imagery again. [Kerrang!](https://www.kerrang.com/twenty-one-pilots-tyler-joseph-josh-dun-clancy-era-world-tour-interview-cover-story) | [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/twenty-one-pilots/next-semester)
26. **"The Craving" / "The Craving (Jenna's Version)" (May 2024; Hot 100 #83)** — kitchen-table biography: "I wrote the song about my relationship with my wife but kinda tapped into earlier on in our relationship when I was trying to woo her... I wrote it at the kitchen table. You know, with a couple of our kids on either side of us" (BBC Radio 1's Jack Saunders). Two released arrangements of the same song (album version opens with Jenna's voice) — a deliberate topline-recontextualization trick. [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/twenty-one-pilots/the-craving) | [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/watch-twenty-one-pilots-debut-unreleased-song-the-craving-live-in-london-3755030) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Craving_(song))

### 8) "The trick" in their words — repeatable vs. one-off

27. **Repeatable (his own framework):**
    - *Solo authorship protects the story:* Q1 above. This is a durable, transferable principle — write alone when the song's value is in its specific personal/lore meaning.
    - *Constraint + obsession:* "It is endless... you have to force yourself to stop" (Q7) and the 3:20-parameters quote (unverified origin) — the repeatable discipline is *endless revision inside a hard length limit*, then forced shipping.
    - *Trust the fans' taste over the room's advice:* Q14 — structural complexity as a loyalty dividend; his stated model is that fans "trust our gut instinct," so he doesn't simplify for radio.
    - *World before song:* Q3/Q4 — build a controllable fictional container first, then write from inside it; every subsequent song reuses the container (repeat listen + repeat purchase across a multi-album arc).
    - *Specificity is the hook:* Q19 (Elizondo's lesson) — name real things (parents' names, treehouse homes, kitchen tables) and let listeners project onto them.
28. **One-off / not portable:** the lore metaverse at TØP's scale (dmaorg.org, ARG, per-track videos, 10-years-later callbacks) is a compounding, audience-co-authored apparatus — it works because the fanbase has spent a decade decoding it; a new act copying "the lore" without the installed base gets the cost without the payoff. Tyler himself frames the story as a one-way door: "it's our story" (Q10) — the freedom comes from *owning* the container, not from the container itself. The baby-coo/kitchen-table accidents are irreproducible by design; what's repeatable is *keeping* the accidents.
