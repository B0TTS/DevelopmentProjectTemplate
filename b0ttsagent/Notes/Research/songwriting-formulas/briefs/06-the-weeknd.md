# Research: The Weeknd (Abel Tesfaye) — Songwriting Formula Brief

**Lane:** The Weeknd (Abel Tesfaye; XO/Republic) — *After Hours* (2020), *Dawn FM* (2022), *Hurry Up Tomorrow* (2025). Core team: Max Martin, Oscar Holter, Belly (Ahmad Balshe), DaHeala (Jason Quenneville), Cirkut (Henry Walter), Mike Dean.
**Distinct angle:** Michael-meets-disco falsetto topline, written in a co-writer room with Max Martin/Oscar Holter; persona-as-concept anchors every album's hook emotion.
**Seam:** (a) co-writer-heavy collaboration, vocals cut in his own/home studios; (b) falsetto-to-chest switch as a hook device; (c) the "melodic math" topline of 'Blinding Lights' / 'Save Your Tears' / 'Take My Breath' / 'Dancing in the Flames' / 'Timeless'.

## 2. The core repeatable workflow/formula (as actually done)

**The room:** Abel + Max Martin + Oscar Holter as the writing/production core, with Belly and DaHeala in the circle. Credits across the lane are near-identical: "Blinding Lights" (Abel, Belly, DaHeala, Martin, Holter; produced by Martin/Holter/Abel), "Save Your Tears" (same five), "Take My Breath" (Abel, Belly, Martin, Holter), "Dancing in the Flames" (Abel, Martin, Holter only). [Billboard credits](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/the-weeknd-blinding-lights-recording-credits-9449471/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_Your_Tears)

Step-by-step (verified from interviews):

1. **Concept first, always.** Every album = a persona/place with one emotional weather system: *After Hours* = one bad night in Vegas (the red-jacket character); *Dawn FM* = purgatory as a car radio station ("the listener is dead... stuck in traffic waiting to reach the light at the end of the tunnel... a radio host guiding you to the light"); *Hurry Up Tomorrow* = the persona burned down ("I'm lighting my persona up on fire"). [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/the-weeknd-blinding-lights-billboard-cover-story-2021-interview-1235001282/), [Billboard, HUT film](https://www.billboard.com/culture/tv-film/the-weeknd-hurry-up-tomorrow-film-music-1235970817/)
2. **Bring a vibe reference, not a demo.** For "Blinding Lights," Abel arrived with an 80s vision (GTA: Vice City nostalgia, Hall & Oates, Michael Jackson); Max Martin's engineer Sam Holland projected a computer-animated car driving through a futuristic city on the wall as in-session inspiration. [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/the-weeknd-blinding-lights-billboard-cover-story-2021-interview-1235001282/)
3. **Write the topline fast, in the room.** "Hardest to Love": "I went into a room for 20 minutes and wrote the entire song, and then Max produced it." Fast first pass; then **long polish** — Abel lives with unfinished songs for a year and can't unhear "wrong notes" ("67 versions of 'The Hills'"). [Variety track-by-track](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/the-weeknd-after-hours-track-by-track-1234574136/)
4. **Bounce everything off the inner circle.** "Belly and DaHeala are my guys — anything I do, I feel like I have to bounce ideas off them." Belly: "Getting to watch the great Max Martin and Abel create is a dream in itself." [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/the-weeknd-blinding-lights-billboard-cover-story-2021-interview-1235001282/)
5. **Cut vocals in his own space, finish in the pro rooms.** *After Hours* was made "at studios in New York, Los Angeles, Toronto, and right here at home" (Rolling Stone, Sept 2020); during quarantine he produced new songs "out of a makeshift studio in his condo." Studio footprint for the lane: MXM (LA & Stockholm), Jungle City (NYC), Conway (LA), House Mouse (Stockholm). [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/the-weeknd-after-hours-grammys-interview-1061041/), [Wikipedia, "Blinding Lights"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blinding_Lights)
6. **Release early, keep the campaign alive with remixes.** "Blinding Lights" was out 4 months before its No. 1 week; catalog tracks get second lives via remixes ("Save Your Tears," "Die For You" both hit No. 1 *after* Grande remixes) and TikTok reactivation. [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/chart-beat/the-weeknd-ariana-grande-die-for-you-number-one-billboard-hot-100-1235280422/)
7. **Perform before it's finished.** "Timeless" debuted live at São Paulo ("Let's perform the song we worked on today") and the studio version dropped 3 weeks later. [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/music/news/playboi-carti-the-weeknd-performed-timeless-before-finished-1236599266/)

**Workload note (verified):** Abel is a workaholic — "We call him 'The Machine' because he's always working" (manager Amir "Cash" Esmailian). [Variety cover story](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/the-weeknd-exclusive-interview-after-hours-1234573754/)

## 3. Lyric method — emotional distillation to one hook feeling

- **One feeling per song, stated plainly, with a dark undertone.** "Blinding Lights" per Abel (Esquire): "how you want to see someone at night, and you're intoxicated, and you're driving to this person and you're just blinded by streetlights, but nothing could stop you from trying to go see that person, because you're so lonely. I don't want to ever promote drunk driving, but that's what the dark undertone is." [via Wikipedia, "Blinding Lights"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blinding_Lights)
- **Emotion over intellect, sound over diction.** "I'm not an intellectual so I can't really explain some of the things I do — it's just emotional. On 'Alone Together' I don't even want you to hear what I'm saying — it's more about the sound... On some of Michael Jackson's best songs, I don't know what the f— he's saying, it's about how he's saying it." [Variety track-by-track](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/the-weeknd-after-hours-track-by-track-1234574136/)
- **Jolly surface, heavy subtext (the core trick).** "It's always been a joke with The Weeknd music, where it makes you sing and dance and it feels jolly. And then when you actually get into the themes of it, it's something much deeper — and maybe a call for help, who knows." [Billboard, HUT film](https://www.billboard.com/culture/tv-film/the-weeknd-hurry-up-tomorrow-film-music-1235970817/)
- **Lyrics are sung as story or as texture depending on placement.** "Scared to Live and the next few songs, I want you to hear what I'm saying. I'm telling a story" — vs. the album's openers, where lyrics are a sonic element. [Variety track-by-track](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/the-weeknd-after-hours-track-by-track-1234574136/)
- **Write as an exercise.** During the pandemic he wrote songs about movie scenes/characters on screen: "I'll write about their relationship or something in a song... That's an exercise. I love doing that." [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/the-weeknd-after-hours-grammys-interview-1061041/)
- **The co-writing room is the filter.** Max Martin/Oscar Holter shape the song; Belly and DaHeala are the lyric/bounce board. Verified credits show Belly on "Blinding Lights," "Save Your Tears," "Take My Breath"; DaHeala on "Blinding Lights"/"Save Your Tears." [Billboard credits](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/the-weeknd-blinding-lights-recording-credits-9449471/)

## 4. Topline/singing method — melodic construction and how the hook is SUNG

- **Voice type & range:** lyric tenor; chest voice from ~F2 to tenor G♯, with head-voice/falsetto carrying the fifth octave; documented range ≈ C2–F5, working pop range A3–C5, with the **B4–C5 zone carrying the most emotional weight** ("Blinding Lights," "Save Your Tears" are built there). His falsetto is the *lead register*, not an ornament — with deliberate vocal grain ("roughness at the edges") and runs used sparingly at emotional peaks. [vocalrangecalculator.com](https://vocalrangecalculator.com/the-weeknd-vocal-range/), [Classic FM](https://www.classicfm.com/discover-music/is-the-weeknd-good-singer-vocal-range/)
- **The falsetto-to-chest switch as hook device (verified pattern across the lane):**
  - "Blinding Lights": mid-chest verses (scale-laddering per Slate) snap into a high, strained falsetto chorus ("I can't sleep until I feel your touch"); verse ornaments are "upper mordents" (little flicks up a scale step) that add drive. [Classic FM](https://www.classicfm.com/discover-music/is-the-weeknd-good-singer-vocal-range/)
  - "Save Your Tears": mid-register, almost conversational verse topline; the hook hangs on the contrast between chest-led melodic leaps and floated falsetto releases on the title phrase.
  - "Take My Breath": "the disco thump, electric piano chords and **call-and-response falsetto vocals**... hark back to vintage Bee Gees" — Jon Pareles, *The New York Times*. [via Wikipedia, "Take My Breath"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_My_Breath)
  - "Dancing in the Flames": vocal span F4–B♭5 (Musicnotes/Tunebat data), with the chorus climbing to the top of the falsetto on the title line. [via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_in_the_Flames)
  - "Timeless": the hook ("Ever since I was a jit, knew I was the shit" — Pitchfork) rides a light falsetto/mix melody over a brooding trap bed; Volta (Ojivolta) says the goal was "a song that resonated with the world but had an atypical structure." [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeless_(The_Weeknd_and_Playboi_Carti_song)), [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/music/news/playboi-carti-the-weeknd-performed-timeless-before-finished-1236599266/)
- **"Melodic math"** is the industry term Slate's Chris Molanphy applied to "Blinding Lights" ("scale-laddering verses, tension-filled chorus... the polish and 'melodic math' for which Max Martin is renowned"). [via Wikipedia, "Blinding Lights"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blinding_Lights)
- **Hook-first construction:** "you press play and you know it's a hit almost immediately — you don't have to wait to get the chorus to know it's a great song... 'Blinding Lights' and 'In Your Eyes' didn't [originally] have those melodies in the first verse" (Roxy Music's "More Than This" as the model). [Variety track-by-track](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/the-weeknd-after-hours-track-by-track-1234574136/)
- **Provenance of the voice:** "My natural singing voice was inspired and shaped by Ethiopian music... my voice became a chameleon going into different characters with each album" (TMRW Magazine). Ethiopian singer Aster Aweke: "Her voice was very influential on my singing, you can hear it, for sure." Michael Jackson's "Dirty Diana" = "the blueprint to my music." [Classic FM](https://www.classicfm.com/discover-music/is-the-weeknd-good-singer-vocal-range/), [Variety cover story](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/the-weeknd-exclusive-interview-after-hours-1234573754/)
- **Performance state matters to the take:** for the *Hurry Up Tomorrow* film he re-recorded vocals, and sound designer Johnnie Burn "made him do push-ups to get kind of worked up" before takes. [Billboard, HUT film](https://www.billboard.com/culture/tv-film/the-weeknd-hurry-up-tomorrow-film-music-1235970817/)

## 5. Vocal-anchoring production — what the track does around the falsetto

- **The voice is mixed as an instrument** — "shaped, layered, and placed within his arrangements as much as any synthesiser line" (reverb, doubling, EQ are integral to the sound). [vocalrangecalculator.com](https://vocalrangecalculator.com/the-weeknd-vocal-range/)
- **A synth line doubles as counter-melody / hook-preview.** Classic FM's analysis of "Blinding Lights" (via Top40 Theory) documents "previewing a hook": an instrumental variation of the melodic hook plays in the intro and again in the pre-chorus, so the chorus lands already-familiar. The song's "crystalline synth hook straight out of 1985" is the frame the falsetto sits inside. [Classic FM](https://www.classicfm.com/discover-music/is-the-weeknd-good-singer-vocal-range/), [Variety Hitmakers](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/weeknd-blinding-lights-hitmakers-record-of-the-year-1234846143/)
- **Tempo as a weapon:** "Blinding Lights" runs at **171 BPM** (LA Times noted "a crisp tempo just a few ticks faster than you're always expecting"); "Save Your Tears" at 118 BPM in C major; "Take My Breath" in C minor at 121 BPM; "Dancing in the Flames" in D♭ major at 117 BPM. [SongBPM](https://songbpm.com/the-weeknd/blinding-lights), [SongBPM](https://songbpm.com/the-weeknd/save-your-tears), [Wikipedia, "Take My Breath"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_My_Breath), [Wikipedia, "Dancing in the Flames"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_in_the_Flames)
- **Production trio plays the band:** on "Blinding Lights," Abel, Max Martin and Oscar Holter are all credited for bass, drums, programming and keyboards — the falsetto is one voice in a three-man arrangement. [Billboard credits](https://www.billboard.com/music/music-news/the-weeknd-blinding-lights-recording-credits-9449471/)
- **Retro synth architecture that reads "new":** "It was crossing two worlds to make something new that fits today, sonically. The older folks like it because it's a throwback, and then the kids love it because it's a new thing that they never heard before" — mixer Şerban Ghenea. [via Wikipedia, "Blinding Lights"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blinding_Lights)
- **Producer-side moves on the 2024-25 lane:** Mike Dean added "piano chords, lead lines and some layers" to the Pharrell-started "Timeless" beat; Twisco used analog synths (SOMA Lyra-08) + granular resynthesis. [Variety Hitmakers 2025](https://variety.com/2025/music/news/playboi-carti-the-weeknd-performed-timeless-before-finished-1236599266/)

## 8. "The trick" — in their words

- **Abel's own statement of the trick (repeatable):** "This is deeper, but you wanna dance to it and make love to it. That's the trick of it." — dark content in a danceable shell. [Variety](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/the-weeknd-after-hours-track-by-track-1234574136/)
- **Hook-engineering trick:** the song must "sound amazing right away" — opening hook melodies added in post to "Blinding Lights"/"In Your Eyes"; the hook is previewed instrumentally before the chorus lands (Top40 Theory analysis). [Variety](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/the-weeknd-after-hours-track-by-track-1234574136/), [Classic FM](https://www.classicfm.com/discover-music/is-the-weeknd-good-singer-vocal-range/)
- **Repeatable vs. one-off:** The *team + process* is deliberately repeatable — same 2-5 writers, same 80s-synth/disco palette, same falsetto-to-chest topline, same concept-first album framing (verified across 2019–2025 releases). But Abel rejects song-level repetition: "When it comes to my albums, there is a cohesive sound going on, but I can't really stick to one style. So you'll hear EDM, hip-hop and three other types of sounds in one song — and somehow, we make it work." [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/the-weeknd-blinding-lights-billboard-cover-story-2021-interview-1235001282/)
- **Risk as part of the formula:** Max Martin credits the vision ("He took a risk, and that was very impressive to us"); Abel only trusted the ambition after validation ("It wasn't until people liked 'Blinding Lights' that I was like, 'This is so not what now is, and people loved it anyway'"). [Billboard cover story](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/the-weeknd-blinding-lights-billboard-cover-story-2021-interview-1235001282/), [Variety Hitmakers](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/weeknd-blinding-lights-hitmakers-record-of-the-year-1234846143/)
