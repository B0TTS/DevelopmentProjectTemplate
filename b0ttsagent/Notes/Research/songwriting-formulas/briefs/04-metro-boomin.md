# Research: Metro Boomin — Producer-Led Song Construction (Trap)

**Lane:** Metro Boomin (Leland Tyler Wayne) — Atlanta trap super-producer; co-signer/producer for Future, 21 Savage, Migos, Don Toliver, The Weeknd, Travis Scott. Solo artist brand: *HEROES & VILLAINS* (2022), *Spider-Verse* score/soundtrack, *WE DON'T TRUST YOU* / *WE STILL DON'T TRUST YOU* (2024, with Future). **Seam:** the beat IS the hook; topline is minimal but pivotal; producer directs the writer-artist without writing the song himself.

**Scope confirmed: 2020–2025 covered** (Heroes & Villains 2022, Spider-Verse 2023, WDTY duology 2024, *A Futuristic Summa* 2025, Savage Mode III teases 2025).

## 2) The core repeatable workflow — as Metro actually describes it

Synthesized from his own interviews (Vice/Noisey 2014; FADER 2013; GQ 2018; Apple Music/Ebro 2022; Full Send podcast 2022; Rolling Stone/Timbaland 2024):

1. **Melody first; drums are never first.** "Drums are always last… they're never first, or really rarely, maybe two times out of ten. I'll start with synths or play out some chords or something, and then I might add drums midway." [Vice/Noisey, 2014](https://www.vice.com/en/article/metro-boomin-interview-metro-thuggin/). The hummable material (synth riff/chords) is laid before the 808s — so the beat itself already sings.
2. **Build original from scratch by default.** He historically avoided samples for publishing/money reasons ("hip-hop doesn't pay as much as other genres, so samples already take away what you got… generally I do it all from scratch") and only later "messed with them a little more… to hop in those other crowds" — the 2022–24 sample-era ("Creepin'", "Like That") is a deliberate later phase, not his base mode. [Vice 2014](https://www.vice.com/en/article/metro-boomin-interview-metro-thuggin/); GQ 2018: "I don't sample shit too much—a lot of my stuff is original from scratch." [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/you-better-trust-metro-boomin)
3. **Produce in bulk, finish in batches, revisit.** "I always just sit down and follow these ideas, play around with ideas, start several ideas, revisit 'em… I just finish a lot at once." [GQ 2018](https://www.gq.com/story/you-better-trust-metro-boomin)
4. **Engineer beats for a specific artist's body.** He builds knowing the artist's bounces, drum sounds, sub sounds, and tempos — the "More M's" (Drake & 21 Savage, *Her Loss*, 2022) story: "I cooked it on the MP 2000 XL… it's just knowing him, and what he likes, and what he don't like… is going to make him do what, and what tempos. I just had all that in mind, and I made a beat… when he heard that one, that was the one he just pulled up instantly." [Apple Music Q&A via Attack the Culture](https://www.attacktheculture.com/previews-reviews-more/heres-6-key-moments-from-metro-boomins-new-apple-music-1-qampa). His A&R generalizes it: with Metro it's the opposite of "send me beats" — "He wants to create with you at a very intentional level." [Billboard cover](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/metro-boomin-spider-verse-producer-interview-cover-story-1235430134/)
5. **Play beats in person; watch the artist's body react.** "It helps watching a lot of rappers reactions, like watching what in a beat makes them wanna align with it. Certain things you have to watch them and see what really gets the *oohs* and *ahhs* out of them, and you come back harder with that." [FADER, 2013](https://www.thefader.com/2013/08/14/beat-construction-metro-boomin). With 21 Savage that filter is brutal: "I'll play this man 35 or 40 beats before he wants to get in the booth… 'Next one, next one, next one.'" [Metro to Rolling Stone w/ Timbaland, Oct 2024, via HNHH](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/852601-metro-boomin-21-savage-draining-collab-process-hip-hop-news)
6. **Skeleton → artist → re-produce loop.** Beats start as "skeletons of stuff… just to get an idea going. Then an artist might do some ideas on it and I'll go back in and fuck with it some more. Have another producer come back and fuck with it some more then have the artist come back in." [Full Send podcast, via How To Rap](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/metro-boomin-teaches-how-to-make-beats-in-5-steps-bd7b054aca62)
7. **Producer as editor, not just beatmaker.** "I've grown from more of a beatmaker role to more of a real producer role… I might give you some bars, I might help you start this off, I might let you know like yo, this was hard, but you started to lose me halfway through. Let's just fix that up… we gotta try to make something out of it." [Full Send via Medium](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/metro-boomin-teaches-how-to-make-beats-in-5-steps-bd7b054aca62)
8. **Post-production and arrangement are part of the job.** Days are split across making beats, "post-production, editing vocals," and arranging — e.g., chopping 21 Savage's and Future's separate takes on "X" so they "sound like they did it together at the same time." [GQ 2018](https://www.gq.com/story/you-better-trust-metro-boomin)
9. **Position the song like an event.** Producer albums are cinematic universes (trilogy concept, Morgan Freeman narration, short film, comic-cover feature reveals, Coachella set, hologram, in-store signings) — the beat-rollout machinery that turns songs into chart moments. [Billboard cover](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/metro-boomin-spider-verse-producer-interview-cover-story-1235430134/); WDTY trailer/rollout. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_That_(Future,_Metro_Boomin_and_Kendrick_Lamar_song))
10. **Let the music make itself; don't manufacture virality.** "I'm big believer in just letting the music make itself." [Apple Music](https://www.attacktheculture.com/previews-reviews-more/heres-6-key-moments-from-metro-boomins-new-apple-music-1-qampa). "The kids… are always gonna sniff out the good shit. Always… you put out a good album, it's just going crazy on there bruh, versus you tryna cater, kinda fake." [Full Send via Medium](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/metro-boomin-teaches-how-to-make-beats-in-5-steps-bd7b054aca62)

## 3) Lyric method — how he drives the hook/lyric while staying "the producer"

- **He does not write songs; he directs their construction.** His lyric intervention is at hook/chorus architecture level: give a few bars, start the artist off, flag where the song "loses" the listener, demand a fix (Full Send quote above). The quality bar is the beat: after 35–40 beat rejections, "I'm like, 'Okay, you've got to overly bring it on this one. I'm not taking any okay shit, n***a.'" [Rolling Stone via HNHH, Oct 2024](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/852601-metro-boomin-21-savage-draining-collab-process-hip-hop-news)
- **Cast artists for melodic/hook roles, not just features.** On H&V he "envisioned Don Toliver portraying a role akin to what Nate Dogg did on Dr. Dre's *The Chronic 2001*" — a designated hook man. [Full Send via Medium](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/metro-boomin-teaches-how-to-make-beats-in-5-steps-bd7b054aca62). Pitchfork on the result: "Few artists melodically thrive over a Metro beat like Don Toliver" ("Too Many Nights", "Around Me"). [Pitchfork via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Around_Me)
- **Hooks as chants, not prose.** His biggest hooks are repeated phrase-chants: "Percocet, Molly, Percocet" ("Mask Off" — "The pair had legions of people chanting… like a pagan mantra" [Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/metro-boomin-interview/)); "I'm a superhero" ("Superhero"); the title-as-diss "Like That"; the title-chant "We Don't Trust You" (derived from the tag, below). Repetition + attitude beats wordcount in trap.
- **Callouts are the brand layer he deliberately plants.** Two of rap's most famous callouts live on Metro's records:
  - "If Young Metro don't trust you, I'ma shoot you" — spoken by **21 Savage** on "X" (Savage Mode, 2016), then amplified when Kanye demanded the tag on "Father Stretch My Hands Pt. 1" at the TLOP premiere: "I didn't put that tag on that beat. It's Kanye's shit. He asked for it… 'I'm finna play the album, but I need the tag on the song.'" [Billboard cover](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/metro-boomin-spider-verse-producer-interview-cover-story-1235430134/). It became the 2024 album title *WE DON'T TRUST YOU*.
  - "Metro Boomin want some more!" — **Young Thug invented it in-session**: "Honestly that's just some shit Thug made up in his head… I remember when we did the song, me, TM88 and Sonny Digital made the beat while Thug was there in the studio… fifteen minutes we came back, the song was done." [Vice 2014](https://www.vice.com/en/article/metro-boomin-interview-metro-thuggin/). (Skeptic note: the "brand-as-tune" tag is artist-authored, not Metro's own ad-lib — the producer curates and monetizes the artist's chant.)
- **The tag is the hook before the hook.** Metro's production signature is described as "eerie synth loops, 808s, soulful samples and orchestral finishes **and branded by his notorious producer tags**" — the tag lands at the drop, doing the work of a chorus intro. [Billboard cover](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/metro-boomin-spider-verse-producer-interview-cover-story-1235430134/)

## 4) Topline/singing method — how minimal-lyric trap topline still functions as a sung hook

- **The topline's job is to land on the loop, not to carry the song.** Because the beat is melody-first (Section 2.1), the vocal only has to supply the phrase that sticks. Verified examples:
  - **"Like That" (2024):** Future's hook is a chant-rapped catchphrase delivered over a looped "Everlasting Bass" / "Eazy-Duz-It" bass-and-break sample (sped up); Kendrick's contribution is one rapped verse + repeated "I'm really like that" — no sung chorus at all. The "hook" is the sample loop + the phrase. [Wikipedia composition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_That_(Future,_Metro_Boomin_and_Kendrick_Lamar_song)), [Genius](https://genius.com/Future-metro-boomin-and-kendrick-lamar-like-that-lyrics)
  - **"Superhero (Heroes & Villains)" (2022):** dual topline — Future chants the title hook ("I'm a superhero"), then Chris Brown sings the second half; Metro's horn line acts as the melodic hook underneath (Billboard notes the "horns on 'Superhero'"). [Genius](https://genius.com/Metro-boomin-future-and-chris-brown-superhero-heroes-and-villains-lyrics), [Billboard cover](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/metro-boomin-spider-verse-producer-interview-cover-story-1235430134/)
  - **"Creepin'" (2022):** The Weeknd's sung hook is a *re-sung* interpolation of Mario Winans' "I Don't Wanna Know" (itself a sample chain from Enya) — a pre-proven sung hook re-contextualized over Metro's drums, with Mario Winans himself on backgrounds; 21 Savage raps deadpan verses with no melody. Peak #3. [Metro's Apple Music account](https://www.attacktheculture.com/previews-reviews-more/heres-6-key-moments-from-metro-boomins-new-apple-music-1-qampa), [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/metro-boomin-spider-verse-producer-interview-cover-story-1235430134/)
  - **"Young Metro" (2024):** the producer's own name is the hook subject; The Weeknd sings the chorus over Metro's production (co-produced with DAVID x ELI and Mike Dean); debuted #9. [Revolt](https://www.revolt.tv/article/future-metro-boomin-and-the-weeknd-link-up-for-young-metro-video), [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/lists/future-metro-boomin-kendrick-lamar-like-that-hot-100-number-one-debut/)
- **He gives melodic artists room to be weird.** NPR on the pairing: "Future gets to explore the full dimensionality of his autotuned yips and murmurs inside Metro production," while Metro "brings the party itself" for guests like The Weeknd, Travis Scott, Playboi Carti. [NPR, Apr 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/04/18/1245528781/metro-boomin-future-we-still-dont-trust-you)
- **The topline bar is vibe, not craft.** "Really the vibe. The vibe is everything." [GQ 2018](https://www.gq.com/story/you-better-trust-metro-boomin). He famously does not dictate melodies — he picks the artist who'll find them and re-produces around what they bring (skeleton loop, Section 2.6).

## 5) Beat-as-hook scaffolding — what the track does to make the vocal/chant land

- **The beat contains the hummable hook before the artist opens their mouth** (melody-first rule). Classic proof: the "Mask Off" flute (sampled from Tommy Butler's 1976 "Prison Song") — the instrumental hook is the song's identity; the chant topline just rides it. [Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/metro-boomin-interview/), [Genius](https://genius.com/a/future-reveals-mask-off-was-released-without-clearing-the-sample)
- **Loop = built-in chorus.** A short, fat loop repeated with 808 drop-ins gives the chant a predictable pocket; the artist's phrase needs no melodic development. The 2024 run shows the extreme: five top-10 songs in one week, each a loop + chant.
- **Sample-as-known-hook (2022–24 pivot):** "Creepin'" and "Like That" scaffold the vocal on *already-hit* source material (Mario Winans; Rodney-O & Joe Cooley / Eazy-E / Michel'le), so the hook arrives pre-charted. [Apple Music](https://www.attacktheculture.com/previews-reviews-more/heres-6-key-moments-from-metro-boomins-new-apple-music-1-qampa), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_That_(Future,_Metro_Boomin_and_Kendrick_Lamar_song))
- **Signature palette as scaffolding:** "eerie synth loops, 808s, soulful samples and orchestral finishes" — including cinematic horns ("Superhero"), choral vocals ("Umbrella"), and "streaking cathedral bells and choral trap" ("Type Shit") — i.e., the beat supplies the emotional hook so the topline can stay minimal. [Billboard cover](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/metro-boomin-spider-verse-producer-interview-cover-story-1235430134/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2024/04/18/1245528781/metro-boomin-future-we-still-dont-trust-you)
- **Tags and drops as structural markers.** The tag hits at the drop ("If Young Metro don't trust you…" before Kanye's album premiere is the canonical example), functioning as an intro-chorus the crowd can shout.
- **Multi-section beats give artists room** (e.g., "Ice Attack"'s two spiraling halves on WDTY — "a second spiraling beat emerges… Future dutifully navigates its cadence" [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2024/04/18/1245528781/metro-boomin-future-we-still-dont-trust-you)); and Metro tests which part gets the "oohs and ahhs" before finalizing (Section 2.5).

## 8) "The trick" — in his words; repeatable vs. one-off

- **His stated "trick" is taste, not technique:** "the difference [between producer and beatmaker] is the ear" — he says he knows a hit "from the beginning… because that's part of the job." [GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/you-better-trust-metro-boomin). And he rejects manufactured hits outright ("the kids are always gonna sniff out the good shit… versus you tryna cater, kinda fake"). [Full Send via Medium](https://medium.com/@HowToRap/metro-boomin-teaches-how-to-make-beats-in-5-steps-bd7b054aca62)
- **Repeatable (his documented process, applicable to any producer-artist session):**
  1. Write melody/chords first; add drums mid-build.
  2. Keep the loop fat and short so the beat is the chorus.
  3. Build/select beats per artist (tempo, bounce, sub character), not per "what sells."
  4. Play beats live; watch which part makes the artist react; adjust to that.
  5. Let the artist write a minimal phrase; re-produce around the vocal; edit/arrange ruthlessly.
  6. Use tags/callouts as the brand hook; name the song/album from the chant (We Don't Trust You).
  7. When sampling, choose *pre-proven* melodic hooks (Mario Winans, Everlasting Bass) — the safest "topline."
- **One-off / not formulaic (flagged):** "Like That" became a #1 because Kendrick's diss verse made it a cultural event — Metro cleared the sample, positioned the song, and then the world did the rest (he told Forbes he didn't take the beef seriously [Audiophix](https://audiophix.com/metro-boomin-apparently-didnt-take-drake-kendrick-lamar-beef-seriously)). "Creepin'"'s interpolation-of-a-classic model is repeatable in theory but depended on The Weeknd's existing desire to touch that record ([Apple Music](https://www.attacktheculture.com/previews-reviews-more/heres-6-key-moments-from-metro-boomins-new-apple-music-1-qampa)). The Coachella spectacle ("spent over four times" what he was paid) is a promotional one-off. [Billboard cover](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/metro-boomin-spider-verse-producer-interview-cover-story-1235430134/)
- **Legend vs. verified correction (important):** the "If Metro don't trust you, I'ma shoot you" line is **21 Savage's** spoken tag on "X" (2016), not Metro's — the producer *curates* the artist's chant into his brand. Likewise "Metro Boomin want some more" was **Young Thug's in-studio invention** ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/metro-boomin-interview-metro-thuggin/)). The "don't mess up the beat" instruction could **not be verified** as a direct Metro quote in any primary source found (closest verified analogs: "you've got to overly bring it on this one. I'm not taking any okay shit" and "we gotta try to make something out of it" — both Full Send/Rolling Stone). Treat "don't mess up the beat" as legend until a primary clip is found.
