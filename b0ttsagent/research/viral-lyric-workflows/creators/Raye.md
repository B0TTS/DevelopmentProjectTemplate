## 2. Step-by-Step Workflow
Self-documented across Song Exploder ep. 264, Rolling Stone's Breakdown, and Broken Record:

1. **Title bank first** — "Titles are usually how a song will start for me… I had title Mary Jane, Environmental Anxiety, Escapism, Body Dysmorphia" (Broken Record). Titles wait for a sonic bed: "Just needed the right sonic bed to lie those lyrics in" (Song Exploder).
2. **Beat triggers the song** — usually lyric-first, but when Sabath played the Escapism beat she committed on the spot; she prays before writing, then "put the headphones on, and I didn't take them off until it was written" (Rolling Stone).
3. **Opening image first** — the first part written was "Just a heartbroke bitch, high heels, six inch…" to "create that picture" of a bad night dressed as a good night (Song Exploder).
4. **Lyric-over-melody** — "I'm a lyrics girl… I want you to close your eyes and see it all in your head. I kind of chose one note… I want the lyric to be the priority over the melody" (Song Exploder).
5. **Juxtaposition** — "If you was to really zone into the lyrics, you'd be like oh, shit, this is a sad story. But musically, it's the opposite thing" (Song Exploder).
6. **Subtraction in the mix** — stacks many harmonies, then cuts: a 3–4 part harmony over the opening "took away the grit" so it was stripped (Song Exploder).
7. **Structural risk-taking** — looped the choir outro "round and round", added a key change and a thunder voice-note; wrote the 8-bar rap outro "in the morning, in my pajamas" (Song Exploder; ~10 min for the whole outro, per Sabath, Music Week).
8. **Feature vocal last** — 070 Shake's part was the final element; she wrote her lyrics on a napkin (Rolling Stone; Music Week).
9. **2024–26 era (Genesis, THIS MUSIC MAY CONTAIN HOPE)** — intention-first: Nina Simone's "an artist's duty is to reflect the times" as north star (Harper's Bazaar); genre-acts structure trusted by gut ("in my gut it felt like they belong together"); 7-minute length defended ("there's so much I wanna say") (Capital FM; The Independent). New-album songs: co-write live with the band → 12-minute cut demo "living with it" → refine in LA; write long songs as letters to a friend; refuses to cut ("I don't want to cut a single second off of it") (Apple Music / Zane Lowe, 2026).

## 4. Tools & Templates
Title bank / backlog ("titles are usually how a song will start"); one-note recitation to foreground lyrics; first-person/third-person mixing ("I love to bounce between first person and third person… I like mixing it"); harmony subtraction; key-change outro + found-sound (thunder voice memo); retreat writing (LA→Utah cabin, road trips); "letter to a friend" reframe; her own voice as instrument ("When a voice can become a pad… it's exciting") (Broken Record; Song Exploder; Zane Lowe).

## 5. Proven vs Claimed
Proven: the documented Escapism process produced a UK #1, multi-platinum, 277M-view MV; the same title-first Utah batch produced a 6x-BRIT-winning album; the 2024–26 method produced WHERE IS MY HUSBAND! (146M views). Caveat: process is self-reported per-song across interviews — no single canonical repeatable framework is published, so cross-song repeatability is inferred from repeated outcomes, not a stated formula.
