## 2. Step-by-Step Workflow

As documented by Billie and Finneas:

1. **Record everything as voice memos.** "My brother and I have voice memos on our phone and we record everything we do" (Song Exploder ep. 197). The aired EIW writing memo shows the method: singing "jibberish and bad melodies over stuff for an hour" until the right thing appears — Finneas: "just make-believing them into existence." On Stern they re-enacted it: "making up words and singing words that aren't even real words" (Variety, 2019).
2. **Melody/vocal-improv first; words surface from the singing**, not the page. Working drafts are phone .m4a files named after the seed line — BOAF's mid-writing file is "i want you to stay.m4a" (Genius). **Gap:** no documented verse/syllable planning.
3. **Write together at one instrument.** "Probably 75-80% of the songs are written with us sitting next to each other at a piano or with a guitar, singing a melody together… we both have to kill our portion of it to get to the finish line" (Variety, 2019). Early training: their mother's 10-week kids' songwriting class (Beatles-song forms); "once you know the basics, then you can change them" — deliberate structural rule-breaking ("Bury a Friend" starts with the chorus) (Variety, 2019).
4. **Emotion/dream/concept seeds the lyric.** "everything i wanted" came from her suicide dream — "we just had to write about it… it needed to be said"; it stalled half a year, finished only after her mental state changed, and its chorus pivots the narrator via "And you say" to turn the verse monologue into a two-voice conversation (Song Exploder).
5. **Long, perfectionist iteration.** BOAF took 11 months: "that is excruciating, because you keep second-guessing it… I have so many memories of listening to version 5 of the song… I've lost my touch" — Billie re-recorded her vocal repeatedly (Variety Hitmakers, 2024). Chords alone came in ~10 minutes: "just basically D, B minor, E minor, A" (Finneas, Mix With The Masters via MusicRadar). They "overthought this simple song," then streamlined it (AmEx Story of My Song, via Genius). Counter-case: WWIMF was "one of the fastest songs we've ever written… honestly like a half hour," submitted as a rough voice memo — "one of four cases total in our career" (Variety breakdown video).
6. **Keep output moving by allowing bad songs.** "Giving yourself permission to write a bad song, because the more you do it, the better you get" (Fresh Air, 2024). Outside co-writes tried at 14 and abandoned: "all the songs we wrote with others were not as good as the songs me and Finneas wrote alone" (Variety, 2019). HMHAS was her most self-written album: "This is the most I've ever written and contributed" (Zane Lowe via MusicRadar).

## 3. Real Song Examples

- **"everything i wanted"** — voice-memo workflow, dream-to-lyric, half-year pause, chorus narrator change ("And you say…") (Song Exploder ep. 197 + transcript PDF).
- **"BIRDS OF A FEATHER"** — 11-month gestation; 4-chord song; working title from its first line; written as an optimistic pose ("she wrote it from a place she was pretending to inhabit" — Neon Music); 913M-view MV, 5x Platinum, GRAMMY Song-of-the-Year nomination (Genius; Variety Hitmakers).
- **"What Was I Made For?"** — ~30-min fast write, voice-memo submission, Oscar-winning (Variety "How Billie Eilish and FINNEAS Created…" video transcript; Fresh Air).
