## 2. Step-by-Step Workflow

Harlow has documented **two distinct methods** — a lyric-first rap method (2020–23) and a melody-first method for *Monica* (2026), a deliberate reset (Song Exploder ep. 311).

**Method A — "focused pen" (rapper era, per Tetragrammaton with Rick Rubin, May 2023):**
1. **Fragment bank all day.** "I have tons of first lines… throughout the day you just get little fragments. And you just write them in your notes."
2. **Write with the beat, always.** He reads stored first lines aloud over beats — "if they just hit the beat perfectly… this is a good starting point."
3. **Statement-first, verse-first writing.** "I've been so statement first, lyrics first" (Song Exploder). He wrote, not freestyled: "hip-hop was like literature to me." A two-year freestyle phase "gained a fun instinct, but it lost a lot of substance."
4. **Edit for zero filler.** "No filler lines… every line contributing to the story" — he read *The Creative Act* in the studio, calling it "the Bible."
5. **Concept > sonic pleasure**, plus audience-mood triage: every song tagged "always" (all moods) or "sometimes" (one mood: club/pre-workout) on the track-list whiteboard (Rolling Stone, 2022). "I have to search for a feeling that's real."

**Method B — melody-first reset (*Monica*, 2026):** after two years on a scrapped album, he took 3 weeks off; his engineer told him to "choose a North Star that is distinct and definitive." He set album-wide writing rules: no braggadocio, no cursing ("curse words can be a crutch"), only melodic singing, all-live instruments except programmed drums. Songs like "Say Hello" then start as gibberish: "I come up with all these melodies without troubling myself at all about the words… word vomit… sometimes while you're scatting the melody, a word will come to you." Lyrics are assembled afterward in "puzzle piece mode" — deliberate labor he says he enjoys.

## 3. Real Song Examples

- **"Say Hello"** (Monica, 2026) — full SE ep. 311 breakdown: 20-min silent beat soak, gibberish-scat melody; the scat line "Maybe I'll get at you when my life gets slow" became the first line and set the theme.
- **"WHATS POPPIN"** (Verified, 2020) — "it ain't a technical thing. It's like a feeling… make something that makes somebody get butterflies"; rapping-as-craft ("like shooting free throws").
- **"SUNDOWN"** (Verified, 2018) — line-by-line; Clipse's *Hell Hath No Fury* as a week-long studio influence.
- **"Blade of Grass"** (2022) — observed in Rolling Stone: group edits on the first line, cutting the intro to go a cappella.
- **Jackman character-POV song** (2023) — one verse each from a younger brother, older brother, and father, sparked by a real scene at his parents' game night (Tetragrammaton).

## 4. Tools & Templates

- Phone-notes **first-line bank**; beat-matching as the selection test.
- The **always/sometimes/never** mood metric on the album whiteboard (Rolling Stone).
- **Constraint lists** as creative rules (no cursing/no braggadocio/live-only instruments, 2026).
- *The Creative Act* by Rick Rubin as studio reference text (Tetragrammaton).
- Regular collaborators: Aksel Arvid (executive producer, *Monica*), Nemo Achida (A&R/co-writer), 2forwOyNE, brother Clay Harlow (producer).

## 5. Proven vs Claimed

**Proven (repeat, measured success):** 12 own-channel releases ≥100k within 24 months; "multiple number one hits across his first three albums" (Song Exploder bio); *Monica* charted with its 10-track own-channel drop all clearing 100k.

**Claimed (self-reported only):** "tons of first lines" (not auditable); the always/sometimes metric is a private heuristic; the freestyle→substance-loss tradeoff is retrospective self-assessment. **Gaps:** no published bar-count/syllable/rhyme methodology; Genius Verified covers only 2018–20; the 2026 NYT Popcast video has no captions (quotes cited via ABC Audio's summary); Lovin On Me-era making-ofs are producer-side, not lyric-side.
