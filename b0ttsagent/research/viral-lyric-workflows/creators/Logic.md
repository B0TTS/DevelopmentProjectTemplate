## 2. Step-by-Step Workflow

As documented by Logic himself:

1. **Collect raw lines constantly.** "If somebody says anything that's funny or remotely weird or different, I write it down… I literally have thousands and thousands of lines, of just lines, about the most random shit" (Variety, 2025). On the Simon Sinek podcast he keeps them in his phone's notes app: "You forget more ideas than you remember… carry a device or a notebook at all times and write them down" — he then reads standalone bars (e.g., a "March/Gensu" internal-rhyme line) that later grow into verses (A Bit of Optimism, 2024).
2. **Emulation as training.** "I literally have thousands of songs… where I've emulated everybody… so that I could understand it… every four bars I'm changing my style" (NPR Microphone Check, 2015).
3. **Speed is situational.** The second verse of "America" "I wrote this shit in five minutes," but "1-800" "took me a few months… I have to take my time, I have to be patient because this song could potentially save people's lives" (Genius Verified, 2017).
4. **Perspective/concept-first construction.** "1-800" is three acts — the caller, the hotline operator, then the caller transformed (Verified). "Anziety" raps from the perspective of anxiety itself; he calls it his realest write (MONTREALITY, 2017). Albums get world-building first: Ultra 85 began as a radio-play idea during his Everybody tour and gestated ten years (Barnes & Noble guest post, 2024).
5. **Binge album sessions in a home studio.** Vinyl Days: ~10 days in his home studio, sampling from 100+ vinyl records (Now-Again/Egon), analog gear, with producer 6ix — captured in the Vinyl Days Documentary (TMDB; HipHopDX). "I made that album in 12 days so I could get off Def Jam" (Impaulsive via Uproxx).
6. **Self-annotate.** He recorded Genius "Verified" breakdowns for essentially every song on Everybody (2017) (Genius article; Wikipedia).

## 3. Real Song Examples

- **"1-800-273-8255"** — three-voice structure (caller → operator → survivor); title = lifeline number; months of patient writing; 8x Platinum, #3 Hot 100 (Wikipedia).
- **"America"** — second verse written in ~5 minutes (Verified).
- **"Homicide" (w/ Eminem)** — #5 Hot 100; BREGMA's Genius Deconstructed covers production (beatmakingvideos.com).
- **"Fade Away"** — from his documented fear of flying (NPR, 2015).
- **Vinyl Days** — 30-track album completed in 12 days (Uproxx).
- **2025-26 singles** — from Paradise Records, a film written from "personal experience… any time I can put that on the page, I do" (Variety).

## 4. Tools & Templates

- **Phone notes app** as a permanent line bank (thousands of standalone bars) — A Bit of Optimism; Variety.
- **Home studio + analog gear**, vinyl crates for sampling, partner **6ix** on production (TMDB; NME).
- No published written-lyrics template; capture-then-assemble is his documented method. **Gap:** no public bar-count/syllable methodology — his flow is described as intuitive ("every four bars I'm changing my style", NPR).

## 5. Proven vs Claimed

**Proven (repeat, measured success):**
- "1-800-273-8255" moved real-world behavior: +6.9% cumulative excess hotline calls and ~245 fewer suicides per a BMJ study period, plus a 50% call spike after the 2017 VMAs (zeroattempts.org BMJ summary; GRAMMY.com; CNN).
- 7 consecutive own-channel MVs (2025-26) cleared 100k+ views — the line-bank method sustains output.
- 8x Platinum, four #1 albums, NYT-bestselling novels (Wikipedia; People).

**Claimed (asserted, unverified externally):**
- "Thousands and thousands" of stored lines (self-reported, not auditable).
- Emulation → unique style; 12-day album; "wrote in 5 minutes" — self-asserted (12-day window corroborated by the doc's 10-day shoot).
- **Myth-bust:** internet lore says "1-800" was written fast; Logic's Verified account says it took months — patience, not speed, is his method for mission-critical songs.
