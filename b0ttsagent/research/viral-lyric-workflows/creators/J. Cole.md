## 2. Step-by-Step Workflow

As J. Cole documented it (2010–2026):

1. **Daily drills before inspiration.** Warm Up era: "every day I woke up, wrote verses, made beats" — the same regimen re-applied for The Off-Season (Applying Pressure doc; Billboard recap). In "The Audacity" (Players' Tribune, 2020): "morning writing drills" before Electric Lady Studios; "the seeds for The Fall Off were planted."
2. **Beat-first, words-second.** "Three, four hours combing through samples… then another five hours making beats. Hopefully something catches and I'll start writing. If not, I finish the day with four or five beats" (NPR Microphone Check, 2013).
3. **Spill out, find the thread later.** "I just start spilling things out and then wait until they take form… until I see a common thread" (NPR); "When I start writing, I don't always know where it's headed… the first thing I write is, 'I hate Rich N----z'" (BET, 2013).
4. **7-Minute Drill for writer's block.** Timer on; write a verse in 7 minutes on a random topic/word. Manager Ibrahim Hamad: "his way of breaking out of overthinking… Cozz, write a verse about pizza" (Say Less podcast via Billboard, 2024).
5. **Morning pages, freehand.** The Artist's Way (Julia Cameron): 3 pages nonstop on waking; back to pen from phone-typing — "notebooks and notebooks full of lyrics… something more intimate about writing it down" (Fuse via AllHipHop, 2013).
6. **Constraint challenges.** "Disc 2 Track 2" (2026): life story in reverse, "four syllables minimum… like putting a puzzle together," 12 hours in one spot, then sat on the verse until the right beat came (Timmhotep Aku interview via HNHH).
7. **Curate ruthlessly.** "The most difficult part about making an album is finishing… ciphering through all this music" (Meet The Musician interview).

## 3. Real Song Examples

- **"7 Minute Drill" (2024)** — born literally from the drill: T-Minus made a 7-minute beat, gave Cole the word "light"; Cole wrote in 7-minute increments (T-Minus via HNHH).
- **"Disc 2 Track 2" (2026)** — the constraint-puzzle method.
- **"Rich Niggaz" (2013)** — first-line-then-discover-theme.
- **"Born Sinner" (2013)** — "spill out" until "A born sinner was never born to be perfect" surfaced and titled the album.
- **"4 Your Eyez Only," "False Prophets"** — rush-of-inspiration songs (The Audacity).
- **"Middle Child" (2019)** — beat-first workflow, producer-side Genius Deconstructed (T-Minus).

## 4. Tools & Templates

- **7-Minute Drill**: timer + random topic/word; also applied to production ("make a beat in seven minutes").
- **Morning Pages**: 3 pages freehand daily, unedited.
- **Pen and notebooks over phone** — paper is "more intimate."
- **Daily quotas**: 3–4h sample combing + 5h beat-making.
- **Self-imposed structural rules** (syllable minimums, reversed narrative).
- Genius Deconstructed covers his beats (T-Minus, Phonix Beats, !llmind) — production-side only; he has never done a Genius Verified lyric breakdown.

## 5. Proven vs Claimed

**Proven** (repeated across eras, independently corroborated): drills-and-volume practice (Warm Up 2007 → Off-Season 2021); beat-first writing (NPR 2013 + Deconstructed episodes); the 7-Minute Drill — manager Hamad and producer T-Minus independently describe it, and it produced a hit single.

**Claimed** (his assertion, unmeasured): morning pages "opened up a lot of things"; "no fulfillment" from audience reactions (HNHH, 2026); The Fall-Off built as a possible final album (Talk With Flee, 2026). Motivational claims, not verified outputs.
