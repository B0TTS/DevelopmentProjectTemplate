## 2. Step-by-Step Workflow

Denzel's documented method is **freestyle-first, no pad** — the inverse of a notes-file workflow:

1. **Abandon the notepad.** Reading Gucci Mane's autobiography made him "get away from the pad" during *TA13OO*: "You're not in a constant flow state with a pad, because you're trying to rehearse this thing, and it may sound too stiff and rigid" (FADER, 2019). By *ZUU* "the whole project is freestyled" — except "SHAKE 88", the one written-down song, written with help.
2. **In-booth writing, chorus first.** On "RICKY" he "started with actually the chorus first," then filled in hooks/verses, all freestyle (Song Exploder ep. 164, transcript).
3. **Build a flow library, not a lyric bank.** He studies reference freestyles (DJ Screw; Fat Pat & Z-Ro's "Been Around the World") and "flip[s] it in my own way"; he's "good at fast flows" and switches flows mid-song "because it keeps people engaged" (Song Exploder). Lord Infamous and Andre 3000 are his twin flow archetypes (Genius Verified).
4. **Flow experiments without a beat.** "ULTIMATE" verses were written beatless as a "flow experiment," pulling Miami's Caribbean cadences (Jamaican/Haitian/Bahamian) into the delivery (Genius "Check The Rhyme").
5. **Steal idols' *moments*, not words.** The "Ultimate, ultimate" beat-drop hook replicates Buju Banton's flow-switch-plus-beat-cut in "Sensimilla Persecution" (Genius Verified).
6. **Work on the fly at home.** *KOTMS Vol. 2* (2024): "I was just making these songs on the fly… the majority of the songs were made in my bed" — "Hot One" downstairs at home, "Hit The Floor" in an Airbnb room (Grammy.com).
7. **Sit with ideas and revisit.** Hooks incubate for years: the *ZUU* title track reuses a two-year-old hook idea over a different beat (FADER).
8. **Constraints + producer feedback.** UNLOCKED (2020): "I don't want no beats that you already made… from scratch," three days, motto "Don't Over Think Shit" (Highsnobiety). FnZ give "brutally honest" verdicts on hook halves (Song Exploder). *ii* (2026): recorded on a Roland SP-404 + "a cheap mic" (Consequence).

## 4. Tools & Templates

No lyric notebook or templates — the booth and the beat are the canvas. Reusable elements: the **reference-freestyle bank** (Southern flows to flip), **flow archetypes** (Lord Infamous / Andre 3000), **beatless flow experiments**, **home-recording sites** (bedroom/Airbnb), **two-project juggling** ("If this one won't see the light of day until next year, this one has to hold fans over" — Grammy.com), **gear constraints** (SP-404 + cheap mic), and the **"Don't Over Think Shit"** motto (adopted from Kenny Beats).

## 5. Proven vs Claimed

**Proven (repeat, measured):** 12 own-channel releases ≥100k within 24 months across three project eras; catalog spans 2012→2026. The freestyle method is independently corroborated by collaborator JPEGMAFIA: "he doesn't write anything down, he freestyles that" (No Jumper) and by FnZ's session accounts. UNLOCKED proved the 3-day constraint method yields a full EP.

**Claimed (self-reported only):** "the whole project is freestyled" (not auditable); the flow-state-over-pad rationale is self-assessment; the years-long hook bank is private. **Gaps:** no published rhyme/syllable methodology — cadence craft is demonstrated (Genius Verified), not documented; monthly audience unverifiable; no captioned making-of series on his channel; *The Scythe* oral history covers group dynamics, not lyric craft.
