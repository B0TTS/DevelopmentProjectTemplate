## 2. Step-by-Step Workflow
As documented by Russ himself across 2016–2026:

1. **Beat first, ~99.9% of the time.** "It's usually the beat for 99.9% of the time and then it's the hook" — and "once you have the hook it's done." If the hook doesn't come instantly, he skips the beat; he keeps a playlist of "thousands of Beats that I love but... I skipped them because the hook didn't instantly come." Then verses are filled in: "once the hook comes... you just fill it in with verses and then you're out. I don't try to over complicate things" (Music Choice, 2017, [transcript](https://youtu.be/TuaoB4ph5xU)).
2. **Melody before words** on vocal songs: for "What They Want" he "followed his usual writing process... creating the melody first, then fitting the words into the melody" (Genius annotation via [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/russ/what-they-want)).
3. **Concept-first variant**: songs like "Inbetween" start with a concept in his head, then he "go[es] make the music to fit the concept, which is actually kind of easier than the other way" (EUPHORIA., 2021).
4. **Daily autobiographical habit**: "My music is like an autobiography. I make music everyday just talking about my life... isn't everyday a new day?" (Hip-Hop Wired, 2016); "We were doing it every day. Literally every day. That was the habit. Making music every day" (SXSW keynote, [MBW 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/russ-talks-ownership-fan-engagement-and-making-music-every-day-at-sxsw/)).
5. **Therapy-exercise writing** (current method): his therapist assigns exercises ("write about what this grief feels like") and he always writes a song; much of *Santiago* was "created from therapy exercises" (Men's Health, 2025).
6. **Release pipeline**: he noticed the first song of each album got the most plays, so he pivoted to one song per week (Variety/NYU, 2018) — and kept a buffer: "when I was doing a song a week, I had 26 in the stash so I could be consistent" (his own live fan Q&A, 2025, [transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MfBMNRaGUvU)).

## 3. Real Song Examples
- **"What They Want"** (2015): melody-first; minimal 4-instrument beat made in studio "instantly"; he recreated it on laptop in 2 minutes (Songfacts; [DJCity](https://news.djcity.com/watch-russ-explain-how-he-produced-what-they-want/), 2017).
- **"Missin' You Crazy"**: beat first, lyrics/melody second — "completely liberating... receiving information from the source" (EUPHORIA.).
- **"3:15 (Breathe)"**: sample found at a Turkish restaurant, sped up, pitched up, "underwater" filter (his Q&A + [SongSense](https://songsense.io/russ/315-breathe)).
- **"Coulda Shoulda Woulda"** (2026): French/Italian soul samples time-stretched through a half-time plugin, deconstructed in his own BTS video (Coast2Coast, 2026).
- **"I Love You Boy"**: therapy exercise — three verses talking to himself at different ages; the title phrase is never sung ("I'm going to call it I Love You, Boy" — clarity chosen over literalness) (Men's Health; fan Q&A).
- **"Paddington Freestyle"** (2022): beat sampled from the *Paddington* movie, shown on Instagram (HipHopDX).

## 4. Tools & Templates
- DAW: Logic (moved from GarageBand) (Hip-Hop Wired).
- Full-stack DIY: writes, produces, mixes, masters, engineers everything (Billboard, 2016).
- Minimalism template: ~4 instruments per beat (Songfacts).
- Stash-and-release template: 26-song buffer for weekly drops (fan Q&A).
- Mindset books: *It's All in Your Head* (2019; chapters named after his songs; pillars "delusion, persistence, gratitude"; HarperCollins) and *It Was You All Along* (2023).

## 5. Proven vs Claimed
- **Proven**: weekly-consistency engine — 200 songs by mid-2015, weekly SoundCloud drops for 2.5 years, compounding into platinum "What They Want" (Billboard 2016; MBW 2026); the same process still produces 100k–1.4M-view official uploads in 2025–26 (see Section 1). The hook-gate filter is asserted but consistent with his documented output.
- **Claimed/unsupported**: "I can make a song everyday" is asserted, not evidenced with counts (Hip-Hop Wired); "receiving information from the source" is subjective; therapy-exercise results are personal narrative (Men's Health).
