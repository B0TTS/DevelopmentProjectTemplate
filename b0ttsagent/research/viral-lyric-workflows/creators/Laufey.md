## 2. Step-by-Step Workflow
From Song Exploder ep. 259 (her own account, [transcript PDF](https://songexploder.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Song-Exploder-Laufey-Transcript.pdf)) plus her channel's Track By Track interview ([watch?v=fcQNqZhXQZY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fcQNqZhXQZY), 2026-01-05):

1. **Capture titles continuously.** "I have on my Notes app, on my phone, just a long list of song titles that I think would be cool, that I literally write down when I'm, like, on the train... right before I go to sleep." She confirmed the practice again in 2026: the "A Cautionary Tale" title "sat on my Notes app for like months" before it became a song.
2. **Write toward the title.** "The title is usually the last word in the chorus that I write, so I know what I'm writing towards."
3. **Start from a style impulse, on guitar.** "From The Start" began from "listening to a lot of bossa nova records" while sitting on her couch; demos were recorded on her phone. Guitar is "my favorite instrument to write on" — its limitations force focus versus piano.
4. **Chorus first, then find the feeling.** After writing the chorus ("a straight up confession of love"), she asked "what's a feeling that I've felt before that would make sense with this?" — landing on unrequited love.
5. **Write literal lyrics + humor.** "My songwriting style is very literal" (Grammy.com, 2025): she borrows directness from the Great American Songbook and relatability from Taylor Swift ("she wrote these lyrics that were quite literal, and quite relatable"). Comedic collisions: modern slang ("She's so perfect, blah blah blah") inside old-sounding music, and old-fashioned words ("loon") — "dramatic and petty. That's what I'm aiming to do."
6. **Transcribe real life.** "I just transcribe my life... I wrote them into my songs" (Teen Vogue). Tough Luck: "I didn't have to make many things up. I just said what he said." A Cautionary Tale gathered "my own and my sister's and my cousin's experience." Too Little Too Late was a character exercise in a male perspective. Some songs start with the end: "I tend to start with the end" (Clockwork).
7. **End stripped to confession.** "From The Start"'s outro drops the jokes — "just this full confession," only strings and voice ("my two voices: cello and singing").

## 3. Real Song Examples
- **"From The Start"** (Song Exploder ep. 259 + [Genius Verified](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p37qgi5lDnk)): full title→chorus→feeling breakdown; "brutal honesty of the lyrics"; jazz-tag outro into a classical cello ending.
- **"Tough Luck"** ([Rolling Stone six-song breakdown](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/laufey-a-matter-of-time-new-album-interview-1235398636/)): anger from a real ex, "a song that writes itself."
- **"Snow White"**, **"Castle in Hollywood"**, **"Forget Me Not"** (RS): body-image honesty; friend-breakup song; Icelandic lyrics because the English version "sounded dishonest."
- **"Clockwork"** (Track By Track): "start with the end" — string disturbances "paint how it feels to have anxious thoughts."
- **"Lover Girl"** (Grammy.com): "musically depict anxiety with this noise."

## 4. Tools & Templates
iPhone Notes app title list; phone voice-memo demos; guitar as the writing instrument; jazz-standard structures (tag outro; Prokofiev's "Cinderella" as inspiration); a literal-lyric template from the Great American Songbook; and her "Lyric Video with Chords" release format — chords are published with every lyric video on her channel.

## 5. Proven vs Claimed
**Repeat success is proven by numbers**: 6 in-window official MVs from 4.5M–42.8M views and a 150M-view back catalog ("From The Start" MV, 2023-08-21). The **method** is corroborated across time and independent sources — the Notes-app-title-first process appears in both 2023 (Song Exploder) and 2026 (her own Track By Track), and the literal-lyric philosophy repeats in Song Exploder, Genius (2024) and Grammy.com (2025). But the workflow is a *retrospective* account of individual songs — she has never published a formal lyric formula, so "title-first + chorus-first + transcribe life" is a consistent, claimed pattern, not a verified repeatable recipe. Chorus-first (Song Exploder) vs start-with-the-end (Clockwork) also shows entry points vary.
