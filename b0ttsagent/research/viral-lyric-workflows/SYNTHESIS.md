# SYNTHESIS — Parallels Across 14 Verified Lyric-Writing Workflows

Phase 2 output. Extracted from the 14 verified `creators/*.md` docs (research date 2026-08-13). All pattern claims here are interpretations of those docs; each doc carries the per-claim source links.

**Headline answer:** three practices are near-universal — (1) **truth-from-life material** (14/14), (2) **a capture bank** for lines/titles/memos (11/14), and (3) **structured quality gates** applied after the draft (10/14). Entry point (chorus-first vs first-line-first vs beat-first) is the least shared element and the biggest divergence — creators switch by song and era.

## Method

- Each `creators/<name>.md` was read and its named workflow elements extracted (PLAN 2.1).
- All 14 verified creators are **tier 1 (verified artists)** — no tier 2/3 in the verified set, so cross-tier weighting does not apply.
- Within tier 1, two creators carry weaker verification per the Phase 1 QA notes: **Billie Eilish** (boundary pass — 2 clean in-window commercial releases) and **Tessa Violet** (thinnest margin — 3 releases just above 100k). Ranked counts below are shown as `total (strong/weak)`.
- Elements that only 1–2 creators share were dropped as noise; entry-point elements are kept despite low counts because their mutual conflict is a divergence (section C).

## (b) Ranked Parallels (weighted by verification strength)

1. **E1 Truth-from-life material — 14/14 (12 strong / 2 weak).** Every verified creator seeds lyrics in real, specific events: voicemails (Lizzo), overheard speech (Puth, Olivia Dean's "songwriting sieve"), real conversations transcribed (Laufey "I just said what he said"), dreams (Billie), real sentences blurted at siblings (AJR), therapy exercises (Russ), journal entries (Gracie). The single most shared element.
2. **E2 Capture bank — 11/14 (9 strong / 2 weak).** Phone Notes line banks (Logic "thousands and thousands of lines", Harlow "tons of first lines"), title lists (Laufey, Raye), voice-memo-everything (Billie, Gracie, AJR), notebooks (J. Cole), speech harvesting (Puth). The two hip-hop freestyle/beat-first outliers (Russ = beat playlist, Denzel = flow library) run banks of *beats/flows* instead of lines.
3. **E8 Structured quality gates — 10/14 (9 strong / 1 weak).** Every passing creator applies a named post-draft filter: hook gate (Russ), catchiness gate (AJR voice-memo auditions), mirror-chorus + delete-repeats test (Puth), body-feel test (Lizzo/Ricky Reed), "if I wouldn't say it out loud" reality barometer (Olivia Dean), repeat-listening test (Olivia Dean), 7-person A/B tests (Tessa), always/sometimes mood triage (Harlow), live-crowd lyric testing (Gracie), brutal producer verdicts (Denzel/FnZ), ruthless curation (J. Cole).
4. **E11 Long gestation / patience — 9/14 (8 strong / 1 weak).** BOAF's 11 months (Billie), 5+ demo re-skins (Lizzo), 18-month Dumb Song (AJR), 8-month See You Again finish (Puth), months on 1-800 (Logic), 12-hour sits (J. Cole), hooks incubated for years (Denzel), titles parked for months (Laufey), demos "living with it" (Raye). Patience is applied to mission-critical songs; fast drafting coexists (see divergence 4).
5. **E4 Sound-first drafting — 8/14 (7 strong / 1 weak).** Syllables before meaning (Puth "Swedish approach"), babble/scat placeholder vocal (Puth, Billie, Harlow), melody-before-words (Russ), mouth-feel testing (Gracie), vowel-level melody edits (Lizzo), freestyle flow-first (Denzel), melody-first with catchiness gate (AJR).
6. **E7 Co-write partnership — 8/14 (6 strong / 2 weak).** Documented two-person/circle formats: Billie+Finneas at one instrument, AJR's Ryan/Jack raw-clay+filter loop, Gracie+Audrey "funniest line wins", Lizzo's living-room circle, Olivia Dean's house residency, Harlow's group first-line edits, Raye's live band co-writes, Tessa's half-solo/half-Olds split.
7. **E10 Self-imposed constraints — 7/14 (7 strong / 0 weak).** 7-Minute Drill + syllable minimums (J. Cole), no-cursing/no-braggadocio/live-instruments rules (Harlow), from-scratch beats + 3-day limit (Denzel), "write about things never written about" (AJR), dry-vocal rule (Lizzo), 4-instrument minimalism (Russ), reality barometer (Olivia Dean). Zero weak-verification contributors — the strongest-weighted constraint cluster.
8. **E12 Concept/thesis-first framing — 7/14 (6 strong / 1 weak).** "What song does the world need to hear" (Lizzo), album world-building (Logic), album-as-response-to-a-book (Olivia Dean), artist-duty north star (Raye), album-wide rule sets (Harlow, AJR), "what am I trying to say" pre-write ritual (Tessa).
9. **E9 Daily discipline / volume — 5/14 (3 strong / 2 weak).** Daily drills (J. Cole), music-every-day habit (Russ), timed daily 9–2 writing (Tessa), "write a lot of bad songs" (AJR), permission to write bad songs (Billie). The most weakly weighted cluster — both weak-verification creators contribute here.
10. **E6 First-line-first entry — 5/14 (4 strong / 1 weak).** Tessa (rarely chorus-first), J. Cole (spill out, thread emerges), Gracie (first line arrives with chords), Harlow's rap era (statement-first), Raye (opening image first).
11. **E3 Title-first drafting — 5/14 (4 strong / 1 weak).** Titles precede and steer: Puth (titles incubate), Raye ("titles are usually how a song will start"), Laufey (title = last word of chorus, "I know what I'm writing towards"), Tessa (idea-first later era), Gracie (title-as-pillar iteration).
12. **E5 Chorus-first entry — 4/14 (4 strong / 0 weak).** Puth ("chorus first, always"), Russ (hook gate right after beat), Denzel ("started with actually the chorus first"), Laufey (chorus then find the feeling).

## (c) Notable Divergences

1. **Entry point is not identity.** Chorus-first (4), first-line-first (5), beat-first (Russ 99.9%, J. Cole, Denzel, Harlow), title-first (5), concept-first (7) all coexist — and creators hold 2+ entries. Harlow's documented reset (focused-pen rap → melody-first *Monica*) is the cleanest evidence that entry point tracks era and project, not person. No single "correct" entry point survives the data.
2. **Pad vs no-pad.** Denzel abandoned the notepad for flow state ("it may sound too stiff and rigid") while J. Cole doubled down on pen and paper ("something more intimate about writing it down") and Logic/Harlow run phone note banks. Three mutually exclusive capture media, all producing verified 100k+ output.
3. **Melody-first vs lyric-first.** Raye is the clean counterexample to the sound-first cluster: "I'm a lyrics girl… one note… the lyric to be the priority over the melody." Both approaches pass the Consistency Test — priority direction is preference, not lever.
4. **Speed and patience are situational, not contradictory.** Fast writes: 5-minute verse (Logic), 30-minute WWIMF (Billie), 7-minute drills (J. Cole), 3-day EP (Denzel), 12-day album (Logic). Slow writes: months-to-years on the same creators' signature songs. Primary accounts contradict the fast-hit folklore — "See You Again in 10 minutes" is content-farm fiction vs Puth's own 8-month account. The split: speed for drafts/verses, patience for songs the creator deems mission-critical.
5. **Solo vs co-write.** Russ is full-stack DIY (writes/produces/mixes alone) while Gracie's method is explicitly relationship-dependent (doc's own caveat: transferability unproven) and Billie reports abandoning outside co-writes ("songs we wrote with others were not as good"). Co-writing (8/14) is common but not required.
6. **Constraints point in opposite directions.** Harlow/Denzel/AJR/J. Cole impose rules to force quality (no cursing, syllable minimums, from-scratch beats) while Raye's documented constraint is *resistance* — refusing to cut a single second of a 7-minute song. Constraint serves either quality discipline or artistic control depending on the creator's market position.
7. **Quality filters disagree on what "good" is.** Catchiness gate (AJR, Russ, Puth's mirror-chorus) vs truth barometer (Olivia Dean, Puth "100% real", Lizzo "resonate with me personally") vs body-feel (Lizzo/Reed). Often the same creator runs several filters in sequence (Puth: truth → mirror test → delete repeats).
8. **Specific vs universal.** Puth claims "the more general you are, the less people relate" while AJR works toward "universal vignettes" and Laufey toward literal Great American Songbook directness. The resolution visible across all 14: *specific, real details as the vehicle; universal feeling as the target.* No verified creator writes from abstract concepts alone (E1 = 14/14).
9. **Anti-formula structure vs formula.** AJR explicitly rejects verse-chorus-verse-chorus ("none of the songs on this new EP adhere to that structure") while Puth engineers mirrored, self-similar choruses. Both clear 100k+ repeatedly — structure is not the differentiator; the gates and material are.
