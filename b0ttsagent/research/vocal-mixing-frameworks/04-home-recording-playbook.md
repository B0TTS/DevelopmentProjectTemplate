# Home Recording Playbook (Tracking Side, Distilled)

**What this is:** the recording-half of the research, compressed into a checklist for a rapper recording himself over MP3 beats at home. The full evidence lives in `case-studies/04-tracking/` — this is the "do" version. The consistent finding across all five tracking files: **the pros' advantage at recording time is discipline (levels, room, comping process), not gear** — Louis Bell tracks Post Malone through an Apollo Twin's built-in preamp; Finneas cut "Ocean Eyes" on an $80 AT2020.

---

## Mic technique

- **Distance:** 6–10 inches with a pop filter (or sock) between you and the mic. Closer = more low-mid "proximity" warmth but more plosives; further = more room sound.
- **Slightly off-axis:** angle the mic ~15–30° instead of singing straight into the capsule — reduces sibilance and plosives before any plugin (less work for step 3/4 of the chain).
- **Same spot every time:** mark it. Consistent capture is what makes a repeatable chain possible (every fixed template in the research assumes consistent input).
- **Gain:** set the interface so your loudest line peaks around **−6 dBFS**. Never clip; don't chase loudness at recording (24-bit gives you huge headroom — the SoS-documented standard).

## Room

- Soft surfaces behind you and above you (closet full of clothes, blankets, mattress). You're killing reflections, not building a studio.
- Face into the softest part of the room; put the mic's back toward the computer/reflective wall.
- **Test:** clap once, listen. A short dry "tick" is fine; a ringing "boing" means more blankets.
- Finneas made Billie Eilish's sound *because* of a small dead bedroom — "the bedroom has a very specific sound, very tight and intimate." A dead room is an aesthetic option, not just a limitation. (`case-studies/03-pop/finneas.md`)

## The headphone monitor chain (do not skip)

The documented pattern (Uzi through Kesha Lee; Chris Brown through Teezio): **artists perform through tuning + a little space**, and the performance changes because of it.

- Put your tuner on the input/monitor path, set the beat's key, choose retune speed for the song's feel (fast for rage/melodic, slow for natural)
- Add a small reverb or ¼-note delay in the headphones
- Record the dry signal (or print the tune if you're committing to the doctrine — Teezio prints; both are documented)

## Comping your own vocals (the Kuk Harrell / Kirkpatrick method, solo-adapted)

From `case-studies/04-tracking/comping-stacking-and-vocal-production.md`:

1. **Record the full song front-to-back, 3–5 times.** Don't punch single lines for hours — Harrell records front-to-back because section-by-section "sounds pieced together."
2. **Comp pass 1:** pick the best take per line. Walk away.
3. **Comp pass 2:** re-comp from scratch without looking at pass 1 (Kirkpatrick's 3-pass method: three independent comps, then the final comp chosen fast and instinctively).
4. **Final comp:** choose between the two/three comps quickly. Speed is the point — "sometimes you lose perspective when comping vocals for hours."
5. **Then** tune/time the winning comp (Kirkpatrick: comp → tune → final comp; Kuk: comp → timing → tuning → stacks).

## Stack architecture (how layers actually work on records)

| Layer | Job | Documented treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Lead (center) | The words | Full chain |
| Doubles (L/R or tucked) | Width/thickness on hooks | Same chain minus some mids (Kinelski cuts mids on doubles); often more tuned |
| Ad-libs | Energy, answers | Differentiated on purpose — Bell puts chorus/doubler FX on ad-libs "to differentiate them from the lead"; Yeat stacks them until they become texture |
| Harmonies/"angel" stacks | Melodic padding | Pitched/formant-shifted versions (Gudwin, Finneas, Hawkins all do formant tricks) |

For your genre lane: hooks get doubles + ad-libs; verses often stay single-track and dry. Yeat's extreme (up to ~90 vocal tracks) is arrangement, not a target.

## Session organization (the boring multiplier)

- Same layout every session: beat track 1 (Kesha color-codes the 2-track purple/green — "the hook is always green"), leads grouped, doubles grouped, ad-libs grouped, FX sends at the bottom
- Name takes while recording, not after
- One "record" template file with your monitor chain pre-built (Teezio keeps recording and mixing templates separate; Bainz's template exists so the tech never slows the idea)

## Capture checklist (print this)

- [ ] Room treated enough (clap test passed)
- [ ] Mic distance/angle marked and consistent
- [ ] Input peaks ~−6 dBFS, no clipping
- [ ] Tuner on the monitor path, keyed to the beat
- [ ] Small delay/reverb in headphones
- [ ] 3–5 front-to-back takes minimum
- [ ] Comp while fresh; tune after comping
- [ ] Files named; session saved in template layout
