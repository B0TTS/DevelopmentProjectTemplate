# Source Library — Where This Knowledge Lives (and How to Keep It Current)

**Purpose:** every framework in this research traces back to a small set of primary-source wells. This file tells you which wells exist, what each is good for, and how to verify anyone's claims (including ours) yourself.

## Verifying credits (do this before trusting any "engineer of X" claim)

1. **AllMusic / Discogs / Qobuz / Apple Music credits** — full personnel per release (Qobuz and Discogs were the workhorses for the Opium/underground lane)
2. **muso.ai** — the industry's credit database; engineers claim their own pages
3. **Wikipedia album pages** — good for major releases, crowd-edited (treat as B/C)
4. **Grammy.com artist pages** — verified wins/nominations
5. Cross-check: a real top-tier engineer leaves footprints in at least 2–3 of these.

**This matters because the loudest "vocal chain" content online is preset marketing.** The pattern to distrust: a blog selling you "Yeat's exact vocal settings" — Yeat has never published any (his file: `case-studies/02-new-wave-rap/yeat.md`). The pattern to trust: an article where the engineer opens the actual session (SoS/MWTM) or speaks in a recorded interview.

## Keeping this research current

- SoS publishes a new Inside Track roughly monthly — checking it quarterly keeps you current on who's mixing the new wave
- New-scene documentation appears first on **Tape Notes, Our Generation Music, and The FADER/Complex** long before technical press
- Engineer AMAs and MWTM community Q&As are where current chains get updated (Jaycen confirmed his current chain in one)
- When a new album you love drops: credits first (Qobuz/Discogs) → engineer name → search `"<name>" interview vocal chain site:soundonsound.com OR site:mixwiththemasters.com OR site:youtube.com`
