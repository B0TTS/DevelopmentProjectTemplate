## Vocal Philosophy
"Polished aggression": the pop-punk/Feldmann-school value of **vocal clarity and hook delivery** carried into heavy music. In a Cervini mix "the vocal is king. It doesn't matter how dense the instrumentation is; the vocal is always intelligible, present, and sits right on top of everything. This is achieved with a chain of small, precise moves" — deliberately **no single do-everything plugin**; each processor does one small job. [Nail The Mix](https://www.nailthemix.com/zakk-cervini)

His stated approach to work: speed + finished product. "I also learned from him [John Feldmann] that — you should not sacrifice the quality of your work for this — but in this business, especially in this day and age, it's super-important to be fast. And I've taken that to heart." [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/2021-zakk-cervini-interview-producing-yungblud/)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
**"A Typical Cervini-Style Vocal Chain"** — the documented framework from his Nail The Mix teaching content [Nail The Mix – How Zakk Cervini Crafts Modern Metal's Polished Aggression](https://www.nailthemix.com/zakk-cervini):

1. **Gain and pitch correction:** clip-gain to level wildly different sections, then *subtle* pitch correction (Melodyne or MetaTune). (In Audacity terms: normalize/level the take first; pitch is usually pre-mixed for the user.)
2. **Subtractive EQ:** FabFilter Pro-Q 3 (or similar) to **notch out resonances, "honky," or unpleasant frequencies** — surgical cuts before anything tonal.
3. **First compressor — gentle optical (LA-2A-type):** e.g., Slate FG-2A, **only 1–2 dB** of smooth gain reduction. "Rides" the vocal.
4. **Additive EQ — Pultec-style:** boost **air around 12–16 kHz** and add some **body in the low-mids** for character (e.g., Slate FG-Pultec).
5. **Second compressor — aggressive FET (1176-type):** e.g., Slate FG-116, **fast attack, fast release** to "pin the vocal in place" and catch peaks.
6. **FX sends to dedicated buses:** lush reverb (ValhallaVintageVerb) and precise delays (Soundtoys EchoBoy), **often side-chained to the lead vocal so the effects duck out of the way while the vocalist is singing.**

Supporting framework (same article): drums go through an SSL-style VCA bus compressor with **Attack 30 ms (slowest), Release 100 ms or Auto, Ratio 4:1, 3–4 dB GR** for glue and pump — the drums-and-vocals work together so the vocal never fights the kit. Guitars are high-passed 80–120 Hz with "mud cuts" 250–400 Hz and fizz notches 4–8 kHz to leave the midrange for the voice. [Nail The Mix](https://www.nailthemix.com/zakk-cervini)

**What's fixed vs song-specific:** the *order and job of each step* is fixed (tune → cut → optical → color → FET → FX); the specific frequencies/amounts are per-song. This is a repeatable template, not a preset.

## Signature Moves
1. **The five-step "small moves" vocal chain** — tune → subtractive → optical (1-2 dB) → Pultec air/body → FET pinning. Each step barely audible; the sum is a vocal "on top of everything." [NTM](https://www.nailthemix.com/zakk-cervini)
2. **Side-chained vocal FX:** reverb/delay buses duck when the lead sings, so space exists but never buries the word. [NTM](https://www.nailthemix.com/zakk-cervini)
3. **SSL-style drum bus glue (30 ms attack, 4:1, 3–4 dB)** so the vocal's bed is tight and consistent — his vocal-forward mixes depend on a controlled rhythm section. [NTM](https://www.nailthemix.com/zakk-cervini)
4. **"Surgical EQ" everywhere:** HPF everything, cut mud 250–400 Hz, notch fizz 4–8 kHz — clarity through subtraction, on guitars *and* vocals. [NTM](https://www.nailthemix.com/zakk-cervini)
5. **Hybrid tone stacking:** real amp + amp modeler blended; live drums + samples (kick sample for attack/sub, snare sample for 5k "crack") — polish without losing the live feel. [NTM](https://www.nailthemix.com/zakk-cervini)

## What To Steal
1. **The 5-step chain, in order, with free plugins:** (1) clip-gain/normalize + subtle pitch (free: your DAW's pitch tool or MAutoPitch for effect); (2) subtractive notches with any parametric EQ (free: TDR Nova); (3) optical comp 1-2 dB (free: Analog Obsession LALA, LA-2A-style); (4) Pultec-style air 12-16 kHz + low-mid body (free: Analog Obsession PREDD or any shelf EQ); (5) FET 1176 fast/fast to pin (free: Analog Obsession FETISH). This exact order transfers to rap vocals on beats.
2. **Side-chain your reverb/delay to the lead vocal** (any DAW can do side-chain ducking; free: TDR Nova as a side-chain ducker). In rap, this keeps ad-lib/beat effects from fighting the main line.
3. **HPF everything below the voice** (guitars 80-120 Hz) — for a rapper over MP3 beats, this is about *your* vocal: high-pass the vocal ~70-90 Hz and, if you have stems, the instruments.
4. **Cut mud 250-400 Hz on anything competing with the vocal.** In a beat-heavy rap mix, this is where the vocal's own boxiness lives too.
5. **Duck-away FX instead of big wet reverbs** — modern rock/alt vocal production keeps the word dry and lets FX peek through in the gaps; same rule applies over beats.
6. **Work fast and check on consumer speakers/phones** — he made hit records during lockdown on a laptop and headphones; your monitoring is not the bottleneck.
7. **Sample-reinforce drums** (if you ever add drums) — kick attack + sub from a sample layer, snare "crack" ~5 kHz — the same logic applies to making a beat's kick cut through.
