## Vocal Philosophy
Hawkins' vocal doctrine: **drums and vocals are the two pillars of a mix, and vocals are worked "from scratch"** regardless of what processing the session arrives with. "The drums and vocals are the two things that matter the most to me. The music is important, but the priority usually is drums and vocals. You can make a bad-sounding guitar fit in a track and it will still kind of work. But if the drums and the vocals don't sound great, the song is not going to sound great." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-machine-gun-kelly-concert-aliens)

He is a "solo-er" — "I like to solo things and find out what is bugging me about particular elements, fix it and put it back in the track" — and an enhancer of the rough: "What I mostly do is enhance the rough, so it feels and sounds the best it can." [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.net/tutorials/mix-masters-distorting-twenty-one-pilots-for-suicide-squad); [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-machine-gun-kelly-concert-aliens)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
**A) Mixing MGK "Concert For Aliens" (SOS 2020 session breakdown) — his documented template chain:**
1. **Soundtoys Decapitator** — "adding a little bit of character. Nothing crazy."
2. **FabFilter Pro-Q2** — high-pass filter.
3. **Waves Scheps Omni Channel** — "my go-to tone shaper. It's riding the low end a bit, knocking things down at 300Hz when they get too woofy, and also de-essing. The compression has a very high ratio. I just turn knobs until it sounds good!"
4. **Waves Renaissance De-Esser** — "Most of the de-essing is done with" this; in the Heathens session: "still my favourite de-esser, even though it's not transparent and changes the sound instantly."
5. **Waves dbx 160** — "adding some polish and sheen. I barely touch it, but it always adds a nice finish to vocals."
6. **Waves J37** — "another character plug-in that changes the tone a bit. I mostly use that for slap delay. I love the slap delay on this plug-in!"
7. **FX sends:** **Soundtoys Little Plate** (plate reverb); **Soundtoys EchoBoy** delays at eighth/quarter/half notes; **Soundtoys MicroShift** — "for some chorus-like doubling effect."
8. **ALL VOCALS master bus** — same chain as the instruments master: Waves NLS saturation + Slate Virtual Mix Rack (DMG Equilibrium kept for delay comp only on vocals).
9. **Master bus:** Waves API 2500 "tiny touch," FabFilter Saturn tape sat "barely touching," FabFilter Pro-L2 to preview mastering ("I prefer to turn the L2 way down when I send mixes to the mastering guy").
- Session also arrived with Waves C4 + RCompressor on vocals, which he worked with.

**B) Recording vocals (Blurryface/Stressed Out/Heathens era — AudioTechnology):**
- **Sony C800G → UA 610 preamp (overdriven) → UA Blue-stripe 1176.** "It's the same signal chain I used on the songs I recorded for Twenty One Pilots' *Blurryface* album, including *Stressed Out*."
- "Although the lead vocal sounds fairly natural, it has some distortion as it was pushed heavily though that tube preamp" + Avid HEAT for "a little bit of crackle and distortion to everything."

**C) Mixing "Heathens" vocals (AudioTechnology):**
- UAD 1176 Bluestripe, **"all buttons in, pretty insanely compressed"** on vocal stems; Pro-Q2 high-passes everywhere.
- Backing vocals: **Auto-Tune + Soundtoys Little AlterBoy** "for those weird pitch parts… it's a current trend to pitch down with a formant shift."
- **"Verse Crystallizer"** track "automated very strongly to add this weird left-right vocal effect."
- Sends: UAD MXR Flanger/Doubler, UAD EMT 250 reverb, four SoundToys EchoBoy delays, Waves HDelay + UAD Dimension D, Valhalla Shimmer.

**D) Automation (Puremix, twenty one pilots "Chlorine"):**
- **UAD EP-34 tape echo inserted on the lead vocal; he automates Echo Repeats (feedback) and Echo Pan** during the chorus — pan drifts left/right of center, repeats rise ~quarter to halfway — "cool sound effect, headphone ear candy," deliberately not quantized, done as a live fader performance. [Puremix](https://www.puremix.com/blog/adam-hawkins-vocal-delay-automation)

**What's fixed vs song-specific:** fixed = HPF → tone shaper → de-esser → gentle polish comp → slap delay → sends (plate, 3 EchoBoy divisions, MicroShift) + vocal group bus; song-specific = the amounts. The album workflow is explicitly a template: "The overall tone and character of all the songs I mixed came from my mix of 'Concert for Aliens'… I'd import the bass or guitar track from one song to the next, and then adjust the settings." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-machine-gun-kelly-concert-aliens)

## Recording / Tracking Side
- **Mics:** Sony C800G for Tyler Joseph's lead vocals (with overdriven UA610 + Blue-stripe 1176) — the documented 21P vocal sound. [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.net/tutorials/mix-masters-distorting-twenty-one-pilots-for-suicide-squad)
- **In the box, hardware unused:** "I never even hooked up the outboard I had in my last studio… I prefer to have as little as possible between the speakers and my ears. Doing it all in the box makes recalls faster and much more efficient." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-machine-gun-kelly-concert-aliens)
- **Monitoring:** Genelec 8351s ("They're brutal. They're like NS10s in that you have to work really hard to make things good on them"); now an all-Genelec Atmos rig (8351B L/R, 8341A center, 8330A surrounds, 7360A subs) — "Everything I've done for the past two and a half years, I've done on these Genelecs solely." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-machine-gun-kelly-concert-aliens); [Headliner](https://headlinerhub.com/adam-hawkins-mixing-avril-lavigne-bite-me-dolby-atmos.html)
- **Session workflow:** color-coded template with 8 VCAs (drums/perc/bass/guitars/keys/effects/vocals/vocal effects) used "mainly for my control surfaces"; volume via Clip Gain and drawn edit-window automation rather than faders. **Starts every mix on drums, then vocals; wipes session vocal processing and rebuilds from scratch.** [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-machine-gun-kelly-concert-aliens)
- **Hot roughs:** "many of the rough mixes come in super-hot… I've had rough mixes come in at −3 RMS, and then I have to compete with that." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-machine-gun-kelly-concert-aliens)
- **Atmos:** prints stems from the approved stereo mix, then places elements around the room — "why not have the background vocals suddenly show up behind you?" [Headliner](https://headlinerhub.com/adam-hawkins-mixing-avril-lavigne-bite-me-dolby-atmos.html)

## Signature Moves
1. **The Scheps Omni Channel as vocal tone-shaper** (300 Hz taming, de-ess, high-ratio comp) + Ren De-Esser + barely-there dbx 160 "sheen" — his core modern vocal insert stack. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-machine-gun-kelly-concert-aliens)
2. **J37 tape slap delay on the lead vocal** for tone + character. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-machine-gun-kelly-concert-aliens)
3. **MicroShift / MXR Flanger-Doubler / Crystallizer for width** — "chorus-like doubling effect," including automated left-right vocal effects. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-machine-gun-kelly-concert-aliens); [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.net/tutorials/mix-masters-distorting-twenty-one-pilots-for-suicide-squad)
4. **Effect automation as ear candy** — EP-34 feedback + pan rides on the chorus vocal. [Puremix](https://www.puremix.com/blog/adam-hawkins-vocal-delay-automation)
5. **Distortion as texture everywhere** — overdriven 610 pre, all-buttons 1176, Decapitator, Avid HEAT: vocals that "sound fairly natural" but carry printed grit. [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.net/tutorials/mix-masters-distorting-twenty-one-pilots-for-suicide-squad)
6. **Template reuse across an album** — import settings from song to song, adjust per song. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-machine-gun-kelly-concert-aliens)

## What To Steal
1. **Build the vocal chain: HPF → tone-shaper (300 Hz control + de-ess + high-ratio comp) → de-esser → gentle "polish" comp → tape slap delay.** All stock/free equivalents exist (TDR Nova for de-ess/300 Hz; any comp with high ratio; any tape-style delay).
2. **"Start vocals from scratch" every time** — don't inherit the beat-maker's vocal processing; rebuild your own chain over the MP3 instrumental.
3. **Use a width/doubler device on the hook** — MicroShift-style pitch-shifted doubling is THE modern rock/alt vocal width move (free: any chorus/ADT effect; in Audacity, duplicate + 10-25 ms offset + slight pitch change, or a free doubler plugin). Transfers directly to rap hooks.
4. **Automate a delay's feedback and pan** for "ear candy" on one word or hook — deliberate, non-quantized movement. Audacity supports automation envelopes for this.
5. **Print subtle distortion into the vocal** (drive the preamp/chain slightly) so the voice "belongs" with loud instruments — for the user: a touch of saturation on the vocal bus, not just reverb.
6. **Make one template and reuse it for every song** — his entire MGK album was the same chain adjusted per song. This is the closest documented "formula" for repeatable vocals.
7. **Keep the vocal dry-ish and in front** ("kept very dry, so it stays in your face" is also his aesthetic via Avron's lineage) — with plate + 3 delay divisions + doubler doing the space work.
8. **Don't fight the hot rough** — match the instrumental's perceived energy with vocal density (compression + saturation), not just fader up.
