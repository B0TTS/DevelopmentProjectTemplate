## Vocal Philosophy
Scheps is the anti-"vocal chain" mixer: his philosophy is that vocals should sit in a **shared parallel-compression web** rather than a dedicated insert chain, and that **ambience is subtractive** — a vocal can sound "bone dry" in the mix while still having slap, room and a pitch-shifted delay barely audible. He mixes "really loud" records, but loudness comes from parallel density (raised RMS), not peak squashing. [Audiofanzine Part 1](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-1.html)

His stated ambience doctrine: "I don't actually do much [reverb]. I think I spent enough years mixing for Rick Rubin, who likes stuff really dry, that I kind of got out of the habit of doing it." [Audiofanzine Part 2](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-2.html)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
Scheps has no fixed insert template for vocals — his repeatable framework is **parallel buses + minimal ambience + automation**. The documented elements:

**Vocal ambience (his fixed set, from Audiofanzine Part 2):**
1. **Slap delay ~110 ms** ("I just have a slap somewhere in the 110 ms range")
2. **Very short reverb — plate or room**, natural-sounding ("a very short reverb — either a plate or a room or something somewhat natural sounding")
3. **Very short stereo delay with a little bit of pitch shifting** ("a very short stereo delay with a little bit of pitch shifting")
4. Result: "even with all that stuff in the mix, you'd probably think the vocal is bone dry… if you soloed it and took that stuff off, it would be like, 'Oh, now *that's* dry.'"

**Parallel framework (his signature, applies to vocals AND the whole mix):**
- Sends are **post-fader at zero gain, following the pan** — "you're basically just picking up a copy of what's in the mix bus, and sending that to the compressor. And then you can blend that back in." [Audiofanzine Part 1](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-1.html)
- **Shared compressors, not per-element ones:** "I don't have a parallel kick, a parallel snare, a parallel overhead, I just have a bunch of parallel drum compressors, and I'll mix and match." Same philosophy applies to vocals — multiple vocal tracks into the same parallel chains.
- **Chains stay simple:** "the chains are actually really simple. It's usually just a compressor. Every once in a while there will be EQ before or after to take care of some of the artifacts."
- **The Rear Bus:** a second stereo bus (originally the quad rear bus on his Neve) used as a *global* parallel compressor — many instruments sent to ONE compressor on that bus; the compressor reacts to whatever's loudest, creating "almost 'automated ducking' effects" and micro-dynamic movement. Documented step-by-step in [SOS – Cubase: Parallel Bus Compression](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/cubase-parallel-bus-compression) and shown on vocals (Ziggy Marley lead vocals) in [Production Expert free lesson](https://www.production-expert.com/home-page/2016/3/4/free-lesson-preview-andrew-scheps-on-his-signature-rear-bus-trick); full session in [Puremix](https://www.puremix.com/library/inside-the-mix/-/ziggy-marley-fly-rasta-all-analog).

**Plugin choices (documented, Audiofanzine Part 2 + TapeOp):**
- Surgical EQ: **Pro Tools EQ III** ("For surgical EQ, and being able to solo up the band very quickly… incredibly low DSP usage")
- Color: **Waves Kramer HLS (Helios)** — "you barely touch it, and it just sort of resonates at the frequency you set it at. I love the midrange on that for guitars"; 1073-style: **Scheps 73** ("the kind of EQ I never cut on, I only boost")
- Compression: **"1176 plug-ins — all of them"** — the family is what matters, not the model ("I'm not trying to replicate hardware, I'm just trying to get a sound")
- Channel strip: **Scheps Omni Channel** — built because "I hate having to mouse around all over the place to get multiple plug-in windows to open… I mix and match like crazy, even with hardware." [TapeOp](https://tapeop.com/interviews/133/andrew-scheps)
- Distortion-as-room: **iZotope Trash** on drums with EQ before/after ("gives me what sounds like a room, but it's not"); **Avid Lo-Fi** "just a tiny bit" on specific things only.
- 2-bus: "usually there's a compressor and at the very end there's a limiter… set to just shave off the red lights." [Audiofanzine Part 1](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-1.html)

**What's fixed vs song-specific:** the *routing* (parallel sends, rear bus, minimal ambience) is fixed; the *compressors and settings* change per song. His downloadable Puremix mixing template shows the full routing he starts every mix from. [Puremix – Andrew Scheps Mixing Template](https://www.puremix.com/library/tutorials/templates/andrew-scheps-mixing-template/andrew-scheps-mixing-template-part-2)

## Recording / Tracking Side
- Mixes on **one pair of late-'70s Tannoy SRM-10B monitors** (he owns four pairs) in an **untreated living-room-type space** — "for mixing, for control rooms, I just like a dead room that's big enough to get out of the way. I just want to hear my speakers." No sub. [Audiofanzine Part 1](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-1.html)
- **Neve 1073 recording technique:** owned ten of them; "When I'm recording on the hardware, I'll always crank the mic pre one click past where you probably should and drop the fader down, because that's when you really start to hear the 'Neve sound' of it." [Audiofanzine Part 2](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-2.html)
- **In-the-box rationale:** floating-point lets him run extreme compression early in the chain and fix level later ("anywhere in your signal path you can grab it and fix it… you can't do any of that in the analog world"); also console recall drift. [Audiofanzine Part 2](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-2.html)
- **Workflow:** never finishes a mix the same day he starts it; cycles 5 songs at once, 10 minutes to 4 hours each, "I'm completely fresh on the one I just opened." [Audiofanzine Part 1](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-1.html)
- Revision strategy: "If they want me to do it with level, I'll use EQ. If they want me to do it with EQ, I'll do it with panning. Because that way I know I'm actually going to listen." [TapeOp](https://tapeop.com/interviews/133/andrew-scheps)

## Signature Moves
1. **The Rear Bus** — global parallel compression: everything (or everything except drums/bass) sent post-fader to one compressor on a second stereo bus; blend to taste; try dual-mono (L/R independent) compression for extra movement. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/cubase-parallel-bus-compression)
2. **Many simple parallel chains instead of one complex chain** (explicitly contrasted with Michael Brauer's complex chains: "I probably have more individual chains, but each one of them has many fewer things on it"). [Audiofanzine Part 1](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-1.html)
3. **Loudness from density:** "my mixes are built up with tons of parallel compression, and that just brings up the RMS level quite a lot, as opposed to bringing the peaks down." [Audiofanzine Part 1](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-1.html)
4. **EQ instead of volume for placement:** "it's much easier to grab an EQ, and EQ it while you're listening to everything, and make it pop out. Because if you start to turn it up it obliterates other stuff" — turning up also hits the parallel compressors harder and ducks everything else. [Audiofanzine Part 2](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-2.html)
5. **Rebuilding dynamics with automation:** "Push the downbeat of every chorus for every instrument… you create this kind of pop at the downbeat"; ride *groups* (e.g., all rhythm guitars) rather than individual tracks. [Audiofanzine Part 1](https://en.audiofanzine.com/sound-technique/editorial/articles/mixing-in-parallel-part-1.html)

## What To Steal
1. **Set up ONE parallel vocal bus:** send the lead vocal post-fader (0 dB, pan-linked) to an aux with an aggressive compressor; blend until it's obvious, then back off. This is the single most documented Scheps move and it transfers perfectly to rap vocals over beats — it adds density without changing the dry attack. Free: Melda MCompressor or VladG Molot (both free), or your DAW's stock comp.
2. **Build the "Rear Bus"**: one aux compressor fed by several instruments (or the whole mix minus drums) — creates glue and "automated ducking" movement. [SOS how-to](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/cubase-parallel-bus-compression)
3. **Minimal vocal ambience, placed barely audible:** slap ~110 ms + short room/plate + short pitch-shifted stereo delay — the rap analogue is "keep the vocal dry and upfront, add just enough space that it isn't dry when soloed." Free: Valhalla Supermassive (free) for delays/space; OrilRiver (free) for room.
4. **Fix placement with EQ, not fader** — especially in dense mixes where a louder vocal just ducks the parallel compressor.
5. **Push the downbeat of every chorus** with grouped automation — direct transfer to rap: automate the vocal group + beat elements up 0.5-1 dB at hook downbeats.
6. **Never finish a mix the same day**; rotate between 2-3 songs so your ears stay fresh.
7. **Know one EQ for surgery and one for color** (his: EQ III + Helios/1073) — free: TDR Nova (surgical dynamic EQ) + any tilt/shelf color EQ.
8. **Don't copy presets — copy routing.** He teaches "the theory, not the presets" — the framework is the formula.
