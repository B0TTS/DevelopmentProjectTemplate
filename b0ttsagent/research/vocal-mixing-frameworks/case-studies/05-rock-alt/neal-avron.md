## Vocal Philosophy
Avron is the "**record it right, then balance**" school: he refuses to "record everything flat and fix things in the mix," and treats mixing largely as balancing because the sounds were already shaped at source. For vocals specifically, compression is an **attitude tool, not a level tool**: "The vocal compression is not so much about level adjusting as about the sound it gives, giving the vocal the right attitude so it sits better in the track." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron)

His mixes keep lead vocals **dry and in your face**, with space created by short delays rather than reverbs: "overall this track was kept very dry, so it stays in your face." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
From the SOS breakdown of "This Ain't A Scene, It's An Arms Race" (Fall Out Boy, *Infinity On High*):

**Recording (the documented tracking chain):**
- **Mic:** Neumann U47 tube "in most cases," U67 elsewhere
- **Compression:** Neve 33264, Distressor, or 1176 — "It may have been 4:1 or 6:1, perhaps even 8:1. Typically I'll hit the vocal pretty hard."
- **Philosophy:** EQ/compression at source is part of the sound: "the sounds that are being generated are integral to the vibe of a song."

**Mixing (Pro Tools → SSL console):**
1. **Two McDSP Filterbank P4 EQs on the lead vocal comp — one for verse, one for chorus, with different settings**: in the verse (lower register) he "wanted a little bit more top end on his voice to match it to the way it sounds in the choruses, so I boost at 8.4k"; on the chorus "I did very little."
2. **Waves C4 multiband compressor on the lead vocal** — "a couple of places where things were getting a little abrasive in the upper midrange… it's only compressing, or lowering, those specific frequencies when he's going into that register."
3. **Very little else:** possibly a short room reverb "just to give it more size"; sometimes short delays or harmonization "to give more size"; no heavy processing — "there's not a lot of reverb on this track. There just wasn't much room for reverbs."
4. **Backing vocals differentiated by design:** Metric Halo Channel Strip (thin the low end, add midrange) to set harmonies apart from the lead; 55%-wet short reverb (~0.5 s, short predelay) on the "funny voices" group; **Mod Delay II at 16th notes** on the 13-track group-vocal submix to create space instead of reverb. Delays "right on the beat," never rushed.
5. **Automation is key:** the 1.5 kHz bass lift and the vocal EQ changes are section-automated; he "checked the vocals regularly" while building bottom-up (drums → bass → guitars → vocals last).

**What's fixed vs song-specific:** the *method* is fixed — section-split EQ, multiband taming of harsh registers, dry lead, delay-for-space; the settings are per-song and per-register. His mixes use "very minor" EQ because the recording was already shaped.

## Recording / Tracking Side
- **Console-centric:** records *through* the desk (Neve 8078 at The Pass; SSL E/G at Paramount for mixing) — "I have an issue with the whole mic preamp situation… I prefer to have everything in front of me, all EQs and faders and compression, on a desk." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron)
- **Drums to 24-track Studer A800 @ 30 ips** (tape saturation on transients), then dumped into Pro Tools; vocals/guitars/bass recorded direct to Pro Tools. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron)
- **Vocal production is a layered event:** lead recorded as early as possible, then BG tracks, then "V" tracks (accents, falsettos, different characters), then 13 tracks of group vocals — the modern pop-punk vocal stack, 2007 edition. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron)
- **No micro-editing religion:** "I'd rather have a performer play several takes than create a performance out of bits they've done." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron)
- **Recent process:** he "doesn't produce much now" (per Fall Out Boy, who had to lock him in for *So Much (For) Stardust*); the 2023 FOB record returns to his fundamentals — band in a room, orchestra, live feel. [DIY Magazine](https://diymag.com/cover-feature/fall-out-boy-march-2023-interview); [Tape Notes TN128](https://tapenotes.co.uk/project/tn128-fall-out-boy)

## Signature Moves
1. **Section-differentiated vocal EQ** — separate verse/chorus EQ settings, including an 8.4 kHz lift for lower-register verses. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron)
2. **Multiband compression to tame only the abrasive registers** (Waves C4 on the lead vocal's upper mids). [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron)
3. **Dry lead + delays instead of reverbs** (16th-note delays on group vocals; short room only for size). [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron)
4. **Voice-character differentiation:** harmonies made thinner + more midrange; different voices get their own 55%-wet short reverb to separate them from the lead. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron)
5. **"Hit the vocal pretty hard" at tracking** (4:1-8:1, 1176/Distressor/33264) so the performance arrives with attitude already printed. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-neal-avron)

## What To Steal
1. **Print attitude at the source:** compress the vocal on the way in (or as the first insert) at 4:1-8:1 — for an Audacity rapper: record with a compressor or apply a hard FET-style comp first, *before* EQ polish. Free: Analog Obsession FETISH.
2. **Split your vocal EQ into verse vs chorus (vs hook) settings** — a lower-register rap verse needs more top-end air (his: +8.4 kHz) to match the hook's presence. Audacity supports automation via envelopes, or bounce two EQ'd versions.
3. **Tame only the offensive register with multiband/dynamic EQ** — free: TDR Nova (dynamic EQ) set to compress 2-4 kHz when the rapper pushes into shout range.
4. **Keep the lead dry; make space with short delays** — a slap/16th-note delay at low mix is the pop-punk way and works identically over beats (free: any stock delay, or Valhalla Supermassive).
5. **Differentiate every background layer** (thinner EQ, own reverb) — applies to rap ad-libs: give ad-libs a distinct tone so they read as a separate voice.
6. **Vocal last, but checked constantly** — build the bed first, then place the voice; "balance is the key thing."
7. **HPF and gentle cuts > heavy processing** when the take was recorded well — for beats you don't control, spend your effort on the vocal take itself (room, distance, energy) rather than rescue-EQ.
