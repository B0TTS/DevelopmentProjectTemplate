## Vocal Philosophy
"Vocals are the star" is CLA's operating principle, but his own framing is more aggressive: vocals must have **attitude and personality**, and the song's power beats any plugin. He famously checks vocal sounds only in the context of the full mix, never soloed. "Nobody will ever hear anything in solo. PERIOD. So the only way to get a great vocal sound is when it's competing with everything else in the mix." [URM Blog – Learn From The Legends Vol 1](https://urm.academy/learn-from-the-legends-volume-1-chris-lord-alge/). His approach is additive and broad — "excitement trumps perfection" is the summary used by analysts of his style. [The Pro Audio Files – 5 Mixing Lessons I Learned From CLA](https://theproaudiofiles.com/chris-lord-alge-mixing-lessons/)

The core chain order doctrine, stated in his own ProWorkshop training material: **"THE EQ SHOULD FEED INTO THE COMPRESSOR… EQ AFTER THE COMPRESSOR LEADS TO SIBILANCE ISSUES."** [High on Technology – ProWorkshop Basic Training (quoting CLA)](http://www.highontechnology.tech/2017/05/recording-and-sound-tips_29.html)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
### Fixed order, two documented versions:

**A) Hardware era (SOS 2007, "Welcome To The Black Parade"):**
1. **Waves L1 limiter** on the lead vocal while comping in Pro Tools — "The L1 adds some spit and attitude to the vocal. It makes it bark a little harder… It works almost like an EQ, rather than a compressor." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-chris-lord-alge)
2. **Blue-stripe Urei 1176**, 4:1, quick release ("The lead vocals all got blue 1176 compression, 4:1, quick release")
3. **Inward Connection TSL3** on backing vocals; **Dbx 263x** de-esser
4. **Delays/reverbs:** Roland SDE3000 or Line 6 Echo Pro for quarter/eighth/dotted-eighth tempo-set delays; Marshall Tape Eliminator for 15ips slap; Lexicon 300 7-second hall for anthemic sections only (shut off when the song turns rock); Sony DRE2000 1-second room.
5. **Automation is the final layer:** "With the vocals you try to get the overall tone for the whole record with the compressors, and then you're chasing the faders to get them really in your face. It's all about automation."

**B) In-the-box vocal bus (Puremix, "Mixing Vifolly with CLA" — the documented template):** multiple vocal aux buses, each with the identical chain:
1. **Waves SSL E-Channel** (used for EQ only): **+9 dB at 8 kHz**, **HPF at ~70 Hz**
2. **Waves CLA-76** (1176 emulation): **Input 30, Output 18, Ratio 4:1, Attack 3 (slow), Release 7 (fastest)** — note 1176 knobs are reversed; slow attack + fastest release = aggressive punch
3. **Waves CLA Vocals** (his signature multi-fx: EQ/compression/reverb/pitch/delay — used here mainly for a quarter-note delay)
4. **Waves L1 limiter**
Individual vocal tracks additionally get **SoundToys Devil-Loc Deluxe, used very lightly** ("like hot sauce, you just want a little, or it will burn your tongue") — the vocals need "the most attitude possible," a treatment "almost like a guitar amp." [Puremix – Chris Lord-Alge Mixing Vocals In-The-Box](https://www.puremix.com/blog/chris-lord-alge-mixing-vocals-in-the-box)

**Settings that repeat across every documented mix (The Pro Audio Files):**
- **1176: 15–20 dB+ of gain reduction**, 4:1, slow attack / fast release — "pummeling." If he wants more compression he doesn't touch the 1176 — he sends more vocal level to it from the SSL. [The Pro Audio Files](https://theproaudiofiles.com/chris-lord-alge-mixing-lessons/)
- **Favorite frequencies: 8 kHz shelf, 4.5 kHz and 2.5 kHz boosts, 60–120 Hz low end.** Vocal EQ = substantial 8k shelf, then broad boosts of 4.5k + 2.5k for presence/bite. Almost **no subtractive EQ** — "All Broad. All Additive."
- EQ is applied **into** heavy compression, which tames the harshness the boosts create.
- **Gain staging:** normalizes vocals to 0 dBFS before mixing (Gain AudioSuite). [Production Expert](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/get-top-chris-lord-alge-mixing-tips)

**What's fixed vs song-specific:** the bus order and plugin set are fixed ("set and forget"); the amounts (boost sizes, delay tempo divisions) are per-song. His permanent console layout puts vocals on channels 17–24 every time: "If everything is parked in the same place, all you have to worry about is the song. It's an ergonomic issue more than anything." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-chris-lord-alge)

## Recording / Tracking Side
- CLA doesn't record much anymore — he receives sessions (often 100–159 tracks) and **comps them down to 44 tracks on his Sony 3348 digital 2"** before mixing, making "a lot of your magic happen in your comps." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-chris-lord-alge)
- **Monitoring:** mixes quietly on original Yamaha NS10s + a $300 Infinity 12" sub — "When you're doing really critical moves, do them at a low level." Critical EQ moves are auditioned by slamming shelves to extremes for 10–15 seconds to calibrate the ear, then dialing back. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-chris-lord-alge); [Pro Audio Files](https://theproaudiofiles.com/chris-lord-alge-mixing-lessons/)
- Hardware chain on his SSL E-series: most outboard is hard-wired to one channel and left on one setting; if he needs a second setting he buys a second unit ("Noah's Ark mentality: two of every effect ever made"). [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-chris-lord-alge)

## Signature Moves
1. **The 1176 pummel:** 15–20 dB GR, 4:1, slow attack/fast release — then drive the send hotter for more, not the compressor. [Pro Audio Files](https://theproaudiofiles.com/chris-lord-alge-mixing-lessons/)
2. **Broad additive SSL EQ before compression:** +9 dB @ 8k + 4.5k/2.5k, HPF ~70 Hz, EQ feeding the compressor to avoid sibilance. [Puremix](https://www.puremix.com/blog/chris-lord-alge-mixing-vocals-in-the-box); [High on Technology](http://www.highontechnology.tech/2017/05/recording-and-sound-tips_29.html)
3. **Limiter-for-attitude:** L1 on the lead vocal as a tone/attitude device, not just level. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-chris-lord-alge)
4. **Saturation as vibe:** Devil-Loc Deluxe sparingly on individual vocal tracks, and distortion inserted **after reverb on the return** to fatten vocals without sounding distorted. [Puremix](https://www.puremix.com/blog/chris-lord-alge-mixing-vocals-in-the-box)
5. **Mixing like a live performance:** extreme LCR panning, one long automation pass sculpting 15–30 seconds at a time; "fader moves are even more important than EQ." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/secrets-mix-engineers-chris-lord-alge)

## What To Steal
1. **EQ → compressor, always, on vocals.** High-pass ~70 Hz, a healthy 8k shelf, presence boosts at 2.5k/4.5k, then the compressor. Stock DAW EQ + any compressor will do.
2. **One aggressive FET-style compressor (1176-type) at 4:1, slow attack, fast release, and don't fear 10-15dB of GR** on a dynamic rock/rap vocal. Free: Analog Obsession FETISH (free 1176-style).
3. **Put a limiter at the end of the vocal chain** as a presence/attitude tool, not just loudness. Free: your DAW's stock limiter (or free Limiter No6) with 2-4 dB of action.
4. **Route ALL vocals through one vocal bus with one chain** (EQ→comp→FX→limiter) so the whole vocal group gets the same character — exactly what he does with 17/18 buses.
5. **Add a tiny bit of saturation/distortion on individual vocal tracks** (or on the reverb return, after the reverb) for "attitude" — free: Softube Saturation Knob, or your DAW's stock drive.
6. **Audition EQ at extremes first:** slam the shelf fully up, then back off — trains your ear fast and gives bolder settings than incremental nudging.
7. **Automate like a performance** — ride the vocal fader through every section. This transfers 1:1 to rap: a rap vocal needs even more section-to-section level shaping over a beat.
8. **"Nobody hears solo" — set vocal level and tone while the beat/guitars are playing.** This is the single most transferable habit to rap-over-instrumentals, where vocals float if mixed soloed.
9. **Tape-recorder mindset:** comp aggressively, commit to 40-44 tracks, then mix.
