## Vocal Philosophy
Lancaster is the archetype of the modern rock vocal framework: **the genre doesn't change the job** — "you want to have the vocals clear, the guitars sounding as wide as possible, things like that. It's not rocket science." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-bring-me-horizon) His vocal sound = **heavy compression ("spank"), saturation/grit as texture, layered walls, and section-specific processing** — each part gets its own space rather than one uniform chain. [Nail The Mix – Unboxing BMTH "Doomed" raw vocals](https://www.nailthemix.com/raw-vocals-oli-sykes-dan-lancaster-bmth)

He learned mixing from Sound on Sound's Inside Track series itself (CLA's Black Parade article, the Fields/Hager Jonas Brothers article) — "I used to read SOS a lot, and several Secrets Of The Mix Engineers articles had a major impact on me… These articles were invaluable for me." [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-bring-me-horizon)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
**A) *That's The Spirit* / "Throne" (SOS 2015 session breakdown):**
1. **Lead vocal split across multiple tracks by section** — "I often chopped up the vocals and spread them out over several new tracks, because I wanted to have different processing for them in different sections of the song." (The session shows 35 vocal tracks; the lead is on 6 with different treatment per section.)
2. **Waves SSL E-Channel + CLA-76 on almost all lead tracks** (his core vocal combo — "The SSL Channel now is a favourite on vocals and drums in particular").
3. **PuigTec EQ boosting at 16 kHz in the main verse** — "which gives a really cool vibe that the SSL can't really do, as it can be a bit cold."
4. **Manny Marroquin Drive for distortion** on the vocal (distortion-as-EQ; he uses the same trick on snare: "I am using the Drive plug-in as EQ").
5. **CLA-76 as a "spreader"** working the lower octave of the lead.
6. **Backing vocals (Jordan Fish): "similar treatments, just not quite as elaborate."**
7. **Master bus template:** Waves NLS (Spike Stent setting) → API 560 (+8k/16k treble) → SSL Channel bus comp (**3-5 dB GR, slow 30 ms attack, fast release, 4:1**) → (Oxford Inflator/Limiter for client reference only, removed before mastering).

**B) *POST HUMAN* era / "Doomed" (NTM session breakdown) — his modern vocal workflow:**
1. **Compress the lead first, aggressively** — "The first order of business is to give the vocal some serious 'spank'… he immediately slams it with some heavy compression… creating a super consistent and upfront sound." (Identified as a hallmark of modern metal/rock production: clamp the performance before anything else.)
2. **Saturation/grit to integrate the vocal** — "Instead of just trying to tame the top end with EQ, Dan breaks up the brightness by adding a layer of distortion or saturation. This move adds harmonic complexity and a bit of grit… distortion isn't just for guitars."
3. **Targeted surgical EQ per layer, not one chain** — he spots each background vocal's mic signature (e.g., SM7B upper-mid "harshness or honk") and notches exactly that; "Every vocal layer has its own unique character."
4. **Arrangement-driven ambience** — long sustained notes = room for "drenching in thick reverbs and long delays"; busy parts stay drier.
5. **Insert reverbs on featured harmony parts** (breaking the send rule): "just sending everything to one or two reverbs can be boring. This technique allows certain parts to have their own little world."
6. **The "push and pull" effect method:** crank the effect level way up to hear what it does, then drag it back to a subtle supporting level.

**What's fixed vs song-specific:** fixed = aggressive lead compression first, saturation layer, per-layer surgical EQ, section-split processing, insert reverbs for featured parts, SSL bus-comp template. Song-specific = all frequencies, delay/reverb choices, and how wet things get.

- **Studio:** Pro Tools HD, minimal outboard (Urei 1176, API 3124+, DAV BG2 pres), SE Gemini II + Reflexion Filter, matched AKG 414s; drums tracked at larger studios. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-bring-me-horizon)
- **Monitoring:** Barefoot MicroMain 27 (upgraded from KRK VXT8 specifically to judge low end for BMTH's synth subs); famously mixed the first BMTH song on Bose QC15 noise-cancelling headphones. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-bring-me-horizon)
- **Session culture:** 96/24 sessions from producer Jordan Fish; he re-organizes/color-codes, adds drum samples, replaces demo samples, and — for vocals — physically re-slices the lead across tracks to enable section processing.
- **Mixing workflow:** "I tended to mix each track to the end, rather than switch between tracks… I always want to hear the end result as quickly as possible!" Album template refined song-by-song, then a final pass of small adjustments with the band. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-bring-me-horizon)

## What To Steal
1. **Compress the lead vocal hard FIRST, before EQ.** His order: spank → then deal with tone. For rap-over-beats this is the single biggest difference from "polite" pop chains — a slammed, consistent lead sits on top of anything. Free: FETISH (1176-style) or your DAW comp at 4:1, 10-15 dB GR.
2. **Add a saturation/distortion layer to the vocal** to glue it to distorted instrumentals — free: Softube Saturation Knob, or the DAW's drive. For rap over an MP3 beat, this is the "make it sound like it belongs" trick; keep it subtle.
3. **Notch out each layer's own mic signature** — if your vocal has a honk, find it (sweep a narrow boost, then cut it). Free: any parametric EQ.
4. **Split the vocal by section** (verse chain vs hook chain) — in Audacity: process/bounce verse and hook separately, or automate effects. This is Lancaster's most distinctive framework move.
5. **Use an insert reverb on one special line** (ad-lib, hook tag) instead of everything through one reverb — gives "its own little world."
6. **Push an effect way up, listen, then pull it back** — the fastest way to learn what a reverb/delay actually does.
7. **Copy the SSL bus-comp template:** 4:1, ~30 ms attack, fast release, 3-5 dB GR on the master (free: TDR Kotelnikov or your DAW's bus comp) — glue that makes the vocal sit "in" the track.
8. **The template-first workflow:** build one repeatable session (buses, colors, bus comp, vocal FX sends) and reuse it across songs — he did exactly this for an entire album.
