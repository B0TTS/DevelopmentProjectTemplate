## Vocal Philosophy
- Compression happens at capture, not after: *"use a Neve/CL-1b and compress until it sounds right with nothing on Pro Tools"* — because *"no software compressor can touch any hardware compressor in [his] experience."* (Tweets, quoted with source link to twitter.com/alextumay/status/778327221106860032 via Equipboard). [Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/pros/alex-tumay)
- Minimalism in the mix: *"The songs aren't made better because I put an extra effect here, or put more reverb, or made the vocal feel a certain way at a specific spot. It's best to let the music speak for itself... I've taken a more minimalist approach to the mixes."* [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/alex-tumay-young-thug-on-the-rvn-engineer-interview/)
- Auto-Tune is a tool, not a cheat: *"AutoTune has been a part of almost every session I've worked on since I started engineering."* [Antares artist page](https://www.antarestech.com/artists/alex-tumay); he publicly defended Auto-Tune against purists (HotNewHipHop, 2016). [HotNewHipHop](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/86801-young-thug-engineer-alex-tumay-destroys-any-anti-auto-tune-argument-news)
- Speed is part of the sound: he mixed *On the Rvn* in 36 hours and *Barter 6* in two days — "every first mix made the cut." [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/alex-tumay-young-thug-on-the-rvn-engineer-interview/)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
Tumay has never published one fixed plugin chain (unlike Bainz); his documented, repeatable system is capture-side + bus-side:
1. **Tracking/compression (fixed):** vocals compressed at tracking with a **Neve 1073 + Tube-Tech CL-1B** (he bought his own CL-1B in Dec 2018) — "compress until it sounds right with nothing on Pro Tools," then only in-the-box adjustments later. [Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/pros/alex-tumay)
2. **Pitch:** Antares Auto-Tune in "almost every session" (he even preferred Auto-Tune 5); Thug's melodic style depends on it as a creative layer. [Antares](https://www.antarestech.com/artists/alex-tumay), [Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/pros/alex-tumay)
3. **Mix bus (fixed):** **Waves SSL G-Master Buss Compressor** "in every mix" for Young Thug; **Slate Digital Virtual Buss Compressors** ('slat' plugin) also used on Thug mixes. [Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/pros/alex-tumay)
4. **Editing/leveling (fixed workflow):** heavy clip-gaining and clip-level editing before plugins — the "level the vocal with gain first" doctrine he is repeatedly cited for (his -12 to -18 dB gain-staging guidance is quoted in community vocal-chain guides). [mastrproductions guide citing Tumay](https://www.mastrproductions.com/golden-vocal-chain/), [Pensado's Place](https://www.pensadosplace.tv/grammy-winning-mix-engineer-alex-tumay-young-thug-21-savage-travis-scott/)
5. **Template (fan recreation, C-tier/unverified):** an AudioSEX thread documents a "Tumay-style" Pro Tools vocal template using Waves plugins + Auto-Tune — used only to corroborate that his documented palette is Waves + Antares; settings therein are NOT verified. [AudioSEX](https://audiosex.pro/threads/alex-tumays-recreated-protools-vocal-chain-template-with-details-and-comments.53790/)
6. **Monitors/phones:** Audio-Technica ATH-M50x headphones (his stated choice). [Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/pros/alex-tumay)

## Signature Moves
1. **Compress at tracking (Neve/CL-1B), mix with "nothing on" the vocal in Pro Tools** — hardware capture instead of plugin stacks. [Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/pros/alex-tumay)
2. **Bus compression as the only constant** (SSL G-Master / Slate VBC on the mix bus) with minimal per-channel processing. [Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/pros/alex-tumay)
3. **Clip-gain/leveling-first doctrine** — gain-stage vocals at -12 to -18 dB, balance with faders before plugins. [mastrproductions](https://www.mastrproductions.com/golden-vocal-chain/)
4. **Auto-Tune as creative bedrock** on melodic rap vocals — never apologetic about it. [Antares](https://www.antarestech.com/artists/alex-tumay), [HotNewHipHop](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/86801-young-thug-engineer-alex-tumay-destroys-any-anti-auto-tune-argument-news)

## What To Steal
1. Level first: get the vocal sitting at -12 to -18 dB and balance with gain/clip-gain before adding anything (in Audacity: normalize each clip, then volume-automate the loud/quiet words).
2. Compress hard at the source, lightly afterward — emulate the CL-1B move with a good opto-style compressor early in the chain (free: TDR Kotelnikov) and then leave the vocal almost untouched.
3. Use a mix-bus compressor as your one constant (SSL G-Master concept; free SSL-style bus comps exist) with gentle settings.
4. Don't decorate: try the mix with no vocal effects first; add only what the song needs ("let the music speak").
5. Auto-Tune/effect tuning is a legitimate creative layer for melodic rap — commit to it rather than hiding it.
6. Work fast: first-mix instinct is often right; avoid endless revision loops.
7. Back up your projects — twice.
