## Vocal Philosophy
- Capture it right and keep it simple: *"I like to keep it simple. Every time you bring in a new variable, there's another chance for some kind of issue."* [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat)
- Feel over perfection: *"sometimes things aren't perfect, but they may feel right. Everything in music is about feeling."* [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat)
- The artist's vocal signature is non-negotiable: Chris's Auto-Tune is printed at tracking so *"no one will ever hear Chris's vocal raw. It just doesn't exist."* [Antares](https://www.antarestech.com/blog/teezio-talks-auto-tune)
- Staged compression over smashing: *"Instead of completely smashing things with one compressor, I will compress lightly in stages."* [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
**Tracking chain (fixed):** Telefunken **ELA M 251** → **Neve 1073** → **Tube-Tech CL-1B** → Pro Tools. While tracking: **HPF below 80Hz** on the vocal, cue reverb from **Valhalla VintageVerb**, quarter-note delay from **Waves H-Delay**; **Antares Auto-Tune on while singing and PRINTED** (through an aux before the record track; alto/tenor setting, **Retune usually 11–13**, sometimes 2 "when we go crazy", sometimes 7 — "It's not about the right settings, but about what it feels like"). [SoS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat), [Antares](https://www.antarestech.com/blog/teezio-talks-auto-tune)

**Mix vocal chain — all Chris Brown leads (fixed, documented):** FabFilter **Pro-Q 2** → **Tube-Tech EQP-1A** (Pultec-style) → **UAD 1176 silver-face** → FabFilter **Pro-MB** (multiband) → Waves **RCompressor** → Waves **De-Esser**. Rationale: "I use several compressors because I don't like to attack the signal too harshly... The 1176 is smooth and captures everything, and then another compressor will catch things that still pop out... The multiband compressor then catches low end, 100-500 Hz, which strangely enough actually adds clarity to the vocal." [SoS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat)

**Sends/FX (documented):**
- Reverb only on sends, never on the track: send '1' → '140Verb' (Valhalla VintageVerb); a LexVerb aux for snare reverb.
- Delays are **printed to audio** (H-Delay quarter- and eighth-note auxes), then manually edited: *"Sometimes, if a word is sung slightly late or early, and you like the feel of that and leave it, the delay will not feel right with the word after that, so I manually move that delay to make it feel correct"* — plus volume-automated delay tails and stutter edits.
- Hook delay treated with **McDSP FutzBox** radio-style effects; background stacks use Waves OneKnob saturation/underwater effects. [SoS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat)

**Mix bus:** Plugin Alliance **Mäag Audio EQ4** first (air) → glue → limiter on the bus (removed before mastering). [SoS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat)

**Template vs per-song:** recording template and mix template are entirely separate ("When I start a mix, I erase the recording template completely... and import my mix template"); templates are bare-bones routing (color-coded: drums blue, bass brown, music green, lead vocals light blue, BVs dark blue); delays pre-placed but "the meat of my delays comes from making them on the spot." Everything ITB: *"I'm completely in the box now. I use analog outboard gear as hardware inserts, but I sum in Pro Tools and bounce everything offline."* [SoS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat), [Antares](https://www.antarestech.com/blog/teezio-talks-auto-tune)

**Gunna (guest) handling:** no Auto-Tune on his tracks, so Teezio applied it; same chain structure, different settings. [SoS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat)

## Recording / Tracking Side
- Chris cuts vocals line by line; Teezio uses three record tracks and shuffles takes up to a work-space track ("cutting, dropping, cutting, dropping"), then moves whole verses to verse tracks — speed matters.
- Records at home/Conway-style "home-like" setups; no acoustic treatment ("There's a rug and a couch... I just use my ears"); PMC TwoTwo 6 monitors, Lynx Hilo converter, UAD Octo.
- Mix order: kick → snare → hats → toms/perc → bass → music → vocals referenced; never final-mix to the stereo MP3 of the beat (chases stems; keeps the stereo instrumental in-session as reference).
- ~6 hours per mix; 32-track album marathon. [SoS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat), [Matt Rad #56](https://www.livewithmattrad.com/episodes/56)

## Signature Moves
1. **Printed Auto-Tune on the way in** (aux → Auto-Tune → record track): the vocal never exists raw; Retune 11–13 default, dialed to feel. [Antares](https://www.antarestech.com/blog/teezio-talks-auto-tune)
2. **Staged light compression** (EQP-1A → 1176 → Pro-MB → RComp) instead of one heavy compressor. [SoS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat)
3. **Printed, hand-edited delays** — delays bounced to audio, nudged to the feel, volume-automated, stuttered. [SoS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat)
4. **Separate recording vs mixing templates** with strict color-coding; bare-bones routing, no pre-loaded processing. [SoS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat)
5. **80Hz HPF + Valhalla VintageVerb + H-Delay quarter-note as the tracking cue sound** — artists hear the "finished" vibe while recording. [SoS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-chris-brown-heat)

## What To Steal
1. **Tune first, commit, move on** — process pitch correction as the first step (print it), before EQ/compression; pick a retune speed and adjust to taste per song.
2. Never rely on one heavy compressor: chain 3–4 gentle stages (EQ → fast comp like 1176 → multiband taming 100–500Hz → final glue comp). Free: stock comps + TDR Nova (free multiband/dynamic EQ) for the 100–500Hz move.
3. Reverb only on sends, and keep it small (VintageVerb-class: free alternatives like Valhalla Supermassive or stock room verbs).
4. Print your delays to audio, then nudge them to the groove and ride their volume — this is the pro move that makes delays feel "in the pocket."
5. High-pass the vocal at ~80Hz while recording/tracking — kills rumble/boom before the chain.
6. Keep two templates: a tracking template (with tuning + cue FX) and a separate mixing template with bare-bones routing — never mix in the tracking session.
7. Mix order: kick, snare, hats, bass, music, then vocals — build the low end first for rap/R&B.
8. Air on the mix bus: a Mäag-style high-frequency shelf (EQ4 concept) adds "openness" — apply subtly.
