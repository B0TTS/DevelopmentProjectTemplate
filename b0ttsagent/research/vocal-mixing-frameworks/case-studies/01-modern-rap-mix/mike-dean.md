## Vocal Philosophy
- Anti-overprocessing. On Twitch he has argued you don't need to EQ/compress every channel because producers' sounds arrive already treated ("MIKE DEAN SAYS STOP OVERMIXING" clip): *"you don't always have to eq and like compress every channel... a lot of the sounds people use these days is already eq'd compressed already when we're making the beat"* (auto-caption, lightly cleaned) — paraphrase-safe theme: leave what's already good alone. [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DoN-gicbA7g)
- Respects the artist's demo and printed effects absolutely: *"When I mix, I stay very true to the demo... For a mixer to take off the effects is just a waste of time. It is like buying a new car, and saying: OK, I am going to take the transmission out and then put it back."* [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)
- Sceptical of heavy modern tuning: *"Tuning can make singers sound very sterile. Sometimes I have two or three rappers on one song, and I can't tell the difference!"* — he prefers Melodyne for minimal fixes. [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)
- Tool simplicity: *"I don't like stuff where I don't know what it is doing. And in general I like regular, stock plug-ins. I don't use a lot of weird stuff."* [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
No fixed template — explicitly anti-template: *"I don't use templates. I start with the session as it comes. You don't want to reinvent the song."* His documented framework is a minimal, repeatable recording chain + simple plugin palette:

1. **Tracking chain (fixed, hardware):** Sony C800G (or Shure SM7B, or Neumann U67) → Neve 1073 preamp (vintage or BAE; Neve Shelford Channel in B room) → **Tube-Tech CL-1B** compressor on the way in. *"My compressor on the way in is the Tube-Tech CL-1B. That's it. I keep it pretty simple."* [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)
2. **Tuning:** minimal Melodyne for pitch-correction-style fixes; manual-pitch-shift heritage (Eventide H3000). Heavy Auto-Tune only as deliberate FX — he originated the "Heartbreak sound" = **Auto-Tune + distortion**, first on John Legend's *Jesus Walks* backing vocals with **Auto-Tune set to zero** (talkbox effect). [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)
3. **Vocal FX (named):** Avid D-Verb (room-ish reverb), Waves H-Delay (delay), Valhalla reverbs — *"I particularly like the D-Verb. It makes stuff sound like it's in a room, what else do you want? The Valhalla reverbs also are go-tos. They are nice, and simple to use."* [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)
4. **Vocal processing plugins (named on streams/interviews, compiled 2026):** iZotope Nectar 3 (he posted *"vocal simplicity"* about it), Waves Renaissance Vox (fast level management), Waves DeEsser, FabFilter Pro-Q 3, Brainworx bx_2098 EQ (a favourite EQ), Soundtoys Decapitator (cited for 808s on Desiigner's "Timmy Turner"). [We Rave You](https://weraveyou.com/tech/mike-dean-vst-plugins/)
5. **Mix bus:** Neve 5060 Centerpiece summing → SPL MixDream; on the 2-bus: Waves SSL G-Master Buss Compressor (primary glue) and Waves L1 Ultramaximizer for loudness/mastering. [We Rave You](https://weraveyou.com/tech/mike-dean-vst-plugins/)
6. **Fixed vs song-specific:** the hardware record chain is fixed; everything else is minimal and song-specific; he mixes fast (30-minute revision passes). *"I mix quickly. I may later go back a couple of times for 30 minutes each, but that's it."* [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)
7. **DAW/rig:** Pro Tools for recording vocals, mixing and mastering (creation in Ableton/FL); SSL UF8 controllers; McDSP APB-16; UAD Satellites; Antelope 10MX clock; monitors: Tannoy 215 + 8 pairs of Yamaha NS10s ("crucial to my workflow"). [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)

## Recording / Tracking Side
- Mics: Sony C800G, Shure SM7B ("Lana's whole history has been about using the SM7B"), Neumann U67 ("I like to record with either a U67 or a Sony C800G vocal mic"). Recorded Lana Del Rey on C800 + SM7B simultaneously, no windscreen. [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean), [Tape Op](https://tapeop.com/interviews/122/mike-dean)
- Recorded 2Pac's vocals on Pro Tools as early as the 1990s; "I've always worked digitally." [Tape Op](https://tapeop.com/interviews/122/mike-dean)
- Vocal production done by himself since the old days ("If a note was out of tune, we'd use an Eventide H3000 to pitch it down a few cents and punch it back in"). [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)
- No template/tracking ritual beyond the simple chain: "I like it more simple." [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)

## Signature Moves
1. **"Heartbreak sound":** Auto-Tune (zero/effectively fixed) + distortion — the talkbox-style vocal effect he invented on "Jesus Walks" and propagated through 808s-era records. [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)
2. **Stay true to the demo:** never strip producer effects/prints — keeps the artist's identity and mix speed. [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)
3. **Stock-plugin minimalism:** D-Verb + H-Delay + Valhalla + Nectar 3 "vocal simplicity" — regular plugins over exotic ones. [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean), [We Rave You](https://weraveyou.com/tech/mike-dean-vst-plugins/)
4. **Stop overmixing:** don't EQ/compress channels that already sound right coming from the producer. [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DoN-gicbA7g)
5. **One opto comp on the way in** (CL-1B), nothing else — capture the tone at the mic/pre so the mix stays light. [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mike-dean)

## What To Steal
1. Fix your tracking chain and keep it minimal: a good dynamic or condenser mic (SM7B-class) → clean preamp → ONE opto-style compressor (CL-1B emulation: free/stock opto comps, e.g. TDR Kotelnikov is a great free VST) — get the tone before plugins.
2. Use Auto-Tune as committed FX: if you want the effect, set retune aggressively and print it; if you want invisible pitch, use gentle/robotic-free correction (Melodyne-style; in Audacity, apply pitch correction as a committed effect BEFORE EQ/compression).
3. Keep the beat's own vibe — don't strip effects baked into the instrumental; mix around them.
4. Reverb = room feel: D-Verb-style small room on vocals; delay = Waves H-Delay-style ping-pong/quarter note. Free equivalents: Valhalla Supermassive (free), any stock delay.
5. Don't EQ/compress every channel — if a sound already works, leave it.
6. Mix bus: SSL-style bus compression (free emulations abound; even stock comp at 2:1, ~10ms) + a transparent limiter (L1-style: many free limiters) with only 1–2dB of gain reduction.
7. Check on small speakers (NS10 philosophy) — a cheap mono/phone speaker tells you if the vocal cuts.
