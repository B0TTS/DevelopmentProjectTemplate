## Vocal Philosophy
- Vocals are the most important element, and they get a subtractive approach: *"I usually begin with vocals. I feel like vocals are the most important part of the song. I take what's called a subtractive approach. I remove mistakes or strong S's... It's a lot of tedious cleanup work that some engineers don't do."* [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)
- Feel over readouts: *"I just turn the knobs until it sounds good. And that's my honest-to-god approach... I'm not interested in technical readouts. It's all about feel."* [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)
- Minimalism is a feature: *"You don't always have to get fancy and get into hot new plug-ins. Basic concepts can make things sound good."* — and *"When the technology uses us, that's when it gets bad."* [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)
- Fast mixes are better mixes: *"The songs I spend the least amount of time on sound the best. That's not a coincidence."* (rap song ≈ 3 hours; pop ≈ 6–8 hours). [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
- No published fixed insert chain; his documented framework is **order + subtraction + a consistent plugin palette**:
1. **Order:** vocals first (subtractive cleanup of sibilance, mouth noises, dead-space edits), then drums + kick/bass relationship ("The relationship between the kick drum and the bass is very important, especially for any type of music that's hip-hop based"), then instruments, then marry vocals back in. [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)
2. **Palette (named):** Little Labs **Voice of God** ("If I had to narrow it down to one plug-in, I would choose Little Labs Voice Of God... it's amazing on basses, kicks, and 808s"), UAD **Fairchild 670** (kicks/bass), **PuigTec/Pultec EQs**, Waves **Renaissance de-esser & compressor**, Waves **SSL plug-ins**, Waves **H-Delay**, Waves **OneKnob** series ("I love that concept. As you can tell, I'm a minimalist"), **Audio Ease Altiverb** convolution reverb (he loves its visual IR navigation). [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)
3. **Tuning doctrine:** Auto-Tune when the artist wants the sound (Lil Uzi always; Migos "Drip" — "that robotic, T-Pain-type sound") and Melodyne for invisible fixes ("I used Melodyne, even on 'Be Careful'. Cardi sang that hook, and I had to Melodyne her"). Note: "AutoTune itself actually has a sound even if it's not technically affecting notes... it kind of smoothes things out." [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)
4. **Documented example — "Money" (Cardi B, 2019):** hi-hats rolled off low end + **4dB boost upper frequencies** ("makes it sound crispy"); piano got high end for "bite" after label notes; **808 boosted at 60Hz** with EQ ("Normally, I would boost at a lower frequency, but this needed to have punch and feel more like a kick drum"). [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)
5. **Rough-mix framework (his signature):** *"Whenever someone sends me a session, the first thing I want to know is: Has the client been listening to the rough? If they've been listening to it for a long time, I'll put it on a track in the session, so I can really scrutinise it and lock into what they love about this rough."* [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/diamond-in-the-rough)
6. **On his "chain" consistency (MWTM Q&A, his own answer):** *"No particular reason other than I just like what I like. I like the sound I get with what I typically use, so I tend to gravitate to that particular chain."* [MWTM community](https://community.mwtm.com/t/cardi-b-vocal-chain/5029)

- Most common recording mistake he sees: clipped/distorted vocal takes — "Distortion is a very hard thing to fix because once it's there, it's there." [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)

## Signature Moves
1. **Subtractive vocal mixing first** — surgical removal of sibilance, mouth sounds, dead-space noise before any boosting ("tedious cleanup work that some engineers don't do"). [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)
2. **"Solid low end without mud"** — VOG + Fairchild 670 + 60Hz-style targeted boosts on 808s; known for *"delivering real solid low end without it sounding muddy."* [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)
3. **Rough-in-the-session A/B** — locks onto what the client loves about the rough, then improves it (same move as Jaycen/Bainz, independently stated). [AudioTechnology](https://www.audiotechnology.com/features/diamond-in-the-rough)
4. **Speed as a mixing tool** — 3-hour rap mixes; "I know I'm done when I start asking too many questions and second-guessing my decisions." [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)
5. **OneKnob-style minimalism** — simple single-purpose tools over complex chains. [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/2/15/18224351/cardi-b-invasion-of-privacy-album-grammy-leslie-brathwaite-mix-engineer)

## What To Steal
1. Start every mix by cleaning the vocal: de-ess, remove mouth/room noise in gaps, fix plosives — subtraction before addition.
2. 808s: one targeted EQ boost around 60Hz for thump + sub reinforcement (VOG concept: free subharmonic or octave-duplicated low end) + tube-style compression (Fairchild concept; free opto/VCA comps).
3. Keep a fixed, small plugin palette you know (his "I like what I like" consistency) — familiarity beats plugin-hopping.
4. Put the original beat/reference in your session and A/B constantly; lock onto what the artist loves about it.
5. Timebox: aim to finish a rap vocal mix in a few hours; stop when you start second-guessing.
6. For tuning: Auto-Tune when the effect is wanted, Melodyne-style correction when it should be invisible — both are valid, choose deliberately.
7. Hats: roll off low end and add a few dB of high-frequency energy for crispness.
