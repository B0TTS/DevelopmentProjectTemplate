## Why This Matters

Kuk Harrell essentially invented the job of the specialist vocal producer — "the only person allowed to stitch together RiRi's words" [Hollywood Reporter, https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/music/music-news/stitcher-pop-stars-rihannas-vocal-418697/]. His documented process is the industry's most complete public framework for comping and stacking: **record front-to-back, comp live in playlists while the artist sings, cut the lead in ~90 minutes, then timing → tuning → stacking → rough mix.** His 2023 Pensado's Place appearance (Rihanna's Super Bowl prep) and the MWTM "For You" series confirm the process is current. ## The Documented Comping Framework (step by step)

1. **Session psychology first:** research the artist's working preferences with their A&R; keep the room fun; artist in the booth, Harrell at the controls: "I prefer to hear things on the monitors, so I get the true sound." [SOS, https://www.soundonsound.com/people/kuk-harrell-vocal-producer]
2. **Record the whole song front-to-back:** "When recording the vocals, I like to start at the beginning of the song, and keep plugging away until we get to the end of the record… If you do one section and then go back to do another section, it will sound pieced together." [SOS, https://www.soundonsound.com/people/kuk-harrell-vocal-producer?page=2]
3. **Comp live, while singing:** "For the most part I am comping the vocals as we go along. I'm recording in Playlists [in Pro Tools], but I'm listening closely the entire time for what the comp is going to be… it's more about making mental notes… Once I know that I have the magic, and five or 10 options to swap different inflections, if needed, I move on. I don't want to bore the artist by asking them to do one part 50 times." [SOS, ibid.]
4. **Time budget:** "If everything is going great, you should be able to cut the lead vocals in an hour and a half." [SOS, ibid.]
5. **Edit order is fixed:** "I start with timing. It's all based on feel, and being knowledgeable about the genre you're dealing with. Some things need to be right on the beat, whereas with R&B it needs to be kind of behind the beat. After that I tune." [SOS, ibid.]
6. **The chain is deliberately minimal:** Sony C800 → Neve 1081 → Tube-Tech CL-1B; in the box: Auto-Tune (if the artist likes it), Renaissance 6-band EQ (low roll-off, mid add, high-mid dip, top sparkle), Renaissance Compressor, medium hall reverb + ping-pong delay for monitoring. [SOS, ibid.]
7. **Deliver a finished vocal to the mixer:** "When I send the session to the mix engineer, the vocal sound is what it is supposed to be… they don't change it, but just enhance it." [SOS, ibid.]

## The Stacking Framework (documented on MWTM "For You")

MWTM Inside The Track #51 dissects Kuk Harrell's production of Why Don't We's "For You" — the multi-track vocal session contains "five male leads, octave doubles, harmonies, and ad-libs." He teaches "melodies, structure, stacking, tuning, tracking chains, plug-ins" and comping/alignment workflow directly from the session. [MWTM, https://mixwiththemasters.com/videos/kuk-harrell-why-don-t-we-for-you]; [https://www.goaudio.net/mixwiththemasters-kuk-harrell-why-don-t-we-for-you-inside-the-track-51/]

The modern pop stack (from that session's documented architecture):
- **Lead:** one comped performance, tuned with Melodyne-style tools, sung "in the pocket."
- **Doubles:** 1–3 additional full takes, tight-tuned, low in the mix for width.
- **Octave doubles:** the same line an octave down (and/or up) for body — documented as a distinct layer in the "For You" session.
- **Harmonies:** 3–5-part stacks (thirds/sixths), each part possibly double-tracked.
- **Ad-libs:** spontaneous single-word/line interjections ("Yeah," "Ooh") placed between phrases.

**His own stacking principle:** "I have Antares Auto-Tune if the artist likes it… The final version may have Melodyne, if it needs it. For me, Melodyne sounds more natural than Auto-Tune. It's not as jerky and you can dial in the bends and stretch things and make things pitch-perfect." [SOS, https://www.soundonsound.com/people/kuk-harrell-vocal-producer?page=2]

**Rihanna's documented approach to takes:** "Rihanna can cut a hit in two takes" (from his own THR interview), and for "Stay" the industry-documented story is a live, minimal-take performance kept raw. [THR, https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/music/music-news/stitcher-pop-stars-rihannas-vocal-418697/]

## The Rap Layering Framework (documented)

- **The artist comps:** "While we are recording, Thug is his own vocal producer. He will pick the takes, or even the bars, he wants to use. Sometimes I will do the comps, but often he gets on the computer and does the comps himself." [SOS Ski, https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-young-thug-gunna-ski]
- **Timing as production:** "Almost every part gets nudged around, forwards or backwards… for the most part we're nudging vocals back a little bit, because it's the feel they want." [SOS Ski, ibid.]
- **Layers in the template:** Bainz's template carries a lead, a "Yeahs" track (ad-libs), and a "Vox Throw" folder of 15 one-shot effect vocal tracks ("Manipulater x Blackhole," "Down low, monster voice," "Wide Chorused Double," "Wet, wide warm single verb repeat") — "It's how I keep excitement in the vocals and essentially it's what makes these rap vocals radio friendly." [SOS Ski, ibid.]
- **Doubles/ad-libs convention:** modern rap stacks a main lead + 1–2 doubled passes + ad-libs that echo key words at the end of bars — documented as the standard convention in pro tutorials. [Soundtrap, https://blog.soundtrap.com/guide-recording-mixing-rap-vocals/]; [Audient, https://audient.com/tutorial/producing-vocal-layers-ad-libs-and-backing-vocals/]
- **Drake:** 40 records every idea because "an open mic in the studio" is always armed; scratch vocals become part of the record ("I'll sometimes sample them" — the wordless background vocals on 'Hate Sleeping Alone' are Drake's own scratch melodies). [SOS Headlines, https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/noah-40-shebib-recording-drakes-headlines]

## Remote Vocal Production (2021–2025 relevance)

During COVID Kuk built a remote system: a six-hour test session between LA and Atlanta using Facetime + AnyDesk into one Pro Tools session, then "ship[ping] a recording kit with a microphone, headphones, stand, MacBook, and audio interface to wherever the recording artists are" — his business reportedly doubled. [American Songwriter, https://americansongwriter.com/kuk-harrell-discusses-god-rihanna-relationships-between-artist-and-vocal-producer/]

## Home Setup Translation

1. **Comp live like Kuk:** in Audacity, record 3–5 full takes of a section, then pick per-line winners and assemble — don't do 20 takes of one line. Free: Audacity's multi-track takes; better: Reaper's take system / Logic's take folders (true playlists).
2. **Record front-to-back, not section-by-section** — the emotional arc is part of the take (documented Kuk rule).
3. **Cut the lead in ~90 minutes max**; move on even if it's 90% — energy beats perfection.
4. **Stack in this documented order:** lead → 1–3 doubles → octave double → 3–5-part harmonies → ad-libs. Tune everything to the lead after comping.
5. **Timing first, then tuning** (Kuk's fixed edit order) — fix rhythm before pitch or you'll re-tune twice.
6. **Ad-libs are track layers, not accidents:** give them their own track, echo the last word of key bars, keep them lower than the lead (Bainz's "Yeahs" track).
7. **Nudge, don't re-record** — pull slightly-back takes forward, or push melodic rap takes slightly back for feel.
8. **One "vocal FX" folder with 3–5 go-to one-shots** (doubler, pitched-down monster, wide verb throw) — Bainz's Vox Throw concept, free plugins (e.g., stock pitch shift, chorus, reverb).
9. **Deliver finished:** bounce your comped/tuned/stacked vocal as audio before "mixing" — mixers enhance, they don't rebuild.
10. **Home remote kit** (Kuk's model scaled): one good mic + interface + headphones + your DAW; he proves a shipping-kit rig can produce chart vocals — your untreated room is not the blocker.

## What To Steal

1. Comp as you go; never wait until after the session to comp.
2. Record takes front-to-back for emotional continuity.
3. Time budget the session: 90 minutes for a lead.
4. Fixed edit order: timing → tuning → stacking → rough mix.
5. Stack pop vocals in the "For You" architecture: lead + doubles + octave + harmony parts + ad-libs.
6. In rap, let the artist comp; the engineer nudges and cleans.
7. Keep 3–5 one-shot vocal FX in a folder for "special sauce" moments.
8. Tune with Melodyne-style tools for natural results; Auto-Tune-style only when the song calls for the effect.
9. Record everything, even scratch — scratch can become the hook (40).
10. Remote-recording skills are real pro skills now (Kuk's kit model).
