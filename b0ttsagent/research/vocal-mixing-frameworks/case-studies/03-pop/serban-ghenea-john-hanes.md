## Vocal Philosophy
Serban explicitly rejects the "secret recipe" framing and any fixed vocal chain:
> "I really have no bag of tricks. Everything is a different treatment. It depends so much on the genre, and on the specific artist and song. The song dictates what you need to do." [Waves, 2021](https://www.waves.com/waves-grammys-2021-serban-ghenea-taylor-swift)

> "It always comes down to what the artist is looking for, or the producer, and how to get there. And that means a lot of different things for different artists." [Billboard, 2024](https://www.billboard.com/music/awards/serban-alex-ghenea-studio-engineers-grammy-preview-1235589062/)

John Hanes on why there is no plug-in recipe: vocals arrive from "100 different producers, recorded by 100 different engineers, in 100 different studios, on 100 different microphones, 100 different mic-pres. There is no way to plug any standard anything into that and get a specific sound." [Gearspace Q&A](https://gearspace.com/board/interviews/1362525-interview-john-hanes.html)

The repeatable framework is not a plugin chain — it is a **delivery contract + prep discipline + consistency** (same DAW setup for years, mix into the master chain from the start, match the rough, gain staging that lets 2 dB of bus compression do the work).

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
MixStar is fully in-the-box (Pro Tools). Documented framework, per John Hanes (2020 Gearspace Q&A) + Serban interviews:

**1. Intake contract (the MixStar vocal spec — this is the template):**
- Lead vocals must arrive **committed with processing but no effects**: "Leave on your Compressor, EQ, but not your reverb and delay."
- Vocal effects printed **separately** (e.g., "Lead vocal chamber reverb, lead vocal 1/4 delay, BGV 1 chamber reverb, BGV 1 1/4 delay, etc.").
- Unprocessed lead vocal delivered too, "in case we want to undo or re-create something a bit differently."
- BGVs can be blended (stereo doubles, stereo harmony 1, stereo harmony 2); consolidated/committed audio preferred; sessions with only a stereo music track + 3–4 vocal tracks have been turned down as "not mixable." [Gearspace Q&A](https://gearspace.com/board/interviews/1362525-interview-john-hanes.html)

**2. Session consistency:**
- Pro Tools Ultimate, deliberately frozen OS/PT versions ("Currently on High Sierra and ProTools Ultimate 2019.6... We try to do updates as little as possible"). [Gearspace Q&A](https://gearspace.com/board/interviews/1362525-interview-john-hanes.html)
- Serban: "I use ChannelStrip on every session. It's like having a channel on a console that you can do anything you want with." — Metric Halo ChannelStrip. [Mixonline, 2004](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/mixer-serban-ghenea-goes-digital-380846)
- Serban's named Waves staples: "R-Comp, H-Delay, the DeEssers, and the Waves reverbs – I really like the convolution reverbs." [Waves, 2021](https://www.waves.com/waves-grammys-2021-serban-ghenea-taylor-swift)

**3. Mix workflow (Hanes' documented procedure):**
- "I'll usually start with the whole song playing, listening down and making bigger volume moves as I learn the song... turn everything off and start unmuting in the order... Drums, Percussion, FX, Bass, Guitars, Keys... BGV, Lead Vocals."
- Vocal editing done soloed after the first full pass; "then I'll tend to leave everything on as I do the meat of the mixing."
- **Mix into the master chain with limiting on from the beginning**: "If you try to put something on at the end of the process it will undo or change a bunch of the work that you already put in. Mix into the glue!"
- Gain staging: "For a standard mid tempo maybe 1.5 to 2dB on a compressor and another 1 to 2dB at the end of the chain in combined compression and limiting. For a really aggressive song, it could be 3dB on the compressor and 2dB down the chain."
- Match the rough mix first ("You have to start from where they left off"), then improve; blind A/B against the rough. [Gearspace Q&A](https://gearspace.com/board/interviews/1362525-interview-john-hanes.html)

**4. Vocal specifics (documented):**
- No parallel vocal tricks: "Don't really do much parallel track mixing; again we're 'Old School' operatives." Standard effects sends; their standard delay is "not ducked and not fed into a reverb as a general rule."
- Doubles are a production matter: "To sound good and convincing as actual doubles, it should be done in production and recorded that way by the singer or BG vocalist." Synthy doubles: iZotope VocalSynth (used on TXT "9 and Three Quarters" bridge).
- Hip-hop vocals get less cleanup: "Vocals can be mumbly and some noises can be left in; that becomes part of the performance. I'm not going to be cleaning up a lot of headphone bleed, mouth noises, etc on a Hip-Hop track."
- The mix goal Hanes names: "vocal clarity and overall clarity which has a lot to do with midrange, frequency masking." [Gearspace Q&A](https://gearspace.com/board/interviews/1362525-interview-john-hanes.html)
- Observed (not his words): Serban's chart mixes famously use 2-bus clipping as a loudness technique — flagged in SOS Mix Review as a "strange waveform clipping artifact... particularly those of Serban Ghenea." Treat as analysis, not his advice. [SOS, 2011](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/mix-review-october-2011)

**5. Division of labor:** Hanes preps sessions, runs the vocal-prep/cleanup passes, prints mix passes and stems; Serban does the mixing and final calls. "It's just me and my engineer John Hanes, who helps prep everything and print all the mix passes and stems after the mix is done." [Waves, 2021](https://www.waves.com/waves-grammys-2021-serban-ghenea-taylor-swift)

## Recording / Tracking Side
MixStar is a mixing facility — they do not track vocals. Their documented "tracking-side" concern is the intake spec above: what state vocals must be delivered in (committed processing, separate FX prints, dry leads). Serban's own history: McGill sound recording program, then engineer for Teddy Riley/Blackstreet ("No Diggity") — i.e., he came up as a tracking engineer before going ITB. [Waves, 2021](https://www.waves.com/waves-grammys-2021-serban-ghenea-taylor-swift)

## Signature Moves
1. **The prep contract** — forcing producers to deliver committed-but-unFXed vocals and separate effect prints is the single most copied MixStar practice; it keeps the mixer's chain clean and recalls fast. [Gearspace Q&A](https://gearspace.com/board/interviews/1362525-interview-john-hanes.html)
2. **Mix into the finished master bus from bar one** (limiter on, "mix into the glue") — decisions are made against the final loudness context, with tiny, deliberate bus amounts (1.5–2 dB). [Gearspace Q&A](https://gearspace.com/board/interviews/1362525-interview-john-hanes.html)
3. **ChannelStrip-as-console** — one channel-strip plugin on (nearly) everything as the default "console" layer, rather than console emulation on every track. [Mixonline, 2004](https://www.mixonline.com/technology/mixer-serban-ghenea-goes-digital-380846)
4. **Rough-mix matching then blind A/B** — never redesigning a production, only "technical corrections... make the vocals present and understandable." [Gearspace Q&A](https://gearspace.com/board/interviews/1362525-interview-john-hanes.html)
5. **Song-first maximalism** — find "whatever is the special thing about that song... and then try to maximize that." [Waves, 2021](https://www.waves.com/waves-grammys-2021-serban-ghenea-taylor-swift)

## What To Steal
1. **Separate FX from vocals before you mix.** Print/keep your reverb+delay on their own tracks, keep a dry lead — the MixStar intake spec works identically for a bedroom session: you can re-create the wet sound without re-recording.
2. **Mix into your master-bus limiter from the start**, and keep bus compression tiny (1.5–2 dB comp + 1–2 dB limiting as your reference zone, not 6 dB).
3. **Match your rough mix before improving it**; then blind A/B (eyes closed, quick flips) and only keep changes you actually prefer.
4. **Build your vocal mix around clarity of midrange** — Hanes' stated goal is vocal clarity and masking control, not effects.
5. **Don't parallel-process everything** — MixStar explicitly skips parallel vocal tricks; standard sends and solid level rides instead.
6. **Record doubles for real** (layer actual takes) rather than faking width — the documented MixStar position; keep plugins for synthy doubles only.
7. **Use one channel-strip plugin as your default "console"** (free: any decent channel strip — e.g., Reaper's stock JS console-style strip or TDR Nova for tone shaping) before adding specialty processors.
8. **Freeze your toolchain** — don't chase updates mid-project; learn one EQ/comp/de-esser set deeply (Serban has used the same ChannelStrip since ~2001).
9. **Do vocal surgery in a dedicated pass, soloed, after the rough balance** — then mix "into" the cleaned vocal.
10. **Don't over-clean genre-appropriate vocals** — for hip-hop, "some noises can be left in; that becomes part of the performance."
