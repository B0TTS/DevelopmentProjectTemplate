## Vocal Philosophy
Gudwin's role on Bieber is total: "Besides vocal recording, production and mixing, Gudwin is involved in the curation of both Bieber's recent albums: *Justice* and *Changes*." He records "line by line" or takes then comps; "Basically my job is to capture him in the right way, and then I design his vocals and place them in the track." [Mixonline Part 2](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/profiles/josh-gudwin-on-mixing-part-2-bringing-it-for-justin-bieber) [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-justin-bieber-purpose)

His mixing stance: "Most effects and treatments in this session are on Justin's vocals" — the vocal is the mix. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-justin-bieber-purpose)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
From the SOS *Inside Track: Justin Bieber 'Purpose'* walkthrough (2016, the most complete public breakdown of a superstar pop vocal chain) plus MWTM 'Electricity' (#18):

**Tracking inserts (recorded, then kept as the mix inserts):**
1. **UA SSL E-Channel (SSL Channel Strip)** — "I use a lot of UAD SSL E-channel plugins"; on vocals it's the default tone/comp layer.
2. **UAD LA-2A** — smooth leveling compression.
3. **Waves C6 multiband** — "I can get a great overall sound using just the compressor and EQ of that plug-in."
- "Almost all the regular vocal tracks have the UAD SSL Channel, LA2 and the Waves C6 multiband compressor. These three plug-ins work great on his vocals, so I tend to stick with them. Each of the vocal tracks has slightly different settings from them."
- Optional 4th per-track insert: **FabFilter Pro-Q 2** ("a great EQ"). [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-justin-bieber-purpose)

**Lead vocal bus ('$JBU' on "Sorry"; 'JB Buss' on "What Do You Mean?"):**
- UAD **1176**, **Waves De-Esser**, **Manley Massive Passive** EQ, **Metric Halo Channel Strip** (on "Sorry").
- On "What Do You Mean?" the bus is: **FabFilter Pro-DS** de-esser + **Manley EQ** "doing light cuts at 330Hz, 560Hz and 3kHz".
- Sends from the vocal bus: **hall reverb, plate reverb, light quarter-inch delay, light ping-pong delay, Dimension D** (UAD Roland Dimension D). [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-justin-bieber-purpose)

**Backing vocal bus ('JBG1'):** Waves De-Esser → SSL Channel → C6; on one song "boosting highs and cutting lows and doing some heavy compression" plus UAD 33609 and Waves Enigma (Mutron setting). [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-justin-bieber-purpose)

**Vocal FX toolkit (documented on Purpose):** SoundToys Little AlterBoy (formant shift "radio" effect), Auto-Tune used as a pitch-up effect then filtered at 350Hz and below, SoundToys Echo Boy (eighth-note delay), Waves H-Delay (quarter-note), UAD Cooper Time Cube (flutter/ping-pong), SoundToys Primal Tap + Panman (delay pan), MicroShift (pitch/chorus width), printed Bricasti hardware reverbs, printed Henson room reverbs pitched up an octave or two via Elastic Audio X-Form and mixed in very low, Lexicon PCM42 prints. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-justin-bieber-purpose)

**Master bus (Purpose):** UAD SSL E-Channel (compression "mid-attack and auto-release"), Manley Massive Passive, Sonnox Oxford Inflator ("a touch"), FabFilter Pro-L. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-justin-bieber-purpose)

**Tuning (who does what):** "When needed, our vocal tuning is normally done by Chris 'Tek' O'Ryan in Melodyne. I sometimes do it myself, if I have the time do it, in the stand-alone version." — tuning happens in production, before/around the mix, in Melodyne. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-justin-bieber-purpose)

**Template:** MWTM 'Electricity' (#18) is billed as "an overview of his simple setup" plus "his innovative and clever ITB mixing template, revealing the plug-ins, parameters, signal chains and routing," including "Vocal track separation and signal chains in detail, parameter settings." [MWTM](https://mixwiththemasters.com/videos/josh-gudwin-silk-city-ft-dua-lipa-electricity)

## Recording / Tracking Side
- **Mics:** "I use various microphones to record him, like the Sony C800G, Telefunken ELAM 251, Neumann U47 or U67. It depends on the sound we want. It's pretty intuitive. We simply pick a mic, he starts singing and if it isn't right, we try another one."
- **Preamp/comp:** "The mic pre usually is a Neve, and I like to use the Tube-Tech CL1B compressor on the way in, because it's smooth and gives me the control I'm looking for."
- **Tracking method:** sometimes multiple takes comped later, sometimes line-by-line ("Sometimes we will do a few takes and I will comp that later, sometimes I record him line by line").
- Most *Purpose* vocals tracked at Record Plant Studio 3 (LA). [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-justin-bieber-purpose)
- **Stems philosophy (what he wants from producers):** "Every sound should be exported the way it sounds in the session... For a vocal, when you start EQ-ing, you also have to EQ that reverb out and if they're tied together, you're affecting too much stuff." He recommends dry version + wet FX printed separately, and always a reference mix: "I'm not trying to rebuild what was already built. By getting a reference mix, I can build from there and take it to the next level." [Mixonline Part 1](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/josh-gudwin-on-mixing-part-1-juanes-bad-bunny-and-dua-lipa)

## Signature Moves
1. **The fixed "trio"** — SSL E-Channel → LA-2A → Waves C6 on every lead vocal track, with per-song variations; the most famous explicit pop vocal template. [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-justin-bieber-purpose)
2. **Vocal bus as the sonic center** — most session FX live on the vocal (1176, de-esser, Massive Passive, Pro-DS, Manley 330/560/3k cuts); instruments are deliberately left plainer.
3. **Pitch effects as design, tuning as utility** — Melodyne for fixing, Auto-Tune only as an audible effect (pitched-up "radio" vocals, formant shifts via Little AlterBoy). [SOS](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-justin-bieber-purpose)
4. **Printed/real reverbs** — Bricasti hardware prints, Henson room prints pitched up and mixed low; reverbs and delays are mostly treated as separate creative objects, not just sends.
5. **Full-stack ownership** — he records, vocal-produces, comps, tunes and mixes the same project (Bieber, and the "Don't Start Now" handoff from Kirkpatrick), so the mix chain is designed to keep the production's intent. [Mixonline Part 1](https://www.mixonline.com/recording/josh-gudwin-on-mixing-part-1-juanes-bad-bunny-and-dua-lipa)

## What To Steal
1. **Build a default vocal trio and stick to it**: channel-strip (EQ+comp) → optical compressor → multiband. Free equivalents: Reaper stock ReaEQ+ReaComp, Audacity's built-in compressor (gentle) for the optical step — or TDR Nova (free) as the multiband "C6" stand-in.
2. **Put 2–4 bus processors on the lead vocal, not 10 inserts**: 1176-style comp, one de-esser, one big EQ, and a channel strip — the documented Bieber bus.
3. **Copy the Manley "light cuts" EQ move**: cut 330Hz, 560Hz and ~3kHz a little on the vocal bus to de-box and de-honk without touching the track's character.
4. **Tune with Melodyne (or any surgical tuner) during/right after production**, not at the mix; keep Auto-Tune for audible effects only.
5. **Keep reverb + delay prints separate from dry vocals** so "you don't have to EQ the reverb" when you EQ the voice.
6. **Record with compression on the way in** (CL1B-style: smooth optical) so the take already sits — a hardware-free option: track through your interface with a gentle plugin comp on the armed track.
7. **Pick mics by trial**: have 2–3 very different mics (condenser/ribbon/dynamic) and swap until the singer sounds right — Gudwin's documented method.
8. **Design FX around the vocal**: put most of your creative effects on the voice (formant shifts, pitched delays, doubling) and keep instruments plain.
9. **Export stems "the way they sound in the session"** — commit your FX when sending anything out; don't make the next engineer rebuild your sound.
10. **Mix into a light master bus chain** (SSL-style comp, inflator/saturator, limiter) so loudness decisions are made in context.
