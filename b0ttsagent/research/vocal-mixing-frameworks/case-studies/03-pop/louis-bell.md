## Vocal Philosophy
Bell's stated goal for Post Malone: "I want his voice to sound tight and futuristic and still have his wideness. Most of all, I want his soul to shine through. His voice is so unique, you don't want to dress that up too much. All I do is add the right reverb, because he likes a lot of reverb... it makes his vocals feel anthemic and arena-esque." [AudioTechnology](https://audiotechnology.net/issues/issue-58/hit-man)

He also frames vocal sound as a psychology tool: "Having the vocalist sounding the way they want to sound – where they feel immortal and untouchable – invites the chance of having a much more creative openness in the studio." [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/gear/louis-bell-cradle-the-spirit-vocal-chain-plugin/)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
From the AudioTechnology "Hit Man" feature (2018) + UA blog interview (2018) + The Spirit plugin reveal (2022):

**Tracking:**
- **Sony C800G** into the **Apollo Twin's built-in preamp** — "I start with the Sony C800 microphone, which is what I use for most vocalists I work with, unless they request their own mic... I'm just using the preamp that's built into the Twin. I want to make sure that I can change the color of the vocal sound after the take." [UA](https://www.uaudio.com/blogs/ua/louis-bell-nailing-tone-for-post-malone)

**Plug-in vocal chain (order as named):**
1. **UAD 1176** — "the 1176 Compressor is a go-to for me, because it gives the vocals a certain attack and a roundness, along with a nice warmth, that just immediately makes my job easier." [UA](https://www.uaudio.com/blogs/ua/louis-bell-nailing-tone-for-post-malone) / "The UAD 1176, which really warms things up and makes them nice and loud." [AudioTechnology](https://audiotechnology.net/issues/issue-58/hit-man)
2. **Waves CLA Vocals** — "a kind of one-stop shop that allows you to do many things easily. If you want more 'bite', it instantly allows you to manipulate the vocal in that way." [AudioTechnology](https://audiotechnology.net/issues/issue-58/hit-man)
3. (UA interview adds) **Neve 1073** or **API 550A/550B** EQ in the vocal chain. [UA](https://www.uaudio.com/blogs/ua/louis-bell-nailing-tone-for-post-malone)
4. **Waves RVerb** — "My favourite reverb... which gives you a classic sound." [AudioTechnology](https://audiotechnology.net/issues/issue-58/hit-man)
- **Ad-libs:** UAD Brigade Chorus — "I tweak the input control to give the vocals a 'not your typical,' in the background sound... something to differentiate them from the lead vocal." [UA](https://www.uaudio.com/blogs/ua/louis-bell-nailing-tone-for-post-malone)
- **Brightness/level weapon:** Sonnox Oxford Inflator — "Throw that on vocals and you can press them to the point where they have a brightness and a volume that you can't get with any other effect." [UA](https://www.uaudio.com/blogs/ua/louis-bell-nailing-tone-for-post-malone)

**Tuning (the documented standard):**
"I do vocal tuning in Melodyne. After that I have Antares AutoTune on, very lightly, just to make sure everything is locked in and tight... I throw AutoTune on specific lines as an effect, like a flanger or slap delay. It's not because he can't sing the part in tune, it's because it sounds cool. People also think that the shaking in his voice, like in Rockstar, is AutoTune, but it's how he sings." [AudioTechnology](https://audiotechnology.net/issues/issue-58/hit-man)

**The Spirit (Cradle, 2022)** — his signature chain distilled into one plugin: "an EQ, two reverbs, doubler effects, a gain rider for tonal consistency and a Character effect with compression and focus controls... zero latency." [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/gear/louis-bell-cradle-the-spirit-vocal-chain-plugin/)

## Recording / Tracking Side
- **Vocal production is his job alone**: "I spend a lot of time listening to every single vocal take, comping, editing and tuning the vocals. I don't know whether it's my OCD side, but I feel that if I don't listen to everything I'm going to miss something. Unfortunately, you can't hire people to have your taste, so I have to do it all myself." [AudioTechnology](https://audiotechnology.net/issues/issue-58/hit-man)
- **Beat/artist workflows:** builds per-artist sound templates in FL Studio ("I'll put together a sound template in Fruity Loops, specific to an artist or song idea. It means I have a collection of sounds that I know fit together"); keeps a 20TB organized sound library. [AudioTechnology](https://audiotechnology.net/issues/issue-58/hit-man)
- **Wetness control:** Post likes wet anthemic vocals; "sometimes I'll dry them up a little before sending them to Manny [Marroquin] for the final mix" — i.e., the producer decides the reverb amount the mixer receives. [AudioTechnology](https://audiotechnology.net/issues/issue-58/hit-man)

## Signature Moves
1. **Melodyne-first, Auto-Tune-second tuning stack** — surgical Melodyne pass, then "very light" Auto-Tune as a lock, then Auto-Tune as an explicit effect on chosen lines (the modern pop/hip-hop standard, stated explicitly). [AudioTechnology](https://audiotechnology.net/issues/issue-58/hit-man)
2. **Minimal gear, maximum consistency** — C800G + Apollo Twin built-in pre (no outboard), 1176 + CLA Vocals as the core; the whole chain fits in a hotel room. [UA](https://www.uaudio.com/blogs/ua/louis-bell-nailing-tone-for-post-malone)
3. **"Everything is committed before the mixer"** — comps, edits, tuning, and reverb decisions are made in production; the mixer (Manny Marroquin) receives a finished vocal picture.
4. **Ad-lib flavor separation** — chorus/doubler effects (Brigade) reserved for ad-libs so they sit "in the background" with identity. [UA](https://www.uaudio.com/blogs/ua/louis-bell-nailing-tone-for-post-malone)
5. **Artist-specific templates** — a curated sound palette per artist so every session starts from a proven set. [AudioTechnology](https://audiotechnology.net/issues/issue-58/hit-man)

## What To Steal
1. **Melodyne (or a surgical tuner like the free-ish options — e.g., Reaper's ReaTune, or Melodyne Essential's trial tier) for real tuning; Auto-Tune only as a light lock and/or an effect.** This is the modern pop standard across Bell, Kirkpatrick and Gudwin.
2. **Comp first, "one-stop" EQ second** — 1176-style attack/warmth then a CLA-Vocals-style multi-button channel EQ (free equivalents: any 1176 emulation freeware, e.g., Analog Obsession's FETish, plus ReaEQ).
3. **Record with an $800–$1,500-class large-diaphragm condenser into a clean interface pre** — the C800G is the famous choice, but Bell's actual lesson is: the preamp doesn't need to be exotic.
4. **Be the one who comps and tunes** — do it yourself, listen to every take; "you can't hire people to have your taste."
5. **Add Inflator-style harmonic brightness** when the vocal needs to cut (free: saturation/soft-clip plugins like Free Clip, or a subtle Waveshaper in Audacity).
6. **Decide the reverb amount in production** — record/mix vocals wetter or drier deliberately, then let the final mixer just balance.
7. **Give ad-libs their own color** (chorus/flanger/doubler) so they read as background texture.
8. **Keep a per-project sound palette** — a small set of go-to vocal sounds you can audition fast.
9. **A "gain rider" step** — The Spirit includes one; Waves Vocal Rider or manual automation keeps every word audible.
10. **Don't chase outboard** — Bell's entire documented chain runs on an interface + plugins; consistency and taste do the work.
