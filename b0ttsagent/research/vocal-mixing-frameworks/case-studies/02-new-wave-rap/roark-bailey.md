## Vocal Philosophy
- **The engineer hears the whole record, not just the vocal:** "I'm big on sending stuff to mastering... I think of dynamics. I think of where the final piece is going to go because you want to be competitive when it comes on in a playlist or when it comes on in a car." [YouTube — Roark Bailey On Mixing With Mastering In Mind](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0aardv_MWWA)
- **Vocal production is a craft you learn in the room:** his crash course was watching vocal producers coach Monica: "That's how you help people do the little detail work on the songs. This is how you help 'em get to the final product when it comes to nailing those ad-libs and those runs." [Revolt](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2022-12-29/263344/studio-sessions-roark-bailey-interview)
- **Preparation beats takes:** on Monica: "She doesn't sing with the tune on. She already got the lyrics memorized. She's not doing 40 takes, she's doing five takes, and all five takes are perfect." [Revolt](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2022-12-29/263344/studio-sessions-roark-bailey-interview)
- **The mixer leaves fingerprints:** on "Sex In The Porsche": "The way the kick and the 808 are balanced next to each other is because of me... You can be sure I influenced anything you've heard that I've worked on." [Revolt](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2022-12-29/263344/studio-sessions-roark-bailey-interview)

## What To Steal
1. **Mix the low end first** — get kick and 808 (or any bass) to sit as one musical gesture before touching the vocal; the vocal then carves around a locked foundation.
2. **Leave headroom for "mastering"** — even if you self-master: mix with dynamics, then do loudness in a separate final step. In Audacity: mix at moderate levels, then use the limiter/export stage for loudness.
3. **Learn to vocal-produce** — coaching takes, ad-libs, and runs in the room is a skill separate from mixing; direct the performance, don't just fix it later.
4. **Five great takes beat forty takes** — rehearsal/preparedness beats volume of takes.
5. **Control the room** — lights, TV off, phones away: the environment shapes the vocal performance as much as the chain.
6. **Have a "fingerprint" element** — pick one balance decision (yours: vocal-above-beat, or a certain low-end relationship) and make it consistent across everything you mix.
