## Vocal Philosophy
- **Genre-blending is the product:** "I think it's the fact that I blend a lot of different genres in my music... I like to push an artist to try different things." [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/bnyx-best-hip-hop-producer-2023-interview)
- **Beats are tested against real vocals:** "I used to put Drake a cappella on my beat to see if they were hard." (his own tweet, quoted in the Complex interview) [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/bnyx-best-hip-hop-producer-2023-interview)
- **Serve the artist's lane, not your own:** for AftërLyfe his goal was beats "in his world but something different" because fan feedback said "He's always doing the same thing." [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/bnyx-best-hip-hop-producer-2023-interview)

- **Workflow with Yeat:** beat sent → Yeat records the song the next day ("we sent the beat, he does the song the next day, and I'm in the house screaming"). This confirms Yeat's self-recording/self-mixing pipeline operates on 24-hour turnaround from beat delivery. [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/bnyx-best-hip-hop-producer-2023-interview)
- **The rage blueprint is production-side:** the Yeat/BNYX sound ("rage," also pushed by Ken Carson, Destroy Lonely, Playboi Carti — [HotNewHipHop](https://www.hotnewhiphop.com/930263-yeat-bnyx-im-yeat-stream)) is built from hyper-distorted, mid/high-frequency-dense instrumentals that leave a narrow but loud slot for the vocal. Fan/industry reconstruction of the vocal side (fast hard-tune + saturation + loud dry placement) is C-tier; no primary documentation exists.
- **Tour/album arrangement work:** for Drake's *For All the Dogs* tour he re-arranged existing songs (added horns/strings, "make it sound cooler"), i.e., the producer-engineer line is fluid at the top level. [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/bnyx-best-hip-hop-producer-2023-interview)

## Signature Moves
1. **Reference-acapella beat testing** — dropping a known artist's a cappella on a new beat to judge whether it's actually hard. [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/bnyx-best-hip-hop-producer-2023-interview)
2. **"In his world but something different"** — reading fan forums (Reddit fan pages, group chats) and deliberately sending the artist the beat they don't expect, within their lane. [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/bnyx-best-hip-hop-producer-2023-interview)
3. **Same-day artist turnaround** — beats delivered into a workflow where the artist records the very next day, keeping the emotional temperature of the demo. [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/bnyx-best-hip-hop-producer-2023-interview)
4. **Genre collision in one record** — "blend of different genres" as the stated production identity (rock/guitar-led "Nun I'd Change" inside a rage album). [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/bnyx-best-hip-hop-producer-2023-interview)

## What To Steal
1. **The a-cappella test** — before you commit to a beat, drop any great a cappella over it (in Audacity: import vocal stem + beat, mute nothing, just listen). If the beat makes a famous voice sound hard, it's a keeper; if it muddies, fix the beat.
2. **Day-one recording** — record the vocal the same day you pick the beat; the demo energy is the product (Yeat recorded BNYX beats within 24 hours).
3. **Different-within-the-lane** — one deliberately "wrong" beat per session (guitar-driven, weird tempo) keeps the artist's palette fresh.
4. **Read the fan forums about your own sound** — feedback ("always doing the same thing") became a creative directive.
5. **Genre-blend as strategy** — pull elements from outside rap (rock, EDM, soundtracks) into the instrumental bed; the vocal then has a fresh surface to sit on.
