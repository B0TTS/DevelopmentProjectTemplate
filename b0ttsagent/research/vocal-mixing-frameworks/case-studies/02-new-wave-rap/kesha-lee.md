## Vocal Philosophy
- "We always go for a warm, full, loud, in-your-face vocal sound, also because we like the vocals to be louder than the beat. I always turn the beat down 1-2 dB." [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
- Auto-Tune is not a secret weapon — it's the instrument, always first in the chain, and Uzi performs THROUGH it: "Uzi hears his Auto-Tuned vocals in his headphones while singing." [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
- Atlanta workflow beats technical preciousness: "You can't be too concerned and technical about signal chains and sound customisation... they just want to get their ideas down." [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
- Mix where the audience listens: "most people listen to music on their laptops or phones using earbuds, so why not mix how they are going to hear it?" — she mixes on $99 Sennheiser HD280s and a 3-year-old MacBook Pro [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
From her Sound on Sound *Inside Track* breakdown of the *Luv Is Rage 2* session "How To Talk" (33 tracks: 2-track beat + 14 beat tracks + 10 vocal tracks + 2 vocal aux + 3 FX aux + mix print + master) [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert):

**Per-vocal-track insert chain (fixed template):**
1. **Antares Auto-Tune — first plug-in on every vocal track, no exceptions.** Default retune 20 → now 12 to 5: "lately he has been like: 'Give me more Auto-Tune!' so now we have the Retune Speed set to anywhere from 12 to 5."
2. **Waves De-Esser** (the "D")
3. **Avid EQ3 7-band** (the "Q")
4. **Waves C1 gate** (the "1")
5. Sends to delay + reverb auxes on every vocal track.

**FX auxes:**
- **Delay aux:** Avid Mod Delay II set to **half notes, feedback 43%** → Waves Renaissance Reverberator, "Hall 1" reverb with **highs cut on the reverb EQ** → **Waves S1 stereo imager** (widens the echo).
- **Reverb aux:** Waves Renaissance Reverberator.

**Vocal bus (the "vocal aux" — where the sound is actually made):**
1. Waves De-Esser acting around **4230 Hz**
2. Avid EQ3 7-band: **high-pass at 96.4 Hz**, dipping muddiness at **200 and 500 Hz**, adding high end at **6.52 kHz** (a trick she learned from Seth Firkins on Future's voice)
3. Waves Renaissance Compressor — "keep the dynamics in check"
4. Waves SSL E-Channel — "more for colour and character"
5. Waves CLA-3A — "more for the sound" (LA-2A-style leveling)
6. RN Digital Detailer — "made Uzi's vocals sound fuller and wider"
7. **Nomad Factory MCL-2269 limiter & compressor** — "for the sound and for more volume"

**Beat 2-track chain (fixed):** Avid EQ3 7-band (turn down the track) → Waves Vitamin Sonic Enhancer ("bring out the highs a little bit") → UAD Precision EQ (added by Don Cannon).

**Master:** no plug-ins; master fader pulled down **-7 to -9 dB** before mastering so the mastering engineer has room.

**Fixed vs song-specific:** The Auto-Tune→De-Esser→EQ3→C1 per-track chain, the aux layout, and the vocal-bus compressor order are her repeatable template. Retune speed (5–12), de-ess frequency, and EQ cuts move per song/artist. Session convention: 2-track beat color-coded purple/green, "the hook is always green" so Uzi can see where the hook ends.

## Recording / Tracking Side
- **Atlanta standard chain:** "The standard for Atlanta seems to be to use Yamaha NS10s or Augspurger monitors, and the recording signal chain consists of an Avalon VT-737sp mic pre and a Tube-Tech CL 1B compressor. The microphone is normally a Neumann U87... but Uzi likes the Neumann TLM103... which we use most of the time." Headphones: Sennheiser HD280s. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
- **Line-by-line punch-ins:** "He doesn't do like a whole verse, instead he'll do things line by line, and I'll be punching in." [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
- **Drops with latch automation:** Uzi presses mute on a latched track to drop the beat in and out himself; she cleans up the placement later so drops land on the one. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
- **Lo-fi on purpose:** the album intro was a phone voice-note played through the booth speaker and re-recorded through the mic ("It sounded really telephone-y, which we wanted"), with highs tamed via Waves OneKnob. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
- **2-track MP3 workflow:** producers send two-track MP3s; track-outs only pulled if Uzi loves the song. She mixes to the 2-track and treats the beat as a finished object. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
- **Organization framework:** per-day date/time folders, song name + producer + Uzi's name in filenames, tempo and key added to file names ("they often ask me for that"); monthly triage into finished/unfinished/collabs. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
- Rough-mixes during tracking because "he'd get used to hearing it a certain way" — change carefully after the artist falls in love with the rough. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)

## Signature Moves
1. **Auto-Tune as a performance instrument** — tuned signal fed to the artist's headphones while singing; retune cranked from 20 down toward 5 as an aesthetic dial, not a fix. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
2. **"Vocals louder than the beat"** — beat ducked 1-2 dB under the voice; warm/full/loud/in-your-face as the house sound. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
3. **Half-note delay with the S1 widener + highs-cut hall** — wide, dark echo as the main vocal space, not reverb-on-vocal. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
4. **Mixing on $99 headphones on purpose** — matches where fans actually listen; studio speakers only as a check. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)
5. **The artist-driven drop (latch automation)** — the rapper becomes the DJ of his own beat for drops/ad-lib sections. [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-lil-uzi-vert)

## What To Steal
1. **Auto-Tune FIRST, before any EQ/compression, and monitor through it while recording** — the tuned sound is part of the performance, not a fix after the fact. Free: Graillon 2 Free or MAutoPitch; stock: any pitch plugin with adjustable retune; start ~20ms retune, push toward 5-10ms when you want the effect audible.
2. **"Vocals louder than the beat"** — in your Audacity session, drop the MP3 beat by 1-2 dB under the vocal rather than pushing the vocal into clipping.
3. **The bus chain order:** de-ess (~4kHz) → high-pass (~100Hz) → cut mud (200/500Hz) → presence boost (~6.5kHz) → gentle leveling comp → character comp → limiter for loudness. Free/stock: any stock parametric EQ + a stock compressor in series + a limiter; free LA-2A-style: Analog Obsession "LALA"; free detail/air: Slate "Fresh Air".
4. **Half-note delay instead of wet reverb** — echo at 43% feedback, dark (highs cut), slightly widened, with a hall reverb barely underneath. Free: stock delay at dotted eighth/half-note, stock reverb with highs cut, free "Wider" by Polyverse on the delay send.
5. **Headphone mixing on purpose** — mix on what your audience uses; check speakers after, don't rely on them.
6. **No master-bus processing, leave -7 to -9 dB headroom** — the loudness war happens at mastering; keep your mix dynamic.
7. **Color-code + name everything** (hook always green; date/time folders; tempo+key in the filename) — speed of recall is part of the job.
8. **Record line-by-line with punch-ins** for rappers — modern rap vocals are assembled, not sung top-to-bottom; comp aggressively.
9. **Let the artist perform the drops** (mute automation) and clean up the timing afterward.
10. **Don't over-polish the raw take** — a deliberately "telephone-y" intro recorded through a phone speaker into the mic was kept on purpose. Intentional lo-fi beats accidental lo-fi.
