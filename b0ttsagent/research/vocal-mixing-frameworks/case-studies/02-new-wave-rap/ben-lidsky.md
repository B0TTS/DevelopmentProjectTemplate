## What To Steal
1. **One team, repeated** — the Opium sound is consistency of personnel: same recording engineer, same mastering engineer, same producer roster. For you: build a template session you reuse for every song (same chain, same auxes), so your records sound like one catalog.
2. **Split the roles even when you're one person** — record with the mix in mind, but do a separate "mix pass" later and a separate "master pass" (or send to an online masterer); three distinct stages, not one blob.
3. **Hard-tune as the default** — per the (C-tier) reconstruction, the Opium vocal default is obvious pitch correction: fast retune, minimal humanize. Free: MAutoPitch with fast response; Graillon 2 Free.
4. **Vocal energy comes from the take** — shout-adjacent delivery captured cleanly beats a polished delivery sung quietly; the aggression is a recording decision first.
5. **Master everything at the end by one person** — Colin Leonard masters every Opium release [SING Mastering](https://www.singmastering.com/); consistency of loudness across your songs comes from one mastering step, not per-song tweaking.
