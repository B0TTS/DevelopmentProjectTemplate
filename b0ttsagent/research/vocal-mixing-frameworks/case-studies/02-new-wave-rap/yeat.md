# Yeat (Noah Olivier Smith)
- **Role:** artist / vocalist / self-recording engineer / self-mixing engineer / self-producer (executive producer of his own records; records, engineers and mixes his own vocals)
- **Verified credits:** *Up 2 Më* (2021, breakout), *AftërLyfe* (2023, #5 Billboard 200), *2093* (2024, #2 debut), *LYFESTYLE* (2024) [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview); "Gët Busy" (2021, viral/radio breakthrough), "Rich Minion" for *Minions: The Rise of Gru* (2022) [032c](https://magazine.032c.com/magazine/yeat-american-truths); "IDGAF" with Drake (2023), most-streamed track on *For All the Dogs* [032c](https://magazine.032c.com/magazine/yeat-american-truths)
- **Recent mainstream relevance:** *2093* and *LYFESTYLE* (2024) — both top-10 Billboard debuts; Drake co-sign + feature; Charli XCX-adjacent circuit; the defining voice of the "second wave of SoundCloud"/rage wave 2021-2026 [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview), [032c](https://magazine.032c.com/magazine/yeat-american-truths)
- **Evidence base:** A: 2 (The FADER feature with manager + producer; 032c interview). B: 1 (Complex 2023 BNYX interview corroborating workflow). C: 3 (vocalpresets.com, cedarsoundstudios.com, bchillmix.com — commercial preset blogs, community reconstruction, clearly labeled below as UNVERIFIED).

## Vocal Philosophy
- Self-contained: "Noah's music-making process is so insulated because he makes music only by himself... Even when I'm with him, he'll be like, 'Okay, I'm gonna go make music now, you guys can leave.'" — manager Zack Bia [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)
- Vocals are part of the beat: "With the amount of ad-libs and soundscaping that I do, there are sometimes 90 vocal tracks. Sometimes you can't tell it's my voice because it sounds more like a beat." [032c](https://magazine.032c.com/magazine/yeat-american-truths)
- Multiple vocal personas per record: "sometimes you sound like 16 different people on one record" — he performed as aliases Kranky Kranky and Luh Geeky on *AftërLyfe*: "It's just how I was feeling at the time." [032c](https://magazine.032c.com/magazine/yeat-american-truths)
- No second-guessing: "I never touch anything after it's done." [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
**DOCUMENTED FACT: Yeat records, engineers and mixes his own vocals.** "Of course, plenty of that continuity is also thanks to Yeat himself, who not only records, engineers, and mixes his own vocals, but frequently also adds that final Wow! factor the instrumentals are missing." [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview). His own words: "I engineer myself and record myself, but I never officially learned how to make beats or produce. To this day, I still don't know what I'm really doing." [032c](https://magazine.032c.com/magazine/yeat-american-truths)

**What is documented about the chain:**
1. **DAW:** FL Studio on the producer side — beats "graduated from FL Studio to DropBox, batched out in packs of 100, 200, 400" [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview). His own sketch tool: **GarageBand** — "If I really like the adlib I was singing — I don't even know how to make beats — I'll pull up a GarageBand synth and then turn that into what I was singing." [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)
2. **Recording location:** a bedside MacBook — manager Zack Bia "will occasionally wake up at 7 in the morning... to FaceTime calls from the Portland rapper, who's just had an idea in a dream and recorded it on his bedside MacBook. Songs are recorded quickly and instinctually." [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)
3. **Vocal stack architecture:** "there are sometimes **90 vocal tracks**" — ad-libs and soundscaping treated as instrumentation ("Sometimes you can't tell it's my voice because it sounds more like a beat"). [032c](https://magazine.032c.com/magazine/yeat-american-truths)
4. **Vocals as production contributions:** he adds harmonies/backing melodies to half-finished songs, turns his own ad-lib melodies into GarageBand synths, and adds texture parts ("On some songs, if I told you I produced this part, you'd be like 'What do you mean?'... You don't even notice it, but it actually makes it feel different"). [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)

**UNVERIFIED / community reconstruction (C-tier — fan-reverse-engineered, no primary source):** No one has published Yeat's actual plugin settings. The widely-circulated "rage vocal formula" attributed to him — heavy, audible pitch-lock ("retune speed very fast/0-10ms so you can hear the pitch snapping"), heavy saturation/distortion on the vocal bus, dry-in-your-face vocal level above blown-out beats, layered doubles — is reconstructed by ear by preset-shop blogs and YouTubers. Representative (labeled as reconstruction, NOT sourced from Yeat): [vocalpresets.com](https://vocalpresets.com/blog/yeat-vocal-chain), [cedarsoundstudios.com](https://www.cedarsoundstudios.com/blogs/news/how-to-make-your-vocals-sound-like-yeat), [rysupaudio.com](https://rysupaudio.com/blogs/news/how-to-sound-like-yeat-2026). Treat any specific numbers there as unverified community guesswork.

## Recording / Tracking Side
- **Records alone, at home, on a laptop:** "Songs are recorded quickly and instinctually, then set aside." [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)
- **The 2093 model (in-person) vs the LYFESTYLE model (isolated):** for *2093* "We had a vineyard in Oregon and we invited all the producers there so they could come see things through your eyes"; for *LYFESTYLE* he went back to recording alone at home. [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)
- **Freestyle, not written:** "I don't write anything, everything just comes off the top." [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)
- **Massive beat pipeline:** 4,800 beats for *LYFESTYLE* in 8 months (~20/day), curated by exec producer Synthetic and the Hologram collective; Yeat requests stems to rework instrumentals himself when beats aren't finished. [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)
- **Turns songs around instantly:** BNYX: "we sent the beat, he does the song the next day, and I'm in the house screaming." [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/j-mckinney/bnyx-best-hip-hop-producer-2023-interview)
- **Deliberate isolation from other rap:** "The only music I listen to is old-ass music... If you sit there listening to other rappers all day, you start to sound like them, even subconsciously." [032c](https://magazine.032c.com/magazine/yeat-american-truths)

## Signature Moves
1. **Ad-libs as sound design** — dozens of vocal layers stacked until the voice becomes a texture ("sometimes 90 vocal tracks... sounds more like a beat"). [032c](https://magazine.032c.com/magazine/yeat-american-truths)
2. **Vocal personas as tracks** — Kranky Kranky / Luh Geeky on *AftërLyfe*; "I can be country, I can do rap, pop, EDM... it always sounds like me." [032c](https://magazine.032c.com/magazine/yeat-american-truths)
3. **Turning sung ad-libs into synths** (GarageBand) — his vocal melody becomes the beat's lead line. [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)
4. **Reworking producer stems himself** — he requests stems to add/alter parts rather than sending notes. [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)
5. **The "final Wow!" contribution** — a small vocal/production addition that takes a 95% beat to 100% and puts it "way more in his pocket" (Zack Bia). [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)

## What To Steal
1. **Be your own engineer** — the most-streamed rage artist of 2021-2026 records, engineers and mixes his own vocals on a laptop. You don't need a studio to have the "real" chain; you need reps and taste.
2. **Stack vocals as instrumentation** — layer ad-libs, doubles, and background "scraps" until the vocal bed has its own texture; mix the layers as a pad, not as a lead with background.
3. **Audible pitch-lock as style** — fast retune speed is a creative choice in this lane, not an accident (reconstruction, but consistent with the documented "mutated vocals" description [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)). Free: MAutoPitch / Graillon 2 with fast retune; or stock pitch correction with speed at minimum.
4. **Sketch on anything (GarageBand/BandLab), finish in your main DAW** — his documented pipeline is MacBook + GarageBand for ideas → FL Studio session with stems.
5. **Record the idea immediately** — dream/walk-in-the-door takes become records; "recorded it on his bedside MacBook" (Zack Bia). [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2024/10/17/yeat-lyfestyle-album-synthetic-producer-interview)
6. **Don't polish forever** — "I never touch anything after it's done." Speed of completion is a creative value.
7. **Isolate from your influences while making music** — no other rappers in the headphones; freshness comes from not imitating.
8. **Use your own ad-lib melodies as song elements** — hum a hook, turn it into a synth line, write the beat around it.
9. **Reject the "one vocal sound"** — switch personas per track (different tuning, delivery, effects) as a feature, not a bug.
