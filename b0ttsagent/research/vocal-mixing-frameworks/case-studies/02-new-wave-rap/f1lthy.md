# F1lthy (Richard Ortiz)
- **Role:** producer / beatmaker — co-founder of Working on Dying (Philadelphia); member of Playboi Carti's Opium label; the primary architect of the blown-out "rage" beat sound and the producer behind much of Carti's hardest vocal settings
- **Verified credits:** *Whole Lotta Red* (2020, #1 Billboard 200) — "Stop Breathing," "Rockstar Made," "New Tank," "King Vamp" etc. [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys) / [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F1lthy); *MUSIC* (2025, #1) and *ALL RED* producer credits [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/album/all-red-mw0004377310); Bladee's *Cold Visions* (2024) — produced a significant portion [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys); Lil Yachty "Poland" (2022, viral hit) [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys); *Eternal Atake* era work with Uzi and *WLR* credits for Working on Dying [Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/working-on-dying-interview/); 2021 collaborative album with LUCKI [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)
- **Recent mainstream relevance:** *MUSIC* (2025, #1, 298k units [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/music/chart-beat/playboi-carti-music-album-number-one-billboard-200-chart-1235929403/)); *Cold Visions* (2024); Yeat, Drake, Ken Carson, Destroy Lonely, LAZER DIM 700, OsamaSon, Nettspend lineage [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)
- **Evidence base:** A: 2 (Highsnobiety WOD interview; FADER WOD feature). B: 2 (severancetime Substack analytical essay with producer corroboration; Wikipedia). C: 1 (fan tutorials — labeled).

## Vocal Philosophy
- **His philosophy is anti-polish, and it's documented as the context Carti's vocals sit in:** "Heavily distorted, with the levels generally pushed as far as they will go. The last thing he does is over-produce." [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)
- **The beat is not a bed for the vocal — it's a wall the vocal must survive:** sub-bass distortion and smeared buses "unmoored" the figure/ground relationship; Carti responds with "baby talk in his adlibs... wordless, libidinal sound-assertions" that read as texture as much as language. [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)
- **DIY is the point:** inspired by SpaceGhostPurrp's *Blackland Radio 66.6* — "I could make that" — and self-taught via YouTube. [Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/working-on-dying-interview/) / [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2018/11/27/working-on-dying-lil-uzi-vert-interview)

## The Vocal Chain (as documented)
**Honesty note:** F1lthy is a beat producer — his documented craft is the instrumental (and how vocals get crushed INTO it), not a vocal insert chain. No vocal-chain documentation from him exists. What IS documented:

**The beat-side framework (which defines how vocals must sit):**
1. **Everything into one bus, one set of effect parameters** — "multiple, distinct inputs are consolidated into a single output track, where they are subjected to a single set of effect parameters" — snare, hats, synths and 808s channeled into the same bus. [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)
2. **Soft-clipping the whole mix:** per producer FRIDGE (peer corroboration via the essay): F1lthy "has tracks where he subjects every input to an overarching soft clipping effect." [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)
3. **Clipping as the aesthetic:** "his characteristic sound is specifically achieved by pushing skin-tingling levels of clipping into his productions such that Oasis sounds relatively tame in comparison." [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)
4. **DAW:** FL Studio ("FruityLoops") since 2012, learned with his brother Oogie Mane — "After half a year of working with FruityLoops, he taught Oogie the basic principles of the program." [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2018/11/27/working-on-dying-lil-uzi-vert-interview)
5. **MP3 as instrument:** works with the two-track/finished file as a sound-shaping target rather than pristine stems. [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)

**Consequence for vocals (documented behavior, not his own instruction):** the vocal must sit ON TOP of a mix that is already near-clipping — hence the rage vocal convention (fast hard-tune, saturation, loud dry level) that Carti/Yeat/Ken Carson employ. The chain details of those vocals are NOT documented by F1lthy — fan reverse-engineering (C-tier) claims: hard Auto-Tune (fast retune), doubled takes, distortion parallel to the dry vocal, and the lead vocal riding hotter than the beat. Representative reconstruction: [vocalpresets.com](https://vocalpresets.com/blog/yeat-vocal-chain) — UNVERIFIED.

## Recording / Tracking Side
- **No studio, no mentors — a basement and one shared computer:** "They all began making music in the basement of F1LTHY and Oogie's mom's house... the one computer they shared between the five of them, with a propane heater and cases of Yuengling." [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2018/11/27/working-on-dying-lil-uzi-vert-interview)
- **Learned by committee:** "They built off each other's growing knowledge and ideas together as a group. 'Nothing was forced,' F1LTHY says. 'This was just regular friendship and we all ended up being good at what we do.'" [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2018/11/27/working-on-dying-lil-uzi-vert-interview)
- **Networking as craft:** "I had to be around and show face. People gotta see you're a real person — you can't just be an internet nerd sending shit over." [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2018/11/27/working-on-dying-lil-uzi-vert-interview)
- **The "tread" sound (collective term, coined by collaborator BOOTYCHAAAIN):** "slowed-down samples and melodies and high-tempo, often distorted, percussion." [FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2018/11/27/working-on-dying-lil-uzi-vert-interview)

## Signature Moves
1. **Bus-everything-then-clip-everything** — all inputs through a shared bus with one soft-clip stage; distortion becomes the mix glue. [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)
2. **"MP3 as instrument"** — treating the compressed, damaged file as a sound source rather than fighting it; anti-fidelity as artistic choice. [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)
3. **Sub-bass as the anchor** — distorted 808s that dissolve instrument boundaries, forcing the vocal to live above the fray. [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)
4. **Radical simplicity** — "any bedroom producer with basic competence will be able to recreate the basic MIDI patterns of his productions in five minutes"; the sound is in the treatment, not the notes. [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)
5. **Blowing up the "loudness wars" frame** — pushing clipping into popular music on purpose, breaking the professional-mix contract (context for why vocals on these records sound "wrong" on purpose). [severancetime Substack](https://severancetime.substack.com/p/mp3-as-instrument-notes-on-f1lthys)

## What To Steal
1. **One bus, one clip** — route your whole instrumental (or your vocal stack) to a single bus and push one soft-clip/saturator stage until it melts together. Free: Softube Saturation Knob (free), or stock compressor in "clip" mode; in Audacity, apply hard/soft limiting on the beat bus.
2. **Embrace the damaged file** — you don't need stems or high-fidelity sources; work WITH the MP3's artifacts. (Directly applicable to the user's MP3-instrumental workflow.)
3. **Simpler patterns, heavier treatment** — don't over-produce; spend the effort on texture and distortion character.
4. **Distorted low end is the canvas** — if your beat's bass is blown out, the vocal must cut via presence (2-6kHz) and loud dry level, not by "sitting in a pocket."
5. **"I could make that" is a legitimate starting method** — pick a sound you love, copy it until it becomes yours; F1lthy and his whole collective came up exactly that way.
6. **Make it wrong on purpose** — if your vocal sounds "too clean" for the beat, that's a style mismatch; the genre norm is deliberate roughness, not accident.
