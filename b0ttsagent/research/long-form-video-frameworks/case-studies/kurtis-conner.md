## 0. Vine trauma is the worldview before any tactic

Kurtis frames YouTube workflow through Vine loss. He built 350k on Vine 2013-2015 doing 2-3 Vines a day, 6-second one-liner setup/punchlines [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE), made one brand deal that never paid, and kept all eggs in that basket: "I really put all my eggs in one basket [...] which was probably not the best idea" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE). He learned Vine shut down mid-shift as a server — phone blowing up from other Viners, finished shift then processed it [Anthony Padilla 2023-04-01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aTw1G4_j4GE). "I was like seriously like scared" after [Anthony Padilla 2023-04-01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aTw1G4_j4GE). That produced durable platform trauma: "I'm still to this day I'm still so stressed about money especially with Vine too that it went away in my brain I'm like well that's going to happen with YouTube also" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) — he labels it "platform trauma cuz it got ripped" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE). Business Insider 2020 corroborates the same beat historically ("it really backfired cuz when vine died I had to [...] 500 YouTube subscribers so I really kind of had to start from scratch" via contemporary paraphrase in [Business Insider 2020-02-09](https://www.businessinsider.com/kurtis-conner-youtube-most-popular-commentary-channels-2020-2)). This explains later cadence/burnout passages — scarcity mindset, not a tactic.

**Verified vs claimed:** 350k Vine followers [Business Insider 2020-02-09](https://www.businessinsider.com/kurtis-conner-youtube-most-popular-commentary-channels-2020-2) and ~2M YouTube by Feb 2020 [Business Insider 2020-02-09](https://www.businessinsider.com/kurtis-conner-youtube-most-popular-commentary-channels-2020-2) corroborated by growth to 4M+ late 2023 [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) and 5.67M Aug 2026 — directionally verified. Exact Vine earnings ("one brand deal never paid") is self-reported, not audited [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).

## 1. "The scripting is usually pretty easy but the finding the topic is like I hate it so much" — the one split he names

The single most repeated first-party workflow claim is the difficulty hierarchy.

> "the scripting is the scripting is usually pretty easy but the finding the finding the topic is like the I hate it so much" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

Interviewers: "is it just you or do you have a team" — "it's just me um just you just looking on the internet for things to talk about" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).

Why scripting at all, then? He started by reacting off the cuff and hated it:

> "I started doing commentary of like of just reacting and like off the cuff talking about stuff and I hate it I I'm not good at it but there are people who are amazing at it like Cody Noel they crush it" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

Root cause is stand-up preference:

> "I was never a fan of improv like when I was trying it out I never liked it it was too much pressure and it just wasn't fun I didn't like that I like knowing what I'm going to say in a video are on stage" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

Result:

> "once I started scripting my videos it opened up like a whole new like Comfort comfortability and like confidence in my video making where I was like I can even like within the script obviously I can if there's something I think of I can you know say it [...] but I can have that one piece I the one script I can fall back on that I know is like I'm confident in because like yeah there's too much pressure I don't think I'm that funny off the cuff I think I I do my best work when I am planning" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

Business Insider 2020 gives the contemporary version of the same beat — writing is usually my favorite part, "It's fun to make dumb stuff that makes people laugh" [Business Insider 2020-02-09](https://www.businessinsider.com/kurtis-conner-youtube-most-popular-commentary-channels-2020-2) — flagged second-hand interview, but corroborates scripting-as-favorite.

**What he does NOT document:** a topic scoring rubric, notebook system, or 1-3 sentence pitch test (contrast Mark Rober). He says topic-finding is hateable and solo, but never names a filter beyond "I want to learn about them and like and then make fun of them" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) (discipline: don't just mock, learn first). No queue length or backlog process documented — thin explicitly.

**Caveat:** "just me" is 2023 claim [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE). By 2026 he reports "thumbnail dude I've been working with [...] he has ideas that we like bounce off each other" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) — thumbnail collaboration is now collaborative; topic still solo per his account.

## 3. Open / hook — "I just want the video [...] to be just like you know the best that it can be" by getting right into it

The most explicit packaging tactic he names is *shortening the front door*. His original signature was the "extra greeting":

> "hello everyone you know who's watching but like to everyone who subscribed hello what's up [...] if you didn't know Curtis town is the best place to live in the world and I'm the mayor [...] that was the to be like you know if you subscribe you you get that extra I thought that was such a comfortable soft way to ask people to subscribe" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

He cut it:

> "I I stopped doing it because it was It was kind I just felt long to me and I wanted I just it felt like so long to get into the actual topic of the video so I wanted to just like just cut it out and be like just get right into the thing" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

Why:

Interviewer: "are you looking at retention data or are you making a gut storytelling decision of like you know what this works better if I go hook and I'm right in"

> "I think it came from just like talking to fellow YouTubers and just being like I've and same as like watching other YouTubers too when they like when they get right into it it's like all right cool here we go it's like and it's like it's what the video is about it's what the thumbnail was it's like I'm here I'm ready for the content so it was definitely a conscious thing to be like I just want the I just want the video that I'm putting out to be just like you know the best that it can be" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

He acknowledges the algorithm as game:

> "there is sometimes where it does suck where it's like sometimes like the algorithm and stuff does sort of like dictate what I'm doing but it it's the game you got to play I guess you know yeah it's just game it's just what it is" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

And the fix he found enjoyable inside that game is the thumbnail collaborator (see §6).

## 5. Editing rhythm — "editing is very similar to writing where you have these blocks"

Core analogy, unprompted:

> "I think YouTube like making YouTube videos and scripting videos and editing and putting together one piece of content has helped out my standup like way more than just like going to a bunch of open mics [...] YouTube videos and you know editing is is very similar to writing where you have these blocks right it's like actually this would be funnier if this piece went over here and now the joke Works differently or the story Works differently if this is over here and this is over here and you're actually there's not many versions of that where like if you're writing it on paper it's not like you can watch it back and hear it and be like oh that's funnier now" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

And the platform affordance difference he stresses:

> "with YouTube there's a lot more freedom and things to sort of improve [...] like fluff up a joke you know you can like do a silly edit or a sound effect or something right and you can make it look a certain way but like I think with [...] stand end up like the it needs to be like a smart like a funny joke [...] you can't just say something that's kind of funny and then hope a sound effect happens" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

> "there's a lot more of like uh Winks to the camera in YouTube videos with a sound effect or a crash where like the expectation I think is not that I am like bursting out laughing on the other end" vs comedy show where "if you're going to hit me with a joke [...] I might need some time to like actually laugh" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

Implication for his rhythm: YouTube edits carry joke weight that stand-up cannot; sound effects/digital zooms/"Bruh" are fluff that must be earned in stand-up but is expected on YouTube.

He also notes the handoff boundary now exists:

> "even when I'm scripting like even when I'm filming something and I pass it off to an editor they're not going to do it exactly how it looks in my head" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

Earlier era was opposite: "I'd be there for eight hours a day and then come home and then film and edit until super late, for months and months" at inkbox [Business Insider 2020-02-09](https://www.businessinsider.com/kurtis-conner-youtube-most-popular-commentary-channels-2020-2) and (verbatim in 2023) "I was head of video content [...] 9 to six [...] I would just come home and then like edit until like two in the morning and then get up like I hit 100,000 subscribers like at my work" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE). Business Insider corroborates quitting to pursue YouTube full-time after a 600k-in-2-days video [Business Insider 2020-02-09](https://www.businessinsider.com/kurtis-conner-youtube-most-popular-commentary-channels-2020-2) — see §7.

Recent-video observables: jump-cut heavy but not quantified, frequent wipes/caption pops timed to deadpan punchlines, sponsor read (SoFi) at ~midpoint of Cameron Dallas video [Kurtis Conner 2026-08-14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hQ5lRlKq454) — placement not discussed as tactic by him.

**What he does NOT document:** cuts-per-minute, music/SFX bible, color pipeline, retention-graph interpretation, or chapter-card cadence. Thin explicitly — he never publishes an editing spec; the only edit theory he offers is the blocks-as-writing analogy [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).

**Contradiction/caveat:** Says editing helps stand-up tremendously [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) while also deliberately keeping stand-up and YouTube "very separate" in the special ("I kept it very separate [...] I don't really think I mention YouTube at all in it" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)). Resolved as craft-transfer vs brand-separation — not a workflow contradiction.

## 6. Thumbnails — "that's not that doesn't matter" → "cottage industry"

Vine-era mental model:

> "just like the the thought of a thumbnail to me was like that's not that doesn't matter it was like cuz I never had to [worry on Vine]" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

First YouTube lesson — thumbnail was the unlock for his breakout:

> "I had the thumbnail was pretty good like and like people would want to click on it I think it had like 1,000 views when I went to bed and then I woke up the next day and it was at like 400 or 600k or something" for "pointless Twitter threads" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

Current system (2023 onward):

> "I've been working with [...] thumbnail dude [...] he has ideas that we like bounce off each other and like it's like that's like a whole new thing for me like having someone else" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

> "thumbnail guy like cottage industry of our space is pretty incredible it's crazy [...] that's an entire economy now of thumbnail guys who are just I don't even understand what they're doing [...] they post the before and after where some like chicken scratch from the critor they like took a picture in light yeah and then they just send it to them and they're like just make me like actually hanging off a cliff and there's like fire below that [...] from my understanding it's not Ai and maybe that's why it's amazing" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

He also voices the copycat grievance in same breath:

> "when people copy thumbnails a lot like blatantly is really annoying or just like video Concepts that pisses me off a lot uh cuz I saw like a thing last night I want to I'm not going to put anyone blast but I saw one last night I was like Matt R [...] Matt R just released a Disney adults video" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) — self-reported anecdote, not verified, included as his framing of thumbnail-as-IP.

## 9. Replication without burnout — "9 to5 is gnarly," "I'm riddled with self-doubt," and no codified system

Kurtis is explicit that output and human limits collide, but unlike Mark Rober he offers *no system*, only disposition and luck.

**Inkbox-era exhaustion:**

> "9 to5 is gnarly that was a lot" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

> "making videos for your day job and then making videos that's like mentally draining [...] you're just still sitting and editing yeah it's just so draining it's hard to like get excited about your thing cuz you're like I just did this for like 10 hours you're looking at the same software oh God" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

**Ongoing anxiety — platform trauma and upload dread:**

> "I think there's someone at YouTube who like really likes me because like even when a video is like 10 of 10 it'll be like on the trending page I'm like there's got to be someone there who's just like let's go Kurt" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) — same line repeated later verbatim [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) — self-reported luck, not verified.

> "every day I'm worried about you know stuff going away or like something one of my videos that was maybe not well received or something or you know I'm worried about a lot of stuff all the time" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

> "we're riddled with self-doubt [...] every time I go to post a video no matter how many subscribers I have or how many how well the other ones perform as soon as I upload a video I'm like okay well this is the last one no one's going to watch it" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

**Why he hasn't systematized:**

He frames sustainability as vision and people, not process: "for the most part like thankfully I've been really really lucky with like the the people I surround myself with and the videos I've been making and like you know just having a good head on my shoulders I think of like of having a Clear Vision of what I want to make has been really beneficial" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).

He also frames YouTube as the most ideal creative job precisely because it lacks the producer layer that constrains TV:

> "YouTube is like it's like it is like the most ideal job for like someone [...] if I were to go on like a series or like if I started writing for a TV show [...] it's like cuz then there's like producers and stuff [...] YouTube is so what I want and that's like the dream for a creative person there's like no like there's to an extent there's like really no limits to what you can like make" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE)

**Thin / not documented:** No sleep/exercise/wellness protocol, no hiring cap, no max weekly hours, no rotation or sabbatical, no burnout playbook beyond "good head on shoulders" and "lucky" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE). ## 12. In his own words — compressed replication checklist (with links, not advice)

1. **Learn by scripting, not improv.** Start from "I like knowing what I'm going to say [...] once I started scripting my videos it opened up like a whole new [...] confidence" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) and "I don't think I'm that funny off the cuff I think I I do my best work when I am planning" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) — but accept that topic-finding will still be hateable [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).
2. **Expect topic-finding to be solo and sore.** "it's just me [...] just looking on the internet for things to talk about" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE); let audience tips compound ("people who send us recommendations" [Business Insider 2020-02-09](https://www.businessinsider.com/kurtis-conner-youtube-most-popular-commentary-channels-2020-2)) but don't expect a system.
3. **Cut the front door.** Kill the extra greeting if it delays the topic — "I stopped doing it because it was [...] so long to get into the actual topic" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE); replace with cold topic hook ("Folks, you guys remember Magcon?" [Kurtis Conner 2026-08-14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hQ5lRlKq454)) plus 10-20s tour/promo max [Kurtis Conner 2026-07-04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YM3OnxNw2f8).
4. **Treat editing as writing.** "editing is very similar to writing where you have these blocks [...] actually this would be funnier if this piece went over here" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE); expect YouTube to forgive sound-effect crutches that stand-up punishes [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).
5. **Aim for at least 20, but let half be field.** "I want at least 20 minutes I think that's like the the ideal like length" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) and keep commentary feel while "dipping toes in like other forms" — last half as field/voiceover (iVision, James Chesey doc) [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).
6. **Make the thumbnail someone else's craft.** Go from "that's not that doesn't matter" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) to bouncing ideas with thumbnail dude who turns chicken scratch into cliff+fire "not Ai" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE); expect copycats and be annoyed but move on [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).
7. **Work weekly through a day job if you must, but stop pretending it's sustainable.** "9 to six [...] edit until like two in the morning" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) and "video every week while working while working full time" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) was "gnarly" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) and "mentally draining [...] you're just still sitting and editing" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) — quit only when overnight evidence (1k→600k) gave momentum, even then "I shouldn't have quit when I did [...] I didn't have that much money" [Anthony Padilla 2023-04-01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aTw1G4_j4GE).
8. **Hire through friends, pay directly, own the special.** Manager via Danny/Drew recommendation [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE), podcast producer buddy Shun found editor [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE), raise handled directly not via Q4 projections [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE), special self-funded with seven cameras independent so "I own it now I it's my special" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).
9. **Expect no burnout system — expect doubt.** "we're riddled with self-doubt [...] every time I go to post a video [...] I'm like okay well this is the last one no one's going to watch it" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) and "every day I'm worried about [...] stuff going away" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) — paired with "I think there's someone at YouTube who like really likes me" [Colin and Samir 2023-11-07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE) as luck narrative, not protocol. Thin explicitly.
