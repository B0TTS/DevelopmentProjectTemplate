## 0. Wendover is "creative mass manufacturing" — his assembly-line framing

> "I think video making is really just kind of manufacturing, but it's creative ... creative mass manufacturing, basically. And so if you think of it as an assembly line, like I've moved earlier ... my average responsibility has moved earlier and earlier into that assembly line." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

That single metaphor gates every later choice. Early: he did everything; then he offloaded post-production entirely; more recently he is "not necessarily the one doing all the scripts or all the production phase" and pre-production — "the phase where you like kind of put together pieces for the project" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) — "is really most of my job right now, because every other phase is it's been easy enough to find people who are very talented at doing that phase ... and you can just sort of hand off a project to people to sort of take it forward." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) Even that is now shared: "Ben and Adam do a lot of the more operational ... day-to-day tasks in pre-production and development for JetLag." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) What remains for him is "that very earliest stage where we literally get into a Zoom call ... and we are just trying to come up with the core premise of what we're doing." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) He believes it is "probably both the place that the most value is gained or lost, and ... I've done it a lot. It's not a skill that a lot of people have ... because most people have not made 800 or something different videos on YouTube through the years." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

For example, post-production, the entirety of it. I my brain just does not mesh with editing software. Especially motion graphic like Adobe After Effects and stuff like that. So from the very get-go that was like I just wanted to get rid of that responsibility because I hated it. That meant I had to hire people. To hire people I needed to have more revenue. ## 4. The writers' room and "the boss is the YouTube algorithm"

Oxford is the only doc where he walks through his writers' room, and it is about Half as Interesting:

> "What I like about that compared to Wendover ... is that I don't have to come up with the topics usually. ... Because it's Ben and Adam's responsibility. ... We get on calls every couple weeks and basically plan out future videos and they just come and give ideas and we get into arguments about why they will or will not work ... because I think we the way that works with like ... in some ways we don't have a boss. In some ways we do and the boss is the YouTube algorithm ... because if we don't listen to our boss, the algorithm, we don't make money and therefore we can't make videos anymore. So, we have to appease the algorithm and I think for us and many YouTubers what that means is we have about a 70% accurate view on how the algorithm works. We are mostly right. We are also often wrong ... because nobody truly knows. Even in fact, the people who run the algorithm at YouTube do not know because it uses ... machine learning and stuff like that to sort of tweak ... So, no one knows fully ... So we go around and have arguments about do we think that this topic will appease the algorithm or not ... and ... because we're mostly but almost certainly not even close to entirely right ... these will go in circles and circles until usually hopefully we reach some sort of consensus and then my worst nightmare is when it gets to the point where they're just like you got to decide cuz you're in charge and I don't like that ... because then it's all down to me if I'm right or not." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

## 6. Pre-production is the job now — Jet Lag as the clearest example

> "Pre-production is the phase where you like kind of put together pieces for the project. ... That is the phase that I'm is really most of my job right now" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

Jet Lag game design is the only place he narrates pre-production at length. Key timeline:

> "We started designing the season that is releasing now, the Taiwan Rail Rush. We started designing that all the way back in August of 2025." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) — told May 12 2026, so ~9-month lead.

> "The first thing we decide on is we want to go to Taiwan ... It looked so cool ... but it has a bit of a unique map of public transit ... small ... very circular network because there's big old mountains in the middle ... fun fact, tallest peak in Taiwan, higher than the tallest peak in Japan ... that's why there's no trains in the middle ... and that network is really hard for us because ... it's essentially ... linear ... you can more or less go left or right ... So doesn't have the kind of optionality that we normally ... look for ... because ... a decision of do we go that way or do we go that way is not that interesting. So, we had to sort of create a game that sort of ... took advantage of the restricted geography." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

He then generalizes failure mode:

> "We keep reinventing games that we realize don't work. ... we keep ... like you know how everything evolves into ... a crab ... we keep designing games that are snake. Which looking back on snake, it was a format that we were sort of least happy with ... But there's ... for some reason there's ... a reason why in the game design process we keep landing on similar formats that don't work." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

And the 95/5 structure of game design:

> "It's always like the first 95% ... is easy and the last 5% is the difficult bit. The last 5% of rules to make sure that players truly acting in their individual best interests will respond to what the other team, again, truly doing the best thing in their own interests ... will they respond in a way that will make the overall game one function and two be entertaining." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

> "We really believe ... we are not ... influencing things to make it work the way that we want it to work because ... as soon as the audience thinks that we're doing that, it becomes uninteresting." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

Execution after broad strokes is iterative compromise + virtual playtest: "We work our way through our rabbit holes and then we finally land on ... the broad shape of a concept. ... At a certain point ... we often refer to it as ... our go no go time ... Like usually ... we have ... short list of a couple concepts, two or three. And we usually have a front runner and we have to decide are we going to like do the sort of in-depth game design for this? Because there's actually ... a huge difference between ... the broad strokes ... and actually putting together the rule set with the right incentives ... So we ... get to play testing, we just do that sort of ... virtually using spreadsheets, Google ... timetables, etc. ... Then we go film the thing ... and then it's post, and post is a huge part of the process, but not one that's honestly that interesting to talk about" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

Corroborating production minimalism from earlier Jet Lag era (same company, same staff, useful for context but Jet Lag-specific, not Wendover main-channel): Wired 2022-06-29 interview [WIRED — Jet Lag Connect Four 2022-06-29](https://www.wired.com/story/worlds-largest-connect-four-game-jet-lag-wendover-denby-interview/) notes camera evolution to "all-iPhone 13 Pro production ... Rode lav mics, and some 360 cameras" because "we needed to streamline production to not let the production get in the way of the content" — first-season Blackmagic/drone/mini-drone discarded. This is Jet Lag, not Wendover essays, flagged as adjacent.

## 7. Scaling the company without venture — internal processes and Nebula's why

> "JetLagged has required us to get a lot better about our ... internal processes. ... workflows and things like we love Notion, we love Slack, we love timekeeping software, all sorts of stuff ... genuinely a big thing ... there's about 20 to 25 people who work on a given season of Jet Lag ... So, that's a lot ... some ... effectively full-time ... some ... close to full-time, and some are ... a couple hours of ... composing ... So, coordinating that is a big ... challenge. Luckily, I don't have to deal with a lot of that myself because ... post-production is ... coordinated by ... our great post-production supervisors." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

> "What it has really shown is ... the value of doing that, which I was pretty reluctant to because I don't like following rules and processes and workflows. ... But in the long run, it ... did prove to be worthwhile. Also showed the value of ... forward planning. ... Oftentimes ... we're ... very ... bogged down in the moment and therefore it's hard to make time for ... long-term planning. ... But this is a channel where ... it's necessary. ... So, in some ways, it's really just made us more of a company rather than just ... a collection of creatives putting stuff together." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

Staff Picks 2023 quantifies time split that operationalizes that "company":

> "35% • Developing Wendover Video Essays ... 'My mindset ... if I'm leading a team of writers, I have to be a writer myself ... also, it's a somewhat opinion-based channel, so it feels like it should come from me.'"
> "20% • 'Jet Lag' — Developing concepts ... Skews up when in production — shooting up to ~3-5 straight days of filming"
> "15% • Role with Nebula ... Checks in with creators every week to help brainstorm"
> "15% • Business Ops — Meetings with post-production, doing payroll and taxes"
> "15% • Content Planning for 'Half As Interesting' — leaves most of the work here to team members, helps nail titles and thumbs" [The Publish Press — Staff Picks 2023: Sam Denby](https://publishpress.squarespace.com/staffpicks23-samdenby)

Nebula's origin and why it matters to Wendover's replication: Oxford traces it to pre-Nebula talent management:

> "It was originally just sort of a talent management agency. ... my involvement started even before that when I was one of the very first clients of ... Dave who went on to sort of found the company and form it ... into a bigger thing. So, sort of talent management ... they book sponsorships ... and eventually ... it grew ... out of word of mouth, and at least originally it sort of specialized in the educational genre. ... So ... a lot of the clients ... sort of knew each other ... and I think what that meant was that we were much more prepared for ... collective action. In this case, the collective action was we got together and thought it'd be cool to start our own streaming service" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

Why a streaming service at all — his ad-model critique:

> "There's certain elements of YouTube that ... are frustrating ... a lot of it is ... policy ... I think ... they on balance do a pretty good job ... So, it's less so ... that side ... but more so the ad-supported business model ... I think has some quite perverse incentives. ... there's no way around the fact that it just is chasing clicks ... the ad revenue comes in pretty immediately to when someone watches ... So, the ... incentives are just first and foremost get someone to click ... YouTube ... has done some successful interventions ... through really rewarding watch time and retention and satisfaction ... But at the end of the day, the balance is skewed towards getting the click. And what that means is you get click bait, you get ... optimized lengths ... like 15 or 20 minutes is just what it turns out is the length ... that ... the average viewer comes onto YouTube for ... And therefore it tends to be most ... effective for ... retention and watch time ... So ... it really incentivizes a certain type of video ... it sort of ... leads to an evolution towards a ... fairly narrow set of types of video. ... What it does not incentivize strongly is investing in production quality. Because ... people ... will click ... based on the concept ... and ... generally stick around based on the concept ... and to certain extent the execution ... but more so it's the narrative execution ... more so than ... the visual execution or the technical prowess ... So ... you don't tend to get ... very high production value stuff because of the business model." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)

Nebula as counter-model:

> "With a paywalled system ... our incentives are we want to keep our subscribers around because they're paying ... 250 a month, 3 a month, or maybe they're a lifetime subscriber ... we want to keep those people around because we're making revenue based on the length of time that we keep giving them stuff that they want to watch. ... the company has now been around for what, 7 years or so? ... ## 11. In his own words — compressed replication checklist

1. **Keep the Wendover promise alive yourself** — "if I'm leading a team of writers, I have to be a writer myself" [The Publish Press — Staff Picks 2023: Sam Denby](https://publishpress.squarespace.com/staffpicks23-samdenby) and "about half of my working time goes to writing those scripts" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) — delegate Half as Interesting but not the opinion channel.
2. **Accept you have no system for ideas, but build a system for evaluating them** — ideas are "shower thoughts" / NPR / current-events background context [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) and "I have no system. I wish I had a system." [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg); evaluation is a biweekly writers-room argument about appeasing the boss-algorithm you only 70% understand [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg).
3. **Treat video making as an assembly line and move yourself upstream** — "I've moved earlier ... into that assembly line ... pre-production ... is really most of my job right now" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) where "the concept really is everything" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) — offload what you hate (for him, "the entirety" of post-production, "Especially motion graphic like Adobe After Effects" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)) first.
4. **Fund the move with volume you can already make** — the virtuous/vicious cycle: hate post → hire → need revenue → need more videos → need team [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) — started via Half as Interesting spun from Wikipedia unusual articles [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) to 560+ at ~1.5/week [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg).
5. **When you scale, let processes you dislike prove themselves** — "we love Notion, we love Slack, we love timekeeping software" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) only after "I was pretty reluctant to because I don't like following rules and processes" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) — 20–25 per season needs it [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg).
6. **Design games (and topics) for incentives, not vibes** — the last 5% is "make sure that players truly acting in their individual best interests will respond ... in a way that will make the overall game ... be entertaining" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg), playtested "virtually using spreadsheets, Google ... timetables" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg); watch for snake-rabbit-holes [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) — Taiwan took ~9 months from Aug 2025 concept [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg).
7. **Build for retention without pretending you can game it** — the boss is the algorithm you ~70% understand [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg); YouTube's balance "is skewed towards getting the click" and "15 or 20 minutes is just ... the length ... most ... effective for ... retention and watch time" [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg) so compete on narrative execution over technical polish [Oxford Union 2026-05-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg); no hook/retention template is claimed — thin.
