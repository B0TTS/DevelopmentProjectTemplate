## 0. "Make the best YOUTUBE videos possible" — and nothing else

> "Your goal here is to make the best YOUTUBE videos possible. That's the number one goal of this production company. It's not to make the best produced videos. Not to make the funniest... Not the highest quality... Everything we want will come if we strive for that." [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)

That single sentence gates every later rule. Immediately after: "Idc how traditional media does things ... This is not Hollywood and I do not want to be Hollywood" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf) and "We are a creative first production company. Because when creative leads the vision of the shoot, the product is always better" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). On Lex he restates the inversion: studied virality while Hollywood hires failed because they "try to build it more the traditional way, and not how it should be online" [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).

**Verified vs claimed:** The "not Hollywood" stance is claimed philosophy, but Business Insider's second-hand reporting explicitly contradicts the *practice* — describing a large-scale operation that "in many ways, mirrors traditional Hollywood productions — only faster-paced and occasionally more chaotic" with 350 staff, multiple production teams labeled Team A/B, specialized set builders and task-force PAs [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8). He rejects Hollywood *process*, not Hollywood *scale* — tension noted as contradiction below.

## 1. Virality is three trackable metrics — CTR, AVD, AVP — and nothing else matters if they fail

> "I spent basically 5 years of my life locked in a room studying virality... probably 20,000 to 30,000 hours... The three metrics you guys need to care about is Click Thru Rate (CTR), Average View Duration (AVD), and Average View Percentage (AVP)." [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)

Definitions in his words: CTR = "how many people see our thumbnail in their feeds divided by how many that click it. If 100,000,000 see... and 10,000,000 click ... we have a 10% ctr" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). AVD = "how long on average people watch a given video" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). AVP is percentage watched; length is decided by him or Tyler, your job is "make people watch as long as possible" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).

He ties the obsession to a superpower YouTube gives that TV never did: "they give us super detailed graphs for every video that show the exact second we lose a viewer on every single video" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). Donaldson repeats this verbatim on BI via the guidebook quote about that graph being "the cool thing" [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8) — but the primary source is the guide.

On Lex he adds the neural-net framing: looking "in terms of thumbnails, and titles, and different aspects of the first five, 10 seconds, and then, throughout the video, the retention, all of that" as training data [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU). Second-hand Forbes summary of his thumbnail/ideas lead "Chucky" at VidSummit 2024 phrases the same triad as data-driven empire but that's second-hand — not relied on here.

**Verified vs claimed:** The 20k–30k hours studying virality is self-reported, not auditable [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). CTR 10% illustration is hypothetical math, not claimed actual. No public CTR/AVD targets are disclosed in either first-party doc — thin on numeric thresholds.

## 2. CTR starts before you shoot: title + thumbnail set the promise, then video must pay it

### Title and thumbnail are "critical components"

> "THIS IS WHY YOU MUST KNOW THE TITLE AND THUMBNAILS OF THE VIDEOS YOU ARE MAKING! How can you know how to start your video if you don't even know what expectations the viewers have of you?" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)

Example chain he gives: "I Spent 50 Hours In My Front Yard" is lame; "I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup" is "easily 100x more viral" — same effort, image of "someone sitting in ketchup in a bathtub is exponentially more interesting" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). Title trick: "'I Survived' instead of 'I Spent' ... would add more intrigue" and more extreme is better: "'Bananas Are The Worst Food On Earth' will [outperform] 'I Don't Like Bananas'" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). Failure mode is expectation mismatch: clicked "World's Largest Bouncy Castle" with yellow castle beside huge buildings, got red castle in a field — "You'd feel like you were lied to and click off" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).

Lex corroborates the title rule he teaches team: clear, not cluttered thumbnails; "Titles tend to be less than 50 characters, intros tend to be this, stories tend to be this" — learned by watching "most viewed videos on YouTube every day... for way too many years" until patterns click [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU). He also describes the viewer test: "When you see the thumbnail, do you say, 'How did they do that? What the f-ck? Is that real?' When you have one of those three reactions, you have to click" — that's Time's paraphrase of Donaldson at TED-adjacent interview, but Lex gives the same "intrinsically interesting" title test [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU): "Do they have to watch it? Is it just so intrinsically interesting that it's just gonna fuck with them if they don't click?"

BI second-hand corroboration (flagged as second-hand): ideation team starts by conceptualizing title/thumbnail, "sketches ... logs them in a database, then pitches the best ones to Donaldson and a team of creative leads. Hundreds of ideas are logged, but most never make it" [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8). The guide itself does not detail sketch process — Lex does, BI fills in.

**What is NOT documented first-party:** thumbnail iteration count, A/B test tooling (ViewStats is mentioned second-hand), color/zoom rules. He says "it's the team's job to know thumbnail iteration" elsewhere but never quantified in these two docs — thin.

## 3. Open / hook — the first minute is the most important minute, and the first 5–10 seconds decide it

> "As with almost every video on Youtube, the first minute has the most loss (go look). This is why we freak out so much about the first minute and go so above and beyond to make it the best we freakin can." [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)

Concrete example from the guide: one video's graph — 60M clicked, "we lost 21 million viewers in the first minute ... and could have been much worse ... if the start wasn't well-lit, I didn't match the expectations of the clickbait, I didn't pre plan what I'd say, and we didn't front load some interesting stuff" then "instead of still having 39 million viewers at the 1 minute mark it would be more like 20 million" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). Lex frames the same unit as "first five, 10 seconds" [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).

Prescriptions in his words:

- Good lighting at the start correlates with less drop-off [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).
- Match title/thumbnail expectations immediately [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).
- Pre-plan what he'd say; front-load interesting stuff [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).
- On Lex: thumbnail/title/intro are generated jointly by the "neural network of your brain" after 10 years of obsessive study — "it just intakes a lot of random inspiration to see what spawns" [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).

**Verified vs claimed:** The 60M/21M example is a single video's first-day graph image shown in the guide — device-specific, not generalizable; still_current per Shortlist but image data is redacted in the PDF text layer. The "better lighting = less drop-off" claim is presented as his observed correlation, not a published study.

## 4. Structure — minute-mark architecture, not acts

The guide's most original contribution is a time-coded retention skeleton he requires everyone to know:

> "The above paragraphs are why you'll hear us ask what minute mark in the video that you are working on is. Whether it is production, creative, or editing you must always know what minute mark the content you are working on is. If you don't then you're not doing it right." [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)

He quantifies the average: "combined seconds of content of our last 100 videos is 81,801 seconds ... average ... 818 seconds or 13 minutes and 37 seconds" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf) — claimed, not independently verified vs current median 111M views, but internally consistent.

Scaffolding in his terms [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf):

- **Minutes 1 thru 3 — hype to execution:** "Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them. An example ... crazy progression. Let's say we have 10 minute video about a guy surviving weeks in the woods. Instead of ... first day then progressing ... We'd tried to cover multiple days in the first 3 minutes ... so the viewer is now super invested." At "around the 3 minute mark" insert a **3 minute re-engagement**: "content that is highly interested that fits the story and makes people genuinely impressed ... 'only MrBeast can do this' ... usually spectacles and sometimes need lots of time and money to perfect" — example, Karl watching Josh in "$10,000 Every Day You Survive Prison."

- **Minute 3 through minute 6 — fall in love:** "plan out all the most exciting and interesting content that is also very simple ... lots of quick scene changes and highly stimulating simple content ... If we can get them to watch the first half ... there's a very high chance they'll watch to the end." At 6 minutes add another re-engagement "that is highly interesting but needs a little more explanation and will push the story in the back half."

- **Back half content — the lull:** "once you have someone for 6 minutes they are super invested ... probably in what I call a 'lull'. They are watching ... without even realizing they are watching ... Typically the not as good content would be in the back half ... Don't ever signal the end ... unless it's to build hype for the prize or payoff ... This is also where long explanation bits can live and if something unexpected happens ... that can be turned into content."

Retention logic for why this works [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf): "To show this ... below is the retention data for two separate videos of almost identical length ... One ... 120 million views and the other only 45 million ... The answer is the lower image. People on average watched this video a minute and 38 seconds longer ... OF COURSE IT GOT TRIPLE THE VIEWS." — again image-redacted in text layer, claim as stated.

Additional structural rules [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf):

- "The video endings must always be abrupt to protect retention." / "Don't ever signal the end of the video unless it's to build hype for the prize or payoff"
- "No dull moments in videos" / "You can't fake intensity"
- "Me like simple. The simpler the better (APPLY WITH A GRAIN OF SALT)"
- Measure success after publish by AVD/AVP: "just look at the AVD and AVP ... you can clearly see if they enjoyed it" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)

Lex adds the experiential layer: his "retention brain" never turns off — "when you talk about something, I'm instantly like, 'what value are they gonna get? How many of them are gonna be interested?'" [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU) — and he finds slow cinema unwatchable because "The retention on The Godfather is horrible" [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).

**Thin / not documented:** He never gives a beat-by-beat template for a specific video type (e.g., "at 8:00 do X"). The minute marks are heuristics + "apply with grain of salt" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). Exact re-engagement spend or placement is not formulaic.

## 5. Pacing — "quick scene changes and highly stimulating simple content"

Pacing prescriptions are scattered but consistent [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf):

- Minutes 3–6: "lots of quick scene changes and highly stimulating simple content that reflects the story"
- Back half: "long explanation bits can live" only after investment threshold.
- Brand deals pacing case study: old way caused a "crater where the brand deal is" in retention graph; new way "BRAND DEALS ARE CONTENT! ... integrate them into the content so it doesn't nuke our retention" — new graph shows "dip is way less dramatic" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). No numeric lift given — image-based claim.

He never publishes a cuts-per-minute target or music/SFX bible in these docs — explicitly thin on editing rhythm. BI second-hand fills gap partially: "very specific editing requirements ... how many seconds into a video are you showing the super funny thing that happens ... retention editing" [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8), but primary doc frames it as minute-mark awareness, not cut cadence.

## 6. "Wow factor" — the non-trackable fourth metric

> "The last of the 3 trackable metrics ... is AVP and tbh this doesn't matter as much to you ... Another non trackable thing ... is what we call the 'wow factor' and it's definitely the most subjective ... Anything that no other youtuber can do." [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)

Canonical example [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf): 100 days in the circle for $500k — "instead of starting with his house in the circle ... we bring it in on a crane 30 seconds into the video ... The fact that we lifted a house on a crane didn't add anything to the title and thumbnail ... but ... who the fuck else on Youtube can do that lol ... the impression it leaves ... is invaluable ... It changes how they see us." He warns: "You can't track the 'wow factor' but ... Anytime we do something that no other creator can do, that separates us ... and makes our videos more special."

Lex corroborates with current evolution: they spend "millions of dollars a video ... 12,000 hours of footage for a 15-minute clip" yet "leaned more on ... 'how can we make people feel something?'" vs pure analytics [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU) (via Time paraphrase, but Lex transcript shows same feeling pivot).

## 7. Formats as retention engines — and why he kills them

> "One way to boost retention ... is to have a good format" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)

Documented formats [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf):

- **"last to leave"** — payoff not revealed until end, "you really want to see who leaves the circle last and wins the $100,000" so "as long as we don't make the video boring ... people are very likely to stick around until the end."
- **"stair stepping"** — "I Bought The World's Largest Firework" opens showing all fireworks then lighting $1, then $10, then $50, then $375, then $1,000, then $10,000, then content, then $40,000, $100,000, then world record — "As you get deeper ... stakes get higher ... I fucken love stair stepping."
- **Chase formats** — "I get chased like bounty hunter, military, FBI" — same end-payoff tension: "Will they catch me? Will I get away? Gotta watch until the end."

Lifecycle rule [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf): "people don't know what they want ... The viewer may think they want a format forever, but they don't. They want new and fresh ... every channel that rehashes formats for years always dies ... Ideally two videos from the same format are not back to back, i'd like multiple different videos in between them if possible." He cites his own "donating to twitch streamers" (12 videos, still begged for more, killed anyway) as proof.

Lex adds the always-experiment principle: "You wanna test things while you're still growing to keep the growing ... If you ... level off, you do a couple tests that go wrong ... you're screwed ... You have one more bad experiment, you're nosediving" [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU). That's the format-killing logic externalized.

## 8. From idea to greenlight — ideation factory + feasibility + critical components

**Ideation.** Lex describes daily consumption of "most viewed videos on YouTube every day" as neural-net training to joint-generate title/thumbnail/intro [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU). BI second-hand details the team that operationalizes it: dedicated ideation team, wide net on YouTube for trending topics/keywords, thumbnail sketches logged in database, pitch to Donaldson + creative leads, "Hundreds of ideas are logged, but most never make it" [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8) — corroborates Lex but is second-hand.

**Feasibility.** If greenlit, "handed to a group of creative producers for cost and feasibility planning. The company recently added a team dedicated to calculating feasibility" [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8). The guide's own term is feasibility as "working to see if the critical components ... are actually possible. Example ... hide and seek in the world's largest building ... if you can't [get building] then that's why it was in feasibility so no one else wasted time" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).

**Critical components.** Defined as "things that are essential ... Without ... there is no video" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). Examples: securing the island for 100-person island giveaway; title/thumbnail themselves (bouncy-castle color example); challenges 3 and 7 if Donaldson flagged them as essential. Rule: "Protect it at all costs, check in on it 10x a day, obsess over it, make a backup, if it requires shipping pay someone to pick it up and drive it, don't trust standard shipping, and speak up the second anything goes wrong. The literal second. Never coin flip a Critical Component (that means you're coinfliping the video aka a million plus dollars)" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).

**Budget throttle.** "We don't have unlimited money ... 'Creativity Saves Money' ... Which sounds cooler ... $20,000 or a year's supply of doritos? ... 1,825 packs ... at a dollar ... prize ... just went from $20,000 down to $1,825 because we didn't just throw money" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). Also: "I want money spent to be shown on camera ideally. If you're spending over $10,000 on something and it won't be shown on camera, seriously think about it" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).

## 9. Production system — bottlenecks, video everything, consultants, "no does not mean no"

These are verbatim operating rules, not generic advice [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf):

- **Bottlenecks:** If video is 45 days out and you need thumbnail + creative, don't dump and set reminder for 5 days — "Every single day you must check in ... Leave. No. Room. For. Error." He gives scripted bottleneck call to Tyler as template.
- **Video everything:** "Most people just go to scout the set, maybe grab a photo ... Then a week later when ... Tyler ... starts asking questions ... you can't quite recall ... This is why we say video everything ... so ... entire team of 10 people have a good mental grip."
- **Say the negatives:** "Don't just tell ... why something is good. It's infinitely more valuable to tell ... why it's not good. 'I FOUND THE CASTLE ... But it's booked all year, overbudget, and someone died in it last week.'"
- **Track the contractor:** "You can't just dump and forget ... If ... JB ... Check in daily, receive videos, and know weeks in advance if you're fucked. Not days."
- **Use consultants:** "Need to make the world's largest slice of cake? Start off by calling the person who made the previous world's largest slice of cake ... 'I will always check for consultants when i'm assigned a task' — repeat 3x."
- **No does not mean no:** "never take a No at face value. If we need a store to buy everything inside ... and the person that answers says 'No, you can't film here'. That literally doesn't mean shit ... Talk to other employees ... boss's boss, have me dm them ... Don't just stop because one person told you no, stop when all conceivable options are exhausted" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). *Note:* BI flags this line as controversial out-of-context; guide's context is location/permissions push [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8) — see Caveats.
- **Higher form of communication:** worst is email; best is in-person — "The lower the form ... the more miscommunication ... Need more toilet paper? A text is probably fine. One week out ... just went south? Minimum a multi way call with the heads ... Ideally grab them in real life."
- **Work on multiple videos EVERYDAY:** "If you ever only work on one video during a day, you failed as a MrBeast employee that day" — otherwise you "murdered any lead time" and snowball into only handling what's in front of you.

Lex corroborates bottleneck/prio system at personal level: his perfect day is "go down the eight companies and just whatever their like two to three biggest pain points ... and just doing those. If I clear the bottlenecks ... then it's a perfect day" [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).

BI second-hand corroborates scale: "At any given moment, MrBeast has multiple production teams ... labeled by letters: Team A, Team B, and so on. Each video can staff ... from one to a dozen or more producers" and "At Beast, you have to be responsible for everything" [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8).

## 10. Capture — quantity over quality, so nothing is missed

> "If you're doing a video with 100 contestants, you need at least 100 cameras ... For a really long challenge, there's a ton of angles. Jimmy is very big on quantity over quality for filming to make sure nothing is missed if anything happens." [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8) — second-hand, but consistent with guide's "Be able to hold a camera" and "video everything."

Guide's version [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf): "Obviously everyone has their roles ... it'd be nice if at some point you got training on our style of holding a camera ... If ... there are only a few scenes that need a couple extra camera men I'd love ... not to have to fly, house, transport ... more camera men when we could just have a few people from production or creative help for those overflow scenes because typically the more cameramen we have filming the less important each shot becomes." And on Lex: videos like 100-day challenges required covering 8 cubes 24/7 with reverb audio issues forcing "Frankensteining in the editing" [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).

Thin on camera model/lens — not documented.

## 11. Editing — retention editing as the real story, minute-mark discipline

Guide's rule [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf): "Whether it is production, creative, or editing you must always know what minute mark the content you are working on is." BI adds editor pipeline specifics (second-hand, not in guide): "up to eight editors, with a few ... handling daily footage cuts, four or five syncing footage and selecting the best parts ..., and a couple of lead editors compiling everything" and "If they shoot a video for a week, by the time they get home ... they already have a very rough cut that someone is doing in real time" [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8). Donaldson's own edict via BI paraphrase: "how many seconds into a video are you showing the super funny thing that happens" [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8) — aligns with guide's retention graph doctrine.

Lex on why this matters: even with 100M views/pop, he kills videos after edit if not meeting standard — example reshooting 24 hours on deserted island because first cut's execution was poor ("it was hot ... we all ... just kinda wanted to die ... miserable") and second shoot in cooler weather was 10x more fun [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU). BI corroborates killing after investment: "Even after investing thousands into a video ... Donaldson will sometimes kill a project if it doesn't meet his quality standards" [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8).

**What he does NOT document in first-party:** cuts per minute, music licensing, sound design, color pipeline, graphics cadence. The guide's image graphs for retention are not numerically extractable from the PDF text layer — claims must be taken as described.

## 12. Cadence and scaling the machine without becoming Hollywood

**Ambition vs reality.** Guide states intent: "We arn't here to make a small movie once or twice a year, I want to make one a week lol" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). Lex gives lived cadence at that time: "we're trying to make weekly videos for rest of year. Past months I filmed 25 to 28 days per month. That's ideal. The only thing I can't delegate is filming" [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU). BI 2025 reports settled cadence: "generally releases one to two longform videos monthly on his main channel" plus a few on gaming/philanthropy and shorts vertical [Business Insider 2025-08-14](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8) — verified active but slower than weekly ambition; tension flagged as evolution, not contradiction (weekly was aspiration, 1–2/mo is measured 2025–2026).

**Replication mechanics.** Guide: "As I write this we have 2 teams, that will grow to 4 in the next year. (and possibly 8 in the next 2 years but I can't talk about that cause james will kill me haha). We need more leaders" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf). And translation replication: "We also have a ... We dub our channels in other languages and we run a translation company that works with other channels as well" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf) — his own definition of cloning intellectual property without calling it that.

Lex on how replication actually worked: tried hiring "one person from Disney ... to come in and help ... obviously that was a dumb idea ... they build it more the traditional way" — outcome: "the eventual solution was just ... draw up my sleeves and do it myself, with James, my right hand man, and just ... No one's ever done this ... We gotta figure the fuck out ourselves" [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU). He describes training from scratch and promoting internally rather than importing structures.

**Work system that enables replication:**

- Work on multiple videos daily (anti-snowball) [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)
- Backup days: "Always have a backup day ... When you're spending hundreds of thousands ... securing a set, renting a tank ... make sure you have the ability to extend ... I'd rather have ... 90% cool looking tank with ... ability to pay extra to add a day. Then a 100% cool ... but ... there is no backup day" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)
- Pull all-nighters weeks before shoot so you don't have to days before; push outwards not inwards [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)
- Own mistakes: "I've never ever ever once fired someone on the spot for messing up ... Own shit so we can address how to fix it" — but C-players "are poisonous and should be transitioned ... IMMEDIATELY" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)

## 13. Replication without burnout — what he actually says (and what he doesn't)

He is explicit that output ambition and human limits collide — and he gives two linked answers across docs.

**Guide's intensity side** [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf):

- "The Amount of hours you work is irrelevant ... you will be judged on results, not hours ... Get shit done and move the goalpost!" — then definition of A-Players as "obsessive, learn from mistakes, coachable, intelligent, don't make excuses, believe in Youtube ... are the best in the goddamn world at their job" and "I only want 'A Players' ... C-Players ... should be transitioned ... IMMEDIATELY."
- "Work with intensity ... Pull all nighters weeks before the shoot so you don't have to days before the shoot."

**Lex's recharge side — the counterweight he added as he scaled** [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU):

> "If your goal is to be a super successful entrepreneur, you need to either be working or you need to be doing something that decompresses and recharges you so you can work again ... the things you're doing in your downtime when you're not working, if it doesn't recharge you, you're screwed. You're just a ticking time bomb, waiting to implode ... watching, for me, anime or whatever it is, playing a board game, that is actually kind of crucial to my success ... I used to be the kind of guy that wanted to work every hour ... I would try to train myself to not need that stuff ... I ... would have like borderline panic attacks when I would do it ... What's best for me is just to work when I feel like working, and then just not work when I don't, and just have no constraints ... it'll be seven or eight days just nonstop going, going, and then ... 'Oh, I need some recharge time, and then go fucking binge a season of anime.'"

And the emotional labor rule [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU): when a video flops, "just ... unemotionally look at the video, determine the problems, and then move on. There is no secret ... If you really can't bring yourself to do it, then you're just screwed. Honestly, maybe you're not meant for this game ... You have to unemotionally be able to look at videos that flop ... Not every video can be a one outta 10."

**What is thin / not documented:**

- No sleep, exercise, or health protocol is ever codified; the guide's "A-Player obsessive" + "grinders that put in the hours" sits in tension with Lex's "give yourself time to recharge" without reconciliation.
- No hiring-cap policy or max weekly hours — only values ("obsessive", "coachable") and exit criteria for C-Players.
- No explicit system for preventing the "25–28 days filming per month" from burning out talent — he frames recharging as individual maturity, not process.

**Caveat:** Burnout mitigation is therefore *personal philosophy + staffing scale* (350 people, Team A/B/C...) rather than a documented welfare system. Sources thin beyond those two passages — stated explicitly, not padded.

## 16. In his own words — compressed replication checklist

1. **Pick only extreme promises** — "I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup" vs front yard is 100x more viral at same effort; add "I Survived" for intrigue [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf); title <50 chars, thumbnail clear/simple [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).
2. **Know the promise cold, then pay it in first minute** — good lighting, pre-planned words, front-loaded interest, or lose half the 60M who clicked [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf); win the first 5–10 seconds [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).
3. **Know your minute mark** — 1–3: hype→execution via crazy progression + 3-min re-engagement; 3–6: quick scene changes, simple stimulating content + 6-min re-engagement; back half: lull, no dull moments, abrupt ending, no end signal unless prize hype [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).
4. **Add one wow only you can do** — crane house at 0:30 in circle video "didn't add ... to title/thumbnail" but "who else ... can do that" — trackless but brand-defining [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).
5. **Build on a format, then abandon it** — last-to-leave payoff, stair-stepping escalation, chase tension; never same format back-to-back; donate-to-streamers killed after 12 despite demand because "people don't know what they want" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf); keep experimenting while growing [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).
6. **Produce via critical components + daily bottleneck checks** — identify island/title/flagged challenge as critical, "Never coin flip," video-scored sets, "check in daily," video everything so team of 10 shares mental model [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf); perfect day = clear bottlenecks across eight companies [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).
7. **Use consultants and creativity to preserve budget** — largest-slice-of-cake guy, $20k → $1,825 via Doritos packs, money shown on camera if >$10k [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).
8. **Push thru no ethically, use highest-form communication** — exhaust store managers, DM, social team before accepting no [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf); call/grab in real life > text > email for complex fails.
9. **Work many videos daily with backups** — never single-video days [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf); secure backup days on tank/sets, all-nighters weeks before not days before.
10. **Choose obsession, then learn to recharge** — be "A-Player obsessive" [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf) but protect longevity by working when compelled and recharging via anime/board games when not, detaching unemotionally from 1-to-10 flops [Lex Fridman Podcast #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).
