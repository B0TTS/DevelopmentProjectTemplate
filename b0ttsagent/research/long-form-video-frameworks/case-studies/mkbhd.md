## 0. Thesis in his own words: "I am not the subject"

> "The answer is, I am not the subject of the videos." [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/)

Expanded:

> "So there is a long history of YouTubers filling a niche by being the funny person or the creative sketch artist... They are inherently the topic of the videos, and in order to continue leveling up... they have to level up themselves and for me, it's the tech that's the subject of the videos. I'm pointing the camera at the thing that has the pressure on it, which is the tech industry. Laptops, smartphones, AR, VR, whatever the next thing is... I love tech, I always have, I don't think that's changing anytime soon. So I don't feel burnt out as long as I'm playing with new tech every day." [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/)

## 1. Two buckets, one script

> "I do separate my audience into two distinct buckets: the SEO bucket, which is, 'I am searching for this product right now because I made a purchase decision,' and then there is just people who are subscribed to see the next video." [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/)

He says the second bucket is now dramatically larger, yet he writes for the first:

> "I am mostly tailoring the production and the message in the videos for the first audience, but that is in fact a significantly smaller audience than the other one, and for whatever reason it works that I talked to this one audience, but everyone else watching is also enjoying that conversation." [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/)

Evidence he gives: most common in-person line is "I watch a lot of your videos, and I don't really buy any of that stuff, but I watch the videos anyway" [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/). The duality explains his writing challenge:

> "if you are early to be helpful, you are probably helping the most people." [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/) vs "product reviews are a genre of entertainment." [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/)

His answer is to stay entertaining to the second bucket without losing the first:

> "I think there's little pieces of our process and our production that are optimized for the larger audience. I think there's the little memes, and the little entertaining bits, and little inside jokes here and there, and also I think packaging too..." [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/)

See §9 for packaging as the bridge.

**Verified vs claimed:** Two-bucket split is his own segmentation, not analytics-sourced with numbers [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/). He offers no bucket sizes.

## 2. Cadence — "one and a half a week" ideal, 24 hours to a week per video, and the September playoffs

He quantifies cadence differently across years, all first-party:

- Stratechery 2024-06-26 window: "We definitely have pre-production days... and then we have production days... Anything between 24 hours and a week pretty much covers our whole thing. We can put a video together in one day, or we can take our time with a big video like this and make it in a week." [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/)
- Cortex 2025-12-12 window: "it feels like it's about one to two per week. I think this year it's a little bit over one a week. At the most I ever did, it was probably two a week. What is your ideal? Probably one and a half a week." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)
- Same interview, Shorts as X-factor: "with shorts now there's a little bit of an extra X factor in there because you can kind of just have an idea and drop something at this drop of a hat, but like one to two a week is usually pretty ideal" [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

Evidence JSON corroborates 2026 cadence: uploads 2026-08-24, 2026-08-21, 2026-08-12 (3 in 12 days) ≈ 1.5–1.75/week in burst, median of last 12 ≈ weekly, consistent with claimed ideal [working/evidence/mkbhd-2026-08-29.json].

Seasonality rule he names the **playoffs**:

> "during the rest of the year in January, February, March, all the like quieter months where we have a little bit more flexibility... that is like when we're playing with our processes and production the most. That's when we try new things. That's when we add stuff, subtract stuff, see how it goes. And then once we get to September, October, that's kind of like the playoffs... you're no longer like getting better at the sport or learning new strategies. You're just locking in what you've been learning all year and executing on it as best you can because that's your plan." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

Corroborated by interlocutor framing: "you kind of have like a lock period like nothing's changing from like August through to January." / "Exactly." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

Business impact of cadence he ties to Shorts: "Have shorts made a noticeable beneficial change to your business? To the business? No." / "they get a lot of views and sometimes you can sort of use that as like the top of the funnel to get more people to subscribe and watch long form but it's hardly substantial to the business" [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) and scale quip "one YouTube view equals 30 TikTok views equals 300 YouTube shorts views" [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM).

**Verified vs claimed:** 24h-to-week window and 1.5/week ideal are self-reported [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/) / [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM). ## 3. Team as octopus → robotic octopus, and why it grew

Earliest hire story:

> "First hire was Andrew Manganelli, and I remember this was CES... I just needed someone there to hold the camera, help me carry camera batteries... I got back to the studio and I was like, 'I need to add some help'. So he was the first hire, and I'd say that was probably around eight years ago" [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/)

The octopus he teaches:

> "I feel like a creator is basically an octopus when they first start, because you're doing multiple jobs at once. You're a camera person, you're doing the inbox, you're doing the editing, you're in front of the camera, the lighting, the sound person, you are all these people, and if you can find pieces of it that you could cut your arm off and hand it to someone who can do it better than you, because it's their full-time job, that can allow you to be more focused" [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/)

And the evolution:

> "It felt like it went from being me to me and one of my friends, to me and a few friends, to me and a small team, and now it's me and 15 people, so it's me and this group that is highly powerful and really curated and precise... so we're like a robotic octopus." [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/)

Think with Google updates the count and frames the same principle as his current structure:

> "Everyone in the studio does something that I was once doing myself... We now have about 17 people on the team and a network of channels known for different kinds of content." [Think with Google 2025-09-01](https://business.google.com/us/think/search-and-video/marques-brownlee-interview/) and the three hearts he will not cut: "being on camera, writing, and reviewing technology." [Think with Google 2025-09-01](https://business.google.com/us/think/search-and-video/marques-brownlee-interview/) (Stratechery version phrases hearts as "reviewing the products and that is content strategy" [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/) — same "what will next video be" core.)

Cortex gives the operational implication now: head of production **Harper** as birds-eye:

> "So, actually the newest ad to the team, Harper is her name is the head of production... And what that means is she has the bird's eye view of everything happening on every channel and how everyone's time is being used." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

Before Harper: "'Hey, I need to shoot this short right now. Is anybody available...? Is Rich here? Oh, Rich is outside.' That was massively simplified. Now that Harper's here, it's like, 'Hey, Harper, today, this afternoon, after lunch, I'm going to shoot these two things. So, let's make sure we have a spot... and a person who can help me shoot those things'" [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

Management philosophy he admits is not his strength:

> "My expertise is not in being a manager or being a boss... I went to business school, but it's like this isn't my expertise clearly. So there is some amount of pressure in trying to run an operation that is fair and generous and equitable" [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) and "Are you a people manager...? I think no. I think that's one of the things I've gotten to off set with people who are way better at that... It's hard. It's really hard" [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

**Verified vs claimed:** Team 15 [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/) (June 2024) vs 17 [Think with Google 2025-09-01](https://business.google.com/us/think/search-and-video/marques-brownlee-interview/) (Sep 2025) vs "like 17 or something" [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) (Dec 2025) — growth trajectory, not contradiction. > "So, I guess the big one is Notion. And the other big one is just Google Docs. So, anytime there's a video document or a script or whatever, all of that is put into a Google Doc that several people can look at and add suggestions to and jump into there. And that's kind of the only two big ones. Everyone uses email and Slack obviously, but like as far as creative collaboration, it's mainly notion and Google Docs." Notion governance irony:

> "Do you have someone kind of managing the notion? ... That's so funny. It's me unfortunately. ... It's me ... who made the templates and who's like kind of building the structure... ## 5. Research — live with the device, don't benchmark it to death

The full loop in his words [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM):

> "That is basically all I do is integrate it into my life. So, I've done the setup process... you get some like information about it like a review bullet list or something like that from the company... But basically, yeah, I unbox it. I pay attention to what's in the setup process... then you move into the phone. You don't really get to start evaluating battery life until maybe two or three days in, but you pretty quickly get to start playing with new features, seeing how well things work or don't, taking lots of pictures and videos, if the cameras are new, and just living with it. And I think that's the important thing is like I mainline the device. I get my SIM card in it right away."

Why mainlining matters: "when you start to find things that it doesn't do very well that maybe other phones did well because it's my main device, then I'm like, 'All right, can I use a workaround?'" [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

Battery philosophy as canonical example of his anti-speeds-and-feeds stance:

> "I also know that... battery life is not really you can kind of scientifically test it, but there's so many reasons why so many battery benchmarks are flawed... That that is frustrating to me. Like I kind of just have to go by how well it it sustains my lifestyle and that's my only benchmark." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) — "I'm fine with that, you know, like I don't need to know [uninterrupted video hours]. So, like screen on time, I think, is a pretty good that's a thing we can all understand." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) — but even screen-on-time he deems over-granular: "either the battery life is good or it's bad or it's meh... when I'm reviewing a device and I'm... 'oh, I got seven hours and 37 minutes... and I got seven hours and 12 minutes' ... That doesn't make a big difference to me. I just want to know if it's good or not good." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) — example: "it's 10:00 a.m. and I've used this phone for an hour already and it has 97% battery left... That's all I have to say. That's all I have to say, right? I don't have to get into the exact nitty-gritty." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

Broader speeds-and-feeds rule:

> "I guess looking at your reviews in general, you are not a speeds and feeds guy, right? ... Is that because that's what you care about or is it just what you think is best...? You know, it's useful sometimes, but again... I'm talking to obviously a bunch of people who are thinking about buying this device, but I'm also talking to a bunch of people who are just watching this for entertainment and have no intention... And I think I could easily do a bunch of benchmarks... And that would be very useful for the people who are about to buy... but I would lose a lot of people who were never going to do that in the first place." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) — "I think a lot of those charts, I try to use them sparingly because sometimes they're useful to give context... but sometimes they're just a little unnecessarily granular" [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

He maps the right test granularity to the buyer: "if I'm reviewing a $160 Moto G, like I'm not going to spend that much time benchmarking how many frames per second... because the person buying a Moto G is not interested... But if I am reviewing a bleeding edge humane AI pin, which is a new firstgen product in a category that only early adopters are going to buy, then... I'm going to try everything this thing is capable of." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

## 6. Writing — the first heart, 90%-written, paragraph-read

> "I write 90 something% of what you're hearing me say... the way it's delivered is sometimes a little bit wandering around what I've written, but I've written everything." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

Delivery method:

> "Some people are really good at [reading teleprompter]. I'm terrible at it. But I have my notes like in my lap and I'm reading them, okay? Knowing like how that feels and how I want to say that and then delivering it... sometimes you can go a little bit off script because there's something I remember in the moment" [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) — "it's not even line reading. It's like paragraph reading... I have five sentences I want to say and even if I don't say it word for word exactly... as long as I deliver the points and the emphasis... success." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

Text-to-time ratio he volunteers:

> "I think the ratio most people who make videos might understand is like for a 12 minute review there's probably about 45 to 60 minutes of A-roll." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) — "from when I start recording to when I stop recording is like 60 minutes. And that is how long it took me to get all of the takes I want for the 10-minute video." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

Quality control evolution:

> "newly this year just to have like ... someone in the room to make sure I'm not going too far off script or ... missing numbers. I'll have someone in the room... looking at what I wrote and if I say a number wrong in the take, I might not process that. [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) — reason: "having higher standards for quality control in general, like lots of feedback on like we want things to be as accurate as possible" [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

## 10. Title + thumbnail — "at least half the story" and the only A/B he gets

This is his most quantified packaging doctrine:

> "They’re at least half the story, because if you talk to anyone who’s been doing this for a long time — if your title and thumbnail are not good, the video does not matter... But on the same token, if the title and thumbnail are incredible, but the video does not deliver, YouTube actually learns from that, their algorithms are optimizing for audience happiness." [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/) and "if people click at a very high rate on that video, and then realize it’s bad and then leave quickly, they will stop serving that video to you, so you have to deliver on both." Earlier he frames the two-bucket packaging tension:

> "I think if I’m reviewing a product, then I just title it, ‘Product A Review’, you can just end it there, and that is a normal title... But if you’re looking to have something just be entertaining to a larger group, you need to play with packaging it for a larger group and the impressions, you’ll see the numbers if you look in YouTube Analytics, that it does work." [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/)

[Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/) after controversy that "This title is clickbaity. Tooling he documents:

> "They have an A/B testing feature now where you can actually see which title, or actually specifically which thumbnail, performs best. [Stratechery 2024-06-26](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/) — refined: "there’s an art form to pairing your title with your thumbnail... Cortex adds timing gamesmanship:

## 14. Replication without burnout — subject not self, system not hustle

## 15. How he says you should copy him — voice over speed, accuracy over hype

On **speed vs voice trade-off** (directly relevant to replication):

> "It is twofold. One is because this is the only company that doesn't pre-brief and so we inherently have less time and by the time the review embargo drops, I've probably only had half the time I really need to review the phone. But the second part... the iPhone is super hot and people want to watch something on day one. And so... I'm giving you something on day one... it's not the full review yet." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) — "I don't see why anyone else couldn't do it. I guess there is a distinct pressure on basically every creator to be as early as possible... And I think it is a luxury or a privileged position to not feel that pressure as much where I think that people will want to hear what I have to say even if it's three four days later." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM)

On **why accuracy is the moat vs gaming launch noise**:

> "How many of these reviews are people really going to watch on that one day versus when things settle down a few days later and then something maybe a little bit more well-formed or at least a little bit more distinct comes out that can live on its own outside of a lot of that noise." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) — "there's also some level of like... the embargo thing... there is an amount of noise that is hard to navigate like when all of these opinions come out at the same time." On **influence horizon** (Humane Pin):

> "if your product is bad, people are going to buy it and find out that it's bad... And if a big review comes out saying it's bad, and a whole bunch of people find out at the same time, not changing the trajectory of the product. I'm more just accelerating what was already going to happen." [Cortex YouTube 2025-12-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQjB5qBFZtM) — paired with his job line: "My job is to speak directly to my audience, who cares about if the product is good or bad...
