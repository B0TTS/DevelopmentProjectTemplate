# Recurring Patterns — Deep-Documented Long-Form Video Workflows

**Purpose:** Cross-creator workflow patterns built bottom-up from the 12 completed case studies in [`case-studies/`](case-studies/). Nothing here was imported from outside that corpus; every pattern was tallied from evidence already linked in the case studies.

**Scope note — who is excluded and why:** Three shortlisted creators — **tom-scott, colin-and-samir, matt-davella** — have **no case study** (Phase 2 closed early after repeated subagent crashes, per `working/MANIFEST.md`). They are excluded from all pattern analysis and are never cited in any table below. Pattern claims rest only on creators with completed case studies.

**Evidence bar (never lowered):** A tactic qualifies as a pattern only when **≥2 case studies** document it, each backed by a specific source (quoted or linked). A tactic seen in only one creator is *not* a pattern — those are listed separately in [Single-creator tactics](#single-creator-tactics-not-patterns). Counter-examples and "thin/not documented" notes are carried per pattern, because the case studies flag them explicitly.

**Format tags:** Each pattern is tagged `format-agnostic` (appears across distinct formats — e.g., spectacle challenge, science essay, commentary-comedy, tech review, fashion essay) or `format-specific` (concentrated in one format family). No pattern is tagged agnostic unless the citing creators span genuinely distinct formats.

## Packaging and the open

### P3 — Packaging is the promise of the video, defined before production — `format-agnostic`

The single most-replicated doctrine in the corpus (7 of 12): title + thumbnail are decided or constrained *early*, and the video is then obligated to pay them off.

- **mrbeast** — "THIS IS WHY YOU MUST KNOW THE TITLE AND THUMBNAILS OF THE VIDEOS YOU ARE MAKING!" — [How to Succeed in MrBeast Production](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf); title and thumbnail are listed as "critical components."
- **ryan-trahan** — all 30 (Penny Series) and all 50 (50 States) thumbnails were finished *before shooting*: "we made all 30 thumbnails beforehand which is something that feels like the only way this would have worked" — [Colin and Samir 2022](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wkDlfvTed1c), scaled in [Colin and Samir 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CQE2E88QJes).
- **airrack** — every pitch must include "three thumbnails… a breakdown of the video and… a how" — [Comeback Interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtMudMODlWU).
- **johnny-harris** — "the marketing… is defined early on… this is the Billboard this is the packaging," with 2–3 packagings tested at greenlight — [David Perell interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM).
- **veritasium** — "The goal of the title and thumbnail should give you a sense of the promise of the video," with 20–50 thumbnail options per video — [Fireside Lisbon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PEzwH50tyPw).
- **mkbhd** — "They're at least half the story" — [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/).
- **mark-rober** — the 1-to-3-sentence pitch test ("if I can't explain it in one to three sentences… it doesn't work for my channel") gates ideas on packaging clarity — [Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1t5oYKEn-1E).

**Counter-example / thin:** drew-gooden, kurtis-conner, mina-le, linus-tech-tips, and wendover-productions document no packaging doctrine at all (each case study marks this thin explicitly). Kurtis-conner's exception is a *delegated* thumbnail collaborator, not a doctrine.

### P4 — Native YouTube thumbnail A/B (A/B/C) testing — `format-agnostic`

Four creators explicitly use YouTube's built-in thumbnail testing, treating it as standard equipment:

- **veritasium** — "we probably have 20, 30, 50 different thumbnail options… YouTube will allow you to ABC test now" — [Fireside Lisbon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PEzwH50tyPw); includes the "asteroids" title/thumbnail rework that he says took a video from ~1.5M to 12–14M+ views.
- **mkbhd** — "YouTube also has an A/B/C thumbnail-testing feature… three different thumbnails with the same title" — [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/).
- **airrack** — "They've given us so much mobility with… A-B split testing, with title split testing. There's a reason that those are there. And if you're not taking them seriously, you're handicapping yourself" — [Comeback Interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtMudMODlWU).
- **johnny-harris** — "now that there's AB testing we want to have very different directions so we can test" — [David Perell interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM).

**Thin:** ryan-trahan mentions "ABC testing" only in passing ([Colin and Samir 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CQE2E88QJes)) — not counted toward the bar.

### P5 — Front-loaded opens: no intros, win the first seconds — `format-agnostic`

Five creators independently converge on cutting throat-clearing and treating the opening seconds as the decisive unit:

- **mrbeast** — "the first minute has the most loss… we freak out so much about the first minute" ([Production Guide](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)); "first five, 10 seconds" on [Lex Fridman #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).
- **veritasium** — "My number one thing… no intros… You need to be straight into it… people make the decision to watch or not watch in the first two seconds" — [Fireside Lisbon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PEzwH50tyPw).
- **airrack** — "A video becomes exponentially less important every second that goes by… the first one second of the video is the most important second" — [Comeback Interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtMudMODlWU).
- **kurtis-conner** — cut his signature greeting because "it felt so long to get into the actual topic… just get right into the thing" — [Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE); his 2026 videos open cold on the topic after ≤10–20s of promo (e.g., [Cameron Dallas Downfall](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hQ5lRlKq454)).
- **johnny-harris** — "first lines that drop you into action… 'look at this'… I'm not saying I want to tell you the story" — [David Perell interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM).

**Counter-example:** mark-rober documents *no* open/hook formula at all — his case study marks this explicitly thin ([Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1t5oYKEn-1E) contains the closest idea, intro filmed last).

### P6 — Deliver the promise: the anti-clickbait ethic — `format-agnostic`

The other side of P3: the click must be honored, or the packaging was wrong. Five creators state it as an ethic, not just tactics:

- **mrbeast** — the bouncy-castle color-mismatch example: "You'd feel like you were lied to and click off" — [Production Guide](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).
- **mkbhd** — "if the title and thumbnail are incredible, but the video does not deliver, YouTube actually learns from that… you have to deliver on both" — [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/).
- **veritasium** — pulls his own team back from "too clickbaity" in Slack: "make it more accurate" — [Fireside Lisbon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PEzwH50tyPw).
- **drew-gooden** — the whole pacing essay argues against padding and retention tricks (he names and rejects fake progress bars): "their time is just something for me to leverage… that's a little shitty" — [Everybody wants to waste your time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=56EyKNjhUDY).
- **ryan-trahan** — "redemptive work… rather than trying to exploit them to stay watching for a few more seconds" — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/ryan-trahan-thumbnail-editing-secrets-to-going-viral/).

## Scripting, research, and structure

### P1 — Scripted, not improvised — `format-agnostic`

Host-led formats converge on full scripting; the case studies document the switch from unscripted to scripted as a deliberate upgrade:

- **drew-gooden** — "I'm writing every word of my videos" (after the unscripted-rant era that "would take me like two weeks to edit") — [Anthony Padilla](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8t_JqQXg5qQ).
- **kurtis-conner** — "once I started scripting my videos it opened up… confidence… I do my best work when I am planning" — [Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).
- **mkbhd** — "I write 90 something% of what you're hearing me say… I've written everything" — [Cortex #174](https://relay.fm/cortex/174).
- **mina-le** — "meticulously crafted as opposed to ad-libbed… Le writes herself a detailed script" — [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/style/mina-le-youtuber-fashion-history-interview).
- **johnny-harris** — scripting week = "me in a room completely engrossed… writing and visually directing" — [David Perell interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM).
- **wendover-productions** — Sam still scripts and voices the flagship himself ("about half of my working time goes to writing those scripts") — [Oxford Union](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg).

**Counter-example (format boundary):** the challenge/build formats script differently — mark-rober shoots from "general bullet points" and finds the story in the edit ([Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1t5oYKEn-1E)); airrack plans A-plot/bits, not verbatim scripts. The pattern holds across essay, review, and commentary formats; it is not a universal rule.

### P2 — Protected, scheduled writing time — `format-agnostic`

Three creators turn writing into a defended calendar block rather than an ambient task:

- **johnny-harris** — "my entire world is framed around a writing block from 9 to 2:00 p.m.… no meetings… no slack… those are sacred" — [David Perell interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM).
- **drew-gooden** — "I really need to be my own parent… set a schedule… sit at your computer start writing" — [Anthony Padilla](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8t_JqQXg5qQ).
- **wendover-productions** — "about half of my working time goes to writing those scripts… across a two-week cycle" — [Oxford Union](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg).

### P13 — Exhaustive research plus fact-checking infrastructure — `format-agnostic`

Essay formats build research and verification systems that look like journalism:

- **mina-le** — "I'll literally read every single article about it that's published by a reputable source" + JSTOR/Semantic Scholar, librarian-curated guides, NYPL — [Washington Post Creator](https://wpcreator.washingtonpost.com/p/creator-q-a-mina-le) and [Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2023/4/11/mina-le-youtube-fashion-social-commentary-style-influencer/).
- **johnny-harris** — researcher spends 3–4 weeks building a 60–80-page info doc; every script assertion carries fact-check citation comments turned into a public source dock — [David Perell interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM).
- **veritasium** — "We run it past [multiple experts]… post early versions to our Patreon supporters… access to a legal team" for high-stakes topics — [The Future of Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=piHGnG4LsmQ).
- **wendover-productions** — pipeline "research → script → animation → edit → fact-check" with named specialist roles — second-hand synthesis of the paywalled [Nebula BTS video](https://nebula.tv/videos/wendover-productions-behind-the-scenes-at-wendover-productions) via [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Wendover_Productions), corroborated first-party on research intensity at [Oxford Union](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg). *Flagged: the pipeline nouns rest on a second-hand summary of a paywalled first-party video.*

### P12 — Repeatable formats, series, and segments — `format-specific` (serialized challenge/game formats)

Three creators — all in serialized challenge formats — build retention out of repeatable containers rather than one-off ideas:

- **mrbeast** — "One way to boost retention… is to have a good format" (last-to-leave, stair-stepping, chase formats) — [Production Guide](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf).
- **airrack** — "if it doesn't have a follow-up, we're not making it… Good ideas are marked by there's more content in there than you can make with one video" (buckets & branching) — [Comeback Interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtMudMODlWU).
- **ryan-trahan** — repeatable daily segments ("jammy time or the game plan") plus a two-layer arc: daily resolution + cumulative master arc — [Colin and Samir 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CQE2E88QJes) (the "Double Arc" label itself comes from second-hand [Gimbal Blog](https://gimbalblog.com/how-ryan-trahans-team-creates-daily-series-that-actually-work)).

**Why format-specific:** this does not surface in the essay/commentary/review case studies (drew-gooden, kurtis-conner, mina-le, mkbhd document no series containers). Adjacent case: wendover-productions' Jet Lag seasons ([Oxford Union](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg)) — a game-show format on a second channel, consistent with the tag.

## Cadence and sustainability

### P7 — Deliberately capped, sustainable cadence — `format-agnostic`

Six creators document *lowering* or capping output as a strategy, not a failure:

- **mark-rober** — "I'm very protective of my treadmill speed… keep it at a jogging speed" (~10 videos/year) — [Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1t5oYKEn-1E) and [TED WorkLife](https://www.ted.com/podcasts/ted.com/podcasts/why-chasing-the-algorithm-leads-to-burnout-with-mark-rober-transcript).
- **drew-gooden** — "I do one a month… I think it's important to just have kind of recharge a little bit" — [Anthony Padilla](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8t_JqQXg5qQ).
- **ryan-trahan** — "if I want to do this for 20 years… two videos a month has been that for now" (Tom Brady longevity analogy) — [Colin and Samir 2022](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wkDlfvTed1c).
- **johnny-harris** — dialed back from 48 to 30 videos/year after 17 concurrent videos "started to suck the magic out of me" — [David Perell interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM).
- **linus-tech-tips** — "we have officially killed the haven't missed an upload in 10 plus years mentality" — [Here's the plan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qAE5KoyFEUo).
- **mrbeast** — weekly ambition ("I want to make one a week lol" — [Production Guide](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)) settled to "one to two longform videos monthly" by 2025 — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/mrbeast-how-production-team-makes-youtube-videos-2025-8).

### P8 — Burnout is managed with structure and recharge, not wellness protocols — `format-agnostic`

Seven creators give burnout answers that are *structural or personal-rhythm* solutions; the case studies uniformly flag "no wellness SOP documented":

- **mark-rober** — treadmill/jogging metaphor + Super Mario Effect (gamify failure so retries are cheap) — [Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1t5oYKEn-1E).
- **mrbeast** — "you need to either be working or… doing something that decompresses and recharges you… You're just a ticking time bomb" — [Lex Fridman #351](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z3_PwvvfxIU).
- **drew-gooden** — don't let the channel be your identity; "you have to live a life to be able to make a joke about it" — [Anthony Padilla](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8t_JqQXg5qQ).
- **mina-le** — names the "psychological terror" of deadlines, then contains it: kinder self-talk, apartment work/life zoning, permitted boredom — [NYLON](https://www.nylon.com/fashion/mina-le-gremlita-tiktok-fashion) and [Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2023/4/11/mina-le-youtube-fashion-social-commentary-style-influencer/).
- **ryan-trahan** — off-season logic: "they even get an off season" — [Colin and Samir 2022](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wkDlfvTed1c).
- **mkbhd** — "I am not the subject… I don't feel burnt out as long as I'm playing with new tech" (structural role design) — [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/).
- **veritasium** — precariousness, not hours, is the enemy; the fix was diversifying income and offloading operations — [The Future of Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=piHGnG4LsmQ).

### P17 — Craft and quality prioritized over algorithm-chasing — `format-agnostic`

Five creators explicitly frame their filter as *not* optimization:

- **mark-rober** — "radical sort of focus on quality over quantity" as the anti-algorithm — [TED WorkLife](https://www.ted.com/podcasts/ted.com/podcasts/why-chasing-the-algorithm-leads-to-burnout-with-mark-rober-transcript).
- **drew-gooden** — "Make a retrospective about some niche hobby you had as a kid… Remember this website is called YouTube not do the thing that you think will become popular tube" — [Everybody wants to waste your time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=56EyKNjhUDY).
- **johnny-harris** — "we definitely play the game but craftsmanship is number one"; data-driven rabbit holes "never… become the heart of our strategy" — [David Perell interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM).
- **ryan-trahan** — "Our content now is doing better than when we were trying to strive for profit or views or retention" — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/ryan-trahan-thumbnail-editing-secrets-to-going-viral/).
- **mina-le** — "I only want to make a video if I feel inspired… isn't looking to chase the short trend cycle" — [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/input/style/mina-le-youtuber-fashion-history-interview) and [Polyester](https://www.polyesterzine.com/features/mina-le-is-writing-fashion-history).

### P18 — The algorithm as a boss you only partially understand — `format-agnostic`

Five creators describe a negotiated relationship with an opaque system — neither ignorance nor worship:

- **kurtis-conner** — "the algorithm and stuff does sort of like dictate what I'm doing but… it's the game you got to play" — [Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).
- **wendover-productions** — "the boss is the YouTube algorithm… we have about a 70% accurate view on how the algorithm works" — [Oxford Union](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg).
- **drew-gooden** — "The algorithm is teaching me that I shouldn't cut things out… They'd prefer if I stretched out every video" — documented and *refused* — [Everybody wants to waste your time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=56EyKNjhUDY).
- **johnny-harris** — "we definitely play the game but craftsmanship is number one" — [David Perell interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM).
- **mkbhd** — "their algorithms are optimizing for audience happiness… you have to deliver on both" — [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/).

## Teams, delegation, and pipeline

### P9 — Delegate editing/post-production, keep the voice through review gates — `format-agnostic`

The most widely shared structural pattern (8 of 12): post-production is handed off, and voice consistency is preserved by a named gate (review, checklist, or alternation), not by the founder editing:

- **wendover-productions** — offloaded "the entirety" of post-production ("my brain just does not mesh with editing software") — [Oxford Union](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg).
- **linus-tech-tips** — Script Review with Linus keeps "all our content… a consistent style" while he wrote "<25% of our videos" — [How Our Videos are Made](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LtNdKoXbZMw) and [How We Make 17 Videos a Week](https://linustechtips.com/topic/1344340-how-we-make-17-videos-a-week/).
- **johnny-harris** — researchers/editors/animators pipeline behind a scripting week he owns — [David Perell interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM) and [Join The Team](https://www.johnnyharris.ch/join-the-team).
- **mina-le** — "a team of one other person… she's really created a style that… is true to my own aesthetic eye" — [Washington Post Creator](https://wpcreator.washingtonpost.com/p/creator-q-a-mina-le).
- **ryan-trahan** — Zach Levet + Cohen Thompson edit the daily 20–30 min episodes overnight — [Colin and Samir 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CQE2E88QJes).
- **airrack** — editors as storytellers (mics + cameras at desk) with nightly paper edits — [Comeback Interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wtMudMODlWU).
- **veritasium** — 30+ staff (writer/directors, researchers, illustrators, animators, editors) behind Derek's scripting — [The Future of Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=piHGnG4LsmQ).
- **mkbhd** — alternated editors (Mariah/me) with notes on the last 20% — [Cortex #174](https://relay.fm/cortex/174).

**Documented counter-example:** mark-rober refuses this pattern — "would you ever let someone else find a story for you? no no absolutely not" — [Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1t5oYKEn-1E). Solo creators (drew-gooden, kurtis-conner) are the degenerate case of the same trade-off.

### P10 — The founder holds the idea/voice core while the team scales production — `format-agnostic`

Four creators name an explicit "things I will never delegate" list at the front of the pipeline:

- **mark-rober** — story and edit are the "secret sauce and the heart"; builds are outsourceable — [Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1t5oYKEn-1E) and [Video Production Daily](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/what-makes-mark-rober-so-successful-with-mark-rober/id1500470401?i=1000515559114).
- **wendover-productions** — "if I'm leading a team of writers, I have to be a writer myself… it's a somewhat opinion-based channel, so it feels like it should come from me"; his job moved upstream to concept — [Publish Press](https://publishpress.squarespace.com/staffpicks23-samdenby) and [Oxford Union](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q8k3qV8Aqg).
- **mkbhd** — "those three hearts are being on camera, writing, and reviewing technology" — [Think with Google](https://business.google.com/us/think/search-and-video/marques-brownlee-interview/).
- **linus-tech-tips** — Linus as the single-node style gate (Script Review) despite writing a minority of scripts — [How Our Videos are Made](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LtNdKoXbZMw).

### P14 — Pipeline and project-management tooling for visibility — `format-agnostic`

Four creators document a shared status system that makes the pipeline legible to everyone:

- **linus-tech-tips** — Excel tracker → monday.com with status-triggered automation: "Before we had management involved, it was just chaos" — [How We Make 17 Videos a Week](https://linustechtips.com/topic/1344340-how-we-make-17-videos-a-week/) and [ONE Day](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X3KLyDWPpEM).

## Single-creator tactics (NOT patterns)

Tactics documented by exactly **one** creator. They are real and sourced, but one swallow is not a pattern — do not treat them as cross-creator findings:

- **mrbeast** — CTR/AVD/AVP metric triad ([Production Guide](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/25146014/how-to-succeed-at-mrbeast-production.pdf)); minute-mark architecture with 3/6-minute re-engagements; "wow factor" as a fourth, untrackable metric; kill-formats rule ("every channel that rehashes formats for years always dies"); "work on multiple videos EVERYDAY"; abrupt endings; brand deals integrated as content; critical-components protection with backup days.
- **mark-rober** — build-then-story: film intro LAST so it matches what actually happened ([Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1t5oYKEn-1E)); Super Mario Effect experiment (5 vs 12 retries when failure is penalized).
- **airrack** — one-word brand filter ("mischief") + shoe-swapping test; nightly voice-note → Claude paper edit; editors with mics/cameras as storytellers; sawdust businesses; 4-day producer prep + one-day in-and-out shoot ([Prank Breakdown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LMi_s4fEyAs)).
- **veritasium** — misconception-first pedagogy (PhD thesis result: confusion-dialogue doubled accuracy vs clear lecture); problem→solution, never thesis→evidence; power-law doctrine ("97% of videos <10k views… figure out how to be in that 0.1%") — all [Fireside Lisbon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PEzwH50tyPw).
- **drew-gooden** — the respect-the-viewer's-time ethic as a standalone essay ([Everybody wants to waste your time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=56EyKNjhUDY)); post-publish comment ritual.
- **kurtis-conner** — editing-as-writing-blocks analogy; the thumbnail "cottage industry" observation — both [Colin and Samir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KWs18nxodNE).
- **johnny-harris** — two-column visual script ("every sentence or two has a new row"); fact-check citation comments → public source dock; two-voices pacing (explainer ↔ contemplative); fresh art direction per video; 150-notes cut chain — all [David Perell interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zq4b96m1AvM).
- **mkbhd** — two audience buckets (write for the searcher, entertain the rest); the "playoffs" (experiment Jan–Mar, lock Aug–Jan); 6:02 top-of-feed timing; mainline-the-device research; alternated editors; comment pulse after 30 minutes — [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2024/an-interview-with-marques-brownlee-mkbhd-about-being-a-youtube-star/) and [Cortex #174](https://relay.fm/cortex/174).

## Gaps and honest limits

- **Three shortlisted creators have no case study** (tom-scott, colin-and-samir, matt-davella) and are excluded from everything above. If they are ever deep-dived, P3/P5/P7 counts could rise — their shortlist metadata suggests packaging and cadence docs exist — but that is inference, not evidence, and is not counted.
- **Retention analytics are thin almost everywhere.** Only mrbeast documents a full metric doctrine (CTR/AVD/AVP). Six case studies explicitly mark retention dashboards as not documented; several others (airrack, johnny-harris) describe retention *reasoning* without numbers. Any cross-creator claim about "retention-data-driven workflows" would be false for most of this corpus.
- **Editing rhythm is the thinnest axis.** No case study documents cuts-per-minute, sound design, or color pipelines first-party. Pacing claims are ethics, heuristics, or analogies — not specs.
- **Wendover's pipeline nouns** (P13) rest on a second-hand synthesis of a paywalled first-party video; the case study flags this and so does this document.
- **Survivorship bias:** these are winners' workflows (see README methodology). Patterns here are *correlated with* success in this sample, not proven to cause it.
