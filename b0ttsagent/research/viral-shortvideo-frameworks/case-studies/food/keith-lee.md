## Workflow

### 0. Origin — anxiety therapy → cooking for a pregnant wife → reviews (2019–2022)

Keith Lee was a professional MMA fighter ("Killa", 8-5 record) whose career required public interviews he could not handle due to social anxiety — "I was so nervous. I was sweaty. It would mess up my entire day just to hear that I had to do an interview" [NYTM]. He started TikTok **as exposure therapy**: "I started recording myself on TikTok, and I would pretend it was like 100 people watching me when I had like three followers" [BL]; he told NYT Magazine he pretended "1,000 people watching me" [NYTM]. Content began as general/life videos [RS], became **cooking videos for his pregnant wife Ronni** ("my wife was pregnant... at first it started with me going to get her pregnancy cravings. And then it turned into me cooking... I would cook for myself four times a day and I would cook my wife four times a day") [BC], and at his wife's suggestion pivoted to restaurant reviews [SX-EA]. First food review: December 2022 [SX-SP]; at that point he had ~1.1M followers, was on food stamps and working as a DoorDash/Postmates driver — "We were still trying to figure out how to make enough money to even buy the food to make the food videos" [SX-EA] [NYTM]. Growth: 1M took roughly a year [NYTM] [RS]; reviews started Dec 2022 at 1.1M [SX-SP]; the Frankenson's Pizzeria review took him 1M → 4.6M in two weeks [SX-DV]; 8M by early 2023 [NYTM]; "It took me less than nine months to get to fifteen million" once reviews began [BC]. Career timeline caveats (Mar 2020 vs Nov 2020 vs 11 years incl. Vine) — see Caveats & Contradictions.

### 1. Restaurant-selection funnel — invite-only → mass-request pool → vet → pray

**2023 (invite-only era)** [BC]:
- "I only go to places that I'm invited to... if you invite me, you invite me on the pretenses that you know what it is that I do" — restaurants reach out via **email/DM, and he shows the DMs or emails on camera** as proof of invitation ("I always show the DMs or the emails that I get directly from whoever tells me to go") [BC].
- Unsolicited visits provoke deflection ("we didn't ask him to come here, he just gave his unsolicited opinion") [BC]. New York exception: he only visits places **recommended to him 30+ times** by the audience ("I only go to places that I've been recommended to over thirty times") [BC].
- The first breakthrough, Francis's Piece of Redemption (Las Vegas), arrived via **owner email**: "we're about to close those doors... we just want you to come and eat the food" — within 12 hours of posting, the restaurant went from two customers/day to a line around the corner [BC] [ES documents the Frankensons/Frank Steele equivalent].

**2025 (mass-request era)** — funnel formalized [BL] [EI]:
- A single city-announcement post generates **10,000–30,000 requests within the first hour** [BL]; "we're actually getting tens of thousands of recommendations, not thousands" [EI].
- **Vetting pass**: "I reach out to the people who are from the city and from the food scene within that city... brush through the list with them... go through with a fine-tooth comb... I also talk to my family... and at the end of the day, **we pray about it** and we go to the ones that we feel like make the most sense" [BL]; "we vet the comments by doing our own research... for the most part, I just pray about it and go where I feel like I'm supposed to go" [EI].
- He deliberately does **not** credit specific recommenders on camera — "that person will get the brunt of the internet's wrath" if the visit goes badly [EI].
- Selection target is intentional, not prestige-driven: "I don't necessarily highlight the best food in each city... I specifically find places that are underprivileged, undermarketed, places that are in food deserts. And we go to those places absolutely free" [SX-EA]; family-owned, often Black-owned mom-and-pops with no marketing budget [BC] [CSS] [EAT] [BA].

### 2. Review-integrity protocol — "the same surface to everybody"

The integrity protocol is the load-bearing wall of the format; it is documented across every first-party source:
- **Family orders anonymously; he pays for everything**: "my family goes in and they get the customer service experience for me... I pay for everything... I'm a customer at the end of the day and I want to experience what everybody else experiences" [BC]. Corroborated: family member orders and collects takeout under a different name [BA] [EAT] [DMN] ("orders takeout under a different name to ensure he gets the same experience as other customers" [DMN]).
- **Refuses comps**: he turns down free food/upgrades — "I walk out with 35 bags of free food. That's never been my MO" [BC]. No payment from restaurants: "I don't take any money. I don't take any endorsement deals from these small companies" [BC]; "I specifically do it for the restaurants and I don't charge them a dime" [CSS]; people told him he could charge $5,000–$10,000 per restaurant video [CSS].
- **Wait-time fairness ("same surface")**: the defining 2023 Atlanta incident — his family was told a 2–2.5-hour wait; when he walked in, he was seated instantly; he walked out and waited in the car: "I'm willing to wait in a three-hour wait time to four-hour wait times. I just want the same surface to everybody else" [BC]. He never skips lines [BC]. At the 2024 Club Shay Shay taping he re-articulated the tour's purpose in these terms: "it's about going out and trying food that my mom can go in the next day and get the same experience that we got" [CSS].
- **Constructive negative reviews**: he does post negative reviews, deliberately — "if I only gave positive reviews... then it wouldn't hold any weight. It only holds weight if you know I'm being honest... regardless if you agree with my taste buds" [BC]; "He is direct with his praise, and gentle with his critiques. A dessert too sweet? He's quick to explain that it might just not be his preference" [RS]. He cut the 2024 Bay Area tour short rather than release a pile of negative reviews: "I've never been big on completely tearing down anybody" [BA].
- **Opinion-framing disclaimer**: "I always try to pre-emphasize that this is my opinion, this is the way I taste, and if you want to go try it... absolutely go try it" [BC]; repeated at Club Shay Shay: "my opinion isn't the end-all be-all" [CSS].
- **Decimal ratings** (e.g., 7.3/10, 9.4/10) with taste/texture/price breakdowns [EAT] [DMN] [RS].

### 3. Capture & format — the car-review template

- Set: car front seat, takeout bag, camera on [RS] [EAT] [DMN]. (NYC critique noted the car format warps walking cities — his own 2023-24 acknowledgment via coverage [EAT].)
- Open: staccato catchphrase "I got it, let's try it, and rate it one through 10" [RS] [DMN] [SX-DV].
- Length: standard 1–3 minutes [DMN]; "quick-paced monotone" rapid-fire delivery [EAT] [SX-EA].
- Structure: show food → price → per-dish taste/comment micro-ratings → customer-service note → overall rating — "The audience then knows he's going to show the food, tell them the price, rate the customer service, and give his overall review of the experience. The familiarity and consistency keep his audience gripped and coming back" [SX-DV].
- Ritual: "Before he films his burger review, he prays three times... 'I thank him for the courage to not be an asshole but still be honest'" [RS].
- Editing/structure can intentionally defer payoff: one restaurant owner reported the video flatlined until the sauce-reveal mid-video ("you have to actually watch the entire video to actually understand what happened" — everything ordered on the side, sauces applied later) [BA].

### 4. Posting & cadence — burst-batch tour model

- Cadence is **tour-batch driven**, not daily: 9 restaurants reviewed across Dallas–Fort Worth in Jan–Feb 2024 [DMN]; up to 12 establishments in a day on some tour days [VAR/NI]; "we go to restaurants almost every other day" [EI]; ~250+ restaurants reviewed from Dec 2022 through Mar 2025 [SX-SP]; 30+ cities across 10 countries by early 2026 [VAR/NI].
- Recent quantified rhythm (2026): 24 eligible videos across ~4 months (2026-04-13 → 2026-08-03), median ~1.55M views — roughly 1.5 videos/week average, consistent with tour bursts plus quiet gaps [BRIEF].
- No time-of-day posting rule documented anywhere in the sources reviewed [N/A — no source].
- Analytics philosophy (his CURRENT stated view, SXSW 2025): "I will have times where all I'm doing is watching analytics, and I'll have times where I don't pay attention to them at all... there's a duality to it... If you operate from a palace of desperation, you'll stay in a place of desperation" [SX-EA]; "sometimes all he does is watch analytics. Other times, he completely ignores them. Both methods can be problematic" [SX-EA].

### 5. Tour scaling — family entourage + proof-to-TV-producers motive

- The multi-city food tour was launched **deliberately**, in response to TV producers offering "cookie-cutter" hosting deals: "they kept referencing Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives... we just want to make you the host of this show that's already created" — so the family "hit the road on our own... to show these TV producers that this can work in every market," accumulating "concrete evidence and concrete facts of how many businesses we've been blessed enough to help" [BC].
- Tour purpose (his words): "when we go for the food tour it's not to find what the best food is — it's specifically to highlight the restaurants that have great food, great customer service, but can't market themselves" — food deserts, location-based, or owners too busy cooking to market [CSS].
- Family is the operating crew: "my family moves with me everywhere, so I want to make sure my family is involved in anything that I do" [BC]; the 2026 Apple TV+ show "Keith Lee: All In The Familee" and the Vox Media weekly podcast both operationalize the family-tour unit [APTV] [VAR].
- Iteration: "redemption tour" return visits after controversy (Atlanta) [NYTM]; re-review offers after incidents (Seattle sushi closure — "I'm not opposed of going back if I'm welcomed") [CSS-Rosetta].
- Documented demand-side outcomes ("Keith Lee Effect"): Puddery 5–10 customers/day → 130 the day after [BA]; Joy Burgers 24 → 6,000 → 120,000 TikTok followers and 10 hires [BA]; the Spot Truk doubled [BA]; Luxe Box sold out by 5pm vs 7pm [BA]; four-hour waits week one, sales quadrupled [BA]; Aroma (Las Vegas) pulled back from closing [BLM]; Frankensons (Las Vegas) business explosion [ES]; one restaurant saw **no boost** (structure-dependent) [BA]; D-FW one-year follow-up: >half saw sales increases, CeeZoos declined then pivoted [DMN]; NOLA Sips and Sweet Thangs saved from closure [AT].

### 6. Monetization — free to restaurants, selective with brands

- **Restaurants: never paid.** "I don't take anything on the back end... What I get out of it is knowing that whatever I put in the world, I get back" [BC]; "only takes money and brand deals from 'big brands' that can afford it" — Chipotle, Pepsi, Hyundai [FB]; full brand trail: Pizza Hut, Chipotle, DoorDash, Gatorade, Wingstop, Microsoft [NI] [BRIEF].
- **Brand-deal criteria** (SXSW 2025): "I only go to places who have a marketing budget to the side. But past that, I specifically go to places that we actually use on a day to day basis" [SX-DV]; "he only works with brands who have an Influencer Marketing budget set aside to pay creators fairly, and... brands he genuinely uses in his daily life" — DoorDash is the archetype (he was a Dasher in 2020) [SX-DV].
- **Declined big money on integrity grounds**: turned down a ~$1M fast-food offer because he doesn't eat that food — "they asked me do I eat that food and I don't. Instead of saying, 'Of course I eat it' trying to get the money... I told him straight to his face I was like, 'No I don't'" [CSS via EU]. NOTE: Keith's own quote never names the chain; the McDonald's attribution exists only in second-hand headlines [HU] — see Caveats.
- Merch with deliberately low prices: companies suggested $180–190 hoodies / $120–130 tees; he charged $75 / $45 ["the profit isn't the main goal"] [BC].
- Generosity as brand asset: ~$300K given out to small businesses in year one; 150 families helped [CSS]; $40K raised for a cancer-stricken owner and $50K for a food truck owner in under 24h each [BC]; $150K Toast grant program (2025) [EI].
- Infrastructure 2025–26: UTA signing + FamiLEE Media (production, consumer products, live events, touring) [THR]; Vox Media weekly video podcast (spring 2026) [VAR]; Apple TV+ docuseries "Keith Lee: All In The Familee" (2026) [APTV]; Digital Voices Creator of the Year (2025) [SX-SP]; early income note: ~$150/day TikTok Creator Fund expectation in 2023 (historical) [EAT].

## Caveats & Contradictions

1. **Start-date inconsistency (minor).** Wikipedia (via mission brief) says career started Mar 2020; Spectrum/SXSW intro says TikTok started Nov 2020 [SX-SP]; Keith at SXSW said he has "been making and posting videos online for about 11 years," starting on Vine and as an "Instagram comedian" [SX-EA]; Keith's own SXSW answer for when reviews began: "I started in 2022" [SX-DV/transcript]. All sources agree the review pivot was 2022, but account-origin dates vary. Treat "started 2020–2021, reviews Dec 2022" as the safe reading.
2. **First-frame timing is unmeasured.** Every source describes the catchphrase as the opening beat ("begins each review with his signature phrase" [DMN]), but none provides frame-accurate timing. `first 0–1s` is therefore descriptive, not verified to the frame.
3. **Curiosity gap is inferred, not stated.** The "will it be a good rating?" tension is structurally implied by the catchphrase promise ("rate it one through 10") + end-loaded ratings [EAT] [SX-DV], but Lee never articulates a retention theory. Any mechanism analysis beyond payoff density/format consistency is inference.
4. **~$1M declined offer — chain attribution problem.** Keith's direct quote (via Eurweb) never names the chain: "they asked me do I eat that food and I don't" [EU]. The McDonald's name appears only in second-hand headlines [HU] and "allegedly" phrasing. Treat chain identity as unverified.
5. **"I pay for everything" vs sponsored content.** Keith says he pays for all restaurant meals [BC] and takes restaurant money from nobody [CSS], yet Digital Voices documents sponsored videos for DoorDash (his own Dasher-era employer) [SX-DV], and Forbes confirms big-brand deals [FB]. The reconciliation — small restaurants free, big brands paid — is explicitly his stance [FB] [SX-DV], but the boundary case (a sponsored review where the brand IS the food/delivery platform) deserves flagging.
6. **The Effect is not uniform — and is double-edged.** Bon Appétit documents one restaurant where the video produced no sustained boost [BA]; D-FW follow-up shows decline cases (CeeZoos lost its chef post-review) [DMN]; fans have spammed/harassed restaurants, prompting Keith to publicly ask followers to "be normal" [EAT] [NTA]; the Atlanta trip drew death threats and Keith threatened to end tours [CSS] [NYTM]. Coverage of negative dynamics exists in every era.
7. **Follower-growth claims conflict mildly.** "1M in about a year" [NYTM] vs "a year or two to get to a million" [BC]; "15M in less than nine months" [BC] vs "8M by early 2023" [NYTM] — same trajectory, imprecise bookkeeping.
8. **Transcript fidelity.** The iHeart transcript is auto-generated (garbled in places: "same surface" for what is clearly "same service/experience") and the Rosetta CSS clips are community transcripts. All load-bearing quotes are cross-corroborated by independent outlets (Bon Appétit, Eater, Dallas News), so risk is limited but real for verbatim wording.
9. **Mild promotional contexts.** The Digital Voices SXSW recap is the agency's own blog (his campaign partner) [SX-DV]; the Eater interview ran adjacent to the Toast grant announcement [EI]; Blavity Fest is a stage interview at a festival he was headlining [BL]. None appear to pay-for-quote, but none are neutral observers either.
10. **Recency drift in his 2023 statements.** The invite-only + "recommended 30+ times" funnel [BC] was superseded by the mass-request + vetting + prayer funnel [BL] [EI] as his volume grew. Both are first-party; the 2025 version is the current one.

## New Terms

Proposed additions to the mission vocabulary (descriptive ecosystem terms, not closed-list schema values):
