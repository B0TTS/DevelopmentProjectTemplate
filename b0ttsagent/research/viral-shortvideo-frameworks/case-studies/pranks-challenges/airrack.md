## Workflow

### 1) Idea → pitch → filter (weekly meeting, paid pitch system)
Ideas arrive in a **weekly pitch meeting** with a few people; Airrack is the sole decider of what gets posted and is "looking for high quality inspiration, like a really good seed" — most meetings produce nothing. Pitchers (team + paid outside creators — "I'll find other smaller YouTubers that I like their ideas and then I'll just pay them to pitch me ideas") must submit a **pitch deck: (a) three thumbnail stories, (b) a breakdown of the video, (c) a "how" — how does this video actually exist in the physical world?** The questions self-eliminate 9/10 ideas before they reach him. [S1]

Ideas then pass the **one-word brand filter**: his brand word is **"mischief"** — "if you can't explain your brand in one word, you're already done for." A video must be mischievous AND end with everyone laughing ("chaotic good"). Rejected examples: dark-web ordering (evil, not playful), LA underground celebrity tunnels (no physical-world how), "ranked most painful insect bites" (great for Coyote Peterson, wrong brand — "terrible video for me"), trick shots (Dude Perfect's video), deadly-militaries testing (another creator's lane). Cross-creator **"shoes-swapping" / "identify and innovate"**: same topic (island, Disney, hotel) mapped through each creator's one word — Mr. Beast = spectacle, Ryan Trahan = quirky, Airrack = "I snuck onto secret billionaire private islands." [S1]

**2021-22 precursor (superseded):** the "Creative Allegiance" — an external group pitching Airrack ideas in exchange for a share of the video's AdSense if produced. [S4]

### 2) Format systems ("buckets") + the follow-up rule
Every greenlit idea must fit a repeatable **bucket/series**: "I secretly lived in…" (grocery store, water park, mall), "I hid in streams/videos," "instant karma" (scam scammers), pranks. **"If it doesn't have a follow-up, we're not making it"** — one-off 100M-view potential is not enough; "making new videos every single week… you're purposefully living on hard mode" (which he says he did for years). Good ideas are "marked by there's more content in there than you can make with one video." Buckets **cross-breed**: "I secretly lived in Kai Cenat's house" = two proven ideas merged → "instead of taking bets that are 87% likely to work, you're taking like 99% shot." Series iteration compounds: location choice optimized for reusability (picked a mall whose security-camera system matched past shoots over American Dream's 1,200 cameras); "the stories get better because you build lore on top of them." [S1] Series structure pre-dates the 2025 system: 40 of his 128 videos (31%) were series as of 2022-04-29, pitched as an algorithm play ("more likely to recommend… you'll binge part one, two and three"). [S4]

### 3) A-plot discipline (his anti-B-plot rule)
**"A-plots and B-plots… completely overrated. If you require a Bplot, your Aplot sucks. It's all about the Aplot."** Definition: the A-plot is the action the title/thumbnail promised (e.g., secretly living in a grocery store); any action must answer "is my action right now related to me secretly living in a grocery store?" B-plots (his example: a mango-eating side quest) are distractions that multiply editing cost and kill the promise. On-brand "bits" are allowed only when **directly correlated to the A-plot** (the girlfriend-date scene: sneaking her in through the back door = living-in-the-store content). "A plot equals the title on YouTube." [S1] This directly contradicts the "stack your plots, cut fast" doctrine he says was common a few years ago — he names his own older podcast advice as the source of viewers' B-plot messes. [S1]

### 4) Intro improvement process (first-second rule + split testing)
Intros are a standing obsession: he reports a monthly ritual with other top YouTubers whose conclusion "every month… is that the title and the intro are more important than they thought they were last month." Mechanics: (a) **first-second rule** — "a video becomes exponentially less important every second that goes by… the first one second of the video is the most important second of the entire video"; the second-1-to-2-min window decides whether the viewer is earned; (b) keep intros **tighter** than he used to and **representative of the content**; (c) **A/B split testing** on thumbnails and titles via YouTube's tools — "we can split test into infinity… live in pretty close to the most optimal reality"; (d) title/thumbnail must read as the brand ("does this feel like an Airrack video?") because sponsors don't watch videos — "some brands only read, they don't watch." Example intro he flags as good: "Over the past three months, I've tracked down four real life scammers. And today, using some ingenious methods, I'm going to be giving them a little taste of their own medicine." [S1]

### 5) Production / stunt execution
**Budget discipline (2025):** videos average **~$6,000, "under $10,000"** — versus 2023-24 videos "averaging around two to three million views… costing above six figures." Rule: "the more expensive an idea becomes, it's usually a worse idea… you're compensating for not brainstorming enough by spending more money." Cost case studies: walking-on-water prank cost $100K, earned $166K total (48M views) — a loss-making "perfect video" era he calls "a losing game"; the trapped-YouTubers box videos ran six figures each with 15 editors for a month; a chartered-airplane stunt cost $150K (plane never showed; reshot; no follow-up possible — "even if that video wins, what's the upside?"). Fixed costs are banned from the mindset: "taking on big fixed costs as a YouTuber is like the dumbest thing you can possibly do… YouTube is a speedboat, not a cruise ship." [S1]

**Stunt production specifics (2025-12 BTS, "arresting KSI" prank):** documented chapter flow = How Airrack preps for videos → Hiring real cops → Setting up the cameras → Inside man → Going undercover → "He has no idea this is happening" → Finding the right details → Fighting the police → Point of no return → "Only time I get nervous" → How the sausage is made → Trying to buy Punk'd. I.e., staged escalation with an inside man, real authority figures (cops), camera placement as a production discipline, and detail-hunting (the "right details" chapter) — the same detail-obsession that shows in his costume work: 3D-scanned head mold, world-class SFX/body-paint artists on a contact list, ~100 hours in the makeup chair in 3 months, rock costume ~$3-4K with a month of prep (still "pretty expensive" by 2025 standards). [S2][S1]

### 6) Long-form → shorts cut (the distribution layer)
Airrack's **documented** pipeline is long-form-first: he optimizes "how do I make a 50 million view video," shooting ~5TB per video; leftover footage becomes the **Also Airrack** second channel (extended cuts / director-commentary, one editor remoting into a studio machine all day, 4 new intros filmed in 30 minutes — a "sawdust business" because it reuses sunk footage with near-zero marginal effort). **Shorts/clips are the clipping economy, not an in-house editing system**: he describes the industry (people "paid tens of thousands of dollars per month to clip people's content… big studios are hiring them to clip their shows and post those clips on TikTok because that's distribution now") and built **ClipFarm** to sit on top of it — connect creators who need clipping with clippers; pay-per-1,000-views campaign model; "Powered by Airrack (16M+ subscribers, 3.9B views)." His own shorts (median ~12M views on @airrack/shorts, hit rates per [S7]) are the visible result of that distribution layer. **No first-party documentation exists of Airrack's shorts cut-by-cut editing rules** — flagged as a thin spot, not invented here. [S1][S6][S7]

### 7) Posting cadence
2025 system: **weekly long-form videos** ("I'm sickeningly focused on making weekly YouTube videos until I finish it… banned from shiny objects"; "showing up every week on the platform"). Contrast 2021-22: 30-videos-in-30-days countdown series (Dec 2023), daily vlogs, "no weekends, no breaks," "we meet at 9 AM to discuss the tasks at hand" team culture — a cadence he has since walked back as unsustainable/spectacle-driven. Time-of-day rules: none documented (N/A). [S1][S4][S3]

### 8) Replication without burnout (his stated anti-burnout system)
The entire 2025 system IS the replication engine: (a) buckets + follow-up rule so no idea is a one-off; (b) reps — teams "get reps" on every repeat ("the second version or third version of video will actually get more views than the first"), including skill accrual (security-footage retrieval, camera-compatible location scouting) and relationship capital (makeup artists, cast chemistry); (c) retention-graph-driven iteration — "I just make a more optimized version of the last video every time"; (d) brand filter so he never makes videos he's not passionate about ("I'm not going to sign up to be forced to make videos that I'm not even that passionate about again"); (e) lean team ("a lot smaller than it was… two people in creative… six editors in person, two or three overseas… production two or three" vs ~20-25 at peak); (f) post-production automation: nightly voice-note → Claude prompt → scene-by-scene "paper edit" for editors; editors get mics + desk cameras to narrate voiceovers and Loom-style timeline walkthroughs for overseas editors. [S1]

## New Terms

Proposed additions to the mission vocabulary (all are Airrack's own documented concepts, outside the closed schema lists):

1. **A-plot discipline** — the rule that a video contains only the storyline the title/thumbnail promised; any secondary storyline ("B-plot") is treated as a defect ("if you require a Bplot, your Aplot sucks"). Related but distinct from hook/loop vocabulary: it governs *narrative containment*, not viewer arousal. [S1]
2. **Format system / bucket** — a named, repeatable video format with an explicit follow-up obligation ("if it doesn't have a follow-up, we're not making it"); the unit of replication is the bucket, not the video. [S1] (2022 precursor: "series content" as 31% of catalog [S4])
3. **One-word brand filter** — a single-word brand definition ("mischief") used as a pass/fail gate on every idea, plus a tonal requirement (playful, "chaotic good", everyone laughing at the end). [S1]
4. **Sawdust business** — a monetization offshoot built from existing byproducts/relationships with near-zero marginal effort (Also Airrack extended cuts from 5TB footage; ClipFarm from his creator network) — an anti-shiny-objects replication principle. [S1][S6]
5. **Retention-graph iteration** — the documented loop of re-running the same format and optimizing each sequel against retention analytics ("a more optimized version of the last video every time") — the closest thing he documents to a pattern-interrupt cadence mechanism (no fixed interval). [S1]
