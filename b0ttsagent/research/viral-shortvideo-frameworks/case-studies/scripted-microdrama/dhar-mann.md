## Workflow (deep narrative)

### 0. Origin: format discovery by failed iteration (2018–2019)

- Started May 2018 on Facebook with talking-to-camera motivational videos: first video got 125 views; took 100+ takes; "creating video after video… never see it get more than 500 views" [S11|FP|IND|2020-02-20|N-pre-window-origin-only]. Same story in his own words later: "it was a conversation, and that content was getting no views" [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y].
- Pivot instruction from his wife: **"Don't tell people, show people."** He was writing a script "again on a napkin" about infidelity when he saw his brother-in-law scrolling Facebook while his wife's friend cooked with her back turned — he filmed them doing nothing but their real actions while he narrated. First video to break 1M views: iPhone camera, studio apartment, family/friends as actors. "Later I came to find out that was considered live action filmmaking — I had no idea." ~10 other failed format iterations happened in between. [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y]; identical account in [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y] and [S13|FP|IND|2026-05-21|Y] — VERIFIED (3 first-party tellings + his own 2020 op-ed [S11]).
- Scrappy economics as the point: started with ~$600 in the bank; "if I took my entire life savings and put it into one video… I would've just spent my whole budget" — bootstrapping gave the runway to fail ~100 times. "It took me 100 videos to go viral… what if I quit on number 99?" [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y]. Corroborated in Kafka episode chapter "100 Scrappy Videos Before A Viral Breakthrough" (apartment, cheap lights, webcam) [S9|SH-of-FP|IND|2026-06-03|Y] — VERIFIED.

### 1. Ideation & script

- **Hero's-journey template**: "we tell a hero's journey… somebody who has some sort of dream… they struggle on that pathway… they find something beautiful, they learn a lesson… some door at the last second opens, some moment happens that turns their life around" — the struggle-to-last-second-prevails structure is the formula [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y].
- **Topic pool**: universal, relatable moral conflicts — infidelity, bullying, poverty, prejudice, family, judgment; "deeply family-friendly and advertiser-safe… not sanitized — it tackles real issues" [S4|SH-of-FP|IND|2025-12-25|Y]; HIBT: "bite-sized, live-action morality tales" [S1|FP(show)|IND|2024-04-29|Y].
- **Data-optimized scripts**: "Every script is optimized through data, test reads, and feedback loops. His team analyzes performance daily, adjusts thumbnails, titles, and narrative arcs in real time" [S4|SH-of-FP|IND|2025-12-25|Y] (CLAIMED, single show-notes source). His own words: "listen to comments, learn from my community, look at my analytics and see where I had falls in retention rates or why my click-through rates weren't as high as possible" [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y].
- **Green-lighting funnel (the 10:1 kill ratio)**: 10 ideas enter packaging (title only) → ~9 pass the title stage → thumbnail illustration → weekly audience survey via Google Form (audience greenlights titles/illustrations) → real thumbnail design + casting → pitch stage ("explain this in 30 seconds… has to be a page or less") → scripting → 3–4 of 5 advance → ~1 of 10 is scrapped even in post — "out of those 10 ideas, ultimately… about one that finally makes it to the YouTube page" [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y] — CLAIMED (single source, but the fullest first-party transcript).
- **Real-time concept breakdown** (the "muscular dad" example, worked live on air): evaluate packaging first — "if it's not great packaging, then by default it can't be a great story"; then character intrigue + relatability + conflict + emotional backstory + clear shareable lesson; then inciting incident, irony, resolution; then audience-platform fit ("our YouTube audience skews a lot younger… Facebook audience skews older. So I would say this would be a great video for Facebook") [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].

### 2. Hook / packaging (the click layer)

- **Pre-test packaging before production**: "I will actually publish these titles, these different thumbnails on Facebook as images and I will run a paid test… you only need about $10 or so to get accurate information"; "I'll do about a hundred different title tests"; lookalike-audience tests; results decide the final title/angle [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y] — the click-level hook is pre-sold, not improvised.
- **Deliver-on-premise rule**: "when you've figured out your packaging… deliver on that premise right away. And so right at the opening the audience should be like, 'oh, I'm here to get whatever I clicked for'"; new viewers click off "the first like 10 seconds" if the opening doesn't deliver, and the algorithm stops serving the video [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].
- **Climax-first content hook**: "Dhar Mann's stories start with the climax rather than building slowly, because social platforms demand immediate engagement. He accelerates traditional arcs into instant hooks, then delivers a turn and resolution to retain viewers" [S9|SH-of-FP|IND|2026-06-03|Y] (AI chapter summary of his own words on the Kafka episode — CLAIMED, single-source, his words).
- Packaging is the non-negotiable gate: an amazing story dies if it can't be packaged (title tests like "weak men or bad fathers" vs "muscular dad judged") [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].

### 3. Production (the 21-day script-to-screen pipeline)

- **21-day cycle, script to screen** — his own words: "We're actually on a 21-day cycle from script to screen. So we can actually move in real time… if things are trending culturally or on social media" [S6|SH-of-FP-verbatim|IND|2025-09-13|Y]; confirmed in [S8|SH|IND|2026-06-03|Y] ("22-minute episodes for $50,000 in 21 days") and [S5|SH|IND|2025-09-16|Y] ("five shows weekly on a 21-day script-to-screen cycle, enabling real-time cultural relevance") — VERIFIED (3 sources; 1 verbatim first-party quote).
- **Scale**: 8 full-time production crews shooting across "65 plus sets" (Next in Media: 66 sets) in ~125,000 sq ft in Burbank; ~200 employees; 1,500+ actors employed per year; 5 hours of premium scripted content per week [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y]; [S5|SH|IND|2025-09-16|Y]; [S12|SH|IND|2026-01-30|Y] — VERIFIED (minor variance 65+ vs 66 — see Caveats).
- **Cost discipline**: "you have to spend somewhere around a thousand dollars per minute to create scripted content, not 10,000 or even a hundred thousand per minute that Hollywood is used to"; "our cost per minute of production is actually going down" [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y]. Kafka episode: "$50K TV-length episodes" (22-min at ~$50K) [S8|SH|IND|2026-06-03|Y]; [S9|SH-of-FP|IND|2026-06-03|Y] — VERIFIED (his words + two summaries).
- **Vertical integration**: in-house scripting, production, post, and distribution; "when you know something is such an important part of your business, then don't outsource that thing — do that internally"; outsourced crews only for testable/new formats (e.g., Fox verticals run on a 2-person team — senior producer + showrunner — with hired crews) [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y]; [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].
- Early contrast: one 3-min video/week shot in a studio apartment; scripts on napkins, "we'd come up with the concept right there. We'd shoot it and within like two hours it would go live" [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y]; [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].

### 4. Edit / post-production (where "the magic happens")

- **Post is 3× production**: "All the best creators that I know spend about three times more amount of effort in post than they actually do in production" [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].
- **Second-by-second scrutiny**: "We analyze every word to make sure… could this sentence be cut out?… add music here… add some sort of graphic to visually capture somebody's interest… cut a little bit faster… build emotion, build a little bit of suspense because we know that something is going to get to this climax moment" [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].
- **Data team in the edit**: a data team analyzes when viewers drop off and why — "examining factors such as characters, colors used, and pacing"; "editors test as many as three versions of each short video they put out"; creators "have 10 times as much data as creators in the traditional entertainment space" [S12|SH|IND|2026-01-30|Y] (CEO Sean Atkins, company-official — SECOND-HAND vs Dhar, company-first-party for practice).
- **Runtime engineering (RPM sweet spot)**: RPM rises steeply in the first ~8–14 minutes then flattens; production cost per minute is flat — "22 minute episodes are actually our bread and butter, which just happens to be perfect for TV length episodes" [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y]. Features are the economic exception: "a one and a half hour movie… would make much more sense for us to do nine 10 minute episodes"; features are made for community, not economics [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y] — CLAIMED (single first-party source).

### 5. Cross-platform posting strategy (the platform-agnostic engine)

- **One format, everywhere**: "all of our videos get millions of views not only on YouTube but our content works across every platform… over 600 million long form views on Facebook last month. So we're the largest creator on Facebook… it also works on Spotify, on TikTok, on Instagram. We do different cut down versions of that content" [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y].
- **Shorts-for-reach / long-form-for-retention**: "His team adapts content formats to fit algorithmic behaviors — using Shorts for reach and long-form for retention and monetization" [S4|SH-of-FP|IND|2025-12-25|Y]; Next in Media: "shoots with Shorts and long-form in mind across YouTube, Facebook, TikTok, and beyond" [S5|SH|IND|2025-09-16|Y] — VERIFIED (two independent show-notes sets derived from his interviews).
- **Audience-platform fit**: different content for different audiences (YouTube younger skew, Facebook older skew) [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].
- **Scale of distribution (2025–2026)**: ~25M+ long-form views/day [S5|SH|IND|2025-09-16|Y]; ~1.7B monthly views across platforms [S8|SH|IND|2026-06-03|Y]; ~300M weekly views and 170M+ followers (Samsung PR — promotional) [S18|SH|MON|2026-05-14|Y]; YouTube main channel: 27.3M subs, ~20.0B lifetime views, 1,914 videos, weekly gains ~26–45M views (mid-2026) [S20|data|IND|2026-07|Y].
- **CTV/FAST and beyond**: Dhar Mann TV FAST channel on Samsung TV Plus (fall 2025), then Samsung's first-ever creator-led original scripted series "Unlikely Romances" (premiered 2026-06-05; 13 episodes ordered) [S18|SH|MON|2026-05-14|Y]; [S19|SH|IND|2026-06-04|Y] — VERIFIED (Samsung PR + Adweek + TheWrap + SamMobile + NetInfluencer; ≥2 independent). Fox/Holywater: 40-title microdrama slate for MyDrama app, worldwide distribution via Fox Entertainment Global, Mann retains ownership + creative independence [S16|SH|IND|2026-01-26|Y]; [S17|SH|MON|2026-01-26|Y] — VERIFIED (Variety + THR + TheWrap + press release).

### 6. Replication (how they repeat without burning out)

- **Cadence ladder**: 1× 3-min/week → 2 → 5 short videos/week → 5× 22-min videos/week → 5 shows/week, ~5 hours/week → "plan to double our output by the end of the year" [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y]; "we went from creating four original videos on Facebook every single week to eight original videos on Facebook and our revenue almost doubled. Our viewership almost doubled. Our subscriber growth almost doubled" [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y].
- **Doubling, not jumping**: "don't think of how can I go from one to 200. Think of it as how can I go to one to two… then… two to four, four to eight, eight to 16" (the six-minute-mile analogy) [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].
- **"You have to nail it before you scale it"** [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y]; "consistency matters more than anything" [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].
- **Delegation system (his bottleneck fix)**: Dhar was the bottleneck (creative + producing + every edit + packaging + ad partners); after a COVID-era health crisis he delegated; 6–12 months of downward growth followed, then the team "actually started really learning how my brain was working… figured out how to level it up and do things that I wasn't even capable of" — new genres (horror, action, comedy, romance), serialized IP, features [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y].
- **Formula as template**: "It's predictable. The good guys win, bad guys lose" — Atkins on why the moral template scales [S12|SH|IND|2026-01-30|Y]; recognizable brand DNA across the library: sets, format, moral arc [S9|SH-of-FP|IND|2026-06-03|Y].
- **Export the assembly line**: "he thinks the assembly line he's built will work, there, too" (Samsung, Fox) [S7|FP(show)|IND|2026-06-03|Y]; 30-day production cycles offered to a major SVOD ("if you like the first series, we can create four more other series within the next 60 days") [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y].
- **Library compounding**: ~2,000 episodes of owned IP; "you continue to own a hundred percent of your content and IP" — advice: build on YouTube, own the IP, then shop it to other platforms [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y].
- Anti-burnout mechanisms documented: work-life balance mandate after the health crisis [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y]; team equity program + 401K — "we're releasing an equity program where our actors, some of our longtime actors and employees actually have equity in the company" [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].

### 7. Monetization

- **Organic platform revenue first**: "a hundred percent of our traction to date has all been organic viewership" (2025) [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y]; RPM engineering (22-min sweet spot) as the core unit-economics lever [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y].
- **Brand partnerships over integrations**: to date "hadn't needed to rely much on small brand integrations"; now pushing deep partnerships — "co-developing a studio together where we support them creating content that they actually own" (talks with a top home-improvement retailer and a large cosmetics company) [S6|SH-of-FP-verbatim|IND|2025-09-13|Y]; CMOs "are so tired of having to rent our audiences" — owned-media pitch [S6|SH-of-FP-verbatim|IND|2025-09-13|Y].
- **Branded case study (NFL)**: YouTube NFL partnership "Chief Kindness Officer" activation — 100M+ views, 1B+ impressions incl. earned media, 94% positive sentiment (vs NFL's ~65% baseline), 10.4 brand lift in an independent study (vs 5.2 for the best Super Bowl spot, Toyota) — "more than double the highest performing Super Bowl commercial… all based on organic views" [S13|FP|IND|2026-05-21|Y] (CLAIMED as numbers — single first-party telling).
- **FAST/linear**: Samsung "joint-success" model with revenue share (financial terms undisclosed) [S19|SH|IND|2026-06-04|Y].
- **Distribution deals with ownership retained**: Fox/Holywater — "Mann will retain ownership and creative independence over his original content" [S16|SH|IND|2026-01-26|Y].
- **Diversification**: revenue mix shifted — "<40% now comes from YouTube and Facebook, down from 95% two years ago" [S8|SH|IND|2026-06-03|Y] (CLAIMED, single AI summary); agency arm Fifth Quarter for other creators [S5|SH|IND|2025-09-16|Y]; podcast "What Happens Next" + short-form nonfiction interviews as a flywheel [S3|FP|IND|2025|Y]; earnings ~$65M in 2025 (host-stated on BigDeal; Dhar did not dispute) [S10|FP|IND|2026-07-29|Y].

## New Terms

- **climax-cold-open** (hook_type extension): opening the video on the story's highest-tension moment (the climax), then accelerating the arc into "a turn and resolution" — his documented hook mechanic for swiping audiences [S9]; distinct from a classic cold-open because the payoff moment itself leads.
- **packaging-gate / pre-tested packaging** (hook layer): title + thumbnail validated via ~$10 paid Facebook tests (~100 variants) BEFORE production; a story that cannot be packaged dies ("if it's not great packaging… it can't be a great story") [S10]. The click-level hook is pre-sold, not improvised.
- **green-lighting funnel** (replication tactic): the staged 10:1 kill-ratio development pipeline — title → illustration → audience-survey greenlight → real thumbnail/casting → 30-second pitch one-pager → script → post — with ~1 of 10 ideas reaching upload [S10].
- **shorts-for-reach / long-form-for-retention** (platform-specific mechanics): the two-format distribution split he uses to serve algorithmic behaviors: Shorts (and cutdowns) for discovery, long-form for retention and RPM-monetization [S4, S5].
- **hero's-journey template + guaranteed moral** (loop/retention descriptor): "someone acts badly, someone learns a lesson, everyone gets a moral by the end" — a closed-loop with a predictable redemption arc ("good guys win, bad guys lose") that makes the emotional payoff the retention engine [S7, S12, S3].
