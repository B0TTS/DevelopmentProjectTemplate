## Workflow (deep narrative)

### 0. Origin & format definition (context)
Zach King's format was born from a constraint: film school + Vine's 6-second limit → "what can I do in 6 seconds?" → magic-infused micro-films; he uploaded **pre-edited** videos into the in-app-only Vine, creating the illusion that the "magic" happened in-camera — the audience called it magic, and he kept the brand even though editors knew it was editing ([DP2026]). This "impossible-looking edit" contract defines every mechanism below: the viewer's brain registers impossibility → curiosity gap → rewatch/share ([AM2025], [AG2026]).

### 1. Ideation (Monday morning, team, T-sheets)
- **Discipline over inspiration:** used to wait for inspiration (skateboarding, Nerf fights); after reading Steven Pressfield's *The War of Art* he switched to "sit down for a couple hours a day and start writing on the page" — routine first, inspiration second ([V2025]). Corroborated: "it's just about sitting down and doing the work… building a process around ideas" ([Y2023]).
- **Weekly Monday brainstorm, ~1 p.m.** — whole team pitches; "anyone can have a great idea"; no formal writer's room; even finance/assistant roles bring concepts ([V2025], [Y2023]).
- **T-sheet format:** 8.5×11 paper with a T: title top, concept synopsis on one side, reference photo on the other — serves visual learners; term attributed by King to Duncan Wardle (Disney) ([V2025], [Y2023]). He now generates T-sheet images with AI rather than by hand ([Y2023]).
- **Volume + pulse vote:** ~100–150 ideas per hour-long session on a wall; each person gets 1–2 sticky votes (green = top pick, yellow = second, in the 2025 version) — a "pulse" of which concepts resonate, "not a popularity contest" ([Y2023], [V2025]).
- **Prompt/themed sprints:** thematic prompts ("holidays", "underwater", "space", "what if Wes Anderson directed my TikToks") → ruminate ~20 ideas per prompt ([Y2023]).
- **Filter: story over trick.** When the team dissects why a concept resonates, "it's usually not the illusion, it's usually story" — e.g., "I waited in line all week → traffic" relatability; producers are told "you're a writer right now, we're not thinking about budget" ([Y2023]). Evergreen filter (2022): "is this going to be around in 15 or 20 years or even in 100 years?" rather than chasing trends ([S2022]).

### 2. Mock-ups (pre-production, same day as brainstorm)
- After Monday's creative meeting, straight into **mock-ups**: "how could we see these videos in 1 hour?" — shoot a phone blocking to feel cuts, trick breakdown, VFX approach; mock-ups are "messy, not pretty," and multiple variants are made (cold-plunge example: prop bought early, framing + water-plate tested) ([V2025]).
- **3D mock-ups** for impossible-to-film concepts: animator builds the whole vision in 3D (tiny-world set, LA-traffic→Swiss-lake) so production executes against a proven plan; the final video matches the mock-up "almost identical" ([V2025]).
- For scripted long-form (MrBeast-money give-away video): beats planned before filming, every transition line scripted with ~10 alternate readings for the editor, alt intros shot on the phone and shown to the team as "alt one/two/three" to pick the "catchier" one ([Y2023]). A/B intro testing: same practice ([Y2023]).

### 3. Production (creativity over money; practical first)
- **"Money is not the fix":** $100,000-vs-$100 comparison; the room-flooding video's $600K solution (giant pumps/pools) was replaced by mounting the camera to a set and sinking it — from the camera's perspective the room "fills" ([V2025]); Corridor Crew episode later broke down the same flooding illusion ([CC2024]).
- **The $100 video:** black Harry Potter robe + mirror (skateboard/broomstick already owned) → the broomstick video, ~2B TikTok views, "one of my most popular videos" ([V2025]); Guinness World Records: most-viewed TikTok video ([WIKI]).
- **Embrace limitations:** private-jet/tarmac illusion shot with cardboard cutout + poster board + carpet-on-sawhorses conveyor belt ([V2025]); 2026 cardboard *Star Wars* fan film: "what if we imposed a creative constraint that forced us to be just as scrappy?" — cardboard world, 5-day shoot, 2-week pre-build ([HGL2026]).
- **Studio mantra: "do it practically when possible"** — "the audience can tell… feel the energy of if it's real" ([V2025]). Corroborated in the Stranded 3 BTS: green-screen skydiving on wires with leaf blowers (control over free-fall movement), real Glamis, CA desert shoot, process trailer for the Jeep/Terry Crews scene, one-day dig-site set (4 a.m. crew call), camera angles + cobwebs to reuse one set as a "maze" ([SB2023]). Dude Perfect collabs kept "an element of realness… they were picky about that" ([DP2026]).

### 4. VFX (post, global team)
- After in-camera capture, "so much more work inside the computer"; VFX team is worldwide, not just at the studio ([V2025]).
- Pipeline demonstrated on the puzzle-piece video: tracking markers → blue-screen hop-in → grid tracking for anamorphic morphing → placeholder art → final CG comp; Trash Monster: practical shoot (real trash dump, cloth interaction) + animated tongue added in post ([V2025]).
- Public VFX disclosure: joined Corridor Crew "VFX Artists React" to break down Black Hole in the Classroom, Mirror Illusions, and the flooding illusion ([CC2024]). VFX team led by named specialists (Ethan Montgomery, VFX; Dave Cortez, DP; Josh Fapp, director) ([HGL2026], [DSPD2022]).

### 5. Sound design (full rebuild in post)
- "Completely redone in post. There's basically nothing usable from our sets" (talking, loud cues, wind machines) ([V2025]).
- A ~20-second short carries **~30 audio layers** ([V2025]); the Mario/Halloween example: raw camera audio → sound-effect pass → team review ("still lacking the energy") → music pass ([V2025]).
- BTS evidence of meticulous post: LinkedIn Monet video BTS ("Did you have any idea this is how we made the Monet video???") with commenters noting "meticulous post-production process" ([LI2024]).

### 6. Posting & distribution
- He personally posts everything, waking at 5:00 a.m. to upload ([Y2023]).
- Cadence (2023): shorts once per 7–10 days; long-form 1/week target, ≥1/month minimum, Mark Rober-style "make a banger, don't spam" ([Y2023]). Not restated in 2025–26 sources — staleness flag ([V2025], [DP2026]).
- Platform behavior: "I always get scared on any platform to post anything else — if something is just killing it I just wait and let it go" ([Y2023]).
- Economics awareness: ~80% of income from brand deals/sponsored content; AdSense ≈ $20K/month (≈5%); YouTube Shorts monetization $7,900.35 on ~537.8M views (2 months) — "way better ratio" than long-form ([Y2023]); Forbes Top Creators 2024: $5.1M earnings, 149M followers ([F2024]).

### 7. Replication (how he avoids burnout)
- The Monday system + votes + mock-ups + budget tiers (C-tier = 1 camera, 2 PAs, 4 hours) is the anti-burnout machinery: ideas are pipeline-fed, bad ideas die for $0, and production is team-scaled ("I don't have time to do all the editing myself anymore… it allows so many different skills into these videos") ([V2025], [Y2023]).
- Team model: writing team + production team; King as "the face or the watermark" ([S2022]); studio environment changed every 3–4 years + commute variety (bus/bike/skateboard) as deliberate perspective shakes ([S2022]).
- AI stance (2023→2026): uses AI for T-sheet images and welcomes rotoscope-automation tools ("no human wants… that's not a creative task"), but "I'm still not using AI on my videos editing" and positions the brand as "100% handmade… human authentic stuff" ([Y2023], [DP2026]).

## New Terms

Mechanisms found in Zach King's workflow that fall outside the mission's closed vocabulary:

- **T-sheet** — one-page idea pitch: title across the top, concept text on one side of a "T", inspiration photo on the other; used as the unit of the Monday brainstorm ([V2025], [Y2023], [GB2025]).
- **Pulse vote** — team resonance vote (green/yellow sticky votes, 1–2 per person) after idea generation, used to gauge which concepts resonate — explicitly "not a popularity contest" ([V2025], [Y2023]).
- **Mock-up (phone blocking)** — rough, deliberately "messy" same-day phone-shot blocking of a proposed video to test cuts, trick breakdown, and VFX feasibility before real production ([V2025]).
- **3D mock-up** — animator-built 3D pre-visualization of a full concept (set measurements, framing) used when an idea is too complex to phone-block ([V2025]).
- **Practical-first mantra** — production rule: execute effects in-camera/practically whenever possible because "the audience can tell" real energy; VFX only for what physics forbids ([V2025], [SB2023]).
- **Budget tiers (A/B/C)** — pre-assigned production budgets (e.g., C-tier = 1 camera, 2 PAs, 4 hours) so every idea is scored against a realistic build envelope ([Y2023]).
- **Evergreen filter** — selection test: "is this concept going to be around in 15–20 years or even 100 years?" to avoid trend-chasing ([S2022]).
- **Intro alt-testing (A/B intros)** — filming 3+ alternate intros and polling the team on the phone for the "catchier" opening before finalizing ([Y2023]).
