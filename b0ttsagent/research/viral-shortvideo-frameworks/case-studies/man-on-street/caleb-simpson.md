### replication_tactic
How he repeats the format without burning out (all FIRST-PARTY):
1. **Same format, rotating subjects/cities** — "how do I keep everyone interested… I need to travel to more cities, I need to see more interesting houses, I need more interesting guests" [S2].
2. **Produce in advance** — "most of my life is producing, to be honest… what's the next home we're getting into, what's the next video" [S1]; morning routine is "edit to post… to get it live" then go film [S1].
3. **Inbound supply replaces street hunting** — "I get a lot of direct messages from people offering their place. I also have a Google Form" [S5 BI]; now "spends a good portion of his time turning people down" [S4]; word-of-mouth referrals: "everybody knows that I do this thing now… they come up to me and say 'Yo, I got this friend'" [S8].
4. **Outsource logistics, keep the edit in-house** — assistant + management team [S1], booking agent + videographer [S7], but "I've tried to hire editors… they can't do what I do" [S1].
5. **Only build formats you can sustain** — "I was like how do I make something that I can sustain for 10 or 15 years? What do I enjoy doing?" [S1]; he stopped chasing views-only content he hated [S2: "it was more chasing views and followers… taxing"].

### platform_specific_mechanics

## Workflow / Framework Breakdown (step by step, each step cited)

### Step 0 — Idea selection: "make what you want to see" + the 10x rule
Origin: after ~7–8 years of making content that didn't land, the format came from (a) the advice "make something you want to see" [S5 BI; S4 Rolling Stone; S2 — attributed by Caleb to YouTuber Brett Conte], and (b) an early-career replication system he still cites: take the top viral videos, "analyze all of them and think why did they go viral… write it out each video and then… recreate it and try to 10x the idea… take an idea that's on TikTok and put it in a new environment… so then it'll go more viral than their video. That was the theory" [S1; S2]. Concrete aha: he watched a Sean Rizwan street interview asking "what's the most expensive thing in your home" ~20 times, then: "What if I asked to go in the home?" [S2; S8]. He also validated demand: "apartment tours were a popular search topic on YouTube" [S10 AD]. **FIRST-PARTY: [S1][S2][S5][S8][S10].**

### Step 1 — The street ask, with a same-day rule
Execution day: "I hired my friend Billy, we went out and spent the whole day in New York… asking 'how much do you pay for rent, can I have a tour of your home'… but I made the rule that I had to do it that day" — he declined a "yes, but not today," took ~100 rejections, and only got a tour at the very end of the day [S2]. Corroborated: "The first 100 people said no, which was devastating" [S7]; "most people just told me 'Haha, fuck off'" [S4]; "I still get nervous every time… I have to run up and ask the person right away before I psych myself out" [S5]. **FIRST-PARTY: [S2][S5][S7].**

### Step 2 — Publish the rejections (the accidental engine)
The refusal clips were content: "I still posted all those other clips of asking people, and they all went extremely viral. And I'd been making videos long enough that I understood: This was the moment" [S4]; "posted their reactions, usually in 15-second clips at first… The clips got millions of views" [S7]; friends forwarded clips to the refusers: "Let this guy be in your house… this is advertising" → refusers DM'd him: "we'll give you the tour now" [S8; S2]. First tour video (Williamsburg duplex, $3,000/mo): "That video got millions of hits. I knew I was onto something" [S7]; ~100K views on the YouTube version [S2]. **FIRST-PARTY: [S2][S4][S7][S8].**

### Step 3 — Single-phone, frictionless, single-long-take shoot
The production system (his 2024 pitch to guests, verbatim): "we're going to do one walkthrough, just talk to me like we're a friend… it's going to take 20 minutes, I'm going to do one pass just filming the rooms and then we're going to run outside, I'm going to ask you one question one time, just answer it, and then that's it" — "it's all filmed on one cell phone"; occasionally brings an assistant [S1]. "I just show up with a cell phone… it only takes 25 minutes" [S1]. He still uses an iPhone: "you need to just use what's around you, just like using a cellphone… It makes it feel more authentic and less produced. Why would I make it more polished when everyone loves it the way it is?" [S8]. He "focuses on doing one long take" so the subject "get[s] comfortable, be themselves, and find rhythm" [S8]. Guest comfort is a design constraint: "I leave it at whatever the person is comfortable with… If there's something they don't want to show, I won't show it. I feel like that's what keeps the show alive" [S10]. **FIRST-PARTY: [S1][S8][S10].**

### Step 4 — In-house editing is the moat
"Whenever I'm going into an edit I might have two hours of footage that I just filmed with somebody and I'm chopping it down to like six minutes"; hired editors can't reproduce it: "I've tried to hire editors… they can't do what I do… I have my flow and my style, it's like really hard to copy" [S1]. ~8 hours total per finished video (2025): "creating a single video tour takes him about eight hours" [S8]. His 2021-era discipline built the skill: "every day when I got home I would drink a coffee and try to edit one video… learn one new editing trick in the software" [S2]. (The host's framing — "the exact second the exact tone is said and the storytelling in the first two seconds" — is Tartick's SECOND-HAND generalization about creators he's worked with, not Caleb's own words [S1]; not counted as verification.) **FIRST-PARTY: [S1][S2][S8].**

### Step 5 — The celebrity-escalation pitch engine
How guests/celebrities are sourced (his own account): (1) create maximum value for the guest: "creating as much value as possible for the guest and making it as frictionless as possible… I know I'm going to get them 20 million views, 30 million views, and it's going to blow their life up for a moment" [S1]; (2) use proof + custom pitch: "this is who I've had on, these are the followers, these are the views, we'll customize it to you" [S1]; (3) watch who starts posting on TikTok and DM early: "anyone who jumps on TikTok who's a celebrity… they're playing the game… getting to them early because I know they're going to be overrun" (Vinnie Guadagnino, Dwight Howard examples) [S1]; (4) use results as the pitch: Corcoran DM'd him ~2–3 months in, her video did ~100M views across platforms and ~200K new followers for her [S1; S2; S6: "within two months, Barbara Corcoran's DMing me"]; (5) later stage: celebrity content is planned/word-of-mouth — "most of the celebrity content is planned. A lot of times it's word of mouth" [S8]; (6) PR-facing pitch: "PR people are not hard to work with, and timing, persistence, and the value you bring to the table are everything" [S5]. **FIRST-PARTY: [S1][S2][S5][S8].**

### Step 6 — Cadence: daily sprint → managed weekly
Breakout period: "I have to make at least one every day, I have to be in a home every day… because I was attacking it so aggressively, everyone's starting to notice" [S2]; Apr 2023 daily routine: wake 6:30, cold plunge, workout, "write emails, make phone calls, and do some editing and posting. In the afternoon, I head out to shoot" [S5]. Nov 2024: "I haven't been every day recently but it's maybe four to six times a week" [S1]; most of the day is advance production: "most of my life is producing… there's always messages flying in… messaging people who have hit me back from like six months ago" [S1]. 2026 (data): ~1 post/week (12 posts in 12 weeks, 2026-05-18 → 08-07) [D1]. **FIRST-PARTY: [S1][S2][S5]; cadence delta 2026: [D1].**

### Step 7 — Monetization structure (three rails, brand deals first)
"Two ways you can monetize, really three": platform payouts, brand sponsorships, own products [S1]. Platform payouts are treated as unreliable: TikTok "a hundred bucks… maybe 2,000" per million views; his 42M-view Scarlett Johansson video ≈ $18K; total view income ≈ $20–50K/month but "not reliable" [S1]. Brand deals are the main rail: "we charge a lot for brand sponsorships… that's where most of it can come in… could be multiple six figures… conversations around seven figures" [S1]; early numbers: Feb 2023 ≈ $100K month, "mostly from brand deals, other than about $3,500 from TikTok and $1,100 from Instagram" [S5]; the home-placement pitch: "how many products are in a home? … every product or service is used inside a home" [S1]; discipline: "I try to do one brand deal a month" and "I would rather build a relationship over time than get paid to promote a bad product" [S5]. He fired a manager who refused to ask for $100K/video and later benchmarked "anywhere between 30 and 150 grand for a brand deal" via direct calls to other creators — "it's much better to call and ask somebody than for somebody to be reaching out" [S2]. **FIRST-PARTY: [S1][S2][S5].**

### Step 8 — Longevity mechanics
Anti-burnout and anti-fad rules: (1) sustainability test at format choice: "how do I make something that I can sustain for 10 or 15 years" [S1]; (2) avoid trend-chasing: "I'm not obsessed with any trends right now, at all. I try to stay away from them personally" [S9]; (3) inspiration from long-running formats ("People who have stood the test of time" — Hot Ones, Chicken Shop Date, Sundae Conversation) [S9]; (4) keep publishing: advice to creators — "keep making the videos… everyone else will quit and you'll still be there… you will get recognized and you'll most likely get your moment if you keep pushing the envelope" [S2]; (5) anti-shiny-object: "not get shiny object syndrome… do what I'm doing right now the best that I could do it is for sure number one" [S1]. Expansion plan (2025): "I'm going to continue making home tours, doing pizza reviews, and interviewing people in new and creative formats" [S9]. **FIRST-PARTY: [S1][S2][S9].**
