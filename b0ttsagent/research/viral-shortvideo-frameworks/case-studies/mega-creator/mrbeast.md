## Workflow

### 1. Ideation — the Purple Cow test (first-party: DOAC-2025, LEX-2023)

- **Never-seen-before or nothing.** "To go viral you have to do something that's never been done before... if you're scrolling through social media to find a video to watch, there's things that you know have been done before... you're just going to scroll past it... and then there are ideas that are like the purple cow idea... things that make you go 'what the f— I've never seen that, like I have to click this or I'm not going to be able to sleep tonight'... those typically are very hard and usually... there's usually a reason because it's very f—ing hard, so you just kind of have to train yourself to not resent very difficult complex hard original problems and actually run towards them." [CITED: DOAC-2025, first-party transcript on disk, Feb 2025, INDEPENDENT]
- **Exponential, winner-take-all viewership.** "Viewership is very exponential... it's way easier to get 50 million views on one video than it is to get a million views on 50 videos... pretty winner-take-all in the top videos, so you just really have to lean into that purple cow effect." [CITED: DOAC-2025] Corroborated in LEX-2023: "much easier to hypothetically get 10 million views on one video than 100,000 on 100... if you make a really good video it's just so evergreen and it never dies."
- **Idea engine = inspiration intake.** "The only way I've really found that I can consistently come up with 100 million view videos is to intake inspiration and then see what my brain outputs." He consumes the most-viewed videos daily ("for way too many years") until patterns "click in your head": "thumbnails... tend to be clear, tend to not have much clutter... titles tend to be less than 50 characters, intros tend to be this, stories tend to be this." [CITED: LEX-2023]
- **The idea dies if the thumbnail fails.** "MrBeast will throw away a video idea if the thumbnail for it would not work." [CITED: LEX-2023 (podcast notes, faithful summary of first-party audio)]
- **Format rotation (anti-format-drift).** After 7–8 "$1 vs $500,000"-style videos that "all went nuclear," he stopped: "I'm a big believer in ending on a format while you know it's not too old... if I did another five, around the third or fourth people would start to be like 'okay, I've seen 10 of these'... for other creators I don't recommend you do what I do — you should probably milk the format more." Freshness is the retention of the channel: "people feel that energy... that keeps them excited... that's why the viewership keeps coming up." [CITED: C&S-2024-BG]

### 2. Hook / first-frame / packaging (first-party: C&S-2021-48M, LEX-2023, C&S-2024-BG, WIRED-2026; team-side: APPLEBY-2024)

- **First 10 seconds (long-form rule; the closest thing to a documented first-frame doctrine).** "I went back and watched the FBI video again — those first 40 seconds were so jam-packed... the thing people undervalue the most is literally the first 10 seconds of the video. I can quote it: 'I tied up an FBI agent. I have a hundred thousand dollars in this bag. Here's a knife. Good luck. And I just run away.' It gives you everything you need — no wasted words, short, concise." [CITED: C&S-2021-48M]
- **Title test: compelled click.** "If someone reads it, do they have to watch it?" Titles must lead into the content ("the title represents content that you would wanna watch for 20 minutes" — title/content congruence is a click factor), stay under 50 characters so they don't truncate, and "typically, the more extreme the opinion, the higher the click-through rate — but the more extreme the title, the more extreme you have to be in the video." [CITED: LEX-2023]
- **"If they don't click, they don't watch."** "You can make the best content ever but if they don't click you're dead... the title and thumbnail matters more than you think... every year thumbnails get more and more important than I thought they were and I realize how bad our thumbnails were the year before." Packaging pipeline: multistep thumbnail sketches; A/B tests ("that one won the AB test... that one lost the AB test"); a closed-mouth facial-expression A/B run won every test ("every AB test the less over-the-top cringe expression one"), which he then adopted and others copied; direction toward realism ("trying to make the thumbnails look a little more realistic... abandoned city I think got it right"). [CITED: C&S-2024-BG]
- **Thumbnail craft at team scale (second-hand, but from his own thumbnail creator).** ~$10,000 per thumbnail; ~50 thumbnail/title concepts per video whittled to a winner; "hundreds of variations" of shirt colors/suits/logo; MrBeast's face in every thumbnail since 2019 as a trust/brand device ("if you trusted Jimmy on the last video... he delivered"); thumbnail must align directly with the video intro; "make your thumbnail easy to understand... if they have to spend 10 or 20 seconds to figure out what's going on, they're going to keep scrolling." Closed-mouth A/B: 30 videos in a row, all with closed mouth led to higher watch time. [CITED: APPLEBY-2024 (Social Media Today repackage of YouTube's interview with Chucky Appleby, 2024-03-08)]
- **His own meta-rule (2026, most recent).** "I just never know how to thumbnail our videos... I'm just second-guessing myself... my thumbnail team probably hates me because I just change my mind all the time and constantly change the thumbnails. Thumbnails are important, but what is ultimately most important is that you just make great content." [CITED: WIRED-2026]

### 3. Retention psychology (first-party: C&S-2021-48M, LEX-2023, DOAC-2025, THEO-2024; second-hand: FUTUR-2025)

- **The retention graph and the 70% rule.** The reason he spends millions on sets "comes down to this one graph — this is called a retention graph... 70 — that's what you need. If it's below that, figure out how to get higher. So that means 70% of the people who clicked on the video are still watching at the end of the video." For longer videos: "introduce a side story... have some plan halfway through to re-engage them so they don't get bored." [CITED: C&S-2021-48M]
- **Front-loaded effort curve.** "The longer people watch something, the more likely they are to keep watching. So you don't have to try as hard in the hypothetically back half of a video as you do in the front... you want to get people where they're immersed in the content and then just kind of hold them there." [CITED: LEX-2023]
- **Narrative + micro.** "You need the overarching narrative, and then you also need the micro, where every second needs to be entertaining." [CITED: LEX-2023]
- **After-action reports (the measurement loop).** "I just upload a video and then the next day I look at the retention and the CTR, and if we f—ed up... we call them after-action reports... a very in-depth breakdown of here's the retention chart, here's every time someone clicked away, here's where was the flattest, here's where was the worst... if I upload a video that's 20 minutes, we take our last 10 20-minute videos... the median retention was 10 minutes and 55 seconds — if the retention on this new video is 11 minutes or above we did a good job... two weeks after we upload, we look at that with all my top people: 'what'd we f— up, what'd we do well, cool, move on.'" Flops are data, not sins: "as long as we articulate why and it doesn't happen again... I'm okay with my people failing... as long as we failed because we made the wrong shot call, not because we were lazy." [CITED: DOAC-2025] Corroborated by the 2025 C&S fifth interview, where C&S note "when we used to sit down with you it was like AVD, CTR, here's the metrics you need to go about YouTube, here's how you do storytelling" — and that he now runs at that scale. [CITED: C&S-2025-5th]
- **Tension architecture (relayed second-hand from his framework).** Jenga storytelling: tension should "rise and fall at disproportionate levels; the overall arc should be that tension rises towards the end — but you don't want a consistent rise" (explicitly attributed: "this is something we learned from MrBeast"). [CITED: FUTUR-2025 — C&S YouTuber Academy content; SECOND-HAND, MONETIZED]
- **Behavioral-psychology game design.** "It's fun to create environments that I've never been creative for and just see what people do — a lot of that is just mass behavioral psychology. If you offer someone money to eliminate other people, how much money would it take?" Stakes scale as retention: "the first three minutes of our show we gave away a million dollars" vs. other shows' $250k prizes. [CITED: THEO-2024]
- **2024–2025 evolution: slower, deeper.** Videos doubled in length ("my average video probably used to be like 11 minutes and now they're closer to 20... they're like 30-40% slower... I'm yelling probably 50-60% less"); letting scenes "breathe" produced phenomenal retention ("the grocery store video... 25 minutes... we really built up that guy... the retention was phenomenal, almost on 200 million views"); he frames the old jam-packed style as "slightly wrong back in the day." [CITED: C&S-2024-BG]

### 4. Production scale — the moat (first-party: C&S-2021-48M, C&S-2024-BG, DOAC-2025, WIRED-2026)

- **Reinvest everything.** "I will spend every dollar back into the channel forever, I don't care." $4M/month across channels (2021); average ~$2.1M production per main video, ~$3.5M all-in (2024); "I spend most of my money on our YouTube content" (2026). [CITED: C&S-2021-48M; C&S-2024-BG; WIRED-2026]
- **Custom sets as visual resets.** "Every set is custom built... this is to make sure that the scenes keep changing — that keeps the pace of the video moving and the viewer watching longer." 90-day video cycle; ~12 videos/month across channels as the ideal; ~25 filming days per month; ~300 in the production company + ~100 Feastables + ~50-100 others (~500 total). [CITED: C&S-2021-48M; C&S-2024-BG; DOAC-2025]
- **Trained "clones" as replication of taste.** Tyler, head writer/director: "I probably talk to him five or six hours a day every day for around four years" until he can run videos without him. "MrBeast has clones of himself that would make the same decision as him 90% of the time related to content creation and production" — built by 4–5 years of immersion. [CITED: DOAC-2025; LEX-2023]
- **Suffering as moat.** "There's a reason no one makes videos like me, because no one wants to live the life I live... being able to push through unhappiness and do things you don't want to do consistently, year after year over the course of a decade, is like the ultimate advantage... once you make a couple million dollars, why would you live the life I live? Why would you not film locally even if it means less views?" [CITED: DOAC-2025]
- **Authenticity infrastructure (show scale).** 1,000 cameras rolling 24/7 on 1,000 contestants, world records for cameras and cabling, 130–150 editors, petabytes of footage — because unscripted reality at that capture density "is why I think partially people gravitate towards our videos — they can tell they're authentic." [CITED: WIRED-2026; DOAC-2025]

### 6. Posting cadence (first-party)

- Main channel: ~weekly, and deliberately sustained — "Beast Games is such a monster of a project and I have to maintain the same YouTube upload schedule" [CITED: DOAC-2025]; "I plan to upload more main channel videos than the previous year" [CITED: C&S-2024-BG]; ideal ~12 videos/month across all channels on a 90-day production cycle [CITED: C&S-2021-48M]. Shorts: very recent activity (yt-dlp activity = 1.0) [CITED: PHASE2-2026]. No documented time-of-day posting rule — N/A.

### 7. Replication — how to repeat without burning out (first-party)

- **Do not codify the formula.** "The more constraints you put on the team, the more repetitive and less innovation you get... after 10 videos people are gonna be like 'all right, I've already seen this'... I'll say one time 'ideally there's a cut every three seconds' and the next thing you know every video has a cut every three seconds. I try to give as little as possible and make suggestions instead." [CITED: LEX-2023]
- **Improvement loop, not replication loop.** "Every six months you should look back and hate your previous videos — if you don't hate them, you're probably not improving." For beginners: "make 100 videos, improve something in each video, and then talk to me about your 101st." "Going viral on YouTube is a learnable skill... it is not random." [CITED: LEX-2023]
- **Benchmark against yourself.** Median-retention comparison across your own last-10-similar videos, reviewed every 2 weeks (the after-action report system). [CITED: DOAC-2025]
- **End formats while hot; rotate the wow.** [CITED: C&S-2024-BG]
- **Use your imitators as negative feedback.** Watching "MrBeast clones" made him catalog annoyances ("I'd put a video on 0.75 speed... this is just aggressive") and fix them in his own content: "without the Beastification... I don't know if I would have adjusted and learned as fast as I did." [CITED: C&S-2024-BG]

## New Terms

- **Beastification** — proposed addition. Definition: the market-level dynamic where an entire cohort of derivative creators copies only the surface features of a dominant format (fast cuts, yelling, loud text) without the underlying retention logic, degrading the pattern to the point where the originator uses the imitators' output as negative feedback to evolve away from it. MrBeast explicitly credits this loop with his own improvement: "one of the best ways for me to improve was just to watch all these MrBeast clones... write down the things I thought was annoying and make sure I'm not doing that in my videos... without the Beastification... I don't know if I would have adjusted and learned as fast as I did." [CITED: C&S-2024-BG]. Not a retention mechanism (out of the closed vocabulary) — a market-level feedback loop, hence proposed here. **Purple Cow** is Seth Godin's borrowed term, not new; **after-action report** is his team's name for the retention/CTR review, kept as standard English.
