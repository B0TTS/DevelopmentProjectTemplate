## Workflow (the system, step by step — idea → script → film → edit → post)
1. **Idea capture (always-on).** Three generators: (a) live life and notice video ideas — "the best ideas... come from just living it and then thinking 'this is a video idea'"
(ratatouille → $5 garden with "unlimited ratatouille"; grandma's free laundry detergent via complaint call) [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 99-108]; (b) watch YouTube
+ AI idea generation [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 98-99]; (c) daily trending-page monitoring — "every single morning I'll go onto the trending page and watch every
single trending short," plus a swipe file from *Steal Like an Artist* [CITED: MFM transcript lines 304-310]. Ideas accumulate in a 1,000-entry Google Doc [CITED: Creator Science
#167 transcript lines 95-97].
2. **Idea filter 100→25→10.** From ~100: cut to 25–50 by asking (1) do I actually want to make it? (2) is it logistically possible? (3) is the hook good? (4) is the mechanism good?
(5) will people rewatch it? — then cut to 25 on "where's the virality aspect" — then her strategist-editor (not "just a video editor") cuts 25→10 on shareability and "simple yet
complex" concept fit [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 110-119].
3. **Four idea criteria (deep filter).** A brainstormed idea must carry novelty, uncertainty (crazy unknown outcome — "I sold everything my family owns" example), knowledge gaps
("I know something that you don't"), and complexity (layers = more reasons to watch, without confusing the viewer) [CITED: MFM transcript lines 54-58, 102-119].
4. **Write the hook first — as a visual.** She sketches first frames on her iPad "almost as if it's tied on thumbnails... until something clicks"; visual first, then words; then
run the line through a readability checker (readabilityformulas.com) and force it to 5th-grade level or under — benchmark from scraping "thousands of shorts" transcripts (MrBeast ≈
1st grade) [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 24-35, 42-45].
5. **Write the LAST LINE second.** "I always know the last line when I'm going to film" — the ending is scripted before the middle exists, often as "I surprised my mom and...
[blank reaction]" [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 180-187].
6. **Write the foreshadow (two lines, ≤3 seconds).** Always spoken, always present: "the hook and foreshadow I always do in every video... two lines, 3 seconds or less" — e.g.,
hook "I'm going to the beach," foreshadow "and I'm going to surprise someone with $100 at the end of the video" [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 152-158]. Foreshadow =
"telling the viewer that there's an Amazon gift card at the end of the video essentially"; best when implied within the hook [CITED: MFM transcript lines 177-182].
7. **Script the middle (rough script or bullets) + transition.** Rough script for word-count sense of length (target ~34s), or bullet points of beats; the transition line after the
foreshadow is deliberately crafted as a "pacing break without the pacing actually breaking" so she doesn't violate primacy/recency by over-dumping detail [CITED: Creator Science
#167 transcript lines 149-169]. Middle beats: but/so conflict chain [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 170-175], escalating real stakes (default to "what's actually
happening in my real life" — "we always default to what's actually happening in my real life that we can actually include as real stakes") [CITED: MFM transcript lines 226-231],
and a visible "mechanism" for progression (e.g., the $10-in-Miami budget counter ticking down; "there's three steps we need to do") [CITED: MFM transcript lines 182-187; Creator
Science #167 transcript lines 119-125].
8. **Film.** Personality-first delivery; she built her on-camera charisma deliberately — a month of nightly improvised private stand-up + recording unposted days to get "reps in"
[CITED: MFM transcript lines 30-42].
9. **Revisit script after filming; revise; finalize.** Then edit [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 183-185].
10. **Edit: every second counts + VFX exaggeration.** Retention-graph discipline: found a 25% last-second dip (70%→45%), trimmed the single dead second, retention 83%→88%, "the
video went flying" — "every single second" matters because 1s ≈ 3% of a 30s Short [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 53-61]. Post team adds VFX to exaggerate reality
(fake fire, giant hole) and cleans the first frame (rotoscoping herself into a spotless Google-image McDonald's) [CITED: MFM transcript lines 162-173].
11. **End on the payoff, cut immediately.** Abrupt, pleasing endings; Peak-End closes on wholesome or funniest moment; twists for rewatch [CITED: MFM transcript lines 256-267;
Creator Science #167 transcript lines 188-190].
12. **Post + measure + iterate (the data loop).** Targets: scroll-through (view vs swipe-away) 80%+ (avg 70%; she averages 80–85%) [CITED: MFM transcript lines 151-153; Creator
Science #167 transcript lines 63-66]; retention 90%+ "bare minimum," team targets 95+ [CITED: MFM transcript lines 155-156]; rewatch is the engine that lifts retention above
scroll-through [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 66-68]. Team-built tools: every video manually labeled by attribute (family in it? malicious? wholesome?) graphed
against subscriber conversions — finding: family+wholesome = 2x conversion but 10x less views; prank content = regular conversion but 10x views, netting 5x more subscribers [CITED:
MFM transcript lines 282-297]. Outlier analysis against her OWN baseline (a 10M-view Short from a 10M-average channel ≠ an outlier; 30M from a 1M-average channel is) [CITED: MFM
transcript lines 299-303].

198K > 100K; hit rate 19/19 ≥ 0.6) and stays in the set, but synthesis must weight her case as "richest framework documentation, weakest current per-video numbers" among the ten.
The two are not in tension: a system can be both deeply documented and currently underperforming at scale.
2. **"Retention doesn't matter as much as people think it does" vs heavy retention-graph analysis — apparent contradiction, reconciled honestly.** She states the hot take twice

Her own reconciliation: retention is a floor, not a differentiator — a Short with 70% retention "still got 10 million views because it was all returning viewers," i.e.,
distribution/reach (impressions × repeat-view behavior) matters more than the retention number alone [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 277-280]. The practices (analyze
graphs, hit 90+) and the hot take (retention isn't the lever that explains 10M vs 1M) are consistent under that reading — record both, don't cherry-pick.

early-stage learning, and current cadence (~18 vids/wk per TrySpansa; consecutive daily uploads in my pull) is sustained volume [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 50-53;
tryspansa.com/channels/jennyhoyos].

The claim is conditional on idea quality ("if the idea is good"); the 2026 numbers show the system converts ideas to floor-clearing output reliably, but not to consistent 10M
virality. Flagged as the central claimed-vs-verified gap (see Verified vs Claimed).

## New Terms (out-of-vocab mechanisms — proposal for synthesis normalization)
1. **foreshadowing** — a (usually spoken, ≤2 lines / ≤3s) promise of the ending immediately after the hook, giving the viewer a concrete expectation the video must then pay off;
she does it "in every video" [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 152-158; MFM transcript lines 177-182]. Distinct from curiosity gap (gap = withheld info; foreshadow =
stated destination).
2. **mechanism** — a visible progress tracker that pushes the viewer to the end without a twist: budget counter ticking down, "three steps we need to do," MrBeast's closing circle;
"people have a good expectation of what they're going to watch and what's actually going to be at the end" [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 119-125; MFM transcript
lines 182-187].
3. **but/so storytelling** — replacing "and then" chains with "but/so" conflict beats so every transition adds a new obstacle ("I was walking, but then it started raining, so I had
to find an umbrella..."); sourced from the South Park writing technique; "probably one of the highest leverage techniques" [CITED: MFM transcript lines 209-222; Creator Science
#167 transcript lines 170-175].
4. **expectation-loop closure** — the closed-loop pattern of hook-promise → foreshadow-confirmation → payoff that lands exactly as promised, with the twist occurring only inside
the delivered payoff (gift delivered, then drops and breaks) [CITED: Creator Science #167 transcript lines 126-136].
5. **twist-ending / Peak-End payoff** — ending on an intense emotional beat (wholesome or funniest moment, often a twist) because "peak end theory" dictates that the ending
retroactively colors the whole video; drives both satisfaction and rewatch [CITED: MFM transcript lines 256-267].
