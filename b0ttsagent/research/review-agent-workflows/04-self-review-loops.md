# 04 — Self-Review Loops

## (a) Angle and wave

**Angle:** Self-review loops — the agent reviewing its own output vs. a separate-context reviewer: what literature and practice say about self-review failure modes, when self-review is or is not enough, and the "fresh eyes" question at the center of the seed note. **Wave:** research wave 1 of the review-agent-workflows series (siblings: `01-code-review-agents.md`, `02-writing-doc-review.md`, `03-adversarial-red-team.md`). Method: live web research (SearXNG returned empty on most queries; fell back to Exa-backed `websearch`, which produced rich results; read arXiv abs pages and full paper excerpts for the material claims) + first-principles reasoning grounded in the seed note. Reasoning is explicitly labeled; everything else is sourced.

## (b) Seed note (verbatim)

> Subagent Review
> When creating something, stop allowing agents to review their own products. Always instruct agents to spawn review subagents on their work. (say 'spawn a read-only reviewer in a separate context; do not trust your own summary')

## (c) Questions answered in order

### Q1 — What does the literature say about self-review / self-correction failure modes? (†)

The academic picture is unusually consistent: **intrinsic self-correction (the model critiquing its own output with no external feedback) fails on reasoning tasks, and often degrades performance.**

- **Huang et al., "Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet" (arXiv 2310.01798, 2023).** Under "intrinsic self-correction" (no oracle labels, no external feedback), accuracy *dropped* across all benchmarks and all models tested; self-correction also underperformed self-consistency (majority vote over multiple samples) at equal inference cost. **T1.**
- **Kamoi et al., "When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes? A Critical Survey" (arXiv 2406.01297, 2024).** The survey's headline: *no prior work demonstrates successful self-correction with feedback from prompted LLMs in general tasks* under fair settings (many positive results used oracle labels or weak initial prompts). Self-correction works where **reliable external feedback** exists (code interpreters, tools, execution signals) and where **large-scale fine-tuning** instills it; some tasks are "exceptionally suited" because verification is trivial (e.g., constrained generation). **T1.**
- **Tyen et al., "LLMs cannot find reasoning errors, but can correct them given the error location" (arXiv 2311.08516, 2023/24).** The bottleneck is *mistake-finding*, not correction: models struggle to locate errors even in unambiguous cases, but when given the error location they correct robustly. A small trained classifier found mistakes better than prompting a large model. **T1.**
- **Zhang et al., "Understanding the Dark Side of LLMs' Intrinsic Self-Correction" (arXiv 2412.14959, 2024).** Self-correction makes models *waver* intermediate and final answers, induces prompt bias, and produces human-like cognitive bias (overthinking, perfectionism) on complex tasks. Mitigations: "question repeating" and few-sample SFT. **T1.**
- **Liu & Meng, "Self-Correction as Feedback Control" (arXiv 2604.22273, 2026).** Frames self-correction as a feedback loop with a measurable stability threshold (near-zero error-introduction rate). A "verify first" prompt ablation on GPT-4o-mini turned a −6.2 pp degradation into +0.2 pp (paired McNemar p<10⁻⁴) — i.e., *prompting alone* can move a model from harmful to beneficial self-correction. **T1.**
- **Chen et al., "The Self-Correction Illusion: Role Relabeling Gates Explicit Error Flagging in LLMs" (arXiv 2606.05976, 2026).** Holds an erroneous claim byte-identical and varies only its chat-template role (own `<thought>` vs. user message vs. tool response vs. `<memory>`). Relabeling the claim from the agent's own thought to an external role lifted explicit-correction rate by **23–93 percentage points** across 7 model families / 3 domains (10 of 13 cells p<0.001). The failure to self-correct is substantially a *chat-template artifact*, not a pure capability deficit. **T1.**
- **Zheng et al., "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena" (arXiv 2306.05685, 2023).** LLM judges show position bias, verbosity bias (GPT-3.5 and Claude-v1 failed a "repetitive list" attack 91.3% of the time), and self-enhancement bias (GPT-4 favored itself +10%, Claude-v1 +25% win rate). A *reference-guided judge* (judge answers independently, then grades against its own answer) cut math-judging failure from 70% to 15%. **T1.**

**Where self-correction succeeds (per the above):** style/quality tasks (Self-Refine's preference tasks), verifiable tasks, tasks with external feedback (code execution, tools), and after fine-tuning. **Reasoning:** the literature's consistent thread is that the *feedback signal* is the weak link — a model asked to critique its own reasoning has no independent signal, so it re-derives the same conclusion. The seed note's "separate context" is one way to inject independence; the literature shows external evidence (tests, tools, references) is the stronger lever.

### Q2 — What do practitioners report about same-context vs. separate-context review? (†)

Practitioner accounts overwhelmingly claim fresh context changes outcomes, and several give concrete incidents:

- **Bernardo Secades, "Claude Code: the writer/reviewer pattern" (claudecodesessions.com, 2026-07-07).** Detailed incident: the writer session had "reviewed" a diff minutes earlier and waved through a blocker (a retry wrapping both a charge and an audit emit → duplicate events). A read-only reviewer subagent with a clean context, told to run `git diff` itself and report only behavior/correctness/security findings, found it plus two should-fix items. Same model, same diff — only the context differed. Cost: ~30k tokens burned in the reviewer, ~600 returned to the main session. **T2.**
- **ClaudeKit, "Verification Loops with Subagents" (getclaudekit.com, 2026-07-16).** "An agent that checks its own work tends to approve it… it already believes the answer, so it confirms it." Recommends an adversarial frame ("Assume it contains a defect until proven otherwise"), read-only reviewer, and a *fresh reviewer each round* (a reviewer that passed round one is biased toward passing round two). **T2.**
- **Shopware AG, "Comparing AI code review models: a real-world experiment" (shopware.com, 2026-05-08).** Measured experiment: 17 runs over 2 days against 7 known defects. No single model found all defects; one defect appeared in only 1 of 6 identical runs; "two runs of the same model mostly retraced each other." Rule adopted: "no load-bearing result counts until a second, independent worker has confirmed it," cross-model-family where possible. **T1/T2.**
- **Nathan Payne, "Agent Approval Workflow and the Genesis of Mergepath" (nathanpayne.com, 2026-04-16).** Same-conversation self-review found real bugs (missing error handling, unquoted shell vars, race conditions). Reviewing under a *different GitHub identity* (author vs. reviewer persona) "measurably" improved review quality across Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex. Fresh-eyes signal: Codex found **17 bugs** in template code that had been reviewed **7 times** by the same reviewer, once it saw the code fresh in a new repo. **T2.**
- **GitHub issue anthropics/claude-code#20304 (2026-01-23).** Practitioner comments: "when a reviewer agent sees the author agent's reasoning… the reviewer consistently rates the code higher than when it reviews the same code blind. The review becomes validation rather than critique." Workaround: separate sessions in tmux panes communicating only via files on disk. **T2.**
- **claudelab.net, Masaki Hirokawa (2026-08-17).** When Claude Code made subagent *forking* (context inheritance) the default, the author's review agent silently became a rubber stamp. His analysis: "The review agent was not being lazy. Given the context it received, it was behaving exactly as it should… independence cannot be restored through instructions." Fix: call review agents by name (no inherited context), deny unnamed forks. **T2.**
- **Hacker News 46524125 (2026).** "Even having Opus review code written by Opus works very well as a first pass… The sub-agent gets fresh context, so it won't get 'poisoned' by the top level context's justifications… repeat the verification step until the sub-agent gives the code a 'pass'." **T2.**
- **Hacker News 48419194 (2026).** "Spawning separate agents to review the original agent's implementation results in a very noticeable increase in code quality and decrease in bugs… two or three rounds of sub-agent review… a fourth agent, usually from the other frontier lab" for load-bearing code. Also: different labs find the same issues but disagree on severity. **T2.**
- **Hacker News 46905770 (2026).** "LLMs [are] significantly better in the 'review' stage than the implementation stage… the same LLM asked to review against the same starting requirement will catch the problem almost always. You have to dump the context window for the review to work good." **T2.**
- **Hacker News 44837789 (2026).** Counterpoint: "Asking the agent to perform a code review on its own work is surprisingly fruitful" — same-context self-review worked for this user, but another user found the *framing* of the review prompt made Claude "almost excessively negative" about its own work. **T2.**
- **Hacker News 47394022 (2026).** A skeptic asks directly: "what's the evidence that the architect → developer → reviewer pipeline actually produces better results than just… talking to one strong model in one session?" Replies argue context cleaning, token-budget spreading, and reviewer quality — but concede the benefit may be "task separation and cleaning the context," not a fundamentally better model. **T2/T3.**
- **Anthropic, "How and when to use subagents in Claude Code" (claude.com, 2026-04-07).** Vendor guidance (capability, not evidence): a subagent "doesn't inherit the assumptions, context, or blind spots from the primary conversation"; "ask for fresh context when unbiased analysis matters." **T3/T4 for the claim that it works; T3 for what the product does.**

**Reasoning:** the practitioner consensus is that the mechanism is *context independence*, not model independence — the same model in a clean context finds what it waved through in the authoring context. The strongest counterpoint (HN 47394022) is that this may be mostly context hygiene plus extra token budget, not a qualitatively different reviewer. Both readings support the seed note's move; they differ on how much of the gain is "fresh eyes" vs. "clean context + more compute."

### Q3 — Which self-review variants are reported to work despite the failure modes?

- **Test-based / execution verification (strongest).** Self-debugging with code-execution feedback (Chen et al. 2023b, cited in Huang et al. and Kamoi et al.): when unit tests or error messages provide the feedback, self-correction reliably improves code. Kamoi et al. conclude external feedback is the condition under which self-correction "works well." **T1.**
- **Rubric / criteria-driven evaluation.** G-Eval (Liu et al., EMNLP 2023, arXiv 2303.16634): LLM judge with explicit evaluation criteria + chain-of-thought steps reached 0.514 Spearman with humans on SummEval — best at the time, but *moderate* agreement, and ablations show the judge model's capability (+0.113) dominates the rubric mechanics (CoT +0.014, probability weighting +0.012). G-Eval also showed bias toward LLM-generated text. **T1.** Practitioner rubrics: severity ladders with "don't pad" instructions (Secades; ClaudeKit) are reported to keep passes short and honest. **T2.**
- **Actionable-feedback self-review.** Self-Refine (Madaan et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2303.17651): specific, actionable feedback beat generic feedback (Sentiment Reversal 85% vs 73%; Code Optimization 15.6% vs 10.4%). **T1.**
- **Reference-guided self-verification.** Zheng et al. 2023: judge answers independently first, then grades against its own answer — math-judging failure 70%→15%. **T1.**
- **Verify-first prompting.** Liu & Meng 2026: a "verify first" instruction flipped GPT-4o-mini from −6.2 pp to +0.2 pp. **T1.**
- **Checklist self-verification.** Self-Refine's multi-aspect numeric scoring; ClaudeKit's "name what you checked when you find nothing" (a "looks good" with no evidence is worthless); Secades' severity ladder. **T2.**

**Reasoning:** the variants that work all add an *external or structured signal* (tests, a rubric, a reference answer, a verify-first step) that the bare "critique yourself" prompt lacks. A rubric alone does not fix the self-bias (G-Eval still prefers LLM text); it improves consistency and actionability.

### Q4 — What is the reported evidence for a "fresh eyes" effect in LLMs? (†)

- **Role relabeling (closest controlled evidence).** Chen et al. 2026 (arXiv 2606.05976): the same byte-identical claim draws near-zero correction inside the agent's own `<thought>` but lifts 23–93 pp when re-presented under an external role. This suggests part of what practitioners call "fresh eyes" is a *role/harness artifact* — the model treats its own intermediate as settled and external content as checkable. **T1.**
- **Changed sampling.** Self-consistency (Wang et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv 2203.11171): sampling multiple independent reasoning paths and majority-voting improved GSM8K by +17.9% absolute over greedy CoT — a "fresh draw" effect without any context change. Huang et al. 2023 note self-consistency beats self-correction at equal inference cost. **T1.**
- **Sampling variance in practice.** Shopware: one of seven known defects surfaced in only 1 of 6 identical review runs; "two runs of the same model mostly retraced each other." **T1/T2.**
- **Fresh-context anecdotes.** Payne's 17-bugs-after-7-reviews incident; HN 46905770 ("dump the context window for the review to work good"); Claude blog's clean-slate claim (no data). **T2/T3.**
- **Gap:** I found **no controlled academic study directly comparing "same-context self-review" vs. "separate-context review" as harness-level interventions.** The controlled evidence nearest to it is the role-relabeling paper (T1) and self-consistency (T1); everything else is practitioner anecdote (T2). This is the single most important gap for the seed note's central claim — see (f).

### Q5 — What do practitioners report on the cost tradeoff, and at what stakes do they switch?

- **Measured token costs.** Shopware: re-validating with a *resumed* reviewer session cost ~42,000 tokens vs. 137,000–312,000 for a fresh review of the same scope — and the resumed session still worked (re-checked the code, stood by open findings). **T1.** Secades: ~30k tokens burned in the reviewer, ~600 returned to the main session. **T2.**
- **Stakes thresholds.** Payne: changes under 300 diff lines or not touching security-sensitive paths get self-review only; above that, external review by a different agent is *required* (enforced by a CI label gate that blocks merge). **T2.** ClaudeKit: "Verification doubles the work, so spend it where being wrong is expensive… For a one-line copy tweak, skip it." **T2.** Secades: "For a three-line fix, read the diff yourself." **T2.**
- **Context-inheritance economics.** Hirokawa (claudelab.net): forking shares the cached prompt prefix, and the saving kicks in from the *second* parallel child onward — so inheritance is cheap for parallel fan-out but "always fork" is wrong for single delegations. **T2.**
- **Vendor note.** Claude blog: subagents "carry overhead… each spins up its own context, consumes tokens." **T3.**

**Reasoning:** the reported switch point is roughly "where a missed defect costs more than the review tokens." Practitioners converge on ~300 diff lines / security-sensitive paths as the threshold, and on reusing a reviewer session (rather than a fresh one) as the cheap re-check — which slightly contradicts ClaudeKit's "fresh reviewer each round" advice (see (f)).

### Q6 — When do practitioners deliberately rely on self-review anyway, and what guardrails do they use?

- **When:** low-stakes and rapid-iteration work — Payne's <300-line rule; ClaudeKit's one-line-copy-tweak skip; Secades' three-line-fix rule; zenn.dev's `/review`-style quick check in a short conversation (zenn.dev/malleus, 2025-12-21). HN 44837789 reports same-context self-review as a useful *first pass* before a human reads the diff. **T2.**
- **Guardrails (reported):**
  - **Read-only tool scoping as a mechanism, not an instruction.** Secades, ClaudeKit, Tim Schipper (tim-schipper.nl, 2026-07-08): a reviewer with no Edit/Write "physically cannot" patch; "an instruction asks, a mechanism enforces." **T2.**
  - **Severity ladder + "don't pad."** Secades: blocker/should-fix/nit with an explicit "never pad findings." **T2.**
  - **Adversarial framing.** ClaudeKit: "Assume it contains a defect until proven otherwise"; report problems, not approval. **T2.**
  - **Return contract.** Schipper: fixed output shape, evidence required, "permission to come back with a short answer." **T2.**
  - **Mechanical enforcement.** Payne: hooks that block PR creation without a `## Self-Review` section; branch protection; label gates. **T2.**
  - **Versioned reviewer prompt in the repo.** Secades: `.claude/agents/diff-reviewer.md` so every session gets the same reviewer. **T2.**

### Q7 — What is the evidence for multi-pass review (review → fix → re-review) vs. single pass?

- **Literature — iterative self-refinement works but with diminishing returns.** Self-Refine (Madaan et al. 2023): ~20% absolute average gain across 7 tasks, but gains concentrate in the first iterations and in preference/constrained tasks; math reasoning gained ~0 (GPT-4: 92.9→93.1), and ChatGPT's feedback on math was "everything looks good" in 94% of instances. **T1.** Reflexion (Shinn et al. 2023) iterates but relies on oracle/exact-match feedback (per Huang et al. and Kamoi et al.), so it is not intrinsic self-review. **T1 (as cited).**
- **Practice — multi-pass with a fresh reviewer each round.** ClaudeKit: "One pass catches the obvious… run the loop" — author → independent reviewer → fix only reported defects → *new* reviewer (new context) → stop on a clean round with evidence of what was tried. **T2.** Secades: "Two rounds, then a human… repeated rounds converge on the reviewer's taste, not on correctness." **T2.** HN 48419194: two or three review rounds encoded into the plan. **T2.**
- **Practice — resumed reviewer re-validation.** Shopware's loop experiment: handing the reviewer its own earlier session after a fix round worked (it noticed the fix, stood by open findings, re-checked code) at ~42k tokens vs. 137–312k fresh. **T1/T2.**

**Reasoning:** multi-pass helps most when each pass gets a *fresh or at least re-grounded* reviewer; same-context iterative self-refinement shows clear diminishing returns (Self-Refine) and can converge on the model's own taste. The practitioner pattern "fix only reported defects, then re-review" matches the literature's finding that correction is reliable once the error location is known (Tyen et al.).

## (d) Workflow candidates (trigger / procedure / evidence)

1. **Writer/reviewer split with a read-only fresh-context reviewer.** *Trigger:* a branch or feature is marked done, before merge/commit. *Procedure:* author implements; spawn a read-only reviewer in a fresh context receiving only the diff + acceptance criteria (no author rationale); reviewer runs `git diff` itself, reads surrounding code, reports findings tagged blocker/should-fix/nit with file:line and "what breaks under which input," explicitly told not to pad; findings cross back as text; author fixes; re-review. *Evidence:* T2 — Secades (2026-07-07): writer session waved through a duplicate-event blocker that the reviewer caught; ~30k tokens reviewer / ~600 back. Corroborated by ClaudeKit (T2), HN 46524125 (T2), GitHub #20304 (T2).
2. **Stakes-gated external review.** *Trigger:* diff >300 lines OR touches security-sensitive paths (auth, payments, secrets). *Procedure:* below threshold, self-review (optionally under a separate reviewer identity); above threshold, a different agent/model reviews and a label gate blocks merge until cleared. *Evidence:* T2 — Payne (2026-04-16): 17 bugs found by a fresh reviewer after 7 same-reviewer passes; identity-switch "measurably" improved self-review across three platforms.
3. **Fresh-reviewer-per-round verification loop.** *Trigger:* high-stakes change (money, auth, data migration, research you'll act on). *Procedure:* author → independent reviewer → author fixes *only* reported defects → new reviewer (new context) → stop on a clean round that names what it checked. *Evidence:* T2 — ClaudeKit (2026-07-16); T1/T2 — Shopware (2026-05-08) shows the cheaper variant (resumed reviewer, 42k vs 137–312k tokens) also works — flag for arbitration.
4. **Two-independent-worker confirmation.** *Trigger:* a load-bearing result (a claim you'll act on, a "pass" verdict). *Procedure:* no result counts until a second independent worker confirms it; cross-model-family where possible; a third worker arbitrates disagreement. *Evidence:* T1/T2 — Shopware: one of 7 defects surfaced in only 1 of 6 identical runs; "two runs of the same model mostly retraced each other."
5. **Same-context self-review as a cheap first pass** *(candidate — the seed note's rule says to stop doing this, but practitioners use it deliberately at low stakes).* *Trigger:* low-stakes, <300-line changes, rapid iteration. *Procedure:* ask the author to review its own work with a reviewer persona, a severity ladder, "don't pad," and a verify-first step; human reads the diff afterward. *Evidence:* T2 — Payne (found real bugs same-conversation); HN 44837789 ("surprisingly fruitful" but framing-sensitive); T1 — Liu & Meng's verify-first prompt flips degradation to gain.

## (e) Failure modes and adoptability barriers

- **Rubber-stamping / self-approval.** The dominant reported failure: an agent "checking" its own work confirms it (ClaudeKit; claudelab.net; GitHub #20304; HN 47360961). The seed note's rule is aimed directly at this.
- **Silent context inheritance.** When the harness passes parent context to the reviewer (Claude Code's forking default; the `Task` tool passing conversation history), the reviewer stops being a third party — and instructions cannot restore independence (claudelab.net, 2026-08-17). **Adoptability barrier:** the beginner must verify their harness actually isolates the reviewer's context; several products default to sharing it.
- **Framing sensitivity.** A negatively-framed review prompt made Claude "almost excessively negative" about its own work (HN 44837789). Prompt wording is a first-order variable.
- **Same-model blind spots survive the split.** Secades: "Context independence, not model independence… blind spots of the model survive the split." Cross-model review is the mitigation (Shopware; HN 48419194).
- **Diminishing returns and taste convergence.** Multi-pass converges on the reviewer's taste, not correctness (Secades); Self-Refine shows flat math gains and 94% "everything looks good" feedback (T1).
- **Cost.** Fresh reviews are 3–7× a resumed review (Shopware); each subagent burns its own context (Claude blog). **Adoptability barrier:** token cost and latency are the most-cited reasons beginners skip review agents.
- **Reviewer prompt quality.** A vague "find problems" prompt produces noise; the severity ladder and "don't pad" are what keep passes useful (Secades). Dispatch prompts can leak the author's framing (Secades: "the `git diff` rule shrinks the channel, it doesn't close it").

## (f) Open gaps and suggested follow-ups

- **No controlled study of same-context vs. separate-context review as a harness intervention.** The seed note's central claim ("do not trust your own summary") rests on strong practitioner anecdote (T2) plus adjacent controlled evidence (role relabeling, self-consistency — T1), but I found no direct A/B. **Follow-up:** a controlled experiment on a fixed bug set comparing (a) same-context self-review, (b) fresh-context same-model review, (c) fresh-context different-model review, measuring findings, false positives, and tokens.
- **Fresh reviewer each round vs. resumed reviewer.** ClaudeKit recommends a new reviewer per round; Shopware measured a *resumed* reviewer working at ~1/3–1/7 the cost. Contradiction at T2 vs T1/T2 — needs arbitration.
- **False-positive rates for separate-context review.** No sourced "X% of separate-context findings are wrong" number found in the self-review context (sibling angle 03 flags the same gap for adversarial review).
- **Cost numbers are single-account.** Shopware and Secades are the only quantified cost reports found.
- **"Fresh eyes" decay.** Payne's 17-bugs-after-7-reviews is one incident; no one has measured how quickly a reviewer "goes stale" on familiar code. **Follow-up:** reviewer-rotation cadence studies.

## (g) Headline — 3 most adoptable patterns

The three most adoptable patterns, by evidence and setup cost: **(1) Writer/reviewer split with a read-only fresh-context reviewer** (T2 — Secades' incident is the clearest: same model, same diff, the writer session waved through a blocker the reviewer caught; ~30k tokens, ~600 back) — this is the seed note's rule made concrete and is the cheapest reliable first step; **(2) Stakes-gated external review** (T2 — Payne's <300-line/security-path threshold with mechanical enforcement) — tells you exactly when self-review is enough and when it isn't, which is the cost question the seed note leaves open; **(3) Two-independent-worker confirmation for load-bearing results** (T1/T2 — Shopware's measured finding that one of seven defects surfaced in only 1 of 6 identical runs) — the highest-evidence pattern, worth reserving for results you'll act on. A zero-setup entry point is same-context self-review with a severity-ladder, verify-first prompt (T2/T1), used deliberately at low stakes — but the literature (Q1) says never trust it as the final gate.
