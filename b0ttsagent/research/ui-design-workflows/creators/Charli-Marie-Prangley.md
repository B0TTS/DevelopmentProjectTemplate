## 2. Step-by-Step Workflow

Prangley's named, ordered workflow is documented across four first-party bodies of content that form one consistent sequence: (A) the planning phase, (B) the wireframing phase, (C) the full ConvertKit marketing-page process (her most complete named sequence, narrated in her own words), and (D) the post-launch iteration loop. A fifth body (E) covers her rebrand process. All steps are first-party; every step is source-linked.

### A. Planning phase — "Designing a website" series, Ep. 1 (2018)

"The very very first thing I do when approaching a new design project is plan." Her named planning steps:

1. **Define the site's goal and the visitor's intent.** She writes down the two frames of mind people arrive with — "content or hire me" — before planning any pages. Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Dwo-UOjDuw
2. **Plan the site map: pages + templates.** She lists every page (home, content hub, portfolio, speaking, about, contact) and the templates each needs (audio/video/written content templates, project template, plain giveaway page). Source: same.
3. **Scope an MVP first.** To avoid overwhelm she starts with a minimal homepage that links out to YouTube/social/Dribbble and carries a bio + email, "so that means I can get my new site, like, up and running" before building all sub-pages. Source: same.
4. **Design the MVP homepage as the seed of a design system/brand** — "In designing this page, I guess I'll be able to develop a design system for it, and kind of like a brand for my website as well that'll make it easier to design these other pages." Source: same.

Her blog post "Which comes first: the design or the copy?" (2021) names the ordering rule that precedes all of this: **content before design** — "Content is different to copy. Copy is the exact words that end up on the page, content is an outline of what points need to be communicated." She writes the content outline herself (competitor research, product knowledge, user insights) and only then "arranging content into a design wireframe to decide on the hierarchy." Source: https://charlimarie.com/blog/design-or-copy

### B. Wireframing phase — "How to wireframe a website" (2015; updated 2020)

"Wireframing is the first stage of the web design process." Her named sub-process:

1. **Know the content before you start** — "Your job is to design the information so you need to know what information is going on the page... before you can start designing." She writes a list of the page's elements (header, bio, footer, post titles, social links, search, newsletter signup) or keeps the copywriter's rough copy in a Google doc beside her. Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PmmQjLqJQlY
2. **Rapid ideation on paper — "Crazy 8's".** Fold paper into 8 sections and draw 8 different wireframe ideas as quickly as possible ("Drawing nicely is not the point"). Source: same.
3. **Develop a personal symbol key** — a straight line = headline, squiggles = body copy, a box with a cross = picture, circles = icons. Source: same.
4. **Star the promising bits and combine them** into a new mockup, then move to the computer (Sketch, later Figma; on iPad she uses Concepts — "Wireframing is the first step in my web design process where I'm getting ideas out rapidly"). Sources: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PmmQjLqJQlY · https://concepts.app/en/stories/sketching-wireframes-web-design/
5. **Wireframe in plain grey boxes + default text only** — "I don't want the person who is critiquing this wireframe to think about anything other than the structure of the site." She makes as many wireframes as she has ideas for. Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PmmQjLqJQlY
6. **"Sleep on it" self-review gate** — "After that it's a good idea to sleep on it at this point and come back to it the next day before you share for feedback or before you start on the visuals... you're going to be able to come back to it with a fresh mind." Source: same.

### C. The full ConvertKit marketing-page process — Inside Marketing Design S1 E1 (2020)

Her most complete named, ordered sequence, narrated in her own words (full transcript):

1. **Project arises** — someone spots a need, or she sees it through her own research (e.g., a new feature page, a homepage redesign, a pricing-page fix from user research). Source: https://insidemarketingdesign.com/at/convertkit
2. **Conversation** — async in Basecamp or over a video call: what the page needs to do, why, what information to express. Source: same.
3. **Synthesize notes** — "not really write a brief as such but more make some notes about what I think the page needs to do." Source: same.
4. **Content outline (not copy)** — "I'm not writing the copy, but I'm figuring out in general what we're gonna talk about." Source: same.
5. **Wireframe based on the content** — done solo; "I'll only really share it with the team when I'm ready for feedback." Source: same.
6. **Share wireframe for feedback — structure gate.** Uploads to Invision with comments; asks for thoughts on the structure/hierarchy ("you've left out this point", "this needs more room", "move this up the hierarchy"). Source: same.
7. **Copywriter + visual design in parallel.** Once structure is agreed, the copywriter (Dani) writes exact copy while she does the "visual design phase", sharing screenshots with the other designers and the developer for quick feedback along the way. Source: same.
8. **Edit copy into the design**, then a **final feedback/sign-off gate** with the wider team — she always ensures the person who requested the page and the director of marketing are happy before build. Source: same.
9. **Prepare for build.** No red-lining: she puts the final design on a separate Figma page (so the developer knows exactly which of the many iteration frames to build), writes a GitHub issue with functionality notes, and exports assets to Dropbox. Source: same.
10. **Developer builds; designer QA-feedback loop.** The developer (Cory) builds and shares when he needs feedback; she replies with full-page screenshots + inline Invision comments, then the wider team pokes around for bugs (no separate QA role). Source: same.
11. **Ship → measure.** After launch she submits a data request to the data team, who build a dashboard for the page's metrics. Source: same.
12. **Goal-setting and testing loops.** OKRs set project priorities; A/B testing via the NorthStar tool ("we try them out, measure them, see what works, and then we know what to double down on"); pre-mortems and postmortems for bigger projects, saved to Basecamp. Source: same.

### D. Post-launch iteration loop — Hotjar video (2021) + Marketing Design Dispatch

Her named iteration loop for improving a live page (the Grayscale font sales page, <1% conversion):

1. **Analyze page performance** — heatmaps, scroll data, and visitor recordings in Hotjar (75% of desktop visitors reached the Buy Now button vs only 48% on mobile). Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OJMuc3zFi_w
2. **Make changes based on the data** — two header buy buttons, a tab system moved higher on mobile, licensing info in a modal, a value-led header ("Annotate Your Designs With") instead of the font name. Source: same.
3. **Review changes & publish**, then **check back in a few weeks** to see if the heatmaps/recordings show the issues fixed; she also adds an exit-intent survey ("Is there anything holding you back from purchasing the Grayscale?"). Source: same.

The newsletter post "Making website improvements when you don't have time for a full redesign" documents the same loop at ConvertKit with measured results: a migrations link in the hero → 721% increase in homepage→migrations traffic; a demo-request callout → 65% more clicks to the demo page; an A/B test adding a revenue calculator → 5% signup improvement. She notes the loop's limit ("a Frankenstein's monster of bolted-on UI") and that full redesigns should be modular. Source: https://pages.charlimarie.com/posts/making-website-improvements-when-you-don-t-have-time-for-a-full-redesign-1

### E. Rebrand process — Unbreakable Brands interview (2024)

For the ConvertKit → Kit rebrand she describes: **rebranding in public** (a documentary series + sharing design screenshots for feedback), a **research phase** to catch negative perceptions of the name, **three brand personality traits (bold, sincere, expert) as the decision frame**, color palette work **alongside accessibility/contrast**, and **post-launch iteration** measured by adoption of the Kit name and branded-search traffic. Source: https://unbreakablebrands.com/convertkit-rebrand-with-charli-prangley/

Her illustration-system newsletter post names the same 4-step pattern for systems work: **moodboard → exploration → decision making (3 levels of illustration) → creating components in Figma**. Source: https://pages.charlimarie.com/posts/the-process-behind-the-new-convertkit-illustration-system

## 3. What Makes It Distinct

- **Content-before-design as a hard rule, with a named distinction between content and copy.** She refuses both "design with Lorem Ipsum" and "copy-first" — the content outline (what points must be communicated) is the shared artifact both designer and copywriter work from. This is her signature ordering principle, stated in both the blog post and the podcast. Sources: https://charlimarie.com/blog/design-or-copy · https://insidemarketingdesign.com/at/convertkit
- **The "two lines that come together and go apart" collaboration model.** She explicitly frames her process as solo phases (wireframing alone) punctuated by deliberate, gated sharing ("I'll only really share it with the team when I'm ready for feedback") — feedback is requested on a schedule she controls, not in standing design reviews. Source: https://insidemarketingdesign.com/at/convertkit
- **"Crazy 8's" + a personal symbol key as the wireframe engine.** A named rapid-ideation technique (8 wireframes on one folded sheet) plus a self-invented shorthand (line = headline, squiggle = body, crossed box = image) that lets her capture ideas faster than neatness. Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PmmQjLqJQlY
- **Grey-box-only wireframes as a critique-control device.** She keeps wireframes in plain grey boxes and default text specifically so reviewers can't react to anything but structure — a deliberate anti-pixel-polish gate. Source: same.
- **"Sleep on it" as a named self-review gate** before sharing or going to visuals. Source: same.
- **No red-lining handoff.** She deliberately skips pixel-spec annotations because the developer can measure spacing in Figma himself — "My time is better spent on moving onto the next project." Source: https://insidemarketingdesign.com/at/convertkit
- **In-house, data-driven iteration as the core skill.** She positions her strength as "knowing what to test and what to iterate on" rather than creating brands from scratch, and runs the full loop herself: user research calls, A/B tests, heatmap analysis, data-request dashboards, pre/post-mortems. Sources: https://unbreakablebrands.com/convertkit-rebrand-with-charli-prangley/ · https://insidemarketingdesign.com/at/convertkit · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OJMuc3zFi_w
- **Rebranding in public as a process tool.** The Kit rebrand was run as a documentary series with live feedback on design screenshots — process as content, and content as a research channel. Source: https://unbreakablebrands.com/convertkit-rebrand-with-charli-prangley/
