## 2. Step-by-Step Workflow

McLeod's process is the challenge-design loop for Blackbox's 81 "lights" (puzzles solved without touching the screen), plus a documented port variant for visionOS. All steps are first-party — his own words in the Going Indie podcast transcript (Feb 3, 2025), the Apple Developer feature (Jan 30, 2024), his Medium posts, and his own talk announcements. The canonical step-by-step walkthrough is his CocoaHeadsNL talk "UIKit Engineers Hate Him: How A Blackbox Challenge is Made" (Feb 17, 2021), in which he walks through designing and building one challenge "combining animations, accessibility, audio and perhaps one more thing" — the video transcript could not be retrieved (all transcript routes blocked), so step content below is sourced from the podcast transcript and Apple feature, which he gave in the same period.

### A. The core loop — designing a Blackbox challenge

1. **Idea capture with the "observable change" test.** "Coming up with an idea for a Blackbox Challenge really only requires something that someone can change and that you can observe the change of to kind of figure out that they figured it out." Ideas arrive during low-cognitive-load activity ("They usually come to me when I'm doing less mental things, like running or biking"), and he keeps a backlog: "I have a long list of ideas... like 50 or 70 or something." Source: https://podcast.going-indie.com/episodes/how-blackbox-won-an-apple-design-award-ryan-mcleod/transcript
2. **Design the interface as a "castle with a moat" (named difficulty dial).** "I like to think of the puzzles as kind of like a Castle with a moat around it and like you can have that just an easy crossing or like lift the drawbridge kind of like a harder approach. So it's like you're trying to create an intuitive interface to something novel. But you don't want it so easy that you look at it and you understand how it works immediately, but that it nudges you in the correct direction." Source: same transcript.
3. **Design satisfying visuals/audio, then dial them back.** "Coming up with the visuals and audio for that, that are satisfying, there's often really obvious ones that people will just understand, but that would just be an interface and not a puzzle. So dialing that back and finding the balance." Source: same transcript.
4. **Playtest by handing the phone over.** Early on: "I would hand them the phone and they didn't want to hand it back because they were having fun playing it, which was really cool. And I thought, okay, maybe there's some potential here." Source: same transcript.
5. **Run the "months" iteration loop (explicit loop).** "There's so many subtle adjustments and things that happen that people might not notice... might tweak it to make it easier for them when they were having difficulty with it or avoid kind of red herrings. Um, those are the things that make a puzzle take months." Source: same transcript.
6. **Beta-testing gate.** Pre-launch beta testers were the first quality gate — "one of its beta testers called it 'Apple Design Award material'." Source: https://medium.com/@warpling/blackbox-turns-four-49da6c1d4b2c (read via https://web.archive.org/web/20231114165007/https://medium.com/@warpling/blackbox-turns-four-49da6c1d4b2c)
7. **Shipping gate: "perfection is the enemy of good."** He launched when savings ran out: "I was running out of money and couldn't keep polishing it anymore. Had to know, like, perfection is the enemy of good. So now it had to go out and make money." Source: Going Indie transcript (same URL).
8. **Post-launch analytics loop (explicit loop).** "I'm switching things to telemetry deck now... follow the onboarding, and I can see like how many people are not making it through the first puzzle. And that's just bad for everyone. That's the only way I would know that." Source: same transcript.

### B. The port variant — Blackbox for Vision (visionOS, 2023–2024)

1. **Sketch first in a notebook.** Apple's feature shows "Ryan McLeod's notebook shows pen sketches of what will become Blackbox on Apple Vision Pro" — the port began as pen sketches. Source: https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=gvesi4wr
2. **Find a medium-native metaphor.** "I wanted something a little floaty and magical, but still grounded in reality... I landed on the idea of bubbles. They're like soap bubbles: They're natural, they have this hyper-realistic gloss, and they move in a way you're familiar with." Source: same.
3. **Redesign for the new context, don't port.** "Unlike Blackbox on iOS, you're not going to play this when you're walking home from school or waiting in line... It had to be designed differently. No matter how exciting the background is... it's not fun to just stare at something." Source: same.
4. **Iterate by following and breaking your own rules (explicit loop).** "I'm trying to figure out what makes Blackbox tick on iOS, and how to bring that to visionOS. That requires some creative following of my own rules — and breaking some of them." He frames the build as iterative: "McLeod is essentially building this plane as he's flying it — something he views as a positive." Source: same.

### C. Cross-project constants

- **Anti-touch as the design engine.** "Blackbox is a collection of now 81 puzzles... that don't involve touching the screen. So, all of it requires you to think really outside the box with sensors or celestial events." Source: Going Indie transcript.
- **Accessibility as a design driver.** He sonified every challenge with Gus Callahan so the game is fully playable by blind players — "the seemingly insurmountable challenge of making a puzzle game with vague visual clues fully accessible to blind players." Source: https://medium.com/@warpling/blackbox-turns-four-49da6c1d4b2c (archive link above).
- **Sharing-by-design.** He "designed for sharing since day one": free game to remove the download barrier, random grid layout to encourage comparison and screenshots, hint credits as referral rewards, customized share placeholder text. Source: https://medium.com/mobile-growth/share-because-you-want-to-c366beeea88c (read via https://web.archive.org/web/20251018121358/https://medium.com/mobile-growth/share-because-you-want-to-c366beeea88c)
- **Prototype-first hardware tinkering.** His Gravity scale-app post documents the same pattern: brainstorm the constraint (conductive, capacitive, common, single-point contact → "a spoon was the perfect solution"), build a barebones test app, record real sensor data, verify linearity ("Dude. It's perfectly linear"), then ship. Source: https://medium.com/swlh/turning-the-iphone-6s-into-a-digital-scale-f2197dc2b6e7 (read via https://web.archive.org/web/20241202145253/https://medium.com/swlh/turning-the-iphone-6s-into-a-digital-scale-f2197dc2b6e7)

## 3. What Makes It Distinct

- **The "observable change" test as the idea filter.** A challenge must be something the player can change and the game can observe — a falsifiable design criterion, not a vibe. Source: Going Indie transcript.
- **The "castle with a moat" difficulty dial.** He explicitly designs an interface to something novel that *nudges* rather than explains — the puzzle must be neither instantly readable (that's "just an interface") nor opaque. Source: Going Indie transcript.
- **"Dialing back" the obvious solution.** The signature move is taking the satisfying, obvious visual/audio answer and deliberately making it less legible so it stays a puzzle. Source: Going Indie transcript.
- **Red-herring removal as a named iteration target.** The "months" loop is explicitly about removing false paths and tuning difficulty from playtester feedback — not adding content. Source: Going Indie transcript.
- **Anti-touch as the design engine.** The entire game is built on the constraint that solutions must not involve touching the screen (sensors, celestial events, singing, USB cable, body movement) — the constraint is the genre. Source: Going Indie transcript; https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=gvesi4wr
- **"Perfection is the enemy of good" as a named shipping gate.** A deliberate anti-polish tripwire: launch when you can't polish anymore, not when it's done. Source: Going Indie transcript.
- **Analytics as a difficulty sensor, not a revenue sensor.** He watches onboarding completion ("how many people are not making it through the first puzzle") to tune the first-challenge experience. Source: Going Indie transcript.
- **Deliberate naivety as an advantage.** "There's an advantage to not knowing expected or common patterns" — he frames not knowing platform conventions as a design asset when entering visionOS. Source: https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=gvesi4wr
