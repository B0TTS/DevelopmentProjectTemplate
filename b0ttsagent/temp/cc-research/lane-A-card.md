# Research: Generic USB 3.0 "1080p60" HDMI Capture Cards (VIXLW B0FW3K52JL class)

**Scope:** Real-world capture quality/behavior of no-name aluminum-bodied "4K@30 in / 1080p60 out" USB 3.0 HDMI capture cards, exemplified by the VIXLW card (ASIN B0FW3K52JL, "Video Capture Card, 4K USB3.0 HDMI to USB C, 1080P60FPS…"). Question: what chip, what pixel format, true 60fps or fake, what artifacts, quality ceiling vs premium cards, and is the card or the OBS fullscreen-projector path the bottleneck at 1080p60.

## Summary

This card class is almost certainly built on the MacroSilicon **MS2130 / MS2131** (or older **MS2109**) HDMI-to-USB capture chip. The MS2130/2131 is a genuine USB 3.0 (UVC 1.0) chip that outputs **uncompressed YUY2 4:2:2, 8-bit, up to 1920×1080@60 (~2.0 Gbps)** or MJPEG — *no H.264* — but with a fixed output frame-rate "regardless of the input frame-rate," so a 30 fps source is **duplicated to fake 60 fps**, and quality is limited by 4:2:2 chroma, firmware-dependent sharpening/scaling, and inconsistent limited/full color-range handling. On a true USB 3.x port with current firmware it does deliver real 60 fps YUY2; on a USB 2.0 port (or an MS2109 card sold with a blue-painted USB 2.0 connector) it silently falls back to MJPEG and effectively 30 fps. In a 1080p60 chain of GPU → OBS fullscreen projector → HDMI → card, **the card is the image-fidelity bottleneck** (resolution/color/chroma ceiling); the projector path is only a *frame-pacing/tearing* risk (a real but separate Windows/OBS issue), not a resolution or color limiter.

**Confidence: HIGH** on chip family, pixel formats, and fake-60 mechanism (datasheets, chip teardown with firmware dump, kernel patches, community firmware repos). **MEDIUM** that this exact VIXLW unit is MS2130/2131 (advertised claims match the MS2130 datasheet exactly, and an identical aluminum body was torn down as MS2131, but no teardown of this specific ASIN exists; some sellers ship MS2109 boards in the same body). **LOW** on VIXLW-specific review claims found online (SEO content farms, and one "review" covers a different VIXLW ASIN).

## Findings

### 1. Chip family: MacroSilicon MS2130 / MS2131 (USB 3.0) — or MS2109 (USB 2.0, the fake-60 trap)

- **The advertised spec sheet is the MS2130 datasheet.** MacroSilicon MS2130: HDMI 1.4b input up to **4K@30**, "default max resolution 1920×1080@60Hz," output YUV422 and MJPEG, UVC 1.0, integrated USB 3.0 device controller with **USB 2.0-compatible mode**. This is exactly the VIXLW's "4K@30 HDMI input, 1080p60 capture" claim. MS2131 is the same chip + HDMI loop-out, 2×I2S, SPDIF, audio mixing. [UltraSemi/MS2130](https://www.ultrasemi.com/en/ic/macrosilicon/ms2130.html) · [MacroSilicon MS2130 product page](http://en.macrosilicon.com/info.asp?base_id=2&third_id=75) · [MS2131 | UltraSemi](https://doc.ultrasemi.com/en/ic/macrosilicon/ms2131.html)
- **Teardown of the identical aluminum body:** an eevblog teardown + firmware dump of these exact aluminum USB3.0 1080p60 cards (AliExpress/Amazon/eBay, black/two blues) found a **"JZY-2131" board, MS2131 chip** (ZB25WD40 SPI flash, two firmware images — one 1080p60-capable, one only 1080p50). Warning from the teardown author: *"in the same aluminum body there could be different implementations on different chips"*; loop-out is "condensed/compressed and looks ugly." [EEVblog teardown](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/reviews/4k-ultrahd-usb3-0-1080p-60fps-hdmi-capture-card-on-macrosilicon-ms2131-chip/)
- **MS5558:** no such chip is documented in MacroSilicon's capture lineup (MS2107/2108/2109/2109S/2130/2130S/2131/2131S/2132). The relevant family is MS2109 (USB 2.0) vs MS2130/MS2131/MS2130S (USB 3.0). [UltraSemi Video Capture selection table](https://doc.ultrasemi.com/en/ic/macrosilicon/video-capture.html)
- **The MS2109 risk:** many cards with the same "4K@30 in, 1080p60 out" claims are actually the older **MS2109 (USB 2.0 only)**. Devices are "sometimes offered as 'USB3' device but have only a blue usb2 connector." [c3voc USB2 UVC wiki](https://c3voc.de/wiki/hardware:usb2-capture-devices) — a documented MS2109 player project: input 4K30/1080p60, but **output MJPEG 1080p30 max, YUY2 1080p5/720p10, and "some device claims 1080P 60FPS output support but that's fake. When selected, they still output at 30."** [yume-chan/ms2109-player](https://github.com/yume-chan/ms2109-player) · [HyperHDR MS2109 teardown/test](https://www.hyperhdr.eu/2020/11/grabber-for-hyperhdr-rullz-usb-20.html) · [MS2109 datasheet (USB2.0)](http://en.macrosilicon.com/info.asp?base_id=2&third_id=50)

### 2. Pixel format delivered to the OS: raw YUY2 4:2:2 (8-bit), MJPEG as fallback — no H.264

- MS2130 outputs **YUV422 (YUY2)** and **MJPEG** over UVC 1.0 — there is **no H.264** in this chip. The osmocom MS2130 talk: *"Output via USB is fixed to raw 4:2:2 YUV (or MJPEG)."* [osmodevcon2024 hsdaoh slides](https://people.osmocom.org/steve-m/hsdaoh_slides/rendered/osmodevcon2024_hsdaoh.pdf)
- **Bandwidth correction:** YUY2 1080p60 = 1920×1080×2 B × 60 = **~249 MB/s ≈ 2.0 Gbps** (the "3.1 Gbps" figure in the task premise corresponds to RGB24 at ~3.0 Gbps, which these cards do not deliver). 2.0 Gbps fits USB 3.0 Gen 1 (5 Gbps, ~350–450 MB/s practical) with room to spare; it is **impossible over USB 2.0** (~40 MB/s practical). [StreamKit USB bandwidth KB](https://streamkit.co.uk/knowledge-base/usb-bandwidth-issues)
- **USB 2.0 fallback behavior (confirmed):** on an MS2130, when connected to a USB 2.0 port "only MJPEG (and NV12 under Windows)" modes are offered; on USB 3.0 "only YUV" (YUY2) — i.e., plugging into a 2.0 port silently switches you to compressed MJPEG. [HyperHDR discussion #499](https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperHDR/discussions/499)

### 3. True 1080p60 vs fake 60: mechanism and real-world failure modes

- The MS2130/2131 **can** do true 1080p60 YUY2 over USB 3.0 (datasheet: 1920×1080@60 YUV422). [UltraSemi comparison table](https://doc.ultrasemi.com/en/ic/macrosilicon/video-capture.html) · [tiny-pilot discussion (raw YUYV at 60fps confirmed)](https://github.com/tiny-pilot/tinypilot/discussions/1593)
- **The "fake 60" mechanism is input frame-duplication:** the chip's "Output is a configured framerate, **irregardless of the input framerate**." Feed it 30 fps and it presents 60 fps UVC by repeating frames — smooth-looking in OBS stats, but every frame is a duplicate (real temporal resolution 30 fps). [hsdaoh slides](https://people.osmocom.org/steve-m/hsdaoh_slides/rendered/osmodevcon2024_hsdaoh.pdf)
- **Firmware lottery:** two firmware versions exist — one capped at 1080p**50**, one reaching 1080p60; behavior differs (test bars on boot etc.). [EEVblog teardown](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/reviews/4k-ultrahd-usb3-0-1080p-60fps-hdmi-capture-card-on-macrosilicon-ms2131-chip/)
- **MS2109 fake-60 is well documented:** 1080p60 MJPEG is advertised but fails or drops every 2nd frame ("in 1080p it always runs 30 fps (while in MJPG mode)"). [drakmor gist](https://gist.github.com/drakmor/ad681f7866b40325291b581fd8e7a71a) · [yume-chan/ms2109-player](https://github.com/yume-chan/ms2109-player) · [HyperHDR MS2109 blog](https://www.hyperhdr.eu/2020/11/grabber-for-hyperhdr-rullz-usb-20.html); a Linux kernel thread shows a generic card announcing 1080p60 MJPEG whose 60 fps interval isn't real — URBs get lost and frames are dropped. [linux-kernel UVC bandwidth thread](https://lists.openwall.net/linux-kernel/2021/02/01/1180)
- **USB 3.0 vs 2.0 port consequences:** on USB 2.0 the card renegotiates to MJPEG (or NV12), adds latency and dropped frames; real users confirm "slight but perceptible lag and occasional dropped frames" on USB 2.0 with this exact card class. [CarXplorer VIXLW review (low confidence — different ASIN, SEO content)](https://carxplorer.com/vixlw-cam-link-4k-review/) · [HyperHDR #499](https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperHDR/discussions/499). Also: AMD/ASMedia/Renesas USB controllers are a known cause of frame drops/audio dropouts with USB 3.0 capture devices. [AVerMedia FAQ](https://www.avermedia.com/en/support/faq/z8p4o5MZ)

### 4. Known quality artifacts (all confirmed in the community/firmware ecosystem)

- **Color range:** washed-out/crushed-blacks is the #1 complaint and it is a real chip behavior. If the source outputs **RGB full-range over HDMI, these cards look washed out** ("blacks and whites being greyish"); the chip's internal processing "loses some of the discrete levels, so gradients might have more visible banding." [drakmor gist](https://gist.github.com/drakmor/ad681f7866b40325291b581fd8e7a71a). The MS2130 has "quite rare **full YUV range and FCC YUV coefficients**" — unusual colorimetry that needs LUT calibration for accuracy. [HyperHDR #499](https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperHDR/discussions/499). One $15-card reviewer's fix: OBS "Color Range = Full" + source HDMI "Limited." [naut.ca review](https://www.naut.ca/blog/2020/07/09/cheap-hdmi-capture-card-review/). A Taiwanese user found colors wrong on *both* range settings with an MS2130 (VID 345f:2130, "MACROSILICON USB3.0 UHD") and concluded the chip's signal processing is at fault. [ivonblog (Chinese)](https://ivonblog.com/posts/ms2130-usb-video-capture-card/). Net: the cheap cards **do not reliably deliver true full-range 0–255**, despite listings implying it.
- **Sharpening/softness:** MS2130 firmware applies **built-in sharpening that adds visible halos** around text/edges; two independent patchers exist just to disable it (steve-m/ms2130_patcher also disables scaling for "optimized video quality"; dvdavd/hagibis patch for the sharpening only). [ms2130_patcher](https://github.com/steve-m/ms2130_patcher) · [hagibis_ms2130_patch](https://github.com/dvdavd/hagibis_ms2130_patch)
- **Brightness dips:** "frequent sudden decrease in brightness in the captured stream, e.g. when switching HDMI sources"; image quality varies by firmware version. [HyperHDR #729](https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperHDR/discussions/729)
- **Thermals:** an eevblog user's card produced "screen flicker with some green lines when hot," fixed with a heatsink. [EEVblog teardown](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/reviews/4k-ultrahd-usb3-0-1080p-60fps-hdmi-capture-card-on-macrosilicon-ms2131-chip/)
- **Loop-out:** if the unit is an MS2131 with HDMI loop-out, the loop-out path is visually compressed/degraded — don't use it for quality monitoring. [EEVblog teardown](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/reviews/4k-ultrahd-usb3-0-1080p-60fps-hdmi-capture-card-on-macrosilicon-ms2131-chip/)
- Ghosting/temporal smearing as a *chip* defect: not reported as a distinct MS2130 issue — the temporal problems in this class are frame duplication (30→60), dropped frames on USB 2.0/weak controllers, and OBS/DWM pacing (see §6).

### 5. Practical quality ceiling vs premium cards

- **Elgato Cam Link 4K** (~$130, UVC stick): 4K30 (4:2:0) / 1080p60 (**4:2:2**), very low latency, "better color accuracy out of the box"; the mainstream comparison verdict is that generic $20 cards are "pretty much identical" to each other and close, but visibly behind on color/cleanliness. [Elgato specs](https://help.elgato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360028240951-Cam-Link-4K-Supported-Video-Sources) · [r/elgato 4:2:2 thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/elgato/comments/1c7ttbf/cam_link_4k_422_at_8bit_1080p/) · [The Verge: cheapest Cam Link clone "works pretty well"](https://www.theverge.com/21346571/cheap-cam-link-capture-card-streamer-clone-review-test-price) · [scottwyden comparison](https://scottwyden.com/cam-link-4k-vs-cheap-capture-card/) · [Paul Hibbert-style $20 vs Cam Link test (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AEPWqFdFvjw)
- **AVerMedia Live Gamer Portable 2+ / Razer Ripsaw HD / Elgato HD60 S+:** hardware H.264 recorders or 1080p60 UVC with passthrough; their value over this class is deterministic frame timing, color handling, and support — at 1080p60 the *pixel* quality gap vs a good MS2130 card is modest, the *reliability/color* gap is real.
- **Elgato 4K60 Pro (PCIe):** HDMI 2.0 in/out, 4K60 HDR10 capture; transfers uncompressed frames over **PCIe 2.0 x4** (encoding offloaded to host CPU/GPU), so there is no USB bandwidth ceiling, no MJPEG fallback, no 4:2:0-4K compromise. This is the true quality ceiling of the category — the generic card is not in the same class for resolution, HDR, chroma, or stability. [Elgato 4K60 Pro specs](https://help.elgato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360028240751-Elgato-Game-Capture-4K60-Pro-Technical-Specifications) · [Elgato 4K Capture Utility encoder docs](https://help.elgato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360027963752-Elgato-4K-Capture-Utility-Software-Interface) · [VentureBeat LG4K vs 4K60 Pro](https://venturebeat.com/pc-gaming/avermedia-live-gamer-4k-vs-elgato-4k60-pro-the-4k-capture-card-showdown/)

### 6. OBS fullscreen projector: pacing risk, not a fidelity limiter

- The projector path is the *recommended* way to feed a capture card (decouples game resolution/Hz from the card; "your gaming resolution and refresh rate are completely independent from what the capture card receives"). [Moonlight OBS capture-card guide](https://guides.moonlight.zip/guides/capture-card/obs-capture-card-setup-guide)
- **Known defects:** (a) Windows 11 fullscreen projector has *constant tearing* for dual-PC capture while the windowed projector does not — OBS issue #8866 (recurrence of #6936); (b) since OBS 28, an active fullscreen projector can drop the game monitor's effective output Hz (game "feels like 60Hz") — OBS issue #7716; (c) game FPS drops cycling down over time with an active projector; (d) preview/projector paths can run below 60 fps in some builds. [OBS #8866](https://github.com/obsproject/obs-studio/issues/8866) · [OBS #7716](https://github.com/obsproject/obs-studio/issues/7716) · [OBS forum: projector not 60fps](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/fullscreen-projector-preview-isnt-60fps.147914/) · [Tom's Hardware thread](https://forums.tomshardware.com/threads/obs-full-screen-projector-dropping-frames.3746539/)
- Key point: at 1080p60 the projector path is a **lossless digital copy at the same resolution** — it cannot soften or recolor the image. Its failure modes are tearing/pacing/frame drops. The card's failure modes are fidelity (chroma, range, sharpening) *and* pacing (USB2 fallback, 30→60 duplication, 50fps firmware).

## Conclusion: where is the bottleneck?

**The capture card is the real quality bottleneck in the GPU → OBS fullscreen projector → HDMI → card chain at 1080p60.** The projector path adds only frame-pacing/tearing risk (Windows 11 bug #8866 is the single most likely cause of "juddery" output in this chain), while the card caps everything about the *image*: 8-bit YUY2 4:2:2 chroma, chip sharpening/scaling halos, inconsistent limited/full range (washed-out or crushed-blacks), gradient banding, possible 30→60 frame duplication, and firmware-dependent frame rate (50 vs 60).

**Realistic expectations for the VIXLW (B0FW3K52JL) at 1080p60:**
- On a **true USB 3.x port** with a healthy USB controller: genuine UVC plug-and-play, YUY2 1080p60 at a real 60 fps, acceptable latency. Do NOT expect crispness or color accuracy beyond "good for streaming": expect slightly soft/oversharpened text, 4:2:2 chroma, and a color-range mismatch that you will have to fix (OBS range + the source's HDMI range, or the card's UVC brightness/contrast/saturation controls).
- On a **USB 2.0 port**: silent fallback to MJPEG (and effectively ~30 fps) — verify with OBS stats, a USB tree viewer, or `lsusb -t` (device should show SuperSpeed/5000 Mbps).
- If it ships as an **MS2109 with a blue USB 2.0 connector**: the "1080p60" is fake; you get 1080p30 MJPEG.
- If the OBS projector output is 30 fps (e.g., a 30 fps projector mode or low-power GPU setting), the card will duplicate frames into a fake 60 fps stream.
- Expect the occasional brightness dip on HDMI source switches and (on some firmware) 50 fps instead of 60.

## Gaps

- **No direct teardown of ASIN B0FW3K52JL.** Chip identity is inferred from (a) exact spec-sheet match with MS2130, (b) teardown of an identical aluminum body (MS2131). A `lsusb -v`/USB tree check (VID 345f, PID 2130/2131/2109) or a heatsink-off photo would confirm. If the unit negotiates SuperSpeed and offers 1080p60 YUY2, it is MS2130/2131; if USB 2.0-only, it is an MS2109 relabel.
- Amazon review corpus for this ASIN was not retrievable (bot-blocked); no verified user-sample of *this exact unit's* firmware version (50 vs 60 fps).
- No independent lab measurements of VIXLW-specific chroma/range behavior; MS2130-class behavior is used as the model.
- Suggested next step: verify on-device via OBS stats (fps + dropped frames), USB tree/SuperSpeed check, and a test pattern (e.g., a 0–255 gradient) to confirm range handling; if the 1080p60 YUY2 mode is missing, test another USB 3.x port/controller before blaming the card.

*Confidence note: findings 1–4 are HIGH-confidence (primary datasheets, teardown, kernel evidence, firmware repos); the VIXLW-specific identity and the OBS-projector-vs-card bottleneck ranking are MEDIUM-HIGH (mechanism-level evidence, not unit-level measurement).*
