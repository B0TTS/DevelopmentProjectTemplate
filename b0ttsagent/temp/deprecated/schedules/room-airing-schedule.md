# Room Airing-Out Schedule (Window + Box Fan)

**Context:** Central Oahu, ~100-200 sq ft sealed bedroom, black-mold remediation in progress. Dehumidifier (Midea Cube 20-pint) targeting 48% RH is the root-cause fix. This doc covers ONLY the airing-out / ventilation schedule — the dehumidifier runs 24/7 regardless of phase.

**Core physics:**
- Mold needs sustained RH >70% for 24-48 hrs to germinate. Brief spikes don't restart growth; sustained wet does.
- Airing out solves CO2/stuffiness (real brain-fog source) but brings in 76-90% RH tropical air — humidity penalty.
- Goal: get CO2 flush with minimum moisture ingress.

**Method (all phases):** Window wide + cheap box fan blowing **out** (exhaust mode) for negative pressure → fresh air pulled in through gaps/door → 3-5 full air exchanges in 10 min. Far more efficient than passive airing.

**Hygrometer is the law, not the clock:**
- RH <55% during airing → keep going
- RH hits 60% → shut within 30 min (1-hr buffer before germination threshold)
- RH hits 65%+ → shut now, run dehumidifier to recover under 50%

---

## Phase 1: Active Remediation (Weeks 0-3) — STRICTEST

The carpet/padding is currently a wet mold reservoir. Every open-window hour re-hydrates it and sets dehumidifier back ~12-24 hrs. Spore load is at peak — your symptoms are worst right now.

| When | Duration | Window | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning (wake up) | 10-15 min | Wide + box fan exhaust | Catches overnight CO2 peak (~1500-2000 ppm) |
| Evening (before bed) | 10-15 min | Wide + box fan exhaust | Clears day's VOC/CO2 before sealed sleep |
| Midday | Skip unless groggy | — | Only air out if you feel brain-foggy |
| **Total daily** | **~20-30 min** | | |

**Hard rules for this phase:**
- Never wide-open for more than 15 min at a stretch
- Never air out during rain (RH near 100%)
- Never air out at night during muggy season (Sept worst — outdoor RH 85%+)
- When you deep-clean the carpet (day 7-10ish), expect RH to spike 60%+ for 6-12 hrs after — dehumidifier on max, windows SHUT until back under 50%
- Hygrometer check every time before/during/after touching the window

**Day 0 (first day home):** Do NOT open the window until RH is under 55%, even if it takes 12-24 hrs of closed-room dehumidification. Room has been sealed and mold-farmed in your absence — dry it before you air it.

---

## Phase 2: 7 Days Post-Cleaning — CAUTIOUS REINTRODUCTION

You've earned slight flexibility: carpet is cleaned (bleach/Concrobium), RH has been sustained <50% for 7+ days, mold is presumed dead/dormant. The carpet is no longer a wet reservoir, so brief humid influxes don't feed anything.

| When | Duration | Window | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning | 15-20 min | Wide + box fan | Slightly longer ok if RH staying <55% |
| Evening | 15 min | Wide + box fan | |
| Midday (sunny/dry days only) | Optional 10-15 min | Wide | Midday outdoor RH is lowest — best window |
| **Total daily** | **~30-45 min** | | |

**Rules for this phase:**
- Still no wide-open overnight
- Still no airing during rain
- Hygrometer still governs: if RH >60% during airing, shut immediately
- If RH recovers to <50% within 6 hrs of airing, you're fine. If not, shorten sessions.

---

## Phase 3: 3 Weeks+ Post-Remediation (Steady-State) — FLEXIBLE

Carpet cleaned, RH sustained <50% for weeks, mold presumably dead, symptoms should be lifting (handoff says reassess at 4-6 weeks; if not improving, consider CIRS workup with a doctor). You're now in maintenance, not remediation.

| Situation | Allowed? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wide-open, midday sunny/dry day | Yes, 30-60 min | Best outdoor RH window |
| Wide-open, dry trade-wind day | Yes, 1-2 hrs | Check hygrometer hourly |
| Crack + box fan overnight | Yes | Dehumidifier keeps up with a crack |
| Wide-open all day on nice day | Yes, watch hygrometer | Close if RH >60% |
| Wide-open at night (muggy season, Sept) | Avoid | Outdoor RH 85%+, undoes work |
| Open during rain | No | RH near 100% |

**Steady-state rule:** Open windows as much as you want **as long as RH stays under 55-60%**. If it climbs, close and let the dehumidifier recover. You're no longer on lockdown — you're just monitoring.

**September is your enemy** (highest dew point, worst month per handoff). Expect to fall back to Phase 2 rules during Sept even after everything else is stable.

---

## General rules (all phases)

1. **Hygrometer placement:** 3-5 ft off ground, away from AC/dehumidifier output, near where you breathe (desk/bedside). Don't put it directly at an AC vent — reads falsely low.
2. **Target 48% RH** (not 50%) — gives 2% buffer so normal fluctuation doesn't cross mold threshold.
3. **Box fan direction:** blowing OUT the window (exhaust), not in. Creates negative pressure → fresh air gets pulled through gaps/door → full room exchange in 10 min vs 60+ min passive.
4. **Window AC vent lever (if present):** default CLOSED. Only open briefly on mild dry days. It's only ~10-20% outside air even fully open — not a real ventilation solution. Don't rely on it.
5. **AC dehumidifies as a byproduct** — "AC on + dehumidifier off" often fine during the day. Standalone dehumidifier earns its keep at night when AC is off for sleep.
6. **CO2 sensitive?** You won't know until RH is sustained <50% for 2+ weeks. Until then, prefer 2x daily 10-min air-outs over 1x longer session — same CO2 flush, less humidity penalty.

---

## When is airing out risky vs fine (quick reference)

| Time/condition | Risk | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nighttime, muggy season | High | Outdoor RH 80-90%+; no AC to offset |
| Rainy day | High | Sources wet, outdoor RH near 100% |
| Midday sunny dry day | Lowest | Outdoor RH may briefly dip to 60-70% |
| Trade wind day | Mixed | Better air-out but still carries moisture |
| Right after carpet cleaning | Don't | You're trying to dry the padding; airing undoes it |
