    There’s never been a better time to make it as a creator. Or a harder one to stay true to why you started.

    You want to make something meaningful.

    Document your journey. Build an audience obsessed with the way you organize your bookshelf and your point of view. And make enough money to fund the thing you love doing.

    But once you open your feed, the noise starts.

    Post daily. No, twice daily. Use this exact hook. Make faster cuts. Copy this viral format. Jump on this trend. Use AI to turn one video into 20. Post on YouTube. And Instagram. And TikTok. And Threads. And whatever platform launches tomorrow. Don’t get left behind. Do more. Scale faster. Move quicker.

    And if you burn out in the process…well, that’s just the price of success.

    Somewhere along the way, people became obsessed with making content over craft

    In the rush to “keep up,” it stopped being about what you make and became about how much you can produce. And the more you tumble down that digital rabbit hole, the more it feels like you have to choose:

    Create what performs or create what you love.
    Be seen by millions or be authentic.
    Feed the machine or feed your soul.

    It’s exhausting and enough to make you want to throw in the towel (or not start at all).

    What if you didn’t have to choose?

    While every creator is sprinting in the same direction…what if you did the opposite? 

    What if you didn’t have to burn out, trade quality for speed, or sacrifice your health or creativity to build something great? 

    What if you could just ‍ ‍go slow?

    Hey, I’m Matt D’Avella

    I’m an Emmy-nominated documentary filmmaker, YouTuber, and the guy who grew a channel to 4 million subscribers by talking about minimalism and wearing the same t-shirt in every video (yes, really).

    About Matt

    What I’ve Seen

    In the last decade I’ve scaled my creator business to a team of 11 (then let them all go), watched all sorts of trends come and go, and hustled and burnt out more times than I’d like to admit.

    I know exactly how it feels to find success and then convince yourself the only way to keep it is to work your face off.

    What I’ve Learned

    No amount of subscribers or external metrics is worth the burnout (I have the anxiety diagnosis and stress-induced hives to prove it).

    And as more creators flood the space and AI makes everyone sound the same, the only sustainable competitive advantage isn’t volume or speed. It’s mastering your craft, building real audience connection, and creating work that lasts.

    So what does being a slow creator look like  

    Posting once a year

    Being late to the party

    Turning your back on your ambition

    Making work you're proud of instead of making content for the algorithm

    Trusting there's room for more of YOU in your content, not an AI script

    Putting meaning over external metrics and building a body of work so good that people would follow you anywhere

    If reading that made you take a giant exhale, you’re in the right place.
