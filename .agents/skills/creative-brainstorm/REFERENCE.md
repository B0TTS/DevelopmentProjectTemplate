# Creative Brainstorm — Reference

Optional enrichment for `creative-brainstorm`. Read when you want a richer menu of move-kinds or notes on the reasoning layer. Nothing here is mandatory — the SKILL.md already states the bar.

## A richer menu of move-kinds

Kinds of moves that tend to satisfy the qualities (diversity across independent dimensions, novelty, defended ranking). These are *resources*, not a checklist — pick zero, one, or several per run; never run all of them in order.

**Reframe the problem**
- Invert the goal — optimize for the opposite, then pull back what's useful.
- Remove a constraint, imagine freely, then reintroduce it.
- Shift the timescale — a 10-second loop vs a 10-year arc.
- Shift the scale — one user vs a million; one instance vs a system.

**Borrow a mechanism**
- Import a working mechanism from another domain — what solves an analogous problem elsewhere?
- Substitute one component's role for another's.
- Adapt a structure that already proved itself somewhere adjacent.

**Recombine**
- Combine parts of two options into a hybrid.
- Put an existing thing to a different use.
- Modify one dimension deliberately; eliminate a different one.

The above restates two well-known lenses — SCAMPER's seven (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other use, Eliminate, Reverse) and a set of lateral triggers (analogy, constraint-flip, user-fantasy, time-shift, scale-shift) — as move-kinds. Use them as inspiration, never as a mandatory sequence.

## Reasoning layer — craft notes

The reasoning layer is the differentiator that gets *more* valuable as models improve. The hard part is delivering its value without the weight that heavy skills use (parallel subagents, scoring formulas, written journals). A few qualities to aim for:

- Name what you deliberately *varied* across the options — the independent dimensions, not surface labels.
- For at least some rejected directions, say *why* — what makes it a trap or a weaker fit. A reason per rejection beats a list of rejections.
- Tie each surviving option to the trade-off it optimizes. Options are commitments to a trade-off; say which.
- Defend the ranking with a sentence or two — including a brief case *against* your own favorite when it's clearly best (kill your darlings). No scoring formula required.

Keep it prose, not a table; honest, not universally positive. Length should track the options, not balloon past them.

## Provenance

The philosophy and bar borrow from several open-source ideation skills, restated as principles rather than adopted as procedures:
- "Differ in underlying mechanism, not just vocabulary" and the diverge-before-select stance — `scdenney/diverge-skills`, grounded in Ismayilzada et al., *Creative Preference Optimization* (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14442.
- "Cluster by angle, not surface keywords" and trap-list reasoning — `bhtru/adhd-claude`.
- The 4-part per-option shape and the "combine ideas?" closing invite — `brianharms/skill-propose`.
- Permissive tone and moves-as-a-menu — `ysskrishna/creative-thinking`.
- "Considered but excluded" reasoning and "why each was eliminated" — `LingJueYa/Commons_Plan_Prompt`, OrchestKit `brainstorm`.
- Kill-your-darlings ranking defense — `smixs/creative-director-skill`.

None of these fit the locked spec in combination; the bar above is the assembly, not any single source.
